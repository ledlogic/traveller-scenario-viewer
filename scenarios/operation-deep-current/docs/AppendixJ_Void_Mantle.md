### Combat Statistics

**Tentacle Strike (primary attack)**
- **Damage:** 2D on contact
- **Special:** Any tentacle contact triggers necrosis (see below); no attack roll required if target moves through tentacle curtain — contact is automatic
- **Range:** 12m (tentacle curtain radius below bell); the Void Mantle does not need to manoeuvre toward a target — the target enters the zone

**Necrosis (secondary effect)**
- Immediate 2D damage on contact
- **END 8+** within the same round to resist spreading: failure means 1D damage/hour for 1D6 hours as toxin spreads through tissue
- **Medic 8+** within 10 minutes to neutralise: success halts progression; failure means the hourly damage continues
- Sealed diving suit (HEV suit, atmospheric diving suit) provides full protection — tentacle toxin cannot penetrate; bare skin or standard wet suit offers no protection

**The Bloom (threat indicator)**
- When feeding or stressed, the bell surface activates deep violet-purple bioluminescence across its full surface
- Visible at 50m+ in dark water — the only reliable visual warning
- If agents see the bloom they are likely already within tentacle range; Athletics (dexterity) 10+ to back out without triggering contact

### Dealing with a Void Mantle — Diver on Foot

The Void Mantle is not aggressive in the way a predatory animal charges. It is passive — a net waiting to be walked into. This makes it more dangerous than an active predator because it does not announce itself.

| Approach | Mechanic | Result |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **Electrostatic deterrent rod** | Automatic on contact | Tentacles contract; mantle drifts away over 1D rounds; diver must exit range before it re-extends |
| **Maintain stillness, no bubbles** | Athletics (dex) 8+ each round | Mantle may not detect stationary rebreather-diver; wait for it to drift clear (1D × 5 minutes) |
| **Rapid exit** | Athletics (dex) 10+ | Exit tentacle zone before contact triggers; failure: 1D tentacle contacts, each triggering necrosis check |
| **Fire weapon into bell** | Gun Combat 8+ | 1D damage per hit; activates full bloom; may attract second Void Mantle within 2D × 10 minutes; sonar-detectable; not recommended |
| **Demolition charge** | Explosives 8+ | Kills mantle; chemical plume attracts other predators 1D hours; sonar-detectable event; harbour security alert likely |

### Void Mantle vs One-Person Submersible — Full Encounter Rules

This is the encounter that matters most for Operation REEF LIGHT. A single-crew survey submersible of the type used in this scenario is 3–5 metres in length, low-powered by design (survey work, not speed), and constructed of carbon-composite material — which creates a specific vulnerability profile distinct from a metal hull.

---

#### Phase 1 — Contact and Grip

A Void Mantle encountering a slow-moving submersible experiences it as a large, warm, vibrating prey object moving through its tentacle curtain. The tentacles wrap the hull instinctively. The occupant is protected from the toxin — polypeptide necrosis agents do not penetrate a sealed rigid hull — but the physical grip is real.

**Contact check:** On a roll of 5–6 on the REEF LIGHT Void Mantle encounter table, or whenever a submersible moves at Slow speed or below through an area where a Void Mantle has been detected (Recon 8+ failed):

Roll **Seafarer (submarine) 8+** (the pilot's reaction speed) — success means the pilot guns the engine before the tentacles fully wrap, requiring only a grip check (Phase 2). Failure means full tentacle wrap — proceed directly to Phase 2 with the mantle at full grip strength.

---

#### Phase 2 — The Grip: Held and Immobilised

Tentacles wrap the hull. The submersible is held. The pilot feels the vehicle stop responding to thrust inputs — the engine strains but the vehicle does not move.

**Contested hold:** Each round the pilot attempts to break free:

| Pilot attempts | Mechanic | Result |
|----------------|----------|--------|
| **Full engine thrust** | Seafarer (submarine) 8+ opposed by Void Mantle effective STR DM (+2) | **Success:** vehicle breaks free; mantle releases or tentacles tear (see Phase 4). **Failure:** 1D structural hull damage from stress at tentacle contact points; air/power supply clock advances |
| **Partial thrust — controlled strain** | Seafarer (submarine) 6+ | No hull damage this round; no progress toward escape; buys time for other solutions |
| **Cut engines entirely** | Automatic | No hull damage; vehicle drifts in mantle grip; mantle may release if no further stimulus — roll 1D each 10 minutes: 1–2 releases, 3–6 holds |
| **Asymmetric thrust (yaw/pitch while pulling)** | Seafarer (submarine) 10+ | On success: **DM+2 to next escape attempt**; torques tentacle wrap, reducing effective grip; on failure: 1D hull damage AND DM–1 to next check as tentacles tighten in response |

**The air supply clock:** A standard one-person survey submersible carries 4–6 hours of life support. Every 10 minutes spent held by the Void Mantle is 10 minutes off that clock. This is the real threat. The mantle does not need to crush the vehicle. It just needs to hold it.

---

#### Phase 3 — Carbon Composite Hull: The Specific Vulnerability

Here the hull material becomes a narrative and mechanical factor.

Standard metal hulls are inert in a Void Mantle grip. Carbon-composite hulls are not — not because the Void Mantle is feeding on them, but because its tentacle surface chemistry (optimised for organic tissue contact) interacts with the epoxy resin matrix of the composite material in a way that standard marine alloy does not experience.

**Short duration (under 30 minutes held):** No structural effect. The hull is strong enough to withstand the encounter without degradation.

**Extended duration (30–120 minutes held):** The tentacle contact chemistry begins micro-cracking the epoxy resin matrix at grip points — the same process MG-2023-15 produces through feeding, but faster due to the direct chemical contact with the tentacle surface.

| Duration held | Structural effect |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Under 30 minutes | None |
| 30–60 minutes | DM–1 to all subsequent hull integrity checks; safe depth rating reduced by 50m |
| 60–120 minutes | 2D structural damage; safe depth rating reduced by 200m; Mechanic 8+ check required before next deep approach or risk seal failure |
| Over 120 minutes | Hull integrity critically compromised; immediate surfacing required; Seafarer (submarine) 10+ to reach surface without catastrophic seal failure |

**Referee note:** The agents almost certainly do not know their hull is carbon-composite when they board Three-Fin's submersible. Ikaika would notice the construction material on inspection (Mechanic 8+ or Engineer (any) 6+). The revelation that the hull they are trapped inside is being slowly degraded by the creature holding them — the same process they just discovered happening to the Imperial cruiser rudders — is a scenario moment that should land hard.

---

#### Phase 4 — Breaking Free: What It Takes

There are five viable paths to freedom. Each has a cost.

**Path 1 — Electrostatic deterrent rod, externally deployed.**
The deterrent rod must be deployed through an external port or manipulator arm if the submersible has one. Survey submersibles in the Seaharvester equipment range typically include a sample collection arm — this can deploy the rod.
- **Mechanic:** Electronics (remote ops) 8+ or Mechanic 8+ to operate the arm under stress
- **Result:** Automatic release; mantle drifts away; pilot has 1D rounds to exit range at flank speed before tentacles re-extend toward the heat signature
- **Cost:** The rod is one-use. If the pilot does not have one — because it was deemed conspicuous equipment for a covert operation — this option is unavailable.

**Path 2 — Sustained engine escape, accepting hull damage.**
The brute-force option. Keep pushing. Eventually the tentacles tear or the mantle releases.
- **Mechanic:** Seafarer (submarine) 8+ each round, opposed by Void Mantle STR DM+2
- **Accumulated damage:** 1D hull damage per failed round
- **Success condition:** Three consecutive successes OR accumulated damage to the mantle from tentacle tear (treat as 1D damage to the mantle per successful escape attempt — at 0 Hits on tentacle cluster, grip releases)
- **Cost:** Hull damage. Necrosis clock if any seal breaches. Noise signature from engine strain.

**Path 3 — Attract a secondary stimulus.**
The Void Mantle's grip is maintained by predatory instinct responding to vibration and heat. A sufficiently compelling counter-stimulus — another heat and vibration source nearby — may draw its attention away.
- **Mechanic:** The pilot activates any external light or noise emitter (survey sonar ping, exterior lights at full, emergency beacon) — this creates a competing stimulus; roll 1D: 1–3 the mantle is drawn toward the new stimulus and releases; 4–6 it maintains grip
- **Cost:** Any external emission is sonar-detectable. Emergency beacon is unambiguously detectable. Sonar ping will register on New Mondulkiri naval base sensors. The pilot is trading tactical compromise for survival.

**Path 4 — Wait it out (engine off, patience).**
If the pilot has sufficient air supply remaining, cutting the engine and waiting is a viable option. The Void Mantle has no specific reason to maintain grip on a non-struggling, non-vibrating object indefinitely.
- **Mechanic:** Roll 1D every 10 minutes: 1–2 the mantle releases of its own accord; 3–6 it holds. After 30 minutes of stillness apply DM+1 cumulative per additional 10 minutes to the release roll.
- **Cost:** Air supply. Every 10 minutes is 10 minutes off the life support clock. And the hull degradation clock, if extended.

**Path 5 — Emergency ballast blow.**
Emergency surfacing. Flood the ballast tanks for maximum positive buoyancy. The vehicle rises rapidly — the Void Mantle, adapted for mid-shelf depths, may release rather than be dragged upward into shallower, brighter water it avoids.
- **Mechanic:** Seafarer (submarine) 8+ (automatic systems handle most of this, but the pilot must manage the rapid ascent safely). On success: vehicle rises; roll 1D: 1–3 mantle releases within 20m of ascent; 4–5 mantle holds to 10m depth then releases; 6 mantle holds all the way to surface (extreme — large Void Mantle)
- **Cost:** Mission is over. The submersible is surfacing in or near a naval harbour. Decompression risk if ascent is too rapid (END 8+ to avoid decompression effects if ascent covers more than 30m in under 1 minute). Cover is potentially blown depending on location.

---

#### The Defeat Condition: What Victory Looks Like

Defeating a Void Mantle in a submersible encounter is not killing it — it is escaping it. The creature is not malicious; it is predatory. Once released or evaded, it returns to passive ambush. A pilot who escapes cleanly, with hull intact, air supply adequate, and no sonar signature, has won completely. A pilot who surfaces in a naval harbour after an emergency ballast blow has survived but the mission may be over.

The encounter is designed so that the *best* outcome requires discipline and preparation (deterrent rod carried despite being conspicuous, pilot maintains stillness rather than panicking), and every escalating response creates a worse secondary problem:

- Fire a weapon → bloom activates → sonar signature → second Void Mantle potential
- Force thrust → hull damage → degraded safe depth → seal risk
- Emergency beacon → naval sensors detect → cover compromised
- Emergency surface → mission over → decompression risk

**The moment of maximum drama** is when the pilot realises the hull is carbon-composite, the air clock is running, and every option for escape has a cost they haven't fully calculated yet. That is the encounter working as intended.

### Void Mantle as Environmental Narrative

The Void Mantle should rarely be a straight combat encounter. In the submersible context it is a sustained pressure sequence where every decision has a cost and the worst outcomes come from panic rather than from the creature itself. The pilot who stays calm, conserves air, and deploys the deterrent rod they remembered to bring survives cleanly. The pilot who guns the engine and activates the emergency beacon survives — but survives loudly, in a naval harbour, with a compromised hull and a Naval Intelligence response inbound.

The bloom is the scenario's most effective single image. Violet-purple light igniting in the dark water around a vehicle the pilot cannot move, while the air gauge ticks down and Ikaika says quietly from the co-seat that he thinks the hull might be composite — that is the encounter working exactly as intended.

### Miniature Recommendations

The Void Mantle requires a large-base, translucent or near-translucent bell form with trailing tentacle elements. It is not a standard fantasy or sci-fi creature — it reads as a giant jellyfish with the scale and menace turned up considerably. The following options are recommended in approximate order of suitability:

---

**Option 1 — Best fit for scale and form: Monstrous Jellyfish by Mini Monster Mayhem**
- **Platform:** MyMiniFactory
- **URL:** myminifactory.com/object/3d-print-monstrous-jellyfish-144807
- **Why it works:** Large bell, dense trailing tentacle mass, designed for bottom-of-ocean-floor placement. Scales well to represent a 2m-bell creature at 28mm tabletop scale. Reviewed as printing easily with good support files.
- **Printing note:** Print bell in clear/transparent resin (Elegoo ABS-Like Clear or Siraya Tech Blu Clear) for the Void Mantle's near-invisible appearance. Paint tentacles with extremely thin ghost-white or silver dry-brush; leave bell unpainted or with a single thin violet wash for the bloom effect.

---

**Option 2 — Good alternative with more dramatic pose: Flumpf Jellyfish by Epic-Miniatures**
- **Platform:** Cults3D
- **URL:** cults3d.com/en/3d-model/game/flumpf-jellyfish-dnd-miniature-tabletop-miniatures-gaming-monster-3d-model-rpg-dndminis-stl-file
- **Why it works:** Epic-Miniatures consistently produces high-detail 28mm-scaled creatures with good tentacle rendering. The Flumpf Jellyfish form factor is close to the Void Mantle's medusan profile. Pre-supported files available.
- **Printing note:** Same clear resin recommendation. The model scales well — print at 150–200% of default 28mm to represent the 2m+ bell at table scale.

Good for a referee who wants to represent the creature's overwhelming scale — print at 300%+ and use as a terrain piece rather than a miniature.
- **Printing note:** At large scale use FDM filament in clear PETG; at standard mini scale use resin.

- Note: search "Wizkids Deep Cuts Jellyfish" at your preferred miniature retailer.

---

**Painting guidance for any option — representing the Void Mantle:**
- **Bell:** Clear/transparent base. No base coat. Optional: one thin wash of Citadel Contrast Aethermatic Blue diluted 50% with medium to suggest the faint refraction. The bell should look like nothing — glass in water.
- **Tentacles:** Extremely thin silver or ghost-white dry-brush. Then a wash of Nihilakh Oxide (Citadel) or equivalent pale teal to suggest the bioluminescent light pickup from reef columns. Hair-fine and barely visible.
- **The bloom:** On the bell's upper surface, stipple tiny points of Citadel Contrast Magos Purple or Volupus Pink, thinned to near-transparency. Build from the centre outward. The bloom should look like it is activating — not fully on, not off. Use UV-reactive paint if available for a table effect under a blacklight.
- **Base:** Dark oceanic blue-black. Optional: a few fragments of reef lantern column (grey-white resin pieces, dry-brushed with fluorescent blue-green glow effect) to suggest the deep reef environment.
