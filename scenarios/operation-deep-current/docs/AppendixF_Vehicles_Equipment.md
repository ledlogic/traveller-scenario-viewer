### SUBMERSIBLE MODIFICATION
*From Traveller Water Vehicles Volume 1, p.13 — vehicle modification rules*

Any Ground Vehicle, Grav Vehicle, or Watercraft can have the Submersible option added at time of construction. Usually combined with an Aquatic Drive and Life Support. Hostile Environment Protection required; Corrosive Environment Protection required if submerging in corrosive or insidious fluid (relevant to Hiroshima's hydrochloric seas).

- **Cost:** Doubles Cost per Space; consumes 10% of vehicle's total Spaces (rounding up)
- **Speed:** One Speed Band lower than equivalent Submersible of same TL
- **Range:** Reduced to 10% of base
- **Safe Depth / Crush Depth:** Half of equivalent Submersible

**Scenario relevance:** Any surface vessel operating in Gashidda's mildly acidic ocean that requires occasional submersion (e.g. Seaharvester LIC's extraction platforms, the Crab-class equivalent) should be built with this modification plus Corrosive Environment Protection — the supplement explicitly calls this out.

The Microwheel is a standard **sample collection canister with integrated micro-life-support**, designed for transporting living biological specimens from deep-sea environments to surface or shipboard laboratory conditions. The name comes from the tiny impeller wheel that circulates the internal water column.

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 22cm |
| Height | 35cm |
| Volume | 8 litres (water) + 2 litre headspace |
| Material | Clear polycarbonate shell, stainless steel end-caps |
| Pressure rating | 600m (equalised with external during dive; sealed at retrieval) |
| Life support | Micro-oxygenator (battery powered, 72-hour runtime) |
| Temperature control | Passive thermal insulation only — no active cooling |
| Lighting | 2× blue-spectrum LED (stimulates photosynthesis in plant/algae specimens) |
| Sensor | Basic: temperature, O2 level, pH |
| Battery | 72 hours micro-oxygenator runtime — CR2 equivalent cell |
| Mass | 0.8kg empty / 9kg full |
| Cost | Cr 400 standard; Cr 1,200 research grade with active cooling |

**Primary Use Case — Driftweb Samples:** The Microwheel was originally designed for soft-tissue marine invertebrate transport, but has been adopted as the standard container for Driftweb atmospheric filament samples (see Section L.11) because its blue-spectrum LED maintains photosynthesis and the micro-oxygenator prevents anoxic collapse during transport. Driftweb in a Microwheel stays viable for approximately 48 hours before filament integrity degrades.

**Clogging Note:** Driftweb in *individual filament* form transports cleanly in the Microwheel. Driftweb that has matted — formed into the web-mat configuration — **clogs the micro-oxygenator impeller within 4–6 hours**, killing the specimen and destroying the container's seal if not cleared. This is the primary technical challenge in Driftweb research transport (see Section L.11).

## L.10 — VOID MANTLE ENCOUNTER MECHANICS FOR SUBMERSIBLES

*(Cross-reference: Operation Deep Current Gashidda Bestiary — Void Mantle full profile)*

The Void Mantle's predatory grip on a small submersible is one of the distinctive hazard scenarios in the Deep Current campaign. This section provides consolidated game mechanics for referee use.

### Can the Void Mantle Crush a Carbon-Composite Hull?

**Short answer: No — on the timescale of an encounter.**

The Void Mantle (STR 14) generates significant constriction force through tentacle wrap. A carbon-composite hull is designed to withstand ocean pressure at 400m depth — approximately 40 atmospheres, or 580 psi. A STR 14 animal applying constriction force generates perhaps 2–4 atmospheres of localised pressure. This is not sufficient to structurally compromise a properly manufactured composite hull in the timeframe of a combat encounter.

**What the Void Mantle can do:**

| Action | Mechanic | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Tentacle wrap | Opposed STR 14 vs Seafarer (submarine) 8+ | Success: held — submersible cannot move |
| Sustained hold | Automatic if initial wrap succeeds | Submersible is immobile; pilot's air supply is running |
| Torque | If grip is asymmetric | Submersible may be slowly rotated — disorienting, not damaging |
| Force-drive stress | If pilot applies full thrust against grip | 1D structural damage per round at full thrust |
| Sustained chemical contact | 4+ hours tentacle contact on hull | Begin micro-cracking process (1 Structure damage per 4 hours — slow, cosmetic initially) |

**The real danger is not crushing — it is immobilisation.** A Void Mantle holding a Crab-class submersible at 200m depth, in absolute darkness, with a 6-hour air supply now counting down faster because the pilot is stressed and breathing harder, is a deeply threatening situation with no violence involved.

### Breaking Free

| Situation | Mechanic | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot attempts thrust | Seafarer (submarine) 8+ | Success: breaks free. Failure: 1D structural damage and still held |
| Manipulator arm strikes tentacle | Athletics (dexterity) 8+ for the arm | Causes tentacle withdrawal — creature releases. Only works once (it learns) |
| External loud noise/sonar pulse | Electronics (sensors) 8+ | Void Mantle startled — releases; 30-second window before reaggravation |
| Wait out feeding instinct | 2D×10 minutes | Void Mantle eventually loses interest and releases |
| Damage the creature | See Void Mantle stats | At 50% Hits remaining, creature releases and retreats |

### Shrink-and-Thicken: Tentacle Mechanics

The Void Mantle's tentacles can **shrink and thicken as they retract**, converting the small cilia that line each tentacle from sensory/swimming organs into constriction-assist muscles. This is the mechanism by which a soft-bodied medusan organism achieves the grip strength to hold a submersible.

**Game effect:** On the round the Void Mantle transitions from initial contact to active constriction, the tentacle diameter increases from ~2cm to ~6cm and the grip STR effectively increases from 14 to 18 as the cilia-to-muscle conversion completes. If the pilot does not break free in the first round, subsequent opposed rolls are against STR 18, not 14.

**Reversal:** The tentacles re-extend at the original diameter if the creature is startled, damaged, or loses interest. The STR drops back to 14 as the cilia re-extend.

**Necrosis toxin:** The Void Mantle's tentacle cells deliver necrotic compounds on contact with organic tissue. Against a carbon-composite hull, no effect on encounter timescale. Against an unprotected diver in the water alongside the submersible, standard toxin rules apply: 2D damage per round of sustained contact, END 8+ each round to avoid secondary tissue necrosis (ongoing 1D/hour for 6 hours if failed).

## L.11 — DRIFTWEB — ATMOSPHERIC FILAMENT ORGANISM

*(Primary ecology: Gashidda upper atmosphere. Cross-reference: Operation Deep Current supplement.)*

*The Driftweb was first identified as a distinct organism type on Gashidda, but the ecological niche — atmospheric filamentous algae forming extensive drift networks — has since been identified on several worlds in the subsector with comparable atmospheric conditions. The following section consolidates game-relevant properties across all contexts.*

### Biological Description

Driftweb is a **filamentous photosynthetic organism** — technically a colonial cyanobacterial mat that has evolved to maintain altitude through aerodynamic adaptation rather than gas bladders or active propulsion. Individual filaments range from 0.1mm to 3mm in diameter and 2cm to 80cm in length. They interlink electrostatically to form larger structures that can range from gossamer wisps to dense mats several metres across.

**The Bernoulli wing mechanism:** Each individual filament cilia is shaped in cross-section as a micro-scale asymmetric aerofoil — the classic Bernoulli wing profile, generating lift on the upper surface relative to the lower. This is why Driftweb maintains altitude: millions of microscopic wing-blade cilia, each generating fractional lift, add up to a network that is genuinely aerodynamically supported rather than simply drifting on thermals. The network actively orients itself — the colony responds to light gradients and adjusts its collective cilia angle to optimise altitude and solar exposure.

### Physical Properties

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour | Grey-green (atmospheric aggregate); bright green (sparse, individual filaments) |
| Altitude range | 2–12km (concentration peak at 4–8km on Gashidda) |
| Density | Variable: wisps (near-transparent) to dense mats (visibility through mat: 0–5m) |
| Buoyancy mechanism | Bernoulli cilia arrays — aerodynamic lift, not gas |
| Tensile strength (filament) | ~0.3N/mm² — comparable to wet spider silk |
| Mat tensile strength | Scales with thickness; dense mat resists ~2N/cm² before tearing |
| Flammability | Low — high water content; burns reluctantly at ground level, more readily at altitude |
| Electrical conductivity | Low baseline; some mat formations develop static charge from cilia friction |

### Flight Hazards

Driftweb poses **three distinct hazards to aircraft**:

**1. Drag and visibility**
Dense mat formation creates significant aerodynamic drag on small aircraft — comparable to flying through light fog in terms of lift interference. Visibility through a dense mat is 0–5m. Standard aircraft sensors are degraded (see below). High-speed passage through a mat tears it but distributes the filaments across the airframe, where they continue to accumulate.

**2. Engine ingestion**
*Individual filaments:* Acceptable for most TL9+ engines — the filaments pass through turbine stages without significant fouling. Short exposure, clean.
*Mat formations:* A dense mat ingested by a jet engine will clog compressor stages within 30–90 seconds. Thrust drops by 30–70% depending on mat density. The mat does not burn — it compacts. Recovery requires engine shutdown and manual clearing. In flight, this typically means one engine operating while the pilot decides whether to continue or divert.

**3. Sensor impediment**
Driftweb's cilia create both physical occlusion and electromagnetic scatter effects:

| Density | Sensor DM | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Wisp/sparse | -1 | Mild scatter; sensors work but range is reduced |
| Moderate | -2 | Significant scatter; radar/lidar range halved |
| Dense mat | -3 | Heavy scatter; effective sensor range 20% of normal |
| Full mat penetration | -4 | Sensors effectively blind; only passive IR works (degraded) |

These modifiers apply to active sensors (radar, lidar, sonar equivalent). Passive thermal/IR sensors are degraded less severely (-1 to -2 for moderate/dense). This makes dense Driftweb formations **natural sensor-degraded zones** — with tactical implications (see below).

### Tactical Value

**Military stealth:** Aircraft flying through or below a dense Driftweb layer are effectively masked from ground-based or low-altitude radar. The -3 to -4 sensor modifier at the layer boundary means a small aircraft (already a marginal return) becomes essentially undetectable from outside. Gashidda's independence movement has exploited this — the Driftweb layer is why their covert courier flights between islands are not routinely tracked by Imperial sensor networks.

**ECM value:** A Driftweb mat in proximity to an active sensor array creates natural electronic countermeasure effects — the scatter pattern is irregular and cannot be reliably compensated for by standard filters. Treat a dense mat within 500m of a sensor installation as providing DM-2 to all active sensors in addition to any range degradation. This is *passive* ECM — it requires no power and cannot be jammed or countered except by repositioning the sensor above or below the mat.

**The mat clogging problem:** The military stealth and ECM properties of Driftweb make it commercially and tactically interesting. The obstacle to exploitation is mat formation and transport — Driftweb in individual filament form is manageable (see Microwheel, Section L.9) but the moment it forms mats, it clogs everything: engine intakes, processing equipment, sample containers, and the atmosphere processor inlet stages described in Section L.12. This is the primary engineering bottleneck preventing industrial-scale Driftweb deployment.

### Ecological Role

On worlds where Driftweb is present, it is typically a **primary oxygen producer** — the atmospheric algae layer contributes significantly to the O₂ balance of thin-atmosphere worlds. On Gashidda, the grey-green sky colour is partly attributable to the Driftweb layer filtering the G8 IV star's light.

## L.12 — ATMOSPHERE PROCESSOR: HARVESTER CONFIGURATION

*Private Aerospace Development — IHJEA Architecture*

### The IHJEA Design

The atmosphere processor described here is an early-development-stage system designed to harvest Driftweb biomass from the 4–8km altitude layer and process it into usable biofuel. The system follows the **IHJEA** architecture:

```
INLET → HARVESTER → (PFR Reactor) → JET → EXHAUST → AFTERBURNER
  I         H              J          E        A
```

**Full expansion:**
- **I — Inlet:** Wide-mouth scoop, designed to collect the Driftweb filament/mat layer during atmospheric transit
- **H — Harvester:** Initial separation and compression stage — removes water, compacts filament mass for the reactor feed
- **PFR Reactor (implicit):** Plug Flow Reactor — continuous-feed pyrolysis/gasification of compressed filament biomass into syngas and lipid fraction
- **J — Jet:** The aircraft's propulsion stage — runs on blended conventional fuel + syngas output from the PFR. The jet is what keeps the harvester platform aloft and moving through the Driftweb layer
- **E — Exhaust:** Syngas and thermal exhaust from the PFR stage, separate from the jet exhaust
- **A — Afterburner:** Secondary combustion of remaining organic fraction from the PFR exhaust — improves efficiency and reduces particulate output

### Funding and Development

The IHJEA system is funded by **Ling Standard Products (LSP)**, the Imperial-aligned megacorporation with a significant manufacturing presence across the Solomani Front worlds. LSP's rationale is straightforward: frontier worlds with thin atmospheres (Code 3–5) and Driftweb analogs represent a significant untapped biofuel resource that does not compete with petrochemical or fusion markets — it is an *additive* fuel source for remote installations. The IP value of a working IHJEA system is potentially enormous.

*(Alternative private aerospace companies: Sternmetal Horizons LIC, Ling Standard Products, McClellan Factors — GM should choose one consistent with their campaign's corporate landscape.)*

### The Mat Clogging Problem

*This is the primary engineering obstacle to production use.*

Individual Driftweb filaments process cleanly through the IHJEA stages — the inlet scoop catches them efficiently, the harvester compresses them, the PFR cracks them into syngas and lipid fractions at reasonable efficiency (approximately 40% conversion by mass). Development bench tests with filament-form Driftweb have been promising.

The problem is that Driftweb does not stay in filament form at the densities required for efficient harvesting. At the concentrations where the biomass yield makes the IHJEA system economically viable, the filaments are dense enough to mat together — and mat-form Driftweb **clogs the harvester inlet within 4–12 minutes of operation**, depending on mat density. The mat's tensile strength is sufficient to resist the compressor's shredding action, forming a packed plug at the harvester inlet that can only be cleared by shutting down the system and manually removing the blockage.

**Solutions under development:**

| Approach | Status | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Rotating inlet screen | Tested | Screen itself clogs; replacement frequency too high |
| High-pressure water wash | Tested | Effective for clearing; water mass penalty is prohibitive at altitude |
| Ultrasonic disruption | Experimental | Disrupts mat structure but doesn't prevent re-matting in the harvester |
| Selective altitude operation | Field testing | Operating at the *top* of the Driftweb layer (7–8km) where density is lower reduces clogging but reduces yield |
| Anti-matting surfactant spray | Theoretical | Surfactant in the inlet zone inhibits electrostatic binding; not yet tested at full scale |

### Game Use

The IHJEA processor is primarily background in the Operation Deep Current scenario — it explains why Seaharvester LIC has atmospheric operations in addition to underwater harvesting, and provides the economic context for why the Driftweb layer is a contested resource. In play, an IHJEA-equipped harvester platform is a:

- **Fast, high-altitude transport** capable of crossing between islands without using the starport or standard traffic corridors (radar DM-3 to -4 in the Driftweb layer)
- **Sensor-shadow vehicle** — operating inside the Driftweb layer means it cannot easily be tracked and has degraded sensor reception itself (DM-2 while in the layer)
- **Vulnerable platform** — a mat-clog event at the wrong moment means a dead engine at altitude; the pilot has 90 seconds to either clear the inlet or initiate emergency descent

### What Hiroshima Contributed to the Gashidda / Operation Deep Current Campaign

The Hiroshima supplement was the primary design reference for the **Gashidda arcology and underwater world concept** in Operation Deep Current. The following elements were drawn from or inspired by it:

**Corporate power structure:**

| Hiroshima Element | Gashidda / Deep Current Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Combine of megacorporations (Erebus, Matsuyama, Tharsis, Wu-Ketai, Voroncovo) — no single entity controls everything | Gashidda arcology governance: Imperial-aligned mega-consortium with competing sector interests, no single colonial authority |
| Shinsei Orbital Station — Class-B starport, spindle-and-ring, patched-together industrial aesthetic | Gashidda orbital infrastructure: similar functional-over-aesthetic design philosophy; decontamination chambers due to atmospheric hazard |
| JDS Amaterasu — heavy patrol cruiser, Compliance Protocol, watching the colony as much as defending it | Imperial naval presence at Gashidda: ship with sealed orders and political oversight officer whose authority exceeds the CO |
| JADF garrison with dual civil/military authority tension (Col. Endō vs Lt. Col. Kitamura) | Imperial ground forces at Gashidda: same internal tension between procedural CO and aggressive XO |
| Tier-2 actors: FSE, Dockside Technical Guild, Long Marchers — operate in gaps between megacorps | Gashidda's Tier-2 equivalent: Seaharvester LIC, independent dive crews, the Marine Science Institute as neutral ground |
| Kuro-Hoshi Syndicate — orbital cargo diversion, modified utility shuttles for sensor-blind extraction (`[HC]` p.27) | Three-Fin Valdez and her Highline-class submersible: same role, underwater instead of orbital — modified utility vessel, routes that don't appear on official charts |
| Tōrō (Lantern) Network — interstellar intelligence brokers, contract reputation checks | Gashidda information brokers: similar function, different name |
| Yellow Mist Front (YMF) eco-insurgents — anti-corporate environmental extremists | Vanthara Truth movement: different theology, same structural role as the conscience of the colonial world vs exploitation |

| Hiroshima Element | Gashidda / Deep Current Adaptation |
|---|---|

### What Hiroshima Does NOT Provide for This Scenario Suite

- **MGT2e rules:** Hiroshima is Cepheus Engine. All rules cited in this scenario suite use CRB22 (Mongoose Traveller 2nd Ed). Hiroshima is a setting/world reference only.
- **Solomani Front politics:** Hiroshima is set in the Hostile near-future (Earth-based expansion, ~2225 CE) — a completely different setting from the Third Imperium 1117 CE context of Operation Deep Current. No canon overlap.
- **Gashidda sea creatures:** The Hiroshima bestiary covers highland, forest, and desert fauna. The underwater ecology of Gashidda (Void Mantle, Reef Lantern, Gikhi, Deep Bell, Shelf Architect, Thermal Angel, Driftweb) is original content developed for Operation Deep Current.
