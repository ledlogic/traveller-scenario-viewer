## Driftweb, Reef Consumer, Void Mantle, and Full Organism Reference

---

**World:** Gashidda (UWP: A56A969-E) · Dingir Subsector · Solomani Rim  
**Environment:** Cool water world; equatorial reef shelf (depth 20m–4,000m); lower atmosphere (0–12km)  
**Compiled by:** Makara Arcology Marine Science Institute, Imperial Survey Branch  
**Classification standard:** Third Imperium Xenobiological Registry (TIXReg)

---

> *The equatorial seas of Gashidda are among the most biologically productive ocean systems in the Dingir subsector. The atmosphere above them is not empty. Colonisation and military occupation have left the deep reef largely unstudied and the atmospheric biosphere almost entirely uncharacterised. What follows represents current knowledge — which is to say, a fraction of what lives here.*

---

## ECOLOGICAL OVERVIEW

Gashidda's biosphere operates across two domains that most inhabited worlds treat as entirely separate: the ocean and the sky. On Gashidda they are connected. The same biological productivity that drives the deep reef ecosystem generates the atmospheric layer of filamentous microorganisms — the Driftweb — that gives the sky its characteristic grey-green colour and filters the already-dim light of the G8 IV primary into something dimmer still.

Gashidda's oceans are cold, oxygenated, and extraordinarily rich. The equatorial reef shelf — the same geological formation on which Makara and the other undersea arcologies are built — supports a layered ecosystem running from sunlit shallows down to abyssal trench systems at 6,000+ metres. The reef itself is a living structure: a composite organism of bioluminescent colonial polyps, mineral-secreting filter feeders, and chemosynthetic bacteria that convert deep geothermal vents into biological energy.

The dominant colour palette of Gashidda's reef is **blue-green bioluminescence** against absolute darkness, punctuated by the amber and orange of vent-adjacent thermal communities and the pale silver of open-water predators reflecting whatever light exists. Visitors descending from the arcologies into external observation bays frequently describe the experience as looking into a living sky — the reef columns rising like inverted mountains, trailing light.

Four ecological zones are relevant to this bestiary:

| Zone | Altitude/Depth | Light | Character |
|------|---------------|-------|-----------|
| **Atmospheric Layer** | 2–12km altitude | Filtered starlight | Filamentous microorganism suspension; Driftweb |
| **Shelf Shallows** | 0–200m depth | Filtered surface light | Dense, competitive, diverse; most food production |
| **Mid-Shelf** | 200–1,500m depth | Bioluminescent only | The primary reef column ecosystem; apex predators |
| **Deep Vent Fields** | 1,500m–4,000m depth | None (chemosynthetic) | Sparse, ancient lineages; poorly understood |

## SPECIES ENTRIES

### 00 · DRIFTWEB
*Gashidda Atmospheric Filament Algae · Filamentophyta atmospherica gashiddana*

**Ecological Zone:** Lower and mid atmosphere (2–12km altitude)  
**Size:** Individual filaments 0.1–4mm diameter, 2–80cm length; mat structures up to several metres across  
**Behaviour:** Passive suspension; photosynthetic; colonial aggregation in thermal updraft zones  
**Threat Level:** None (non-motile microorganism)  
**Commercial Value:** ★★★★☆ — Emerging biofuel feedstock; significant industrial interest at TL E

---

**Description:**

The Driftweb is the most visible component of Gashidda's atmospheric biosphere and the organism most directly responsible for the world's characteristic grey-green sky. It is not a single species but a complex of related filamentous photosynthetic microorganisms — cyanobacteria-analogs and single-celled algae producing long-chain polymer strands — that collectively form a semi-continuous biological layer in Gashidda's lower and middle atmosphere.

Individual Driftweb filaments are translucent to pale green, 0.1–4mm in diameter and ranging from a centimetre to nearly a metre in length. Under magnification they are visibly segmented, with regular nodes where the organism produces lipid-rich reproductive bodies — small amber beads visible to the naked eye in dense concentrations. The filaments are slightly charged, causing them to orient loosely along atmospheric electrical field lines and aggregate into the mat structures visible from the surface as the characteristic grey-green overcast. In direct light — when breaks in the cloud cover allow the star to illuminate a section of the Driftweb layer — the mats appear as slow-moving silver-green veils, sometimes trailing individual long filaments below them like the undersides of jellyfish.

The Driftweb's biological relationship to the ocean ecosystem below is not yet fully characterised, but the working hypothesis of the Makara Marine Science Institute is that it represents an extension of the ocean biosphere into the atmosphere rather than an independent ecosystem. Updraft currents carry spores and fragments from wave-surface foam into the lower atmosphere, where the organisms reproduce and form aggregations; precipitation eventually returns biological material to the ocean, where it enters the reef food chain. The entire system functions as a planetary-scale pump, fixing atmospheric carbon through photosynthesis and delivering organic material to the ocean below.

**The lipid fraction:** Driftweb filaments are approximately 18–24% lipid by dry weight — a consequence of the organisms' need to maintain structural integrity and buoyancy in the atmospheric layer. This lipid fraction is chemically similar to medium-chain triglyceride oils used in standard biofuel production and can be processed into a usable aviation and atmospheric fuel feedstock using TL 9+ biorefinery equipment. At TL E, onboard biocracker units capable of processing harvested Driftweb material in real time during flight are entirely feasible.

**Scoop-jet harvesting:** The logical exploitation model at TL E involves atmospheric vehicles fitted with wide-mouth intake scoops that collect Driftweb material during flight through the 4–8km altitude band where concentrations are highest, feeding it to onboard bioprocessing units that crack the lipids into fuel and discharge the remaining biological matter (primarily the cellulose-analog structural polymer, which is biologically inert) as a fine mist. A sufficiently efficient scoop-jet skimmer can theoretically achieve energy-neutral or net-positive fuel production on long atmospheric transits over Gashidda — harvesting more fuel from the Driftweb than it consumes flying through it.

The technology exists and is understood. What does not yet exist is commercial infrastructure at scale. The Makara Marine Science Institute has published preliminary yield data. Ling Standard Products has filed three provisional patents on Gashidda-specific biorefinery designs. The Seaharvester LIC business development team has produced an internal feasibility assessment describing Driftweb harvesting as "the single most underexploited economic opportunity on Gashidda." The Imperial military administration has not yet regulated the atmospheric harvesting sector because it has not yet understood that it needs to. This window will not remain open indefinitely.

**Political and tactical dimensions:** The Driftweb layer is difficult to monitor. Conventional ship and vehicle traffic sensors lose resolution in the atmospheric algae layer, particularly at 6–10km altitude where the mat density is highest. A scoop-jet skimmer operating in that band — legitimately harvesting Driftweb or otherwise — is substantially harder to track on sensors than a vehicle operating in clear air. This is not lost on the resistance networks. The practical range of scoop-jet vehicles between Gashidda's scattered islands, flying through the Driftweb rather than above it, is a surveillance gap the Imperial Army has identified but not yet resolved.

**Vanthara Truth significance:** The Driftweb has been incorporated into Vanthara Truth theology with a directness that surprised Imperial cultural analysts when they first encountered it in movement literature. The theological argument is elegant: the Driftweb breathes the same atmosphere as Gashidda's people. It is sustained by the same star — a distant, cool echo of the First Sky's sun, but the same category of thing. When practitioners speak of *breathing what Terra breathed* (the Second Breath theology), the Driftweb is the visible proof that the atmosphere itself is alive and participating. To breathe filtered, arcology-processed air — air that has passed through the same biological layer as the Driftweb — is to participate in a planetary communion. Filter masks worn in the lower industrial tiers acquire a specifically painful valence in this context: to require a mask is to be separated from the breathing world by the occupation's industrial legacy.

The Wheel of Earth movement takes this further. In Wheel theology the Driftweb is a manifestation of the Returning Current in the atmosphere — life completing a cycle from ocean to sky and back to ocean, a visible demonstration that the pattern of return is real and operating at every scale. Some Wheel practitioners maintain small Driftweb cultivation vessels — sealed glass containers with a small amount of the organism growing in a nutrient medium — as meditative objects. The slow growth of the filaments over days and weeks is contemplated as an analogue for the patient work of the Cause.

**Game Notes:** The Driftweb has no direct mechanical interaction with characters at the table. It is texture, atmosphere (literally and figuratively), and world-building infrastructure. Three specific uses:

*Visual:* When the scenario calls for an exterior shot of Gashidda from approach, from orbit, or from the surface, the Driftweb is what the characters see — the grey-green overcast that never fully clears, the slow movement of mat structures visible from the arcology's upper-tier observation decks on still days, the occasional silver veil catching light through a break in the cloud.

*Navigational:* Any scene involving atmospheric flight or vehicle transit should note that the Driftweb layer at 4–8km provides sensor cover. A chase scene, an extraction flight, or an emergency transit could legitimately use the Driftweb layer as cover — at the cost of some navigational precision and at the risk of engine fouling if intake filters are not maintained.

*Plot hook:* Seluun Mian-Ashta's research interests, as noted in the scenario, extend to novel organic chemistry. The Driftweb lipid fraction has properties — specifically its atmospheric stability across the temperature and pressure range of Gashidda's weather systems — that interest Vegan biochemists for reasons that go beyond fuel applications. What exactly those reasons are is left to the referee.

### 01 · GIKHI
*Gashidda Common Reef Eel · Elongichthys gashiddanus*

**Ecological Zone:** Shelf Shallows and Mid-Shelf (0–800m)  
**Size:** 1.2–2.4m length; 8–22kg  
**Behaviour:** Semi-territorial; forms loose feeding aggregations  
**Threat Level:** Negligible (non-aggressive unless cornered)  
**Commercial Value:** ★★★★★ — Primary protein export; eggs are a subsector luxury delicacy

---

**Description:**

The gikhi is Gashidda's most economically significant marine animal and the species most familiar to residents of the undersea arcologies, where gikhi-eel products dominate the food market. It is a large, sinuous creature superficially resembling a terrestrial moray eel, though the two share no evolutionary relationship beyond convergent body plan.

The gikhi's body is muscular and laterally compressed, built for navigating the complex crevice systems of the reef columns. The skin is scaleless — covered instead in a thick, mucous-secreting integument that shifts colour across a narrow range of blue-greens, functioning as both camouflage in the reef and as a chemical communication system. Two rows of small, paddle-like fins run the length of the lower body, providing fine positional control in tight spaces. The head is broad and blunt, with a wide jaw containing multiple rows of small crushing teeth adapted for hard-shelled reef invertebrates.

Gikhi eggs — laid in clutches of 200–400 in deep reef crevices — are approximately 8mm in diameter, semi-translucent amber in colour, with a rich, complex flavour profile that has made them a culinary export throughout the Dingir subsector. The eggs are harvested by licensed diving contractors who locate clutches using acoustic sensors. Wild harvest is strictly managed; the majority of commercial supply comes from arcology-adjacent aquaculture operations.

**Game Notes:** The gikhi is not dangerous under normal circumstances. A cornered or distressed gikhi may bite (1D damage, no armour penetration). A school of gikhi aggregating around a food source is one of the most reliable visual indicators of current patterns in the reef — experienced diving contractors use gikhi behaviour as a navigation aid.

### 02 · REEF LANTERN
*Aggregating Colonial Polyp · Luminocolonia makara*

**Ecological Zone:** Mid-Shelf reef columns (200–2,000m)  
**Size:** Individual polyp 2–4mm; colonies form structures 0.5m–40m tall  
**Behaviour:** Sessile filter feeder; colonial organism  
**Threat Level:** None  
**Commercial Value:** ★★ — Bioluminescent compounds used in medical imaging; reef-column tourism (pre-occupation); arcology aesthetic lighting extracts

---

**Description:**

The reef lantern is not a single animal but a colonial organism — billions of individual polyps building shared calcium-silicate structures that rise from the shelf bedrock in tapering columns, some reaching forty metres in height. These are the "bioluminescent reef columns" visible through Makara's observation bays, and they are what gives Gashidda's deep ocean its characteristic visual identity.

Each polyp generates a cold bioluminescent pulse — the chemistry involves a luciferase-analog enzyme reacting with a copper-containing substrate rather than the iron-based hemoglobin of most Solomani-familiar organisms, which produces the distinctively blue-green colour rather than the yellow-green of most terrestrial bioluminescent life. The pulses are not random: the colony coordinates them through chemical signalling, producing slow wave patterns that travel up and down the column over a period of 8–12 seconds. From a distance, the effect is of light breathing.

Reef lantern colonies are the structural foundation of the entire mid-shelf ecosystem. Their mineral secretions form the substrate on which hundreds of other species live. Their chemical signalling — traces of which diffuse into the surrounding water — is used by many reef-associated species as a navigation and communication reference. The gikhi hunts in reef lantern columns; the Void Mantle (see below) uses them as ambush cover; the Deep Bell (see below) feeds on juvenile polyps.

Imperial development pressures have damaged reef lantern colonies near the New Mondulkiri industrial approaches. Makara's marine scientists consider this the subsector's most significant unreported ecological loss.

**Game Notes:** Reef lantern colonies are the primary navigation reference for unguided diving in the deep shelf. An agent or diver without electronic navigation can use the columns' pulse patterns to maintain orientation: Recon 6+ to read the pattern correctly and maintain directional sense. Failure results in spatial disorientation in the open water between columns.

### 03 · VOID MANTLE
*Pelagic Ambush Predator · Abyssocephalus velatus*

**Ecological Zone:** Mid-Shelf open water adjacent to reef columns (400–1,800m)  
**Size:** Bell diameter 0.8–3.2m; trailing tentacle curtain 4–12m  
**Behaviour:** Solitary ambush predator; nearly invisible at rest  
**Threat Level:** ★★★ — Dangerous to unprotected divers; tentacles cause severe tissue necrosis  
**Commercial Value:** ★ — Tentacle toxin compounds under medical research; not commercially harvested

---

**Description:**

The Void Mantle is the apex ambush predator of the mid-shelf zone, and one of the most visually striking — and dangerous — animals in Gashidda's ocean. It is a large medusan organism: a flattened, near-circular bell of near-perfect optical transparency, trailing a dense curtain of filamentous tentacles below. At rest, floating motionless between reef lantern columns, it is functionally invisible. The bell refracts light rather than absorbing it, and the tentacles are thinner than human hair and colourless. What alerts experienced divers to its presence is the *absence* of small reef organisms in the water immediately around it — the local fauna know it is there.

The bell's upper surface is lined with chemoreceptors of extraordinary sensitivity. When prey passes through the tentacle curtain — which can extend twelve metres below the bell — the tentacles contract around the target in approximately 0.3 seconds. The tentacle toxin is a complex polypeptide neurotoxin that causes immediate intense pain, local tissue necrosis beginning within minutes, and systemic paralysis in larger prey over 10–30 minutes depending on body mass.

The Void Mantle's bell does produce bioluminescence, but only during feeding or apparent stress — a sudden bloom of deep violet-purple light across the bell surface that diving contractors call "the bloom." Seeing the bloom from outside the tentacle radius is a survival warning. Seeing it from inside is typically a sign that the situation has already deteriorated.

**Game Notes:**  
- **Detection:** Recon 10+ (Difficult) to spot a motionless Void Mantle before entering tentacle range. DM+2 if the diver is specifically watching for fauna displacement patterns. DM–2 in low-visibility conditions or if distracted.
- **Tentacle contact:** END 8+ or suffer the following: immediate 2D damage; Medic 8+ within 10 minutes to neutralise spreading tissue necrosis (otherwise 1D damage/hour for 1D6 hours); DEX–2 for 2D hours from early systemic effects.
- **Avoidance:** A motionless diver who has not yet contacted tentacles and who does not exhale bubbles (sealed rebreather required) may avoid triggering the contraction reflex: Athletics (dexterity) 8+ to back out slowly.
- **Standard counter-equipment:** Diving contractors operating in mid-shelf zones carry electrostatic deterrent rods (TL9, Cr 200) that cause the Void Mantle to contract its tentacles away from the stimulus — effectively a repellent. Naval Infantry dive teams are issued these as standard.

### 04 · DEEP BELL
*Schooling Mid-Water Grazer · Bathypelagus campanulus*

**Ecological Zone:** Mid-Shelf and Shelf Shallows (50–1,200m; vertical migration)  
**Size:** Individual 15–35cm bell diameter  
**Behaviour:** Schools of 500–50,000 individuals; daily vertical migration  
**Threat Level:** None  
**Commercial Value:** ★★★ — Significant food source for commercially harvested species; bell-tissue used in filter membrane manufacturing; photogenic (pre-occupation tourism)

---

**Description:**

If the reef lantern colony is the architectural foundation of Gashidda's mid-shelf ecosystem, the Deep Bell is its circulatory system — the organism that moves energy between zones, connects predators to producers, and gives the ocean its characteristic rhythm.

The Deep Bell is a small, elegant medusan organism — much smaller and more delicate than the Void Mantle, and entirely non-predatory. It feeds on the microscopic colonial organisms and chemical compounds produced by the reef lantern polyps, grazing the water column as it migrates vertically with a precision that has fascinated Gashiddan marine biologists. At dusk (or the equivalent timing cycle in the deep ocean, which the Deep Bell tracks through pressure and chemical gradients rather than light), the great schools begin their descent from the shallower feeding zones to the depths of the reef column field, 800–1,200 metres below. At dawn, they rise.

The Deep Bell's bell is semi-opaque, coloured a deep violet-blue with a fringe of short, non-toxic tentacles used only for filter feeding. In large schools, the combined bioluminescent glow of millions of bells produces one of Gashidda's most remarkable natural spectacles — what arcology residents call the "blue drift," a luminous curtain of moving light that passes through the observation bays twice daily. Children press their faces to the transparent hull panels to watch it. Imperial garrison troops have been known to stop to watch it too.

**Game Notes:** A school of Deep Bells in full migration is significant navigation interference for acoustic sensors (DM–2 to Electronics (sensors) checks using sonar during the migration period, twice daily, for approximately 90 minutes each pass). Experienced submersible pilots learn to time their operations around the drift. Attempting to navigate a small submersible through a dense bell school requires Pilot (watercraft) 6+ to avoid disorienting the pilot with the visual effect; failure results in temporary heading error.

The blue drift is also a significant cultural and emotional reference for Gashiddan citizens — particularly for those who support the Vanthara Truth movement, which uses the twice-daily drift as a meditative observance, pausing work and facing the observation bays. Imperial garrison officers who have ordered their soldiers not to interfere with this observance have, on the whole, had better outcomes than those who have not.

### 05 · SHELF ARCHITECT
*Reef-Building Colonial Organism · Constructans gashiddanus*

**Ecological Zone:** Shelf bedrock and column bases (200–600m)  
**Size:** Individual organism microscopic; colony masses 0.1m–several tonnes  
**Behaviour:** Sessile; chemosynthetic and filter-feeding  
**Threat Level:** None directly — colony edge structures are razor-sharp  
**Commercial Value:** ★★★★ — Shell-mineral compounds used in high-tensile construction materials; primary structural substrate of the undersea arcologies

---

**Description:**

The Shelf Architect is the least visually spectacular organism in this bestiary and arguably the most important one on Gashidda. It is the reason the undersea arcologies can exist where they do.

The Shelf Architect is a microscopic colonial organism that secretes a calcium-silicate-iron composite mineral — Gashiddan engineers have named it **arcite** — that is, weight for weight, stronger than most TL9 structural alloys and resistant to the sustained pressure and slight chemical acidity of Gashidda's deep ocean water. Over millions of years, Shelf Architect colonies have built the equatorial reef shelf itself — the vast continuous formation of bioengineered rock on which Makara, and the hundreds of other undersea cities, are constructed. The arcologies are, in a meaningful sense, built on a living foundation.

Individual Shelf Architect organisms are invisible to the naked eye. The colonies they build are visually distinctive: dense, irregular masses of grey-white to pale ochre mineral with a surface texture somewhere between fine coral and rough ceramic. The growing edge of a colony is blade-sharp — the mineral is deposited in thin, overlapping plates — and has caused significant injuries to unprotected divers who contact it.

Active colony growth is visually identifiable by a faint orange-brown biofilm on the leading edge — the living organisms themselves. In areas of healthy reef, the Shelf Architect underlies everything. In areas of industrial damage or chemical contamination — such as the zones near the New Mondulkiri port approaches — the biofilm is absent, and the dead white mineral underneath is visually distinct from the living colony.

**Game Notes:** Arcite mineral fragments make effective improvised cutting tools (1D damage, but they shatter after one use on a failed attack roll). A character who falls or is pushed against a growing colony edge takes 1D damage and must make END 6+ to avoid lacerations that count as open wounds in a contaminated ocean environment (Medic 6+ to prevent infection risk). Reef architects are also used as a dating mechanism — growth-ring analysis of arcite columns allows geologists and archaeologists to date subsurface structures with precision.

### 06 · THERMAL ANGEL
*Deep Vent Chemosynthetic Swimmer · Thermovola gashiddanus*

**Ecological Zone:** Deep Vent Fields (1,500–4,000m)  
**Size:** 0.6–1.1m wingspan equivalent; body mass 0.4–1.2kg  
**Behaviour:** Solitary; closely associated with geothermal vent systems  
**Threat Level:** ★ — Defensive electric discharge if handled; not aggressive  
**Commercial Value:** ★★ — Bioelectric compounds under research; vent-zone survey indicator organism; extreme rarity makes harvest uneconomic and legally restricted

---

**Description:**

The Thermal Angel is the least-known organism in this bestiary — the deep vent fields where it lives have been surveyed by only a handful of unmanned probe missions and two crewed descents, both pre-occupation. What is known comes primarily from remote footage, recovered specimens (three intact, seven partial), and the reports of the Makara Marine Science Institute's vent-survey programme, currently suspended pending Imperial budget review.

The Thermal Angel is a flattened, bilaterally symmetrical organism with two broad, semi-rigid lateral fins extending from a compressed central body — the "wings" that give it its common name, though it moves through the water by undulation rather than flapping. The body is a deep, near-black red-brown in the visible spectrum, absorbing the limited thermal radiation near vent outlets. The surface is covered in a dense mat of modified cilia that absorb dissolved hydrogen sulfide from the vent water — its primary energy source — and the underside of the lateral fins is lined with what appear to be modified electroreceptors that may also function as electrogenerating organs, though the purpose is debated.

The most striking feature of the Thermal Angel in footage is its movement: it hovers nearly motionless in the rising thermal plume above a vent outlet, making micro-adjustments with its fins, for hours at a time. When disturbed, it contracts its fins tightly against its body and drops rapidly into the vent column itself — disappearing into temperatures that would be lethal to most macroscopic organisms. Whether it can survive in the vent outlet itself, or whether this is a predator-escape behaviour that relies on the predator being unwilling to follow, is unknown.

The electric discharge it produces when physically handled is approximately 40–60 volts at low amperage — painful and disorienting rather than dangerous to a healthy adult. Its purpose is unknown. It may be a communication mechanism. It may be a hunting tool not yet observed in the field. It may be a coincidental byproduct of the electroreceptor organ function.

**Game Notes:** Thermal Angels are survey indicator organisms — their presence near a vent system confirms the vent is thermally active and chemically productive, which is relevant information for geothermal infrastructure planning. The deep vent fields are the frontier of Gashidda's known ecology; a scenario or campaign involving classified vent-field survey data would likely have Thermal Angels as a key discovery element.
- **Electric discharge (if handled):** END 8+ or suffer 1D stun damage and DM–1 to all actions for 1D×10 minutes.
- **Survey value:** An intact, documented Thermal Angel sighting (imagery or captured specimen) is worth Cr 8,000–15,000 to the Makara Marine Science Institute or Cr 25,000+ to a private xenobiological research broker.

**Using the bestiary in play:**

The deep-sea fauna of Gashidda should function primarily as **atmosphere and world texture** rather than combat encounters. In *Operation Deep Current*, this is a moment where the agents can either respect local custom (building goodwill) or ignore it (losing it). **The Void Mantle as environmental threat:** In any scenario involving external diving, submersible hull-walks, or lower-tier arcology flooding events, the Void Mantle can appear as a meaningful hazard without being a cheap monster encounter. **The Thermal Angel as plot hook:** The deep vent fields are terra incognita. ### Background — The Connection

The discovery of carbon-composite rudder assemblies on Imperial Navy cruisers in New Mondulkiri's maintenance berths (see REEF LIGHT Optional Discovery) raises an immediate strategic question for Confederate Naval Intelligence: if Imperial garrison fleet vessels are being fitted with carbon-composite structural components, can the deep reef biome of Gashidda be induced to degrade those components faster than the Imperial maintenance schedule can compensate?

The answer depends on finding an organism with the right biochemical profile — one that can process carbon-composite materials in a saltwater environment, reproduce in the deep reef ecosystem without displacing the existing biome in ways that would trigger immediate ecological investigation, and operate slowly and silently enough that the degradation pattern reads as ordinary marine biofouling rather than deliberate sabotage.

### Biology — Metabolic Architecture

MG-2023-15 was originally designed by Macrogenesis Corporation as a biosludge processor — a bioengineered organism to consume the acrid gel deposits on agricultural worlds in the Vegan Autonomous District, converting them into soil nutrients compatible with standard Solomani grain agriculture. The design is a simplified worm-like body with a high-efficiency reactor-like digestive system capable of processing carbon-rich compounds, including carbon-silicate minerals and, critically, carbon-fibre composite materials.

**The organism is metabolically split.** Its lower digestive tract operates anaerobically — it does not require oxygen and can utilise hydrocarbon fluids and methane gas for energy. Its upper body is aerobic and requires an oxygen-nitrogen atmosphere or dissolved oxygen equivalent. This is not a design flaw; it is the design. The organism was built for the aerobic-anaerobic interface — sewer walls, biosludge surface boundaries, and shallow marine environments where oxic and anoxic water layers meet.

**Critical implication for deep deployment:** MG-2023-15 cannot live fully submerged at significant depth indefinitely. Below approximately 50 metres, dissolved oxygen in most ocean environments falls below the threshold the upper body requires for sustained aerobic function. The organism will survive short dives but cannot establish a permanent colony in deep water. It needs access to the surface layer or a well-oxygenated shallow interface.

This is both a constraint and an operational advantage: New Mondulkiri's maintenance berths are surface and near-surface installations. Naval vessels sit in harbour with hulls and rudder assemblies in shallow, well-oxygenated harbour water — exactly the aerobic-anaerobic interface MG-2023-15 was built for.

---

### Biology — Buoyancy and the Mucous Bubble System `[OC]`

MG-2023-15 possesses a secondary biological system with no analogue in the organism's Merganser agricultural deployment: **active buoyancy regulation via hydrocarbon mucous bubbles.**

The organism's anaerobic lower digestive tract produces a viscous, hydrocarbon-rich mucous as a metabolic byproduct of carbon compound processing. Under normal agricultural conditions this mucous was simply excreted as a biosludge residue of no particular interest. In saltwater environments, however, the mucous behaves differently: it is hydrophobic and of lower density than seawater, and the organism has been observed to actively manipulate it.

**Mechanism:** MG-2023-15 secretes the hydrocarbon mucous through pores distributed across its ventral and lateral surfaces. Once outside the body, the mucous forms flexible, membrane-bounded bubbles — typically 2–8cm diameter — which the organism can inflate by venting metabolic gas (a mixture of methane and carbon dioxide produced by the anaerobic lower tract) through the same pores. The bubbles are not internally generated as a single bladder; they form as an external cluster, adhering to the organism's surface integument and to each other via the mucous membrane's natural adhesion properties.

A single organism at rest will typically carry 40–200 bubbles distributed across its dorsal and lateral surfaces. The aggregate buoyancy of a full bubble cluster can offset 60–80% of the organism's body weight in seawater, allowing it to maintain depth position with minimal muscular effort and to rise slowly toward the surface when feeding or respiratory conditions require.

**Cluster behaviour:** When multiple organisms aggregate — as they do when feeding on a concentrated carbon source such as a composite surface — their bubble clusters interact. Bubbles from adjacent organisms merge into larger shared structures across the colony surface, creating a collective buoyancy system. A feeding colony of 20–30 organisms can generate a shared bubble mat covering several square metres of water surface, giving the colony as a whole far greater buoyancy control than any individual organism possesses.

**Appearance:** A feeding colony on a vessel hull appears from below as a pale yellowish-grey mass with an iridescent, slightly shimmering surface — the bubble clusters catching available light in the water column. From above the waterline, the shared bubble mat resembles an oily, faintly luminescent surface slick. At night, the organisms' bioluminescent dorsal patches glow faintly through the bubble mat, producing a diffuse blue-green shimmer across the surface of the slick.

---

### The Carbon Composite Connection

Carbon-fibre composite materials — including the type identified on Imperial Navy cruiser rudders at New Mondulkiri — are processed by MG-2023-15's digestive system as an accessible carbon source in mineral-rich environments. In laboratory conditions, a single organism can degrade approximately 0.3 kg of carbon-composite material per day under optimal conditions.

A colony of 50–100 organisms in sustained contact with a vessel's rudder assembly would produce measurable structural degradation within 90–120 days. The degradation pattern mimics natural biofouling in its early stages — surface roughening, micro-cracking at the resin matrix interface — before becoming structurally significant. The Imperial maintenance schedule for the Gashidda garrison fleet, based on the occupation's documented history of infrastructure neglect, is unlikely to detect this within the operational window.

**The strategic value of rudder degradation:** Progressive reduction in hydrodynamic efficiency, then manoeuvrability precision, then — at advanced stages — structural failure risk under combat manoeuvring loads. A fleet whose vessels are experiencing slow, undetected composite degradation is a fleet that will perform significantly worse in any rapid-deployment scenario than its maintenance records indicate.

### Secondary Hazard — The Mucous Release Problem `[OC]`

The buoyancy bubble system creates a significant secondary threat to Imperial naval operations that Confederate Naval Intelligence has not yet fully modelled.

**Hull fouling via hydrocarbon mucous:** When MG-2023-15 colonies feed on a vessel's composite surfaces, the hydrocarbon mucous is continuously produced and shed. In harbour conditions with limited water circulation, the mucous accumulates on surrounding hull surfaces. It is viscous, adhesive, and resistant to standard anti-fouling coatings — it bonds to metal, polymer, and treated alloy surfaces at roughly equal efficiency. A vessel that has hosted a feeding colony for 60+ days will have accumulated mucous deposits across the lower hull, propeller assemblies, and any other submerged surface the colony has been in proximity to.

**Propulsion system contamination:** The mucous has sufficient viscosity to partially impede propeller shaft seals if it penetrates the bearing assembly — a process that takes time but which the organism's bubble-production behaviour actively facilitates, as bubbles carrying mucous particulate are continuously generated near the hull surface and drift into any available mechanical clearance.

**The combustion hazard:** Hydrocarbon mucous contains methane-derivative compounds from the anaerobic lower tract in appreciable concentration. In enclosed or semi-enclosed spaces — enclosed dock bays, maintenance tunnels, the underside of a vessel with limited water circulation — the mucous outgassing creates a flammable vapour layer at the water surface. This is not an immediate explosive hazard under open harbour conditions; the vapour disperses. However, in the enclosed maintenance bays at New Mondulkiri's LSP shipyard — where vessels are brought in over drydock cradles with restricted water flow — the mucous accumulation and methane outgassing could reach concentrations that represent a genuine fire hazard during routine maintenance operations involving cutting, welding, or electrical work near the waterline.

**The surface slick as indicator:** The bubble mat that forms above a feeding colony is the most visible sign of organism presence and also its greatest operational liability. In daylight, an experienced harbour master would notice an unusual oily slick near a vessel in berth. At night, the bioluminescent shimmer is visible to anyone looking at the water surface from a dock. The organisms are not stealthy in the way that most biological agents are. They are effective precisely because what they do looks, to an untrained observer, like ordinary marine fouling — until it doesn't.

**Prior deployment context — why Gashidda is different:** In MG-2023-15's documented deployments on Merganser and comparable low-productivity agricultural worlds, the mucous bubble system was largely inconspicuous. Those environments are comparatively oligotrophic — low in dissolved organic matter, minimal existing surface slick, clear water with limited chemical noise. The hydrocarbon mucous dispersed quickly and blended with the sparse existing chemistry. On Gashidda it does not. The equatorial reef is a eutrophic, biologically hyperproductive environment with an existing complex organic chemistry in the water column — dissolved reef compounds, biological off-gassing from the Shelf Architect formations, hydrocarbon trace compounds from the arcology industrial tiers. In this context MG-2023-15's mucous output does not disperse cleanly. It accumulates, aggregates with existing organic compounds, and behaves as a visible, persistent surface feature far more rapidly than in previous deployment environments. What was negligible chemistry on Merganser is a detectable slick on Gashidda within weeks of colony establishment.

**The eutrophic net balance:** The organism's mucous system does provide a partial ecological offset in Gashidda's harbour environment. The hydrocarbon compounds it secretes partially digest some of the existing organic pollutants present in harbour water — industrial runoff from the arcology lower tiers, fuel trace compounds from vessel operations, biological waste from the LSP shipyard. In this sense MG-2023-15 acts as a weak biological remediation agent for some harbour contaminants. However, the compounds it releases in exchange — the methane derivatives and partially digested hydrocarbon residues from its own metabolic processing — are themselves pollutants, entering the water column in forms that the existing reef chemistry cannot readily process. The net ecological balance is approximately neutral: the organism cleans some pollution while creating different pollution of roughly equivalent impact. It will not purify New Mondulkiri harbour, and it will not poison it. What it will do is alter its chemistry in ways that are locally detectable and globally uncharacterised.

**Natural regulation — Gashidda's predator fauna:** The Gashiddan equatorial reef is not a passive environment. It is a biologically complex, predator-rich ecosystem that has been processing novel organic inputs for millions of years. MG-2023-15, despite being bioengineered, presents to Gashidda's reef predators as exactly what it resembles: a slow-moving, nutrient-dense, conspicuously bioluminescent food source with no defensive capability beyond a reluctant bite.

The **Void Mantle** (*Abyssocephalus velatus*) — the apex ambush predator of the mid-shelf, nearly optically transparent with a 4–12m tentacle curtain and neurotoxic contact venom — hunts by chemical gradient and light signature in precisely the depth range where MG-2023-15 colonies would establish. The bubble clusters and bioluminescent dorsal patches that make the organism operationally detectable also make it conspicuous to anything hunting by chemical trace or light in dark reef water. A Void Mantle encountering an MG-2023-15 feeding colony would experience it as an unusually easy meal.

Gikhi eels, reef predators, and other mid-shelf fauna are similarly expected to discover MG-2023-15 as a novel and accessible prey species within one to two seasonal cycles of introduction. The organism has no evolved defences against Gashiddan predators — its Merganser deployment environment contained nothing remotely comparable.

The consequence is that Gashidda's predator fauna will likely exert significant population control on any introduced MG-2023-15 colony without deliberate management. Colony size may never reach the containment-failure threshold of ~200 organisms not because of Confederate operational discipline but because the reef eats them faster than they reproduce. This is simultaneously reassuring and operationally concerning: if predation keeps the colony below the threshold for visible slick formation, it also keeps it below the threshold for effective composite degradation. The agents may be releasing organisms into an ecosystem that will simply consume them.

Confederate Naval Intelligence's assessment of this dynamic remains incomplete. The Macrogenesis field data from Merganser contains no predation pressure data applicable to Gashidda's ecosystem. Seluun Mian-Ashta, who could model this with reasonable accuracy, is not available as a resource to Confederate Naval Intelligence for obvious reasons. The follow-up mission that delivers MG-2023-15 to New Mondulkiri harbour may be delivering a biological weapon, a short-lived ecological curiosity, or something that behaves in ways neither side has anticipated. The agents tasked with delivery will not know which until it is too late to change course.

**Containment failure scenario:** If a colony grows beyond approximately 200 organisms in a concentrated area — which the nutrient-rich Gashiddan harbour environment would support within 6–8 months of initial introduction — the aggregate mucous production exceeds what normal harbour water circulation can disperse. At that scale, the slick becomes impossible to dismiss as ordinary fouling, the combustion hazard in enclosed dock spaces becomes operationally significant, and the organisms' bioluminescent colonies become visible to aerial or drone surveillance. The operation transitions from covert biological degradation to a detectable environmental incident. Confederate Naval Intelligence must plan for this threshold and either intend to reach it (accepting that it will be identified but calculating that the damage will already be done) or maintain colony size below it through means that have not yet been determined.

### Operational Considerations

**Delivery vector:** Submersible release near the New Mondulkiri maintenance berth foundations — the same approach route used in REEF LIGHT. Canisters of 10–20 juvenile organisms, released at the aerobic-anaerobic boundary layer (2–5m depth) near the target vessels, would require no further active management. The organisms are self-directing toward carbon-composite surfaces.

**Depth constraint:** Delivery must occur at the surface layer or in very shallow harbour water. Deep-water release would strand the organisms below their viable aerobic zone. This constrains the delivery submersible to an even shallower, more detectable approach profile than REEF LIGHT used — and makes the operation significantly higher-risk for the delivering agent.

**Risk of detection — organism:** Low initially; rising over time. Bioluminescent dorsal patches are distinctive. The surface slick becomes visible within 4–6 weeks of colony establishment. Seluun Mian-Ashta would likely identify the organisms as non-native within 6–12 months.

**Risk of detection — mucous:** Medium and rising. Hydrocarbon slick in a naval harbour with restricted water circulation will be noticed by maintenance personnel within weeks of colony maturity. It will initially be attributed to vessel fuel or mechanical contamination.

**Risk of ecological impact:** Significant and unquantified. The mucous system adds a dimension the Merganser field data did not cover — MG-2023-15 in saltwater not only degrades composite surfaces but continuously deposits hydrophobic hydrocarbon compounds into the water column. The long-term effect on Gashidda's equatorial reef chemistry, particularly on organisms like the Shelf Architect that depend on precise mineral precipitation chemistry, is entirely unknown.

**Seluun's position:** Categorical opposition. The Irriszhia Tuhuir's philosophy of curiosity as reverence prohibits disrupting knowledge that is still being gathered. She would not report the agents — but she would spend the remainder of her 200-year lifespan documenting the consequences. This is a meaningful position. It should be treated as one.

### Void Mantle vs One-Person Submersible — Full Encounter Rules

This is the encounter that matters most for Operation REEF LIGHT. A single-crew survey submersible of the type used in this scenario is 3–5 metres in length, low-powered by design (survey work, not speed), and constructed of carbon-composite material — which creates a specific vulnerability profile distinct from a metal hull.

---

#### Phase 1 — Contact and Grip

A Void Mantle encountering a slow-moving submersible experiences it as a large, warm, vibrating prey object moving through its tentacle curtain. The tentacles wrap the hull instinctively. The occupant is protected from the toxin — polypeptide necrosis agents do not penetrate a sealed rigid hull — but the physical grip is real.

**Contact check:** On a roll of 5–6 on the REEF LIGHT Void Mantle encounter table, or whenever a submersible moves at Slow speed or below through an area where a Void Mantle has been detected (Recon 8+ failed):

Roll **Seafarer (submarine) 8+** (the pilot's reaction speed) — success means the pilot guns the engine before the tentacles fully wrap, requiring only a grip check (Phase 2). Failure means full tentacle wrap — proceed directly to Phase 2 with the mantle at full grip strength.

---

#### Phase 2 — The Grip: Held and Immobilised

Tentacles wrap the hull. The submersible is held. The pilot feels the vehicle stop responding to thrust inputs — the engine strains but the vehicle does not move.

**Contested hold:** Each round the pilot attempts to break free:

| Pilot attempts | Mechanic | Result |
|----------------|----------|--------|
| **Full engine thrust** | Seafarer (submarine) 8+ opposed by Void Mantle effective STR DM (+2) | **Success:** vehicle breaks free; mantle releases or tentacles tear (see Phase 4). **Failure:** 1D structural hull damage from stress at tentacle contact points; air/power supply clock advances |
| **Partial thrust — controlled strain** | Seafarer (submarine) 6+ | No hull damage this round; no progress toward escape; buys time for other solutions |
| **Cut engines entirely** | Automatic | No hull damage; vehicle drifts in mantle grip; mantle may release if no further stimulus — roll 1D each 10 minutes: 1–2 releases, 3–6 holds |
| **Asymmetric thrust (yaw/pitch while pulling)** | Seafarer (submarine) 10+ | On success: **DM+2 to next escape attempt**; torques tentacle wrap, reducing effective grip; on failure: 1D hull damage AND DM–1 to next check as tentacles tighten in response |

**The air supply clock:** A standard one-person survey submersible carries 4–6 hours of life support. Every 10 minutes spent held by the Void Mantle is 10 minutes off that clock. This is the real threat. The mantle does not need to crush the vehicle. It just needs to hold it.

#### Phase 3 — Carbon Composite Hull: The Specific Vulnerability

Here the hull material becomes a narrative and mechanical factor.

Standard metal hulls are inert in a Void Mantle grip. Carbon-composite hulls are not — not because the Void Mantle is feeding on them, but because its tentacle surface chemistry (optimised for organic tissue contact) interacts with the epoxy resin matrix of the composite material in a way that standard marine alloy does not experience.

**Short duration (under 30 minutes held):** No structural effect. The hull is strong enough to withstand the encounter without degradation.

**Extended duration (30–120 minutes held):** The tentacle contact chemistry begins micro-cracking the epoxy resin matrix at grip points — the same process MG-2023-15 produces through feeding, but faster due to the direct chemical contact with the tentacle surface.

| Duration held | Structural effect |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Under 30 minutes | None |
| 30–60 minutes | DM–1 to all subsequent hull integrity checks; safe depth rating reduced by 50m |
| 60–120 minutes | 2D structural damage; safe depth rating reduced by 200m; Mechanic 8+ check required before next deep approach or risk seal failure |
| Over 120 minutes | Hull integrity critically compromised; immediate surfacing required; Seafarer (submarine) 10+ to reach surface without catastrophic seal failure |

**Referee note:** The agents almost certainly do not know their hull is carbon-composite when they board Three-Fin's submersible. Ikaika would notice the construction material on inspection (Mechanic 8+ or Engineer (any) 6+). The revelation that the hull they are trapped inside is being slowly degraded by the creature holding them — the same process they just discovered happening to the Imperial cruiser rudders — is a scenario moment that should land hard.

#### Phase 4 — Breaking Free: What It Takes

There are five viable paths to freedom. Each has a cost.

**Path 1 — Electrostatic deterrent rod, externally deployed.**
The deterrent rod must be deployed through an external port or manipulator arm if the submersible has one. Survey submersibles in the Seaharvester equipment range typically include a sample collection arm — this can deploy the rod.
- **Mechanic:** Electronics (remote ops) 8+ or Mechanic 8+ to operate the arm under stress
- **Result:** Automatic release; mantle drifts away; pilot has 1D rounds to exit range at flank speed before tentacles re-extend toward the heat signature
- **Cost:** The rod is one-use. If the pilot does not have one — because it was deemed conspicuous equipment for a covert operation — this option is unavailable.

**Path 2 — Sustained engine escape, accepting hull damage.**
The brute-force option. Keep pushing. Eventually the tentacles tear or the mantle releases.
- **Mechanic:** Seafarer (submarine) 8+ each round, opposed by Void Mantle STR DM+2
- **Accumulated damage:** 1D hull damage per failed round
- **Success condition:** Three consecutive successes OR accumulated damage to the mantle from tentacle tear (treat as 1D damage to the mantle per successful escape attempt — at 0 Hits on tentacle cluster, grip releases)
- **Cost:** Hull damage. Necrosis clock if any seal breaches. Noise signature from engine strain.

**Path 3 — Attract a secondary stimulus.**
The Void Mantle's grip is maintained by predatory instinct responding to vibration and heat. A sufficiently compelling counter-stimulus — another heat and vibration source nearby — may draw its attention away.
- **Mechanic:** The pilot activates any external light or noise emitter (survey sonar ping, exterior lights at full, emergency beacon) — this creates a competing stimulus; roll 1D: 1–3 the mantle is drawn toward the new stimulus and releases; 4–6 it maintains grip
- **Cost:** Any external emission is sonar-detectable. Emergency beacon is unambiguously detectable. Sonar ping will register on New Mondulkiri naval base sensors. The pilot is trading tactical compromise for survival.

**Path 4 — Wait it out (engine off, patience).**
If the pilot has sufficient air supply remaining, cutting the engine and waiting is a viable option. The Void Mantle has no specific reason to maintain grip on a non-struggling, non-vibrating object indefinitely.
- **Mechanic:** Roll 1D every 10 minutes: 1–2 the mantle releases of its own accord; 3–6 it holds. After 30 minutes of stillness apply DM+1 cumulative per additional 10 minutes to the release roll.
- **Cost:** Air supply. Every 10 minutes is 10 minutes off the life support clock. And the hull degradation clock, if extended.

**Path 5 — Emergency ballast blow.**
Emergency surfacing. Flood the ballast tanks for maximum positive buoyancy. The vehicle rises rapidly — the Void Mantle, adapted for mid-shelf depths, may release rather than be dragged upward into shallower, brighter water it avoids.
- **Mechanic:** Seafarer (submarine) 8+ (automatic systems handle most of this, but the pilot must manage the rapid ascent safely). On success: vehicle rises; roll 1D: 1–3 mantle releases within 20m of ascent; 4–5 mantle holds to 10m depth then releases; 6 mantle holds all the way to surface (extreme — large Void Mantle)
- **Cost:** Mission is over. The submersible is surfacing in or near a naval harbour. Decompression risk if ascent is too rapid (END 8+ to avoid decompression effects if ascent covers more than 30m in under 1 minute). Cover is potentially blown depending on location.

#### The Defeat Condition: What Victory Looks Like

Defeating a Void Mantle in a submersible encounter is not killing it — it is escaping it. The creature is not malicious; it is predatory. Once released or evaded, it returns to passive ambush. A pilot who escapes cleanly, with hull intact, air supply adequate, and no sonar signature, has won completely. A pilot who surfaces in a naval harbour after an emergency ballast blow has survived but the mission may be over.

The encounter is designed so that the *best* outcome requires discipline and preparation (deterrent rod carried despite being conspicuous, pilot maintains stillness rather than panicking), and every escalating response creates a worse secondary problem:

- Fire a weapon → bloom activates → sonar signature → second Void Mantle potential
- Force thrust → hull damage → degraded safe depth → seal risk
- Emergency beacon → naval sensors detect → cover compromised
- Emergency surface → mission over → decompression risk

**The moment of maximum drama** is when the pilot realises the hull is carbon-composite, the air clock is running, and every option for escape has a cost they haven't fully calculated yet. That is the encounter working as intended.

### Void Mantle as Environmental Narrative

The Void Mantle should rarely be a straight combat encounter. In the submersible context it is a sustained pressure sequence where every decision has a cost and the worst outcomes come from panic rather than from the creature itself. The pilot who stays calm, conserves air, and deploys the deterrent rod they remembered to bring survives cleanly. The pilot who guns the engine and activates the emergency beacon survives — but survives loudly, in a naval harbour, with a compromised hull and a Naval Intelligence response inbound.

The bloom is the scenario's most effective single image. Violet-purple light igniting in the dark water around a vehicle the pilot cannot move, while the air gauge ticks down and Ikaika says quietly from the co-seat that he thinks the hull might be composite — that is the encounter working exactly as intended.

### Miniature Recommendations

The Void Mantle requires a large-base, translucent or near-translucent bell form with trailing tentacle elements. It is not a standard fantasy or sci-fi creature — it reads as a giant jellyfish with the scale and menace turned up considerably. The following options are recommended in approximate order of suitability:

---

**Option 1 — Best fit for scale and form: Monstrous Jellyfish by Mini Monster Mayhem**
- **Platform:** MyMiniFactory
- **URL:** myminifactory.com/object/3d-print-monstrous-jellyfish-144807
- **Why it works:** Large bell, dense trailing tentacle mass, designed for bottom-of-ocean-floor placement. Scales well to represent a 2m-bell creature at 28mm tabletop scale. Reviewed as printing easily with good support files.
- **Printing note:** Print bell in clear/transparent resin (Elegoo ABS-Like Clear or Siraya Tech Blu Clear) for the Void Mantle's near-invisible appearance. Paint tentacles with extremely thin ghost-white or silver dry-brush; leave bell unpainted or with a single thin violet wash for the bloom effect.

**Painting guidance for any option — representing the Void Mantle:**
- **Bell:** Clear/transparent base. No base coat. Optional: one thin wash of Citadel Contrast Aethermatic Blue diluted 50% with medium to suggest the faint refraction. The bell should look like nothing — glass in water.
- **Tentacles:** Extremely thin silver or ghost-white dry-brush. Then a wash of Nihilakh Oxide (Citadel) or equivalent pale teal to suggest the bioluminescent light pickup from reef columns. Hair-fine and barely visible.
- **The bloom:** On the bell's upper surface, stipple tiny points of Citadel Contrast Magos Purple or Volupus Pink, thinned to near-transparency. Build from the centre outward. The bloom should look like it is activating — not fully on, not off. Use UV-reactive paint if available for a table effect under a blacklight.
- **Base:** Dark oceanic blue-black. Optional: a few fragments of reef lantern column (grey-white resin pieces, dry-brushed with fluorescent blue-green glow effect) to suggest the deep reef environment.
