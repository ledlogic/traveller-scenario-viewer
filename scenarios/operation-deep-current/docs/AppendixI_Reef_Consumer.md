## APPENDIX I: ORGANISM MG-2023-15 — THE REEF CONSUMER
### Proposed Bio-Intelligence Asset · Confederate Naval Interest `[OC]`

---

> *This appendix presents a lifeform proposed for introduction to the Gashiddan reef ecosystem as a long-term Confederate Naval Intelligence asset. It is not a scenario objective — it is a campaign seed planted by the carbon composite rudder discovery in Operation REEF LIGHT. The organism exists in canon (Solomani Confederation biotech files, Macrogenesis corporation — see companion scenario "My Gawd, It's Full of Slugs") and is presented here as a potential follow-up mission element.*

### Background — The Connection

The discovery of carbon-composite rudder assemblies on Imperial Navy cruisers in New Mondulkiri's maintenance berths (see REEF LIGHT Optional Discovery) raises an immediate strategic question for Confederate Naval Intelligence: if Imperial garrison fleet vessels are being fitted with carbon-composite structural components, can the deep reef biome of Gashidda be induced to degrade those components faster than the Imperial maintenance schedule can compensate?

The answer depends on finding an organism with the right biochemical profile — one that can process carbon-composite materials in a saltwater environment, reproduce in the deep reef ecosystem without displacing the existing biome in ways that would trigger immediate ecological investigation, and operate slowly and silently enough that the degradation pattern reads as ordinary marine biofouling rather than deliberate sabotage.

**Organism MG-2023-15** is a candidate.

### Biology — Metabolic Architecture

MG-2023-15 was originally designed by Macrogenesis Corporation as a biosludge processor — a bioengineered organism to consume the acrid gel deposits on agricultural worlds in the Vegan Autonomous District, converting them into soil nutrients compatible with standard Solomani grain agriculture. The design is a simplified worm-like body with a high-efficiency reactor-like digestive system capable of processing carbon-rich compounds, including carbon-silicate minerals and, critically, carbon-fibre composite materials.

**The organism is metabolically split.** Its lower digestive tract operates anaerobically — it does not require oxygen and can utilise hydrocarbon fluids and methane gas for energy. Its upper body is aerobic and requires an oxygen-nitrogen atmosphere or dissolved oxygen equivalent. This is not a design flaw; it is the design. The organism was built for the aerobic-anaerobic interface — sewer walls, biosludge surface boundaries, and shallow marine environments where oxic and anoxic water layers meet.

**Critical implication for deep deployment:** MG-2023-15 cannot live fully submerged at significant depth indefinitely. Below approximately 50 metres, dissolved oxygen in most ocean environments falls below the threshold the upper body requires for sustained aerobic function. The organism will survive short dives but cannot establish a permanent colony in deep water. It needs access to the surface layer or a well-oxygenated shallow interface.

This is both a constraint and an operational advantage: New Mondulkiri's maintenance berths are surface and near-surface installations. Naval vessels sit in harbour with hulls and rudder assemblies in shallow, well-oxygenated harbour water — exactly the aerobic-anaerobic interface MG-2023-15 was built for.

### Biology — Buoyancy and the Mucous Bubble System `[OC]`

MG-2023-15 possesses a secondary biological system with no analogue in the organism's Merganser agricultural deployment: **active buoyancy regulation via hydrocarbon mucous bubbles.**

The organism's anaerobic lower digestive tract produces a viscous, hydrocarbon-rich mucous as a metabolic byproduct of carbon compound processing. Under normal agricultural conditions this mucous was simply excreted as a biosludge residue of no particular interest. In saltwater environments, however, the mucous behaves differently: it is hydrophobic and of lower density than seawater, and the organism has been observed to actively manipulate it.

**Mechanism:** MG-2023-15 secretes the hydrocarbon mucous through pores distributed across its ventral and lateral surfaces. Once outside the body, the mucous forms flexible, membrane-bounded bubbles — typically 2–8cm diameter — which the organism can inflate by venting metabolic gas (a mixture of methane and carbon dioxide produced by the anaerobic lower tract) through the same pores. The bubbles are not internally generated as a single bladder; they form as an external cluster, adhering to the organism's surface integument and to each other via the mucous membrane's natural adhesion properties.

A single organism at rest will typically carry 40–200 bubbles distributed across its dorsal and lateral surfaces. The aggregate buoyancy of a full bubble cluster can offset 60–80% of the organism's body weight in seawater, allowing it to maintain depth position with minimal muscular effort and to rise slowly toward the surface when feeding or respiratory conditions require.

**Cluster behaviour:** When multiple organisms aggregate — as they do when feeding on a concentrated carbon source such as a composite surface — their bubble clusters interact. Bubbles from adjacent organisms merge into larger shared structures across the colony surface, creating a collective buoyancy system. A feeding colony of 20–30 organisms can generate a shared bubble mat covering several square metres of water surface, giving the colony as a whole far greater buoyancy control than any individual organism possesses.

**Appearance:** A feeding colony on a vessel hull appears from below as a pale yellowish-grey mass with an iridescent, slightly shimmering surface — the bubble clusters catching available light in the water column. From above the waterline, the shared bubble mat resembles an oily, faintly luminescent surface slick. At night, the organisms' bioluminescent dorsal patches glow faintly through the bubble mat, producing a diffuse blue-green shimmer across the surface of the slick.

### The Carbon Composite Connection

Carbon-fibre composite materials — including the type identified on Imperial Navy cruiser rudders at New Mondulkiri — are processed by MG-2023-15's digestive system as an accessible carbon source in mineral-rich environments. In laboratory conditions, a single organism can degrade approximately 0.3 kg of carbon-composite material per day under optimal conditions.

A colony of 50–100 organisms in sustained contact with a vessel's rudder assembly would produce measurable structural degradation within 90–120 days. The degradation pattern mimics natural biofouling in its early stages — surface roughening, micro-cracking at the resin matrix interface — before becoming structurally significant. The Imperial maintenance schedule for the Gashidda garrison fleet, based on the occupation's documented history of infrastructure neglect, is unlikely to detect this within the operational window.

**The strategic value of rudder degradation:** Progressive reduction in hydrodynamic efficiency, then manoeuvrability precision, then — at advanced stages — structural failure risk under combat manoeuvring loads. A fleet whose vessels are experiencing slow, undetected composite degradation is a fleet that will perform significantly worse in any rapid-deployment scenario than its maintenance records indicate.

### Secondary Hazard — The Mucous Release Problem `[OC]`

The buoyancy bubble system creates a significant secondary threat to Imperial naval operations that Confederate Naval Intelligence has not yet fully modelled.

**Hull fouling via hydrocarbon mucous:** When MG-2023-15 colonies feed on a vessel's composite surfaces, the hydrocarbon mucous is continuously produced and shed. In harbour conditions with limited water circulation, the mucous accumulates on surrounding hull surfaces. It is viscous, adhesive, and resistant to standard anti-fouling coatings — it bonds to metal, polymer, and treated alloy surfaces at roughly equal efficiency. A vessel that has hosted a feeding colony for 60+ days will have accumulated mucous deposits across the lower hull, propeller assemblies, and any other submerged surface the colony has been in proximity to.

**Propulsion system contamination:** The mucous has sufficient viscosity to partially impede propeller shaft seals if it penetrates the bearing assembly — a process that takes time but which the organism's bubble-production behaviour actively facilitates, as bubbles carrying mucous particulate are continuously generated near the hull surface and drift into any available mechanical clearance.

**The combustion hazard:** Hydrocarbon mucous contains methane-derivative compounds from the anaerobic lower tract in appreciable concentration. In enclosed or semi-enclosed spaces — enclosed dock bays, maintenance tunnels, the underside of a vessel with limited water circulation — the mucous outgassing creates a flammable vapour layer at the water surface. This is not an immediate explosive hazard under open harbour conditions; the vapour disperses. However, in the enclosed maintenance bays at New Mondulkiri's LSP shipyard — where vessels are brought in over drydock cradles with restricted water flow — the mucous accumulation and methane outgassing could reach concentrations that represent a genuine fire hazard during routine maintenance operations involving cutting, welding, or electrical work near the waterline.

**The surface slick as indicator:** The bubble mat that forms above a feeding colony is the most visible sign of organism presence and also its greatest operational liability. In daylight, an experienced harbour master would notice an unusual oily slick near a vessel in berth. At night, the bioluminescent shimmer is visible to anyone looking at the water surface from a dock. The organisms are not stealthy in the way that most biological agents are. They are effective precisely because what they do looks, to an untrained observer, like ordinary marine fouling — until it doesn't.

**Prior deployment context — why Gashidda is different:** In MG-2023-15's documented deployments on Merganser and comparable low-productivity agricultural worlds, the mucous bubble system was largely inconspicuous. Those environments are comparatively oligotrophic — low in dissolved organic matter, minimal existing surface slick, clear water with limited chemical noise. The hydrocarbon mucous dispersed quickly and blended with the sparse existing chemistry. On Gashidda it does not. The equatorial reef is a eutrophic, biologically hyperproductive environment with an existing complex organic chemistry in the water column — dissolved reef compounds, biological off-gassing from the Shelf Architect formations, hydrocarbon trace compounds from the arcology industrial tiers. In this context MG-2023-15's mucous output does not disperse cleanly. It accumulates, aggregates with existing organic compounds, and behaves as a visible, persistent surface feature far more rapidly than in previous deployment environments. What was negligible chemistry on Merganser is a detectable slick on Gashidda within weeks of colony establishment.

**The eutrophic net balance:** The organism's mucous system does provide a partial ecological offset in Gashidda's harbour environment. The hydrocarbon compounds it secretes partially digest some of the existing organic pollutants present in harbour water — industrial runoff from the arcology lower tiers, fuel trace compounds from vessel operations, biological waste from the LSP shipyard. In this sense MG-2023-15 acts as a weak biological remediation agent for some harbour contaminants. However, the compounds it releases in exchange — the methane derivatives and partially digested hydrocarbon residues from its own metabolic processing — are themselves pollutants, entering the water column in forms that the existing reef chemistry cannot readily process. The net ecological balance is approximately neutral: the organism cleans some pollution while creating different pollution of roughly equivalent impact. It will not purify New Mondulkiri harbour, and it will not poison it. What it will do is alter its chemistry in ways that are locally detectable and globally uncharacterised.

**Natural regulation — Gashidda's predator fauna:** The Gashiddan equatorial reef is not a passive environment. It is a biologically complex, predator-rich ecosystem that has been processing novel organic inputs for millions of years. MG-2023-15, despite being bioengineered, presents to Gashidda's reef predators as exactly what it resembles: a slow-moving, nutrient-dense, conspicuously bioluminescent food source with no defensive capability beyond a reluctant bite.

The **Void Mantle** (*Abyssocephalus velatus*) — the apex ambush predator of the mid-shelf, nearly optically transparent with a 4–12m tentacle curtain and neurotoxic contact venom — hunts by chemical gradient and light signature in precisely the depth range where MG-2023-15 colonies would establish. The bubble clusters and bioluminescent dorsal patches that make the organism operationally detectable also make it conspicuous to anything hunting by chemical trace or light in dark reef water. A Void Mantle encountering an MG-2023-15 feeding colony would experience it as an unusually easy meal.

Gikhi eels, reef predators, and other mid-shelf fauna are similarly expected to discover MG-2023-15 as a novel and accessible prey species within one to two seasonal cycles of introduction. The organism has no evolved defences against Gashiddan predators — its Merganser deployment environment contained nothing remotely comparable.

The consequence is that Gashidda's predator fauna will likely exert significant population control on any introduced MG-2023-15 colony without deliberate management. Colony size may never reach the containment-failure threshold of ~200 organisms not because of Confederate operational discipline but because the reef eats them faster than they reproduce. This is simultaneously reassuring and operationally concerning: if predation keeps the colony below the threshold for visible slick formation, it also keeps it below the threshold for effective composite degradation. The agents may be releasing organisms into an ecosystem that will simply consume them.

Confederate Naval Intelligence's assessment of this dynamic remains incomplete. The Macrogenesis field data from Merganser contains no predation pressure data applicable to Gashidda's ecosystem. Seluun Mian-Ashta, who could model this with reasonable accuracy, is not available as a resource to Confederate Naval Intelligence for obvious reasons. The follow-up mission that delivers MG-2023-15 to New Mondulkiri harbour may be delivering a biological weapon, a short-lived ecological curiosity, or something that behaves in ways neither side has anticipated. The agents tasked with delivery will not know which until it is too late to change course.

**Containment failure scenario:** If a colony grows beyond approximately 200 organisms in a concentrated area — which the nutrient-rich Gashiddan harbour environment would support within 6–8 months of initial introduction — the aggregate mucous production exceeds what normal harbour water circulation can disperse. At that scale, the slick becomes impossible to dismiss as ordinary fouling, the combustion hazard in enclosed dock spaces becomes operationally significant, and the organisms' bioluminescent colonies become visible to aerial or drone surveillance. The operation transitions from covert biological degradation to a detectable environmental incident. Confederate Naval Intelligence must plan for this threshold and either intend to reach it (accepting that it will be identified but calculating that the damage will already be done) or maintain colony size below it through means that have not yet been determined.

### Operational Considerations

**Delivery vector:** Submersible release near the New Mondulkiri maintenance berth foundations — the same approach route used in REEF LIGHT. Canisters of 10–20 juvenile organisms, released at the aerobic-anaerobic boundary layer (2–5m depth) near the target vessels, would require no further active management. The organisms are self-directing toward carbon-composite surfaces.

**Depth constraint:** Delivery must occur at the surface layer or in very shallow harbour water. Deep-water release would strand the organisms below their viable aerobic zone. This constrains the delivery submersible to an even shallower, more detectable approach profile than REEF LIGHT used — and makes the operation significantly higher-risk for the delivering agent.

**Risk of detection — organism:** Low initially; rising over time. Bioluminescent dorsal patches are distinctive. The surface slick becomes visible within 4–6 weeks of colony establishment. Seluun Mian-Ashta would likely identify the organisms as non-native within 6–12 months.

**Risk of detection — mucous:** Medium and rising. Hydrocarbon slick in a naval harbour with restricted water circulation will be noticed by maintenance personnel within weeks of colony maturity. It will initially be attributed to vessel fuel or mechanical contamination.

**Risk of ecological impact:** Significant and unquantified. The mucous system adds a dimension the Merganser field data did not cover — MG-2023-15 in saltwater not only degrades composite surfaces but continuously deposits hydrophobic hydrocarbon compounds into the water column. The long-term effect on Gashidda's equatorial reef chemistry, particularly on organisms like the Shelf Architect that depend on precise mineral precipitation chemistry, is entirely unknown.

**Seluun's position:** Categorical opposition. The Irriszhia Tuhuir's philosophy of curiosity as reverence prohibits disrupting knowledge that is still being gathered. She would not report the agents — but she would spend the remainder of her 200-year lifespan documenting the consequences. This is a meaningful position. It should be treated as one.
