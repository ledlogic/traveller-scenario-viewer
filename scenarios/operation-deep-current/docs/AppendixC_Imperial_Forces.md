# APPENDIX C — IMPERIAL FORCES
## C.1 — RSP-7-G REEF PICKET STATION

### C.1.1 — Overview

The Imperial Navy maintains a network of fixed sensor picket stations on the outer reef shelf surrounding New Mondulkiri, monitoring sonar coverage of the deep-water approaches that the shipyard's vessel traffic cannot adequately screen. These stations fill the gap between the base's active sonar arrays and the acoustic noise floor created by legitimate reef-shelf commercial traffic in the inner approaches.

A typical outer-shelf picket station is a **semi-submerged fixed installation** — anchored to the reef cap at approximately 80–120 metres depth, with its upper section breaking the surface. It is not a vessel and cannot manoeuvre. It is crewed by a small team of technical specialists and a security detachment, rotated on 60-day assignments from the New Mondulkiri naval base. It has no jump capability, no main drives, and no weapons systems heavier than personal arms and one defensive turret. It is, in short, a listening post — and its intelligence value lies entirely in its data storage and live sensor feeds.

**Designation format:** RSP-[number]-G, e.g. RSP-7-G (Reef Sensor Picket 7, Gashidda). The station relevant to *Operation Deep Current* is **RSP-7-G**, positioned on the outer shelf at bearing 340° from New Mondulkiri, covering the northern deep-water channel.

**Intelligence value for the scenario:** The REEF LIGHT mission photographs sensor array *configurations* from outside. A boarding of RSP-7-G recovers the actual *sensor data archive* — patrol schedules, vessel identification signatures, detection thresholds, and the specific acoustic profiles used to identify Confederation vessels. This is a substantially higher-value intelligence package than REEF LIGHT's photography, at substantially higher risk. It can replace or supplement REEF LIGHT as a scenario objective.

---

### C.1.2 — Physical Description

RSP-7-G is a **cylindrical pressure structure** approximately **28 metres tall and 12 metres in external diameter**, anchored vertically to the reef cap. The lower 20 metres are permanently submerged; the upper 8 metres extend above the waterline as a low surface platform with sensor masts, communications arrays, and a landing pad sized for one small atmospheric or aquatic craft.

The station is constructed from **standard Imperial Navy hull-grade alloy** — the same material specification as system defence boats — with an internal pressure rating of 600 atmospheres (far exceeding any operational requirement at 120 metres depth; the overspecification reflects standard Imperial naval engineering doctrine rather than necessity).

External colour is the **grey-green anti-corrosion coating** standard to Imperial Navy installations in the Dingir subsector. At surface level the upper section is streaked with Driftweb accumulation — the filamentous algae adheres to any horizontal surface exposed to the atmosphere — giving the station a characteristically organic appearance that makes it harder to identify visually at range. The sensor masts are kept clear of Driftweb by automated cleaning systems; the rest of the hull is not.

**External features:**
- **4 sensor mast clusters** (surface level), each 6m tall: sonar transceiver heads, passive EM arrays, identification-friend-or-foe transponder
- **1 communications dome** (surface level): encrypted burst-transmission, hardline to New Mondulkiri via seafloor cable, backup tight-beam directional antenna
- **1 defensive turret** (surface level, retractable): single laser, TL12, anti-air and anti-surface capable; range 10km atmospheric
- **1 landing pad** (surface level): 15m × 12m, rated for 60-ton craft; no hangar
- **2 external docking collars** (submerged, Level 2): standard Imperial Navy fitting, accommodates submarines and diving bells up to 200 tons
- **Emergency escape pod** (Level 3): single pod, 6-person capacity, surface buoyant

### C.1.4 — Level Detail

**LEVEL 5 — Surface Platform** *(8m above waterline, open to atmosphere)*

Diameter 12m external, approximately 9m usable deck space accounting for sensor mast bases and the communications dome housing. The landing pad occupies the northern two-thirds; the defensive turret occupies a retractable housing in the southern quadrant. The turret is normally retracted below deck level when no threat is assessed — it rises and locks in approximately 4 seconds on alert.

Access from surface to Level 4: one primary hatch (1.2m diameter, pressure-rated, biometric lock) and one emergency hatch (0.8m diameter, manual override available from both sides).

The sensor masts cannot be disabled from Level 5 without cutting tools — they are hardwired to Level 1 processing. Destroying a mast cluster (Armour 6, Hull 4) alerts the duty officer immediately and triggers a distress broadcast on military frequencies.

**LEVEL 4 — Command and Watch** *(2m above waterline, internal diameter 10m)*

The operational heart of the station. A circular compartment divided into three areas by low internal partitions rather than full bulkheads — there are no lockable internal doors on Level 4, by design, to allow the watch team to see the full compartment at all times.

*Operations centre* (north quadrant): Four console stations running sensor monitoring software (TL12). The duty watch officer sits here. Live feeds from all sensor arrays; alert panels; direct comms to New Mondulkiri naval base. The consoles are hardened against EMP (Armour 3 vs electronic attack). Each console has a hard-wired physical destruct charge — a small shaped explosive that destroys the processing core without damaging adjacent stations. These are armed by a key-and-code procedure requiring two crew members. If the duty watch has time and warning, the consoles will be destroyed before capture.

*Watch station* (south quadrant): The secondary watch position with a view of the landing pad via a reinforced viewport. One crew member on watch here at all times during normal operations.

*Duty officer quarters* (west alcove): A small private space — bunk, desk, personal locker. The duty officer sleeps here during their off-watch hours.

*Armoury* (east alcove): Weapons locker (biometric, same code as primary hatch). Standard loadout: 4 × ACR with underslung grenade launcher (4D frag, 4D stun rounds, 4 each), 2 × snub pistol, 4 × combination mask, 4 × cloth armour vest, 1 × PGMP-12 (locked separately; requires two-person authentication). `[MGT2]` The PGMP is present because the station's rules of engagement explicitly cover the possibility of capturing hostile vessels attempting to board — it is a deterrent, not expected to be used in the confined interior.

**PGMP inside a pressure hull:** The PGMP deals 2DD damage (roll 2D then double the result — average 14, maximum 24). In open field conditions this makes it a crew-served anti-armour weapon. Inside RSP-7-G it is effectively a last resort that destroys the station along with everything in it. The compartments are 10m diameter at most; plasma discharge at that range superheats the atmosphere, risks rupturing internal conduits and life support plumbing, and creates fire and pressure hazard in a sealed environment with no external venting. Any crew member who fires a PGMP inside the station has committed to killing everyone aboard including themselves. The duty watch knows this. The PGMP's presence in the armoury is about signalling to boarders what the crew are *prepared* to do — not what they *intend* to do. To raise the stakes in the final phase of a boarding encounter have the duty officer unlock the PGMP safe on hard alert and let the players see it happen.

*Stairwell* and *ladder access* connect Level 4 to Level 3 (down) and Level 5 (up). The stairwell is 0.9m wide.

**LEVEL 3 — Crew Quarters and Support** *(4m below waterline, internal diameter 10m)*

Eight bunks in two four-bunk alcoves, separated by a central corridor. The galley occupies the northern section — a fully automated food-prep unit (Imperial Navy standard) capable of 60-day operation without resupply. Adjacent to the galley is a small common area with seating for six, screens (entertainment and briefing capability), and a window — one of only two on the station, a 0.4m diameter reinforced port with a view of the reef face.

*Medical bay* (south section): One treatment table, full TL12 autodoc unit, 30-day pharmaceutical supply including combat drugs and anagathics for the crew's personal supply. The autodoc can treat wounds up to near-fatal without surgeon assistance (Medic equivalent 2 for automated procedures).

*Escape pod bay* (south-east): The pod is a streamlined cylinder, 6-person capacity, surface-buoyant. Launch is vertical — it exits through a hatch in the Level 3 floor and rises. The pod has a 48-hour atmospheric life support capacity, an emergency beacon, and no propulsion beyond the buoyancy system. It cannot submerge once launched.

**LEVEL 2 — Engineering and Access** *(8m below waterline, internal diameter 10m)*

The entry point for any underwater approach. Two docking collars on the east and west faces — standard Imperial Navy fittings. A vessel or submarine docking here connects to an **airlock chamber** (4m × 3m, rated to 20 atmospheres) that cycles in 45 seconds. The inner airlock door opens into the Level 2 main compartment.

*Power plant*: A compact TL12 fusion plant, 180-day fuel cycle, occupying the northern third of the compartment. Armour 6, Hull 8. Destroying the power plant takes the station dark and offline — life support switches to battery backup (8-hour capacity). It does not destroy the data archive on Level 1, which has its own battery.

*Life support*: Atmospheric recycling, water recovery, thermal regulation. Occupies the southern section. Disabling life support is a slower approach to forcing evacuation — crew can operate on combination masks for approximately 4 hours before CO2 build-up becomes incapacitating.

*Wet storage and equipment lockers*: Diving gear, maintenance tools, spare parts for the sensor arrays. Two sets of full diving equipment, four sets of underwater maneuvering harness, one small underwater maintenance drone (unarmed, TL10, range 500m).

*Emergency manual controls*: A panel here allows manual override of the docking collar external locks, airlock cycling, and ballast tank adjustment. This is the panel that an agent who gets to Level 2 needs to reach to allow a submersible to dock silently without triggering the standard docking alert on Level 4.

**LEVEL 1 — Sensor Core** *(14m below waterline, internal diameter 8m — the level narrows here as the hull tapers)*

Access from Level 2 by a single ladder only — no stairwell. The hatch at the top of the ladder is a standard pressure hatch, not biometric locked under normal operations (the assumption being that anyone already on Level 2 is authorised).

The sensor core is the station's reason for existence and its primary intelligence value. The compartment is dominated by a central processing column (floor to ceiling, 2m diameter) containing the array processing hardware. Around it: four workstations, a data archive terminal, and the hardline cable terminus where the seafloor cable to New Mondulkiri enters the hull.

*Data archive*: The archive terminal holds up to 90 days of raw sensor data plus the processed intelligence assessments generated by the duty watch team. It is encrypted (TL12 encryption, requires Electronics (computers) 10+ to break without the access codes, or 6+ with the duty officer's codes). The archive is on a dedicated battery — it will continue to run for 72 hours after total station power loss. **This is the primary target for the REEF LIGHT upgrade objective.** A successful download yields: patrol schedules (60-day cycle), vessel acoustic signatures, detection threshold parameters, and the specific sonar filter configurations used to screen out reef-shelf biological noise (Driftweb particle rain, Deep Bell migration, Thermal Angel discharge events).

*Hardline cable terminus*: The seafloor cable runs from this point to New Mondulkiri, carrying live sensor data. It also carries the station's distress signal. Cutting the cable (requires sharp tools and 2 minutes of work) severs the live data feed but does not trigger an immediate alert — the base's monitoring system will flag a cable fault in approximately 10 minutes. Cutting the cable before boarding is an option but creates a 10-minute alert window rather than providing cover.

**MECHANICAL BASEMENT** *(20m below waterline, access via maintenance hatch from Level 1)*

Not normally crewed. Contains the anchor system (four reef bolts rated to 800 tons each), secondary power batteries, and ballast tank controls. The maintenance hatch is a simple manual lever — no lock. The ballast tanks cannot sink the station (it is positively buoyant overall) but can be flooded to settle it lower in the water column by up to 3 metres, which brings the docking collars into a slightly more favourable depth for large vessels.

**Duty rotation: 9 crew (8 + 1 officer), 60-day assignment**

| Role | Number | Key Skills | Notes |

| Sensor Technician | 3 | Electronics (sensors) 2, Electronics (computers) 1 | Run console rotations; 1 on watch at all times |
| Engineering Technician | 2 | Engineer (power) 2, Mechanic 2 | Maintain power and life support; sleep Level 3 |
| Security Rating | 2 | Gun Combat (slug) 2, Melee (unarmed) 1, Athletics 1 | Armed at all times on duty; share watch with sensor tech |
| Medical/Support Rating | 1 | Medic 2, Admin 1 | Operates medical bay; secondary watch capability |

**At any given time:** 1 sensor tech and 1 security rating are on watch (Level 4). The watch officer is either at the operations centre or in their quarters. All others are sleeping, eating, or performing maintenance.

**Combat characteristics:** The crew are trained naval personnel, not combat specialists. The security ratings are the station's combat capability — the sensor technicians and engineering staff will retreat to defensible positions (Level 3 or Level 1) rather than fight. The watch officer will attempt to send a distress signal before anything else. **The station has no marine complement.** It was not designed to resist a determined boarding — it was designed to be too isolated and too uninviting to attract one.

### C.1.6 — Approach and Entry Options

**Option A — Underwater docking (recommended)** `[OC]`

Approach via submersible along the reef shelf below the station's sonar coverage threshold. RSP-7-G's own sonar is aimed outward and downward — toward the deep-water channel it monitors. It does not have good close-range coverage directly underneath itself. Three-Fin's submersible can approach from below, dock at one of the Level 2 collars, and agents can enter through the airlock.

Risk: The docking collar connection triggers a soft alert on Level 4 (a tone and a docking indicator light). The duty watch will query the docking via intercom before it becomes a hard alert. Agents have approximately 30 seconds to respond convincingly (maintenance crew, routine inspection, supply delivery) or the hard alert goes to New Mondulkiri. **Deception 8+** to pass the intercom query; DM+1 with legitimate-seeming Seaharvester credentials.

Silencing the docking alert requires getting to the manual controls panel on Level 2 before docking — which requires a swimmer to precede the submersible. **Athletics (dexterity) 8+** to navigate the exterior hull in current conditions; **Electronics (computers) 8+** to reach and operate the manual panel.

**Option B — Surface entry** `[OC]`

Land on or approach the surface platform. The defensive turret is the primary hazard — it will engage any unidentified vessel approaching within 2km without a valid IFF transponder signal. If the agents have a valid IFF (obtained separately — this is its own mission, involving the TIDE MARK operation or prior intelligence work), a surface approach is viable.

Entry via the primary hatch requires either the biometric code (from the duty officer, obtained by other means) or **Electronics (computers) 10+** to bypass the lock (4 minutes of exposed work on the surface platform). The emergency hatch can be forced with **Strength 10+** or cutting tools (6 minutes; loud).

**Option C — Distraction and insertion** `[OC]`

Create a situation that draws the watch officer's attention to the surface (a false contact on the sensor screens, a debris strike on the hull, a fabricated sonar anomaly) while agents enter via the docking collar. Requires an external asset to generate the distraction. Kofi's network has one contact with access to a maintenance submersible — the contact can create acoustic noise on a specific bearing if given 4 hours advance notice. This draws the watch team's active attention outward while agents enter from below.

**Option D — Loud Entry** `[OC]`

For a group that chooses to go in hard — accepting that stealth is over and the mission clock starts immediately.

**Precondition:** Agents must first acquire weapons. Three legal sources exist within the scenario:

| Source | What's Available | How |
|--------|-----------------|-----|
| RSP-7-G armoury (Level 4) | 4× ACR, 2× snub pistol, 4× cloth armour | Must reach Level 4 undetected first — contradicts loud entry unless one agent goes ahead |
| Three-Fin's emergency cache | 2× snub pistol (waterproofed), 1× electrostatic rod | Three-Fin has these. She will loan them for the boarding. She wants them back. |
| Tarquin's network | 2× blade weapon (illegal, Law 9 violation if caught) | Requires prior contact with Tarquin and a specific request — he will provide without comment |

**Loud Entry Sequence:**

1. Surface approach to landing pad (Option B rules apply for turret)
2. **Force primary hatch:** STR 10+ or cutting tools (2 min, loud). Hatch forced triggers soft alert immediately.
3. **45-second window:** Agents descend to Level 4 before hard alert fires. **Athletics (dexterity) 8+** each to reach Level 4 in time.
4. **Hard alert fires** when agents are visible on internal sensors. Duty officer arms crew. Console destruct sequence initiated (90 seconds).
5. **Combat resolution:** 9 crew. Security ratings fight (Gun Combat 2, Melee 1). Sensor techs and engineering staff retreat to Level 1 or Level 3. Watch officer goes for the PGMP safe if not restrained within 2 rounds.
6. **Data archive wipe** initiates at hard alert +90 seconds. Must reach Level 1 and cancel within 4 minutes.

**Loud Entry Advantage:** Speed. The entire station is under agent control in 3–5 minutes if the combat goes well. No deception checks, no intercom queries, no slow-burn tension.

**Loud Entry Cost:** New Mondulkiri response vessel (Shafin-class) launches immediately on naval base alert. **25–30 minutes** to station arrival at maximum speed. Extraction window is hard. Voss Suspicion track goes to 5 automatically. Any future scenario appearance at New Mondulkiri requires new cover identities.

**Combat Note — confined spaces:** The station's 10m diameter compartments make ranged combat at Medium range the maximum. ACRs are slightly unwieldy (−1 to hit in confined quarters at your discretion). Snub pistols and melee are better suited. The duty officer knows this — she will try to close to melee range with the PGMP rather than fire it, using it as a threat rather than a weapon. She is not suicidal.

**The PGMP moment:** If the duty officer reaches the PGMP safe and opens it, the room changes. She holds 2DD average-14 damage in a confined space and everyone in the room knows what happens if she fires it. This is the scenario's highest-stakes negotiation moment. What does she want? She wants her crew alive and the station intact. What do the agents want? The archive. There is a deal here if someone thinks to make it.

### C.1.7 — Alert States and Response

| State | Trigger | Response |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Normal** | No contact | 1 tech + 1 security on watch; weapon locker locked |
| **Soft alert** | Unidentified docking contact; sensor anomaly | Duty officer to watch station; intercom query; New Mondulkiri notified by message (not distress) |
| **Hard alert** | No response to query; confirmed hostile; destruct ordered | All crew to stations; PGMP unlocked; distress signal sent; console destruct initiated (90 seconds) |
| **Destruct in progress** | Hard alert + 90 seconds | Consoles destroyed; data archive wipe initiated (4 minutes); crew arm and prepare to resist or evacuate |

**Data archive wipe** is the crew's primary defensive action — recovering the archive requires reaching Level 1 and cancelling the wipe within 4 minutes of it initiating. The wipe command can only be issued from Level 4, and can only be cancelled from Level 1. This creates an inherent tension: the agents need to control both decks simultaneously to prevent data loss.

### C.1.10 — Connections to the Main Scenario

**REEF LIGHT upgrade:** RSP-7-G boarding can replace or significantly enhance the REEF LIGHT photography mission. The patrol schedule data recovered from the archive is what allows the agents to time the REEF LIGHT submersible approach safely in the first place — making it a natural prequel to the shipyard photography, if if you want to add a multi-stage operation.

**Three-Fin Valdez:** Already the agents' submersible pilot for REEF LIGHT. She knows the outer reef shelf well enough to approach RSP-7-G from below. She will not board — she stays with the submersible — but her local knowledge gives DM+1 to the approach navigation check.

**Director Voss:** A missing crew member, an anomalous docking contact, or a delayed check-in from RSP-7-G will eventually reach Voss's desk. The Voss Suspicion Track consequences apply as normal. A clean extraction that leaves the crew restrained but unharmed and the station apparently undamaged is the optimal outcome — the crew will report the boarding, but not for 60 days (when their rotation ends and resupply arrives).

**Driftweb cover:** The approach route at 4–8km altitude is not relevant here, but the sensor filter note is: RSP-7-G specifically filters out the acoustic signature of Driftweb particle rain on its sonar feeds. Three-Fin knows this. A submersible approaching from directly below the station, in the shadow of the Driftweb acoustic noise, is in one of the quietest spots in the station's coverage pattern.
