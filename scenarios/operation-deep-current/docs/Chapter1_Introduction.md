## 1.2 — HISTORICAL TIMELINE — GASHIDDA AND THE SOLOMANI FRONT

*This timeline provides essential context for the scenario. The year 1116 is chosen deliberately: the Third Imperium stands at the apparent height of its power, yet the Solomani Confederation has never stopped fighting — and in the corridors of SolSec, intelligence assessments from 1115 and early 1116 suggest something is shifting at the highest levels of Imperial politics. The agents do not know what. SolSec's analysts do not know what. But the briefings carry a new urgency that wasn't there two years ago.*

Timing is driven by three converging factors: Kofi's assessment that the Vanthara Truth network is at peak organisational strength; intelligence that Councillor Achterberg has accumulated enough personal debt to make the financial frame viable; and classified signals intelligence suggesting unusual activity in Imperial high politics that SolSec's analysts cannot yet characterise but believe may create a narrow window of reduced Imperial counter-intelligence capacity. `[OC]` |

> **Referee's Note on Dramatic Irony:** What the agents do not know — what no one outside a small circle of Imperial insiders knows — is that **Emperor Strephon will be assassinated in 1116**, triggering the Rebellion that will tear the Third Imperium apart. The occupation of Gashidda, the Solomani Front, the entire strategic context of the scenario — all of it is standing on the edge of a cliff that only the referee can see. You do not need to use the Rebellion in this scenario; it runs cleanly as a standalone. But if you want to deepen the experience, consider: the slight distraction in Imperial counter-intelligence posture, the urgency in SolSec's briefings, the agents' sense that history is accelerating without knowing why. The deep current is moving. Even SolSec doesn't know how fast.

## 1.3 — REFEREE OVERVIEW

Operation Deep Current is a social-heavy covert operations scenario in which the players portray SolSec field agents tasked with destabilising Imperial control over **Gashidda** through subversion — social, economic, and political — rather than violence. Gashidda is an Amber Zone under Imperial military occupation, its population deeply sympathetic to the Solomani Cause despite the defeat of all active resistance.

The scenario takes place primarily in **Makara**, one of Gashidda's largest undersea arcologies. `[SF]` The world-building draws heavily on the hostile colonial atmosphere of **Hiroshima Colony**: `[HC]` sealed industrial habitats, tainted atmospheres, corporate control of basic infrastructure, and workers whose lives depend on equipment that the authorities are not interested in maintaining. Players should feel the claustrophobic weight of a world that is dangerous even in its quiet moments.

**The three mission objectives:**
1. **TIDE MARK** — Economically: frame Imperial loyalist Councillor Deven Achterberg with fabricated evidence of accepting Confederation payments, destroying his financial credibility and political usefulness.
2. **SOUNDING LINE** — Socially/Politically: recruit fusion reactor chief technician Harada Mitsuko as a long-term SolSec asset, embedding Confederate sympathies deep in Makara's industrial workforce.
3. **REEF LIGHT** — Intelligence: photograph Imperial Navy warship sensor configurations in the New Mondulkiri shipyard for Confederate Naval Intelligence.

**SolSec values agent survival and network integrity above mission completeness.** Make this clear to players. A partial success with an intact cover is worth more than a sweep with a burned network.

### Gashidda `[SF]`
A cool, dim water world with a grey-green sky. Surface islands are small, storm-battered, and mostly industrial or military. Most citizens live their entire lives below the surface. They were born Solomani in culture, fought Solomani in the Rim War, and remain Solomani in their hearts. The occupation is a wound that has never closed.

**The grey-green sky** is not simply overcast. Gashidda's lower atmosphere — from roughly 2km to 12km altitude — supports a semi-continuous layer of filamentous photosynthetic microorganisms known as the **Driftweb** (*Filamentophyta atmospherica gashiddana*). These organisms are the biological engine of Gashidda's oxygen atmosphere: long-chain filaments 0.1–4mm in diameter, slightly charged, aggregating into slow-moving mat structures that filter and tint all incoming light. From the surface the sky appears as a shifting grey-green overcast that never fully clears; from above, the Driftweb layer is a silver-green biological veil. The reef-gas off-gassing in Makara's lower tiers and the taint in the coastal fog are surface expressions of this same biosphere — the atmosphere of Gashidda is alive from the ocean floor to 12km altitude, a single interconnected biological system.

At TL E the Driftweb has attracted significant commercial interest as a biofuel feedstock. Driftweb filaments are 18–24% lipid by dry weight; scoop-jet atmospheric vehicles harvesting the 4–8km concentration band can collect and bioprocess this material into usable fuel in flight. The technology exists; the infrastructure does not yet at scale. The Imperial military administration has not yet regulated the sector. Seaharvester LIC has filed patents. The window will not stay open indefinitely. See the *Gashidda Bestiary* for full biological and tactical detail.

**Referee notes on the temperature profile:**

The equatorial band (rows 0–1) is thermally stable year-round — the ocean buffers seasonal swing almost entirely at the equator, where the Shelf Architect reef platform and New Mondulkiri sit. Surface temperatures here range from 13°C to 22°C depending on latitude and season — cool temperate, perpetually overcast, with a grey-green sky filtered through biological aerosols and cloud cover driven by the warm-cold ocean boundary. This is why the equatorial reef shelf supports complex biology and human habitation without environmental gear.

The mid-latitude band (rows 3–5) is where the storm systems form — the collision of equatorial heat retention and polar cold creates persistent cyclonic weather. Summer daytime temperatures of -1°C to +10°C in this band, dropping to -19°C to -36°C in deep winter, produce exactly the kind of dramatic seasonal storms the scenario references. Reef biology is still active in this band but increasingly sparse at the colder end.

The polar regions (rows 6–7) are permanently below freezing in winter, barely above freezing in summer. Permanent ice caps. No habitation.

The equatorial stability explains the Vanthara Truth's deep roots in the arcology communities — Gashiddans have never had to adapt to harsh seasonal swings at the surface. Their world is cold and grey but consistent. The unpredictability is lateral — storms from the storm belt, not seasonal temperature shifts — which shapes a culture of communal resilience rather than seasonal preparation.

Low gravity shapes everything about Gashidda and its people in ways the agents will notice immediately. The arcology structures are taller and more open than equivalent standard-gravity cities — the Shelf Architect mineral formations that Makara is built into support more ambitious vertical engineering at under half standard gravity, and the tiers feel correspondingly airy and vertiginous rather than compressed. Equipment, furniture, and vehicles are all built lighter. Seal and pressure fittings use lower-rated tolerances than standard-gravity installations, which partly explains the endemic maintenance failures in the lower industrial tiers.

The Gashiddan population — particularly those whose families have lived here for multiple generations — are visibly adapted: lean, long-limbed, with the light bone density and fluid movement of people who have never had to fight gravity the way standard-world inhabitants do. Tarquin's physical presence owes something to this; he is large by Gashiddan standards, which means something different here than it would elsewhere. Harada moves through the reactor complex with an economy of motion that is partly professional habit and partly the unselfconscious grace of someone born into 0.45G.

The agents, most of them from standard-gravity worlds, will feel the difference. Movement is slightly floaty. Jumps carry further than expected. Thrown objects arc differently. The referee should not make this a mechanical burden — no penalties unless characters are actively doing something gravity-sensitive — but should use it as texture. A character who overcorrects on a leap in the lower industrial tier during Act 3 is a small, vivid detail that costs nothing and earns the table something.

**Low-gravity and cover identity:** Long-term Gashiddan residents can spot off-worlders by how they move. Not reliably, not as a skill check, but as a social tell. The agents have been briefed on this. Moving consciously in 0.45G is something they can train — Athletics (dexterity) 8+ as a one-time check at the start of the scenario represents whether each agent has calibrated well enough to pass casual observation. Failure doesn't blow cover; it just means a Gashiddan who is paying attention might remark that the survey crew moves like people who grew up somewhere heavier.

**Geology — old, cooling, tectonically quietening** `[OC]`

Gashidda is a geologically old world in late middle age. At Size 5 — roughly 63% of Earth's diameter — it has been losing interior heat faster than a larger world for the same stellar age. The result is a planet past its most tectonically active period: the mantle is partially solidified, global plate tectonics have slowed to localised hotspot activity, and the dramatic arc-volcano chains of younger worlds are absent. What remains is a planet that is still geologically alive — hydrothermal vents in the deep equatorial rift zones release enough heat to maintain the biosphere and drive the reef-gas chemistry that permeates the lower arcology tiers — but whose surface expression of that interior activity is muted and ancient.

The near-total ocean coverage reflects this history. Without vigorous plate tectonics to continuously build continental crust, Gashidda's surface is dominated by ancient oceanic basalt everywhere below the waterline, with islands appearing only where two processes have operated long enough to build above sea level: hotspot volcanism punching through the ocean floor in isolated plumes, and the biogenic carbonate-silicate accretion of the Shelf Architect organisms building reef platforms over millions of years. The equatorial reef shelf — where all the arcologies including Makara are built — is the product of the latter: a vast biological construction project running since before the First Imperium, built on a basaltic foundation and capped with the arcite mineral the Shelf Architects secrete.

**Island types:** Gashidda's islands fall into two geological categories. The first are young volcanic seamounts — basaltic, steep-sided, dark, storm-lashed, geologically recent, with active or semi-active fumaroles and hydrothermal venting into surrounding shallows. These are generally too unstable for permanent habitation. The second are ancient reef-cap islands — older basaltic formations now largely covered by centuries of carbonate and arcite biological accretion, geologically stable, flat-topped, and capable of supporting heavy construction. New Mondulkiri is this second type: ancient basalt below, biological mineral accumulation above, geologically quiet, foundations solid enough for a Class A starport and major naval base.

**Practical implications:** The biological off-gassing creating the reef-gas hazard in Makara's lower tiers is a surface expression of deep vent geological activity. The hydrothermal chemistry is the engine of the entire reef ecosystem and, by extension, the arcology's biological infrastructure. The corroded seals and pipe failures in the reactor complex are not purely a maintenance problem — the mineral-rich vent water permeating the lower shelf is mildly corrosive to standard Imperial engineering alloy specifications. The Shelf Architect mineral is actually more chemically resistant than those alloys, something that has not escaped the notice of materials scientists — and one reason Seluun Mian-Ashta's research interests certain Vegan tuhuir with holdings in advanced materials.
