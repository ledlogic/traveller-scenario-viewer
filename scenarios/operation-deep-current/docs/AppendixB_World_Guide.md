### B.2.1 — Gashidda `[SF]`
A cool, dim water world with a grey-green sky. Surface islands are small, storm-battered, and mostly industrial or military. Most citizens live their entire lives below the surface. They were born Solomani in culture, fought Solomani in the Rim War, and remain Solomani in their hearts. The occupation is a wound that has never closed.

**The grey-green sky** is not simply overcast. Gashidda's lower atmosphere — from roughly 2km to 12km altitude — supports a semi-continuous layer of filamentous photosynthetic microorganisms known as the **Driftweb** (*Filamentophyta atmospherica gashiddana*). These organisms are the biological engine of Gashidda's oxygen atmosphere: long-chain filaments 0.1–4mm in diameter, slightly charged, aggregating into slow-moving mat structures that filter and tint all incoming light. From the surface the sky appears as a shifting grey-green overcast that never fully clears; from above, the Driftweb layer is a silver-green biological veil. The reef-gas off-gassing in Makara's lower tiers and the taint in the coastal fog are surface expressions of this same biosphere — the atmosphere of Gashidda is alive from the ocean floor to 12km altitude, a single interconnected biological system.

At TL E the Driftweb has attracted significant commercial interest as a biofuel feedstock. Driftweb filaments are 18–24% lipid by dry weight; scoop-jet atmospheric vehicles harvesting the 4–8km concentration band can collect and bioprocess this material into usable fuel in flight. The technology exists; the infrastructure does not yet at scale. The Imperial military administration has not yet regulated the sector. Seaharvester LIC has filed patents. The window will not stay open indefinitely. See the *Gashidda Bestiary* for full biological and tactical detail.

**Temperature profile notes:**

The equatorial band (rows 0–1) is thermally stable year-round — the ocean buffers seasonal swing almost entirely at the equator, where the Shelf Architect reef platform and New Mondulkiri sit. Surface temperatures here range from 13°C to 22°C depending on latitude and season — cool temperate, perpetually overcast, with a grey-green sky filtered through biological aerosols and cloud cover driven by the warm-cold ocean boundary. This is why the equatorial reef shelf supports complex biology and human habitation without environmental gear.

The mid-latitude band (rows 3–5) is where the storm systems form — the collision of equatorial heat retention and polar cold creates persistent cyclonic weather. Summer daytime temperatures of -1°C to +10°C in this band, dropping to -19°C to -36°C in deep winter, produce exactly the kind of dramatic seasonal storms the scenario references. Reef biology is still active in this band but increasingly sparse at the colder end.

The polar regions (rows 6–7) are permanently below freezing in winter, barely above freezing in summer. Permanent ice caps. No habitation.

The equatorial stability explains the Vanthara Truth's deep roots in the arcology communities — Gashiddans have never had to adapt to harsh seasonal swings at the surface. Their world is cold and grey but consistent. The unpredictability is lateral — storms from the storm belt, not seasonal temperature shifts — which shapes a culture of communal resilience rather than seasonal preparation.

Low gravity shapes everything about Gashidda and its people in ways the agents will notice immediately. The arcology structures are taller and more open than equivalent standard-gravity cities — the Shelf Architect mineral formations that Makara is built into support more ambitious vertical engineering at under half standard gravity, and the tiers feel correspondingly airy and vertiginous rather than compressed. Equipment, furniture, and vehicles are all built lighter. Seal and pressure fittings use lower-rated tolerances than standard-gravity installations, which partly explains the endemic maintenance failures in the lower industrial tiers.

The Gashiddan population — particularly those whose families have lived here for multiple generations — are visibly adapted: lean, long-limbed, with the light bone density and fluid movement of people who have never had to fight gravity the way standard-world inhabitants do. Tarquin's physical presence owes something to this; he is large by Gashiddan standards, which means something different here than it would elsewhere. Harada moves through the reactor complex with an economy of motion that is partly professional habit and partly the unselfconscious grace of someone born into 0.45G.

The agents, most of them from standard-gravity worlds, will feel the difference. Movement is slightly floaty. Jumps carry further than expected. Thrown objects arc differently. Do not make this a mechanical burden — no penalties unless characters are actively doing something gravity-sensitive — use it as texture instead. A character who overcorrects on a leap in the lower industrial tier during Act 3 is a small, vivid detail that costs nothing and earns the table something.

**Low-gravity and cover identity:** Long-term Gashiddan residents can spot off-worlders by how they move. Not reliably, not as a skill check, but as a social tell. The agents have been briefed on this. Moving consciously in 0.45G is something they can train — Athletics (dexterity) 8+ as a one-time check at the start of the scenario represents whether each agent has calibrated well enough to pass casual observation. Failure doesn't blow cover; it just means a Gashiddan who is paying attention might remark that the survey crew moves like people who grew up somewhere heavier.

**Geology — old, cooling, tectonically quietening** `[OC]`

Gashidda is a geologically old world in late middle age. At Size 5 — roughly 63% of Earth's diameter — it has been losing interior heat faster than a larger world for the same stellar age. The result is a planet past its most tectonically active period: the mantle is partially solidified, global plate tectonics have slowed to localised hotspot activity, and the dramatic arc-volcano chains of younger worlds are absent. What remains is a planet that is still geologically alive — hydrothermal vents in the deep equatorial rift zones release enough heat to maintain the biosphere and drive the reef-gas chemistry that permeates the lower arcology tiers — but whose surface expression of that interior activity is muted and ancient.

The near-total ocean coverage reflects this history. Without vigorous plate tectonics to continuously build continental crust, Gashidda's surface is dominated by ancient oceanic basalt everywhere below the waterline, with islands appearing only where two processes have operated long enough to build above sea level: hotspot volcanism punching through the ocean floor in isolated plumes, and the biogenic carbonate-silicate accretion of the Shelf Architect organisms building reef platforms over millions of years. The equatorial reef shelf — where all the arcologies including Makara are built — is the product of the latter: a vast biological construction project running since before the First Imperium, built on a basaltic foundation and capped with the arcite mineral the Shelf Architects secrete.

**Island types:** Gashidda's islands fall into two geological categories. The first are young volcanic seamounts — basaltic, steep-sided, dark, storm-lashed, geologically recent, with active or semi-active fumaroles and hydrothermal venting into surrounding shallows. These are generally too unstable for permanent habitation. The second are ancient reef-cap islands — older basaltic formations now largely covered by centuries of carbonate and arcite biological accretion, geologically stable, flat-topped, and capable of supporting heavy construction. New Mondulkiri is this second type: ancient basalt below, biological mineral accumulation above, geologically quiet, foundations solid enough for a Class A starport and major naval base.

**Practical implications:** The biological off-gassing creating the reef-gas hazard in Makara's lower tiers is a surface expression of deep vent geological activity. The hydrothermal chemistry is the engine of the entire reef ecosystem and, by extension, the arcology's biological infrastructure. The corroded seals and pipe failures in the reactor complex are not purely a maintenance problem — the mineral-rich vent water permeating the lower shelf is mildly corrosive to standard Imperial engineering alloy specifications. The Shelf Architect mineral is actually more chemically resistant than those alloys, something that has not escaped the notice of materials scientists — and one reason Seluun Mian-Ashta's research interests certain Vegan tuhuir with holdings in advanced materials.

## B.3 — NEW MONDULKIRI — GEOGRAPHY, GEOLOGY AND POLITICAL STATUS

### B.3.1 — What the Canon Establishes `[SF]`

The *Solomani Front* describes New Mondulkiri in a single sentence: *"The large starport at the island-capital of New Mondulkiri hosts one of the subsector's largest Imperial Navy bases and an associated Ling Standard Products shipyard."* All further detail below is extrapolated from world data and the broader Gashidda entry.

---

### B.3.2 — Geography

New Mondulkiri is a **reef-cap island** in Gashidda's equatorial zone — one of the world's very few permanent surface features above sea level. It sits atop an ancient basaltic seamount, its original volcanic formation now almost entirely buried under millennia of Shelf Architect carbonate-silicate accretion. The island is flat-topped, geologically stable, and chemically inert at surface level — all necessary conditions for the Class A starport and naval installation built on it.

Size is constrained by its geological origin. New Mondulkiri is not large — perhaps 15–20 km at its widest, with low coastal cliffs of pale grey-white arcite mineral on the windward side and a shallow-gradient reef approach on the leeward. The ocean in all directions is the same cool grey-green. Surface weather is persistent and unpleasant: equatorial storm cells circulate around Gashidda's ocean with nothing to interrupt them, and New Mondulkiri sits directly in their path. Wind is constant. Rain is frequent. The sky is rarely fully clear.

The island has no natural freshwater — all water is processed from seawater or recovered from atmospheric condensation. No agriculture. No natural resources of value. It exists because a flat stable surface above sea level is extremely rare on Gashidda, and the Imperium needed one.

---

### B.3.3 — The Starport and Naval Base

**Class A Starport** — New Mondulkiri Interplanetary. Imperial territory under standard starport extrality — legally a pocket of the Imperium regardless of the surrounding world's political status. Full refined fuel, complete repair facilities, naval yards, and the Ling Standard Products shipyard occupying the eastern half of the island's industrial zone.

The starport handles the world's entire interstellar traffic, which means every agent, supply shipment, corporate representative, and piece of intelligence flowing to and from Gashidda passes through here. It is the occupation's logistical throat.

**The Naval Base** — one of the largest in Dingir subsector. `[SF]` The Dingir subsector fleet maintains a permanent presence: the subsector fleet flagship (a cruiser or carrier), supporting destroyers, and system defence boats operated directly by the Imperial Navy since Gashidda has no permitted planetary navy under military rule. Additional transport and assault ships are assigned to support Imperial Army occupation duties. `[SF]`

**The cruisers in REEF LIGHT's maintenance berths** are subsector fleet assets, likely mid-range tonnage by Imperial standards (50,000–100,000 tons), in for routine refit between patrols. Their sensor array configurations are of interest to Confederate Naval Intelligence because they reveal the Imperium's current electronic warfare doctrine on the Solomani Front.

**The LSP Shipyard** occupies the eastern industrial zone. Civilian in classification but effectively an extension of the naval base — it handles warship maintenance contracts and the refit cycles of the garrison fleet. LSP's relationship with the Imperial military government is comfortable and mutually profitable. Harada's union activity touched this organisation indirectly: the reactor workers whose unionisation drive was blocked in 1100 include those who service the shipyard's power grid.

---

### B.3.4 — Political Status

New Mondulkiri is the administrative seat of Gashidda's Imperial military government. The military governor's offices are here. So is Director Voss's Naval Intelligence section. So is Harbour Officer Pramanik and the administrative intake the agents process through on arrival.

It is simultaneously the most Imperial and most isolated place on Gashidda. The Gashiddan population lives almost entirely in the undersea arcologies. New Mondulkiri is where the occupiers live — naval personnel, LSP corporate staff, Imperial administrators, and the support population that serves them. It has the atmosphere of a garrison town that knows it is not welcome in the surrounding country and has stopped pretending otherwise.

The Vegan trade attaché Dzhuur Ve-Ashtuk's office is here, in the starport's diplomatic quarter — a small cluster of non-Imperial consular offices that the occupation maintains as evidence of normal commercial relations. Dzhuur's 22 years on New Mondulkiri represent an unusual institutional memory, and the only non-human perspective on the occupation's long arc.

## B.4 — MAKARA UNIVERSITY `[OC]`

Makara University occupies a cluster of interconnected reef-cap chambers in the mid-tier university district, positioned between the upper-tier corporate and administrative levels and the lower industrial zone. Some chambers are carved directly into the living coral limestone; others extend outward into the water column with reinforced transparent observation panels. The marine xenobiology and oceanography departments have direct observation bays looking into the equatorial reef shelf. At depth the light through those panels is diffuse blue-green, interrupted by the bioluminescent pulse of passing reef organisms.

The architecture dates from the mid-occupation period — built and expanded between 1030 and 1090, when the Imperial military government needed to demonstrate that the occupation was civilising rather than extractive. The buildings have an imposed grandeur: high ceilings, formal corridor proportions, institutional stonework, in uncomfortable contrast to the reef-carved walls and the waterlogged informality of the surrounding mid-tier. The Imperium built a university that looks like a university. The Gashiddan population staffed it and quietly made it something else.

### B.4.1 — Academic Character

Makara University is genuinely excellent at what the ocean forces it to be: marine biology, xenoecology, deep-pressure engineering, and arcology systems design. Its oceanography faculty is among the best in the Solomani Rim for deep-water organism study — Gashidda provides field conditions no other institution can replicate. The Void Mantle, the Reef Consumer, and the Driftweb were all formally described and classified by Makara University researchers, in papers that reached the Imperial scientific press and were quietly stripped of their Solomani institutional affiliation before publication.

The humanities and social science departments are a different matter. History is taught carefully. Political science is taught very carefully. The faculty know which lectures are attended by Naval Intelligence observers and which are not. There is a thriving informal seminar culture in the reef-access dive preparation rooms — conversations that would not survive in a formal lecture theatre.

### B.4.2 — The Occupation Layer

Makara University is nominally administered by an Imperial Education Authority liaison office in the upper tier, which sets curriculum standards, controls degree accreditation, and can defund entire departments. In practice it exercises this power rarely — the technical faculties are too valuable to the Imperial Navy's own deep-water infrastructure — but the threat is permanent and understood by everyone.

Faculty who are known Vanthara Truth members hold their positions on the tolerance of whoever currently commands the Naval Intelligence section. Voss has so far considered the university more useful as an observation point than a purge target. This calculation could change.

### B.4.3 — Dr. Mian-Ashta's Position

Dr. Seluun Mian-Ashta holds the marine xenobiology chair — one of the few positions the Imperial administration cannot easily fill from off-world, because the field knowledge required takes fifteen years of Gashidda-specific fieldwork to acquire. This gives her unusual job security relative to colleagues in softer disciplines. Her research on Reef Consumer carbon-composite integration is funded partly by LSP infrastructure contracts, which gives her access to the lower reef-shelf dive facilities without requiring Naval Intelligence clearance for each excursion.

### B.4.4 — In Play

The university district provides a mid-tier contact point that is socially distinct from the Vanthara Truth chapter house, the cargo dock district, and the upper-tier corporate zone. Meetings here carry a cover rationale — survey crews legitimately consult marine biology faculty on deep-water safety conditions. Dr. Mian-Ashta is the primary connection; she can introduce agents to other faculty if trust is established, and her laboratory provides a location for secure conversation that Voss has not yet flagged as sensitive.
