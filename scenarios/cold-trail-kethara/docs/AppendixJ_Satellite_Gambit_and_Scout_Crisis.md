## J.1 — WHAT THIS APPENDIX COVERS

Chapter 6 already treats it as an established fact that scout ships have crashed near the Imperial relay, and that Breckenridge intends to stay behind and pose as a survivor of one of them (§6.21). This appendix is the missing middle: how the crash happens, who it happens to, and what it costs. Referees who want the extraction to carry this complication should read this appendix before running Act Four; referees who want a cleaner extraction can ignore it entirely — nothing here is load-bearing for the core scenario.

**The short version:** Breckenridge, with Pelczar's help, hacks the Imperial orbital communications satellite over Lakamsal and uses it to manufacture a crisis between two IISS scout vessels. The manufactured crisis works too well. Both ships go down. Breckenridge uses the wreckage as his exit cover.

---

## J.2 — THE INTRUSION

This was never in the mission brief. It gets improvised somewhere around Hour 24–30, when the team is running out of clean options and Breckenridge decides a compromised satellite is worth more to him than a quiet signals posture.

The Lakamsal orbital communications satellite is a standard IISS TL10 civilian-grade relay — Formidable (12+) computer defences under normal conditions. Three things stack against it:

1. **TL advantage.** Breckenridge's SIGINT suite (TL12, Appendix on data storage) outclasses the satellite (TL10) by two tech levels — DM+2 on the intrusion attempt.
2. **Storm cover.** Timing the intrusion to a storm peak, when the satellite's diagnostic systems are busy correcting for atmospheric noise, distracts its security monitoring — DM+1, referee's discretion for creative timing.
3. **Pelczar's task chain.** Her Electronics (comms) 4 shapes the intrusion signal to read as a routine IISS administrative update. Electronics (comms) 10+ (Effect +3) hands Breckenridge an additional DM+2.

**The roll:** Electronics (computers), Formidable (12+). Breckenridge's skill 3 + INT DM+2 + TL advantage DM+2 + storm cover DM+1 + Pelczar's chain DM+2 = total DM+10 — effective target 2+ on 2D. Near-automatic. It still matters, because a natural 2 (snake eyes) trips the satellite's intrusion alarm and exposes the team immediately.

**What Breckenridge broadcasts, once inside:**

- **Message 1** — a falsified internal security alert flagging the crew of one of the two IISS scout vessels currently in-system as carrying confirmed Solomani Confederation sympathies. Classification: URGENT — INVESTIGATE IMMEDIATELY.
- **Message 2**, sent later once the crisis is already moving — a fabricated distress call, purportedly from the relay station itself: *"RELAY STATION UNDER ATTACK — REQUESTING IMMEDIATE ASSISTANCE — ALL AVAILABLE VESSELS RESPOND."*

**Why he does it:** to pull Imperial attention away from BLIND COBRA and onto a problem the Imperials will believe is entirely their own. It works. It works too well.

## J.3 — THE TWO SCOUT SHIPS

Two IISS vessels are in-system servicing Lakamsal when Message 1 goes out: the courier that runs Osei-Bonsu's station's regular logistics circuit, and a survey vessel passing through on routine sector patrol.

### Scout Captain Adeyemi
**IISS Scout Service — Courier *Quiet Meridian***

A cautious, by-the-book officer running Lakamsal's resupply circuit. She receives Message 1 flagging the *other* ship's crew and attempts to verify with Osei-Bonsu directly — but comms are degraded by the storm, and she can't raise him. Rather than open fire in earnest, she puts a **warning shot** across the other vessel's bow at 10km — a laser burst deliberately aimed to miss — then tries to close for a face-to-face resolution.

**Skills:** Pilot (spacecraft) 3, Astrogation 2, Electronics (sensors) 2, Gunner (turret) 2, Recon 1, Tactics (naval) 1

**Equipment:** *Quiet Meridian* (Type S Scout/Courier, standard IISS livery, CRB22 p.189), turret-mounted pulse laser

**Personality:** Careful, procedural, genuinely trying to de-escalate. She fires the warning shot because IISS doctrine says a controlled show of force reduces ambiguity faster than radio silence does. She is wrong, this time, but not unreasonably so.

### Scout Captain Torino
**IISS Scout Service — Survey Vessel *Patient Archive***

Passing through on a routine sector patrol when the same Message 1 reaches him — from his side, it names *Adeyemi's* crew, not his own. Mutual suspicion, not shared intelligence. When Adeyemi's warning shot arrives, Torino reads it as confirmation of hostile intent and breaks into evasive manoeuvres rather than closing to talk.

## J.4 — THE CRASH

Before the two captains can resolve the confusion directly, Breckenridge sends Message 2 — the fabricated relay-station distress call. Both ships break off and attempt to land at the relay station to investigate, flying straight into peak storm conditions: EM interference, 40–60kph variable gusts, and reduced visibility make it a Difficult (10+) Pilot (spacecraft) check at storm DM-2.

- **Adeyemi rolls a natural 2.** *Quiet Meridian* loses attitude control in a wind shear at 200m and strikes the delta floodplain 3km short of the relay station. Damaged but largely intact — crumpled landing struts, a hull breach in the cargo bay, drives offline. Adeyemi and crew survive with injuries.
- **Torino rolls a natural 2.** *Patient Archive* overshoots the landing pad on a rain-slick approach and cartwheels into the jungle 1.5km past the station. Severely damaged — hull broken in two sections, drives destroyed. Torino survives. Two of his crew do not.

Both ships' automated emergency beacons begin broadcasting continuously on Imperial frequencies before and after impact. The 291st Fleet duty officer at Banasdan will detect them within 24 hours and dispatch a response — this is a second, independent trigger toward Imperial military attention, running in parallel to the threat layers already tracked in Appendix F.

**The relay station's six civilian operators** — who genuinely believe they run a geological survey, per their own cover — are now thoroughly panicked: two scout ships down near their installation, one apparently fired on the other, and a distress call claiming they were under attack. They lock the facility down and stop communicating with anyone.

**SHEPHERD gets through anyway.** Mrazová descends in the same storm window, but she is a 3-term Scout veteran (Pilot (spacecraft) 3, Chapter 6 §6.23) who times her approach to a brief clearing between storm cells. She lands at KR-7 without incident. The team extracts — minus Breckenridge (§J.5).

**Referee timing note:** this whole sequence — intrusion, both messages, both crashes — plays out over roughly 20–40 minutes of in-fiction time, almost entirely off-screen from the players' perspective unless Breckenridge is a PC. It is heard about, not witnessed: relay chatter, the beacon signals Pelczar picks up on the SIGINT suite, Osei-Bonsu's reaction if he is monitoring traffic. Treat it as a piece of news that arrives rather than a scene the whole table plays through.

## J.5 — BRECKENRIDGE STAYS BEHIND

*This expands the stay-behind sequence already outlined in Chapter 6, §6.21 — read that section first; this fills in the specific cover he uses.*

At the moment of extraction, Breckenridge steps back from the ramp. He is staying on Lakamsal, and he did not clear it with Vasiliou first.

**His cover identity:** a survivor of *Patient Archive* — Torino's ship, which broke apart on impact with two dead among the crew. In the confusion of the crash, the storm, and the relay station's lockdown, nobody has done a definitive headcount of survivors. Breckenridge's Imperial-born identity, fluent Imperial Standard, and genuine familiarity with Imperial systems make the cover plausible. His cover-identity kit carries Scout Service documentation he can modify to fit (Deception 2; Forgery via Electronics (computers) 3).

**Alternative cover, if that one is too risky:** BLIND COBRA's own geological-survey identity, worn as a solo survivor rather than a team — he "found" the team's crashed survey drone and has been sheltering in the area since.

**His mission, self-assigned:** with two scout ships down, a compromised satellite, a panicked relay station, and Imperial response vessels inbound, Lakamsal is about to fill up with Imperial investigators. Breckenridge intends to be inside that investigation, feeding intelligence back to SolSec through dead drops and pre-arranged covert protocols.

**Vasiliou's reaction:** fury, mostly at not being consulted. She cannot order him onto SHEPHERD — the mission is technically complete, and he has, technically, the right to make this call. She tells him this is suicide. She may be right. She is last on the ramp, watching him not board.

**Pelczar's reaction:** she says nothing. She hands him a data wafer — a copy of Partition 2, the file on the burned Imperial asset — without explanation. He takes it without looking at it. Neither of them discusses what it is.

**Playing this at the table:** it is a quiet scene, not a confrontation. If a player is running Breckenridge, this is *their* choice to make in the moment, not a scripted outcome — his intention can be planted in Chapter 2 as character background, but nothing forces it. If the player wants him on the ship, he is on the ship, and this entire appendix becomes something that happened to two NPCs the team never meets.
