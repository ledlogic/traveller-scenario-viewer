## L.1 — BESTIARY FORMAT NOTE

All animal stat blocks follow the MGT2E Core Rulebook (CRB22) animal profile format (pp.154–157). Stats use the standard animal characteristics: Hits, Speed, Armour, Attack (damage type), and Skills. Traits follow CRB22 p.156 conventions.

**Size classification** follows CRB22 p.154: animals are rated Small, Normal, Large, or Huge. Attacks from larger animals deal more damage and have greater knock-back potential. All Lakamsal megafauna described here are evolved for Atmosphere Code 4 (Thin) and suffer no penalties from it.

**Source note:** Core animal encounter tables, trait definitions, and damage types follow CRB22 pp.154–160. The Kethara basin fauna have no published canon entries — these stat blocks are original scenario content consistent with CRB22 format conventions.

Carnivore / Killer per CRB22 p.155 table. The Kethara Tyrant is territorial rather than actively predatory toward creatures it cannot identify as prey — it investigates novel stimuli (heat signatures, sound, smell) before committing to an attack. **Encounter States:**

| State | Trigger | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Investigating | Novel heat/sound at 200m+ | Approaches slowly, 4m/round, not attacking |
| Territorial | Anything inside 30m without prior deterrence | Charges — 14m/round rush, Bite on arrival |
| Feeding | Live prey available | Full attack sequence, prioritises largest target |
| Deterred | Successful deterrence check | Retreats; returns in 1D+2 hours |
| Agitated (post-gunfire) | Heard/felt weapons discharge nearby | Charges immediately, no investigation phase |

**Defeat Thresholds:**
- 30 hits remaining: speed reduced to 8m/round (slowed)
- 15 hits remaining: will attempt to disengage if able
- 0 hits: incapacitated; death in 1D rounds without medical intervention (no scenario reason for this)

## C.1 — THE KETHARA SHIELD BEETLE: REVISED PROFILE

The Shield Beetle is the smaller cousin of the Titanic Hercules Beetle — a herbivorous reducer species, 2 metres long and approximately 400 kilograms, feeding on rotting wood, fungal growth, and decomposing organic material. It is not a predator. It is not aggressive toward humans under normal circumstances.

Under abnormal circumstances — specifically, the circumstances created by a warm, damp cave that has housed five humans and their food waste for 23 days — it is a 400-kilogram armoured animal in an enclosed space that will not leave voluntarily and cannot be reasoned with.

**Full stat block:**

| Animal | Hits | Speed | Armour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kethara Shield Beetle | 40 | 8m/round | +10 |

**Skills:** Athletics (strength) 3, Survival 2, Athletics (dexterity) 1

**Attacks:**
- Mandible Snap: 4D (when cornered or defending itself from perceived threat)
- Trample: 2D (unintentional — the beetle walking through equipment)
- Carapace Shove: STR contest — beetle STR 14 vs target STR, success means the target is moved 2m and knocked prone. This is not an attack — it is the beetle moving through a space that a human happens to be occupying.

**Traits:** Armour (+10), Heightened Senses (chemical — smells organic material at 500m range in the right wind conditions), Camouflaged (forest — dark chitin against basalt reads as shadow at a glance; Recon 8+ to spot a stationary beetle in low light)

**Size and access:** 2m long, 1m wide, 0.8m high. Fits through the 3m × 2.5m entrance throat — barely, with 10cm clearance on each side. Fits through the motor pool / ops centre open floor plan. Cannot fit through standard interior doorways (0.9m width — the beetle is 1m wide). If a doorway is open and the beetle wants to go through it, it will try. The doorframe does not survive this attempt intact.

**Behaviour:** Reducer/Herbivore. The beetle is attracted to: warmth, moisture, rotting organic material, fungal growth, and enclosed sheltered spaces. The cave interior of BLIND SERPENT, after 23 days, provides all of these. The waste storage sealed containers smell, to a Shield Beetle, like a very large and promising food source. The beetle is not coming in to hurt anyone. It is coming in because the cave smells like dinner.

**Numbers:** Shield Beetles are semi-social — they do not form herds but they do aggregate around food sources. The initial group that investigates the base should be **2D3** (2–6 individuals). Each subsequent hour the base remains occupied and unsecured, roll 1D: on a 5–6, one additional beetle finds the entrance.

## C.2 — THE SUPPLY GATE: WHY THE BEETLES MATTER FOR COMBAT

The crash site (4km northeast) contains the only laser rifle available to the team: a **TL10 laser rifle (5D, Zero-G)** stored in the survey drone's geological sample extraction kit. Without it, the team's options against Shield Beetles are significantly constrained.

**Weapon effectiveness against Shield Beetle (Armour +10):**

| Weapon | Damage | vs Armour 10 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gauss Rifle (3D, AP5) | 3D vs eff. Armour 5 | Penetrates — average 5.5 net | Works. Messy inside the cave. Ricochets. Equipment damage risk (see C.6). |
| Laser Pistol (3D) | 3D vs eff. Armour 7 (energy -3) | Penetrates — average 3.5 net | Slow. Each shot does limited damage. Port risk near vents (see Appendix F, F.8). |
| Autopistol (3D-3) | 3D-3 vs Armour 10 | Does not penetrate | Ineffective. |
| Advanced Combat Rifle (3D, Auto 3) | 3D vs Armour 10 on Auto | Does not reliably penetrate on single shots; Auto 3 bursts average one penetrating hit | Marginal. Loud. |
| Blade (1D+2) | 1D+2 vs Armour 10 | Does not penetrate | Ineffective. Do not try this. |
| **Laser Rifle (5D) — crash site** | **5D vs eff. Armour 7 (energy -3)** | **Penetrates well — average 10.5 net** | **The correct tool. Requires crash site retrieval.** |
| Chemical deterrent (Kowalski) | No damage | N/A | Harder against beetles than megafauna — Science (biology) 10+ (vs 8+ for Tyrant). On failure (Effect -2 or worse), the pheromone mix agitates rather than deters. |
| Demolition charge (4D, 10m blast) — crash site | 4D vs Armour 10 in blast radius | Penetrates; kills multiple beetles | Also destroys the TSU-9. Last resort only. |

**The design intent:** The Shield Beetle encounter is deliberately harder without crash site supplies. This gates the encounter so that retrieving the coolant canister — already the mission-critical TSU-9 repair item — also unlocks the correct tool for the beetle problem. A team that has already run the crash site has the laser rifle and the approach is manageable. A team that has not run the crash site yet faces the beetles with marginal weapons and must make harder choices.

**This is not punishment.** The beetles are not lethal to a prepared team even without the laser rifle — gauss rifles work, Kowalski's deterrent works at the harder difficulty, the base-lockdown route (seal the entrance and retrieve later) works. But the crash site run becomes urgently desirable on two simultaneous axes: the TSU-9 needs the fluid, and the beetles need the rifle. This is the moment Vasiliou sends Haramoto and Mouton without further debate.

## C.3 — THE VENTS ENCOUNTER: HOW IT HAPPENS

Shield Beetles investigating the base do not simply walk in through the front door. The first beetles to arrive are drawn by smell and warmth — they follow the scent gradient up the east cliff face, toward the TSU-9 port array.

The ports are 22cm diameter holes in the cliff face, currently venting warm air (air-cooled mode) or emitting residual heat (if partially liquid-cooled). To a Shield Beetle, these holes smell like the interior of a warm, rotten-wood-filled space. They are exactly the right diameter for a beetle to investigate by pressing its sensory appendages into the opening. They are not quite large enough for the beetle to enter — but the beetle will try.

### The Setup

When Haramoto (and whoever is with her) is on the east cliff face servicing the TSU-9 ports, the following sequence unfolds:

**The cleaning itself is part of the trigger.** A freshly reamed port — see Appendix F, Section F.5f — vents more heat and releases trapped organic volatiles for roughly 90 minutes after servicing. This is in addition to, not instead of, the general possibility of a beetle wandering up to investigate an unserviced port. Any port-cleaning action should be checked against the attraction table in F.5f before falling back on the standard hourly roll below.

**First sign:** Haramoto notices one of the Zone A ports (8–12m AGL) has been partially occluded — the airflow sensor reads reduced. She investigates. The port cap has been pushed partially inward by something pressing against it from outside. There is a smear of dark, waxy organic residue around the port rim — beetle secretion.

**Recon check:** Recon 8+ to spot the beetle before it spots them (or rather, before it registers their presence — beetles sense chemically, not visually). Success: they see the beetle before it reacts to them. Failure: the beetle is already at arm's length when they notice it.

**The encounter:** 1D3 Shield Beetles on the cliff face, distributed across Zones A and B. They are not attacking. They are investigating the port holes — pressing their sensory appendages against the openings, moving slowly along the cliff face, showing no territorial aggression. They are entirely calm. They are also 400 kilograms each, on a 20-metre cliff face, with a human in a grav belt three metres away.

### The Problem

The beetles are not a threat unless provoked or frightened. But:

1. **They are blocking ports.** A beetle pressing against a port reduces that port's airflow by approximately 60% while it is investigating. If two or three beetles are simultaneously investigating ports, the TSU-9 load increases by 3–5% per beetle per port. This is not immediately critical — but it is a load spike while Haramoto is trying to service the system.

2. **They are curious about the humans.** A beetle at close range will extend its chemosensory appendages toward a human — tasting the air. This is not aggression. It feels like aggression. The chemical sensor appendages are 30cm long and move independently. They will brush against a human within arm's reach if the human does not move away. Haramoto is in a grav belt on a cliff face. Moving away means moving into open air.

3. **Firing a laser near the ports.** See Appendix F, Section F.8. A laser pistol fired at a beetle on the cliff face has a 1-in-2 chance of hitting a port. A laser rifle (not yet retrieved) fired at a beetle 3 metres from a port cluster has a 1-in-3 chance. The beetle's Armour 10 means that a panicked shot with a laser pistol does limited damage to the beetle and potentially destroys a port permanently.

4. **Gauss rifles from below.** If Mouton is covering from ground level (standard security posture), he has an upward firing angle at beetles on the cliff face — this is a worse angle for accidental port hits (1-in-1 at close beetle-to-port proximity from below). Vasiliou should have told Mouton not to fire upward unless Haramoto is directly threatened. Whether Mouton received and retained that instruction is a player decision.

## C.4 — HANDLING THE VENTS ENCOUNTER

### Option 1 — Wait Them Out
**No skill check.** The beetles will investigate the ports for 1D6 × 10 minutes, find nothing edible, and move on. During this time: Haramoto cannot service any ports the beetles are occupying (3–5 ports temporarily blocked per beetle). TSU-9 load ticks up slightly. No damage to ports. No injuries. Haramoto spends 10–60 minutes stationary on the cliff face, which is its own exposure problem during an Imperial sweep.

*Referee note:* This is the correct answer and many players will not choose it. The beetles are visually alarming. A player whose character is hanging from a grav belt on a cliff face with a 400kg insect pressing its face-parts against them three metres away is going to want to do something. The referee's job is to make "nothing" feel like an active choice rather than a passive one.

**Vasiliou, over comms (after the encounter is reported):**
> "Haramoto. Is it eating the ports?"
> *[No.]*
> "Is it eating you?"
> *[No.]*
> "Then wait."

### Option 2 — Kowalski's Chemical Deterrent
**Science (biology) or Science (chemistry) 10+** (harder than the Tyrant deterrent — the beetle's chemical communication system is more primitive and less predictable than the Tyrant's territorial scent response).

**On success (Effect 0+):** The beetle registers the deterrent pheromone as a threat signal and retreats from the port area. It descends the cliff face and moves away from the installation. The port is clear in 2 minutes. The deterrent effect lasts 2 hours for the treated zone — other beetles approaching from below will avoid it.

**On good success (Effect 3+):** All beetles in the immediate area register the deterrent simultaneously and leave together. Clean resolution. Haramoto can resume servicing immediately.

**On failure (Effect -1):** The pheromone mix is neutral — the beetle ignores it and continues investigating. No harm done; the attempt was simply ineffective. Kowalski can try again with a second dose.

**On bad failure (Effect -2 or worse):** The wrong pheromone — a territorial aggression trigger rather than a retreat signal. The beetle's behaviour shifts from curious to defensive. It does not charge immediately (it is not a predator), but it does press itself against the port and extrude its mandibles — a defensive posture. The next human that enters its 2m radius triggers a Mandible Snap (4D). Haramoto is currently in a grav belt 3 metres away. This is bad timing.

**Kowalski's delivery problem:** She is not on the cliff face — she is inside the base or at ground level. To deploy the deterrent effectively she needs to either: reach Haramoto's altitude (no grav belt — she would need the rope system, 10 minutes to rig), have Haramoto deploy it (pass the dose up; Haramoto applies it per Kowalski's radio instructions — DM-1 without direct observation), or spray it from below (range penalty — effective as if she rolled 2 lower on the skill check).

### Option 3 — Drive Them Down (Non-Lethal)
**Athletics (dexterity) 8+ or Recon 8+.** Haramoto or whoever is on the cliff face makes themselves large, loud, and threatening — movement, voice, banging on the cliff face. Noise deterrence is generally ineffective against beetles (slow metabolism, poor acoustic response), but sudden large physical movement at close range can trigger a retreat reflex.

**On success:** One beetle retreats per Effect point spent (minimum 1). Success with Effect 3+ clears all beetles from the immediate port area. They retreat to the ground and circle the base perimeter — still present, but no longer on the cliff face.

**On failure:** No effect. The beetle continues investigating. The human has now made noise on an exposed cliff face during an Imperial sweep window.

**On failure (Effect -2 or worse):** The beetle interprets the movement as territorial competition — Carapace Shove. Haramoto must make DEX 8+ or be knocked from her position on the cliff face. If the grav belt is active, she recovers in 2 rounds but is now 3 metres further from the port she was servicing. If the grav belt has a power interruption (it has been in use for 4+ hours — at Hour 8+ of port servicing, the belt is at 75% power), she falls. Mouton catches her if he is in position and makes STR 10+ (her 50kg gear + her weight = 70kg total).

### Option 4 — The Base-Lockdown Route (No Crash Site Supplies Yet)
If the beetles arrive at the base entrance (not just the cliff face) before the crash site run has been completed, the team faces them inside the motor pool without the laser rifle. The base-lockdown sequence:

**Step 1 — Seal the entrance (group STR check):** Stack equipment crates across the entrance throat. Group STR total 30+ seals it (3 people at STR 8–10 can manage this in 4 minutes). The beetles can push against the crates — each round roll STR 14 vs the crates' total blocking value (effective STR 20 for a well-stacked barrier). The crates hold for 2D6 rounds before the beetles push through.

**Step 2 — Extract the crash site supplies while the base is sealed:** Someone goes out the back (west side, through the geological survey area — less visible, no perimeter wire). They take a truck (if fuelled) or go on foot (1 hour each way) to the crash site, retrieve the laser rifle, and return. The beetles are contained inside. The sealing crates will hold approximately 20–60 minutes. If the crash site run takes longer — Stalker encounter, bad rolls, Osei-Bonsu arriving in his air/raft at the same moment — the beetles are through the crates before the rifle arrives.

**Step 3 — Clear the base with the laser rifle:** 5D vs effective Armour 7 (energy weapons). Two well-placed shots per beetle brings it down reliably. Work from the entrance inward, single file, no crossfire. Kowalski on standby for injuries. Haramoto monitors equipment damage throughout (see C.6).

## C.5 — THE CLIFF FACE AT NIGHT: COMPOUNDING FACTORS

The vents encounter is most likely to occur **at night or during the storm peak** — the beetles are more active in warm, humid conditions, and their approach from below the canopy line is invisible to perimeter sensors not specifically calibrated for small-profile ground contacts.

**Night on the cliff face:**
- Haramoto's grav belt has running lights (she has taped them — Stealth consideration)
- Visibility at 15m altitude: Recon (visual) checks at DM-2 in darkness without light source
- Beetle carapace: dark chitin, nearly invisible against basalt at night (Recon 10+ to spot a stationary beetle without thermal overlay)
- Okonkwo's drones: the armed recon drones can provide thermal overlay on the cliff face from above — if Okonkwo is running them on overwatch, the beetles show up as warm blobs on the thermal feed 30 seconds before Haramoto sees them. **This is the best early warning system available and requires no skill check — just Okonkwo paying attention.**

**During the storm:**
- Wind at the cliff face during storm peak: 40–60kph variable gusts. Grav belt stability: Athletics (dexterity) 8+ per 10 minutes or drift 1D metres laterally. Not dangerous on its own — but combined with a beetle investigation and active port servicing, the grav belt instability becomes the third simultaneous problem.
- EM interference: the beetles' chemical sensory system is unaffected by EM. The storm makes them no more or less likely to approach. It does make Kowalski's radio instructions to Haramoto harder to hear (DM-1 on comms checks during peak).

## C.7 — THE HERBIVORE'S PERSPECTIVE

The Shield Beetle is not the scenario's enemy. It is a creature doing what its species has done for millions of years in the Kethara basin: following warmth and organic smell into sheltered spaces to feed. The cave smells like food. The cave is warm. The holes in the cliff face smell like the inside of the cave. This is completely rational beetle behaviour.

From the beetle's perspective, it has climbed 15 metres of basalt cliff face to investigate a promising feeding site, encountered some unusually loud and agitated creatures that do not smell like prey or predator (confused chemical signal — humans are not part of this ecosystem's vocabulary), and is now doing its best to determine whether the warm hole is worth pushing into or whether the agitated creatures are a better reason to leave.

The beetle is not brave. It is not territorial. It is not protecting a nest. It is hungry and warm and investigating. A sufficiently convincing deterrent — chemical, physical, or simply waiting — will send it away without incident.

**What breaks the patience:**
- A loud noise at very close range (less than 1m) — beetles have poor acoustic sensitivity but extreme sound can trigger a startle response: Carapace Shove, not Mandible Snap
- A laser hit that does not kill it — a wounded beetle in a pain response is more dangerous than a calm beetle, and its Armour 10 means laser pistol hits are likely to wound without kill
- Another beetle being killed in its presence — the chemical death signal triggers flight response in nearby beetles, which means they move quickly away from the dead one, which may be toward a human

The most elegant outcomes with the Shield Beetles come from working with its nature rather than against it: let it investigate and leave, guide it away with chemistry, or seal the space and go get the right tool. The firefight inside the base is the least elegant outcome and the one that damages the most things.
