## 2.0 — THE BLIND COBRA CELL: OVERVIEW

BLIND COBRA is a SolSec Field Remote Operations Cell. Five operatives — six with the optional specialist — inserted under civilian cover, operating deniably on Imperial soil. They are not soldiers in the conventional sense. They are people with particular skills that SolSec requires in a particular place at a particular time. Most of them did not choose this work so much as find it choosing them.

**Standard SolSec Field Kit (all characters):**
- Encrypted comms unit (TL10, scrambled — all traffic routed through BLIND COBRA's secure relay node)
- Cover identity documents (Kethara Geological Survey Partners — registered Altiplano, Solomani Rim 3017, Imperial territory)
- Personal medkit (1D healing, one use)
- 1,000 Credits' worth of Ensi chits — Lakamsal's local colonial scrip, issued by the Civil Service Bureaucracy administration and exchangeable for Imperial Credits at a fixed, price-controlled rate. Untraceable outside Lakamsal's own banking system, which is precisely why BLIND COBRA carries it instead of standard Imperial credit.

### Vasiliou's Stimulants — Exhausted Supply

Vasiliou's personal stim supply ran out sometime in the previous 36 hours. She has not announced this. At some point early in the scenario — during the opening brief or shortly after — she will approach whoever is nearest (most likely Mouton, secondarily Haramoto) and ask quietly, not carrying to the room:

> *"Do you have a stim in your kit?"*

She does not explain why. If given one, she pockets it immediately and uses it within 20 minutes.

**The effect:** 35–40 minutes after taking the stim, she becomes noticeably — and for her, uncharacteristically — animated. She processes information faster, issues orders with greater speed and precision, and talks slightly more than usual. The comms minimisation reminders come faster during this window. Okonkwo finds this mildly alarming. Haramoto finds it quietly funny. Mouton finds it reassuring in the way that adrenaline responses are reassuring.

**The crash:** Approximately 90 minutes after the stim, she returns to her Day-23 exhausted baseline. The drop is visible. She does not acknowledge it.

**Kowalski's private note** *(if she observes this):* *"Commander used a stim from someone else's kit. This is the fourth from external sources in 36 hours. Returning to baseline now. Will not raise it. She won't listen and this is not the moment."*

His family maintained Solomani cultural identity through two generations of Imperial administration — not as political resistance, but as quiet cultural practice. The music, the language, the particular way they saw the galaxy. It was Tomas who decided to make it political.

He made contact with SolSec in his late twenties through a family connection — a cousin who had crossed the border years before and never came back. After extensive vetting, given his Imperial birth, he was accepted. He has spent fifteen years being the most useful person in the room on any signals operation, because he understands Imperial systems the way only someone who grew up inside them can.

**The thing he has never told SolSec:** Early in his career, Imperial Naval Intelligence identified him as a Solomani sympathiser and approached him with an ultimatum — work as a double agent or face prosecution. He agreed. He fed them three months of carefully managed intelligence: enough to be credible, nothing that would genuinely damage the Confederation. Then he quietly cut contact. Imperial intelligence filed him as a burned asset and moved on. He has carried this for twelve years.

Pelczar, with her eight years of documented operational anomalies, has a file on a burned Imperial asset with Breckenridge's signals signature running through it. She has not yet connected it to the man sitting three metres away from her. The day she does will be the most dangerous day of Tomas Breckenridge's life — not because she will betray him, but because she will have to decide whether she trusts him anyway.

**What he knows that he hasn't said:** He has been tracking two anomalies in the relay's transmission patterns.

The first — six hours old — is a timing shift in the outbound burst pattern. He has seen this before, from the inside: it is a calibration adjustment from the relay's civilian-spec processors struggling under thermal load. He knows this. He has not told Pelczar, who has been working herself toward a mole theory for six hours. He is not sure how to raise it without explaining how he knows what Imperial relay calibration patterns look like.

The second — six weeks old — is the data surge itself. The cipher identifier patterns tell him these messages are not coming from the 291st Fleet. They are coming from Domain of Sol command level, and some from higher still. He does not have clearance to know what they contain. He has not told anyone what he has deduced. He is increasingly certain that the people routing traffic through this relay are frightened in a way that senior Imperial Naval officers are not supposed to be frightened.

**Personality:** Precise, methodical, quietly intense. Hums classical Solomani folk music while working — he is entirely unaware he does this. Most likely person on the team to notice something is wrong before anyone else. Also most likely to be wrong about what that thing specifically is. Finds Vasiliou's decisiveness occasionally frustrating — not because she is wrong, but because she makes decisions before he has finished thinking. Respects Pelczar as an equal and is faintly afraid of her for reasons he could not easily articulate. The ideological commitment that brought him here is real. Whether it is enough has become a question he tries not to ask.

**Breckenridge's stay-behind plan:** At the moment of extraction, Breckenridge has made a decision he has not shared with Vasiliou. He intends to stay on Lakamsal, presenting himself as a survivor of one of the crashed scout ships. His Imperial-born identity, fluent Imperial Standard (Language 2), and genuine familiarity with Imperial systems make this plausible. His self-assigned mission: deep-cover intelligence on the incoming Imperial investigation. Vasiliou will be furious. Pelczar will hand him a data wafer without explanation. He will take it without looking at it.

**Behavioural prediction:** Once per encounter, Science (biology) 8+ to predict the apex predator's next action — charge, investigate, retreat, redirect. On success, DM+2 on the team's next action. On Effect 5+, she identifies the specific trigger and can advise neutralisation without engagement.

**Medical support:** With Medic 3 and the advanced kit, she handles injuries the team's basic supplies cannot. Against a 5D bite from the Tyrant, she is the difference between a casualty and a fatality.

**The crash site run:** Ideal second person for the retrieval — Survival 2 and Recon 2 in the open delta, Science (biology) to read megafauna behaviour on the approach. Pairs best with Mouton (security) or Haramoto (parts identification).

## 2.10 — HOW TO PLAY VASILIOU

Major Sera Vasiliou is not a player character in the convention scenario — she is the **cell commander NPC**, present in the base throughout the scenario, issuing orders, processing information, and making the decisions that nobody else has the authority or the stomach for.

She is the players' boss. She is also, quietly, the person most responsible for them being alive after 23 days in the Kethara Reach.

**The referee plays Vasiliou.** She should feel like a presence — not a quest dispenser, not an exposition delivery mechanism, but a commanding officer who knows these people, knows what they are capable of, and is not above using that knowledge to get the mission done. She respects competence. She has very limited patience for second-guessing, ego, and inaction. She has no patience at all for people who mistake caution for cowardice or boldness for stupidity.

---

### Physical Appearance — Day 23

Vasiliou at the start of the scenario does not look the way she looked on insertion Day 1. Twenty-three days of compressed operational stress, broken sleep cycles, and the particular slow drain of commanding a team on enemy soil with a degrading equipment set have left marks.

The most visible: **the skin beneath and around her eyes has taken on a permanent dark, ruddy discolouration** — the deep bruised-burgundy that appears not from a single sleepless night but from weeks of accumulated sleep debt and circulatory stress. It is not makeup. It does not look like makeup. It looks like what it is: the face of a person who has been running on stimulants and command responsibility for three weeks in a cave. Her dark eyes are still sharp — the exhaustion has not reached them yet — but the contrast between those alert eyes and the raw discolouration beneath them is striking. It reads, at a glance, like permanent damage.

She is aware of it. She has not looked at a mirror in eleven days and does not intend to. Haramoto noticed on Day 8 and said nothing. Mouton noticed on Day 12 and also said nothing. Kowalski noticed on Day 14 from a medical assessment standpoint and made a private note in her field log: *"Commander — periorbital hyperpigmentation consistent with chronic sleep deprivation. Vascular. Not cosmetic. Not something a week of rest will fully correct. Will not raise it unless mission status requires."*

Vasiliou would not thank her for raising it.

**For the image prompt revision:** *"...sharp dark eyes set in a face showing twenty-three days of operational exhaustion — the skin beneath and around her eyes carrying a permanent deep ruddy-burgundy discolouration, not makeup but the vascular mark of weeks of broken sleep and command stress. The discolouration is prominent, almost bruise-like, a stark contrast to her otherwise controlled presentation. It is the only thing about her appearance that she has not managed."*

---

### Voice

**Her voice is:**
- Economical. She does not say more than necessary.
- Specific. She names the person, the task, and the reason in that order.
- Present. She is not issuing orders from a safe room. She is in the same cave, breathing the same thin air, watching the same sensor screens.
- Never theatrical. She does not speechify. The mission is urgent; long speeches are a luxury.

**Her tells when things are bad:**
- She stops using contractions.
- She uses full names instead of callsigns or nicknames.
- She pauses before speaking — one breath, no more — when she is working out something she does not want to get wrong.

**Her tells when things are going well:**
- Almost nothing. She nods. Once.

---

### The Comms Minimisation Contradiction

Vasiliou has, at various points across the 23-day operation, told every member of the team some version of the same instruction:

> *"Keep comms to a minimum. Every transmission is a signal. Every signal is a risk."*

She has said it when the storm cycles began. She said it when Marchetti-Solis's sweep drones went active. She said it during the node desynchronisation crisis. She will say it again during the scenario.

**She also breaks this rule. Repeatedly. Without noticing.**

This is an exhaustion tell — one of the few cracks in her operational composure that she has not caught and corrected, because catching it would require the kind of self-observation that twenty-three days of command stress has eroded. She will issue a comms minimisation reminder and then, twenty minutes later, send a detailed status query to Okonkwo on the same encrypted channel she just told him to use sparingly. She will instruct Breckenridge to hold non-essential comms and then break into his frequency to ask about Pelczar's progress. She rationalises each break silently — *this one is essential, this one is time-critical, this one doesn't count* — but she does not track how often she is making that rationalisation.

**The team has noticed.** Nobody has said anything. Mouton has noticed the most and is the least likely to mention it. Haramoto has noticed and finds it privately reassuring — it is the one way Vasiliou reads as human after three weeks. Okonkwo has noticed and has quietly stopped logging her channel pings as separate transmissions in his op record, which he considers a professional courtesy.

**At the table:** If a player character gently points out the contradiction — ideally at a quiet moment, not in the middle of a crisis — Vasiliou will pause. One full beat longer than her usual pause. Then:

> *[She does not answer immediately.]*
>
> *[One breath. Two.]*
>
> "...You're right."
>
> *[She returns to the sensor screen. She does not explain. She does not apologise. She does not do it again — for approximately forty minutes.]*

This is not played for laughs. It is a small, human moment in a character who does not offer them often. The referee should pitch it as the only acknowledgement she has that something is slipping, and move on before it becomes a scene.

**Scripted instances of the contradiction (for referee use at natural scenario beats):**

*After issuing the comms minimisation reminder at Hour 0:*
> *(Hour 0 + 20 min, unprompted, over encrypted channel to Okonkwo)*: "Okonkwo. Priya's telemetry — what's her battery state?"

*After repeating it at Hour 4 when sweep drones go active:*
> *(Hour 4 + 35 min, to Breckenridge)*: "Breckenridge. Where are we on the satellite timing pattern?"

*After saying it a third time during the beetle incursion:*
> *(Hour 12 + 15 min, to Haramoto)*: "Haramoto. Port count — how many did we get to before they came in?"

Each of these is a legitimate operational query. None of them required a comms transmission. She could have walked ten metres and asked in person. She did not.

## 2.15 — WHAT VASILIOU DOES NOT SAY

As important as her lines is what Vasiliou will not say, regardless of circumstances. These are doors the referee should not open with her voice.

**She will not:**
- Apologise for the mission parameters, the weather, the equipment, the TSU-9, or any other aspect of the situation she inherited
- Praise effort — only results and decisions. "You worked hard" is not a category she operates in.
- Express fear, even when she is afraid — her fear manifests as increased precision, not increased volume
- Give a speech about the Solomani Cause, SolSec's mission, or the political stakes — she is not a recruiter, and the team didn't come here to be persuaded
- Use first names in orders — she uses callsigns or ranks in operational mode, first names only in private, one-on-one moments that the scenario rarely creates
- Tell anyone she is glad they are alive — she shows this through action (she selected them, she protects them, she is last on the ship every time), never through words
- Acknowledge the comms contradiction — except once, quietly, if a player character raises it at the right moment (see 2.10)

**One exception to the extraction question.** If asked directly — by a player, to her face, in a quiet moment — whether she thinks they will get out:

> *[Long pause. She looks at them — full name, rank.]*

*It is not a yes. It is also not a no. It is the most honest thing she will say.*

## VASILIOU'S STIMULANTS: EXHAUSTED SUPPLY

At scenario start, Major Vasiliou has consumed all three of her personal stimulant doses. This is not announced. It is visible to anyone paying attention — and Kowalski has been paying attention since Day 14 from a medical assessment standpoint, noting it in her field log without raising it.

**At some point in the first hour of the scenario** — during the opening brief or shortly after, when the weight of the situation becomes evident — Vasiliou will quietly approach whoever is nearest (most likely Mouton, secondarily Haramoto) and ask, in a low voice that does not carry to the rest of the room:

> "Do you have a stim in your kit?"

She does not explain why she needs it. She does not explain that she is out. If given one, she pockets it immediately. She will use it within the next 20 minutes, privately, in the ops centre while reviewing sensor data.

**The effect:** Approximately 35–40 minutes after taking the stim, she is noticeably — and for her, uncharacteristically — animated. She processes information faster, cross-references sensor data more aggressively, and issues orders with a precision and speed that exceeds her already high baseline. She also talks slightly more than usual. The comms minimisation reminders come faster and with less gap between them during this window. Okonkwo will find this mildly alarming. Haramoto will find it quietly funny. Mouton will find it reassuring in the way that adrenaline responses are reassuring — something is working.

**The crash:** Approximately 90 minutes after the stim, she returns to baseline — which is her Day-23 exhausted baseline, not her normal operational baseline. The drop is visible. She does not acknowledge it.

**Kowalski's private assessment (if she observes this):** *"Commander used a stim from someone else's kit. Returning to baseline now. Recommend rest at first available window. Will not raise it. She won't listen and this is not the moment."*

**If Kowalski is the one asked for the stim:** She gives it without comment. She does note, looking at Vasiliou's face — the deep ruddy-burgundy periorbital discolouration, the controlled exhaustion — that this is the fourth stim in the past 36 hours from various sources she has tracked. She says nothing. There is nothing productive to say.

**Referee note:** This is a small human moment, not a mechanical event. No skill check required, no gameplay consequence in the immediate term. Its purpose is to show the team — and the players — that Vasiliou is running on will and discipline and not much else by Day 23. She asked for help. That is the most unusual thing she has done in three weeks.
