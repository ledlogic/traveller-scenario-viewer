### Tile-by-Tile Description

**TILE 1 — Operations Centre (Interior)**
The deepest interior space. Matte-black equipment panels on basalt walls. Four drone control stations with haptic rigs facing a central holographic display (represented by a transparent hexagonal tile overlay). Pelczar's console is the largest station — central, most screens. TSU-9 Heat Exchanger Core on the rear (west) wall — a grey rectangular unit the size of a large field generator, amber warning lights glowing on its panel. Cabling runs along the ceiling. Cave walls are unfinished basalt — damp in places. Blue and amber ambient light. Packed-dirt floor, smooth-worn by 23 days of foot traffic.

*Miniature terrain notes:* Dense terrain — multiple line-of-sight breaks from equipment stacks. The drone control rigs are waist-high obstructions (+1 cover). Pelczar's console is a solid obstruction (full cover if prone behind it). No windows. One exit: the corridor to the motor pool.

**TILE 2 — Motor Pool / Maintenance Bay (Interior)**
Larger floor space than the ops centre — the motor pool needs to fit two trucks and four drones. Two rental trucks (TL9, civilian utility) are parked against the north wall, fuel cells dry. The four BLIND COBRA drones are racked on the south wall — Priya and Garner in their charging cradles (armed, quad-rotor), the surveillance drone folded on a wall mount, the ground scout (wheeled) parked to the right of the trucks. One auto-sentry unit is positioned near the entrance corridor — a tripod-mounted gauss weapon on a motorised turret. Parts locker on the east wall (open, mostly empty). A central workbench with Haramoto's tools laid out. Floor: bare basalt with oil stains, tool marks, decades of geological survey residue overlaid by 23 days of SolSec use.

*Miniature terrain notes:* The trucks provide solid cover (treat as vehicles, 20 Hits each). The drone racks are chest-height obstacles. The auto-sentry's arc of fire covers the entrance corridor mouth — characters entering from the east take auto-sentry fire if it is in ACTIVE mode.

**TILE 3 — East Cliff Face (Exterior, Zones A + B)**
The most complex tile. The cliff face is represented in a split-level format: ground level at the bottom of the tile, cliff face rising to the top. The river bank at the base is a narrow strip — 3–5m of gravel before the water. The cliff face rises 40m (off the top of the tile, represented by height markers). TSU-9 port positions are marked as small circular indicators on the cliff face at Zone A heights (8–12m) and Zone B heights (13–18m). Camo netting overlaps the cliff face at the junction with the canopy. The cliff ledge access points where a grav belt user can work are marked with ×symbols. Alert: the perimeter wire runs across the base of this tile from south to north.

*Miniature terrain notes:* Characters on the cliff face require a grav belt or climbing kit — falling damage applies (3D per 10m). The cliff face provides full cover against ground-level attacks from the river bank. Zone C ports (13 additional, 19–24m AGL) are off-tile-top. A counter or token on the altitude track represents height position.

**TILE 4 — Crew Quarters / Medical (Interior)**
The westernmost and quietest interior space. Five bunk units along the walls (one clearly unused by the occupant — Pelczar's bunk is still made, tight, untouched). A small medical station on the north wall: the basic medkit, now depleted, its packaging containers open and mostly empty. The empty stimulant storage tray is visible to anyone who knows what to look for. Emergency vacc suits rack (6 suits, TL10, 2-hour air supply) on the west wall, with a shelf of 6 filter masks stocked directly beside it. A personal effects shelf with small items — Mouton's holster, Haramoto's spare tool loops, Breckenridge's reading material.

*Miniature terrain notes:* Lightest terrain density — this is where the team sleeps, not where it fights. Bunks provide minor cover (+0 — they're thin). Vacc suit rack: a character who dons a vacc suit here takes 1 full round to seal and pressurize. West wall access to the tunnel (2km north through the formation — see below).

**TILE 5 — Cave Entrance Throat + Perimeter (Exterior/Interior transition)**
The critical chokepoint. The entrance throat (3m × 2.5m) is represented at actual scale — tight. Two Marines can enter abreast. One Shield Beetle fits with 10cm clearance on each side. The transition from interior (tile) to exterior is marked by the camo netting — heavy fabric hung from the overhang ceiling that breaks line of sight from outside. Two auto-sentry units are visible from this tile: one covering the north approach arc, one covering the south. Gravel approach from the east. The perimeter wire arc is shown in the lower third of the tile — a thin tripwire-height barrier with sensor stakes at 5m intervals.

*Miniature terrain notes:* The entrance throat is a fatal funnel — attackers entering are silhouetted against exterior light during daylight, in thermal overlay contrast at night. Defenders in the motor pool doorway have full cover and a clear field of fire through the throat. The camo netting can be used as concealment (DM-2 to spot characters behind it from outside at >30m) but not cover (it stops nothing).

**TILE 6 — River Bank + Approach (Exterior)**
The full approach to the cave entrance from the east. The river bank is a narrow gravel strip — the Kethara River presses close to the cliff base at the bend. 40m of vertical cliff above. Jungle canopy visible to the north and south — the open river bend is the only place in the scenario with a clear sky view above. Osei-Bonsu's air/raft approach comes in from the north across this tile. The G-carrier (if deployed) lands on the widest section of the gravel bar — approximately 15m of usable flat space. Auto-sentry coverage arcs are shown as shaded wedges on this tile.

*Miniature terrain notes:* No interior cover available — characters in the open on the gravel bar are fully exposed. The river (edge of tile) counts as difficult terrain (fast-moving, knee to chest depth). The jungle edge to the north and south is 30–40m from the cliff face — a character sprinting for the tree line from the entrance takes fire for 2 rounds before reaching concealment.

## 3.4 — THE WEST SIDE: PUMP PORTS AND THE OLD GEO EXPEDITION

The battle map and dual schematic both treat the west side as an exit note rather than a mapped location, because the team's own attention never went there. This section fills in what a referee needs if play moves past the cliff face.

### Formation Geometry

The basalt formation is approximately **80 metres wide** at the station section — narrow enough that the west side is reachable from the east either through the tunnel (2km north, §3.2 and Chapter 6) or by climbing over the top (60–80m, grav belt or climbing kit required).

```
EAST SIDE (cliff face)          WEST SIDE (stepped/tiered)
     |                               ___
     |                          ___|   |
     |  BASALT FORMATION       |      |___
     |  (~80m wide)           |          |___
     |                       |    plateaus   |___
     |                      |  with vegetation    |___
     |  40m sheer cliff    | 30m stepped descent       |
     |                    |  (3-4 natural terraces)      |
     |___________________|_________________________________|
   RIVER (east)                              JUNGLE (west)
```

Unlike the sheer east cliff, the west side descends in **3–4 natural basalt terraces**, each roughly 8–10m high with flat plateaus between them. The plateaus carry dense vegetation — alien ferns, ground cover, canopy trees rooted in basalt cracks — and megafauna use the lower plateaus for grazing and resting. No camouflage netting, no perimeter wire, no sentries: the team's defensive attention went entirely to the east side, the direction of the relay and any approach along the ridgeline. The west side was assessed as low-threat because the terrain is difficult and nothing was expected to come from that direction.

**That assessment is wrong. The beetles come from the west** (Appendix I, §I.10–I.12).

### The Pump Ports

**Roll 3d6** for the number of pool-outflow pump ports present on the west side — circular covers approximately 40cm in diameter, connected to the TSU-9's secondary cooling loop, discharging waste heat into natural rock pools on the stepped terrain. They were installed during base setup and are painted to match the surrounding rock (Recon 10+ to notice without already knowing they exist). The covers are distributed across the second and third terraces.

### Old Geo Expedition Equipment

On the third terrace, roughly 20m below the ridge top, a cluster of meteorological and seismic monitoring equipment survives from a geological expedition that predates BLIND COBRA by centuries. Weathered and overgrown, most of it is dead — but not all of it.

| Equipment | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Meteorological station (mast, wind gauge, temp/pressure sensors) | Partially functional | Solar-powered, TL7–8 era |
| Seismic sensor array (3 ground-mounted units) | Functional — registers ground vibration | Hardened design, buried in basalt, centuries of local data storage |
| Geological sample storage shed | Collapsed — roof gone, walls standing | Contains century-old survey records on degraded media |
| Survey marker posts (×12) | Standing, faded markings | Solomani-era insignia — see below |

**The seismic sensors matter mechanically.** They register large-animal footfalls at 2km range. If the team discovers them and someone can interface with the ancient data bus (Electronics (computers) 10+ — the protocols are centuries old), they gain an early-warning system for anything approaching from the west. It cannot identify species, but it can tell you something very large is coming.

**Who this expedition was:** the equipment and the tunnel lighting (§3.2) were installed by a geological survey operating on Lakamsal roughly 300 years ago, during a period when the world was under Solomani administration — before the Solomani Rim War transferred it to the Third Imperium. The marker posts carry faded Solomani Confederation insignia, a detail worth noticing rather than explaining: this land was Solomani once, and someone on the team is standing on it now under a very different flag.

**The collapsed shed's records**, if recovered (Investigate 10+, 2 hours; the data is on degraded crystal media requiring a TL11 reader — the SIGINT suite has one), contain detailed maps of the basalt formation, including the tunnel, the lava-tube network, and the geological faults responsible for the local EM interference. This data is better than anything currently in Osei-Bonsu's survey database.

## 3.4 — CLIFF GEOMETRY: THE KETHARA RIDGELINE

Understanding the cliff formation is essential for any encounter involving the east cliff face, the approach of Marchetti-Solis's drones, the Marines' advance from KR-7, and the TSU-9 port servicing sequences.

### The Ridgeline Is Not a Tower

The cliff housing BLIND COBRA is **not an isolated pinnacle**. It is a section of a continuous linear basalt escarpment — the Kethara Ridgeline — running approximately **4 kilometres northeast-southwest** along the eastern bank of the Kethara River. The geology is a horizontal lava flow escarpment: the cliff formed when a lava flow cooled into dense basalt and the softer surrounding rock eroded away, leaving the cliff edge as the surviving margin of the flow. This is the opposite of a volcanic intrusion like Devil's Tower — it is a shelf edge, not a column.

**Why this matters for play:**
The ridgeline connects the station to everything around it via the same terrain feature. Marines approaching from KR-7 (north) descend along this ridgeline. The game trail to the crash site follows this ridgeline northeast. There is no isolated defensive perimeter — the station is a point on a continuous terrain feature that provides approach routes in both directions.

### Dimensions

| Feature | Dimension | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Full ridgeline length | ~4km | Northeast-southwest along river |
| Station face width | ~400–500m | The river bend section where the overhang is deepest |
| TSU-9 node array width | ~200m | Central section of station face — 49 ports across this width |
| Cliff height at station | 40m | River bank to cliff top |
| Additional height above cliff top | 20–40m | Jungle-covered ridge above the cliff edge |
| Ridge depth (face to eastern back) | ~150–200m | Solid basalt before terrain drops away east |
| Approach from east (over ridge) | 60–80m climb | Possible with grav belt or climbing kit |

### Approach Vectors

| Direction | Approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| South (river bank) | Limited — the river eliminates this approach | River presses close to cliff base at the bend |
| West (cliff face from river) | Impossible without grav belt or climbing kit | 40m sheer basalt |
| North (ridgeline from KR-7) | The Marines' route — 40km along ridgeline | Game trail, megafauna territory |
| East (over the ridge top) | 60–80m climb then descent | Haramoto's secondary escape; Marines with rappelling equipment need 2–3 hours |
| Northeast (game trail) | Crash site route | Follows ridgeline, open delta sections, Stalker territory |

### The TSU-9 Port Zones on the Cliff Face

The 49 exchange ports are distributed across the central 200m section of the cliff face in three altitude zones. The distribution is the result of the boring machine's one-pass sequence — it worked systematically from south to north across Zone A, then B, then C before destroying itself at the start of port 50.

| Zone | Ports | Height AGL | Access Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zone A | 17 | 8–12m | Ladder (no grav belt required) — most serviced during original installation |
| Zone B | 19 | 13–18m | Rope system or grav belt — the zone with most coolant degradation |
| Zone C | 13 | 19–24m | Grav belt required — highest wind loading during storms, exposed to aerial observation |

*Full port servicing procedures and port damage rules: see Appendix A.*

> The survey drone came down in the secondary jungle — not the primary megaflora, but the denser, wetter understory where the canopy breaks at the ridgeline margin. ## 3.7 — MODELLING AND TERRAIN GUIDE

*For convention referees who run this scenario with physical miniatures and terrain.*

### Recommended Scale
**28mm (Heroic scale)** — standard for Traveller miniature play. All tile dimensions at 1 square = 1.5m work cleanly at 28mm when tiles are printed at 36″×36″ full scale.

### Minimum Terrain Requirements

| Element | Suggested Representation |
|---|---|
| Cave walls (interior) | Dark grey foamcore or printed card flats — just needs to define the space |
| Equipment consoles (ops centre) | 28mm sci-fi crates/consoles — any dark-finish sci-fi scatter terrain works |
| Drone racks | Small flat rectangles marked with drone silhouettes |
| Rental trucks | 28mm civilian vehicle models or card standees |
| Grav belt characters on cliff face | Vertical card track with altitude positions marked — characters use tokens on the track rather than physical position |
| Auto-sentries | Small turret models or tokens with arc-of-fire indicators |
| Perimeter wire | Fine black thread or printed line on the tile |
| River (Tile 6) | Blue acetate or printed water texture |
| Camo netting | Actual mesh fabric cut to size — tactile element the players will remember |

### What Makes This Table Distinctive at a Convention

The **vertical dimension** — characters on the cliff face at 8–24m altitude while ground-level action occurs below — is the scenario's most memorable physical element. A convention referee who finds a way to represent height (even with a simple card track) will get a reaction from players that a flat map cannot produce.

The **TSU-9 load indicator** — a visible counter or dial tracking percentage from 100% to 160%+ — gives the table a persistent pressure element that flat text cannot replicate. Even a simple paper strip with a sliding marker changes how players relate to the repair problem.

## 3.8 — KETHARA REACH WEATHER SYSTEM

### The Meteorological Shift

For 16 of BLIND COBRA's 23 days on Lakamsal, the Kethara basin experienced dry, hot, stable conditions — exactly what the IISS planetary survey database and the SolSec mission brief predicted. The thin atmosphere (Code 4) produces intense solar heating during the day, rapid radiative cooling at night, and almost no cloud cover. The EM interference existed but was mild — background noise Breckenridge compensated for without comment.

**Seven days ago, something changed.**

A large-scale meteorological system — driven by seasonal thermal differentials between Lakamsal's shallow inland seas to the south and the volcanic highlands to the north — shifted its circulation vector. What had been a dry continental airflow from the northeast became a moist maritime flow from the northwest, pulling water vapour from the coastal delta across the basalt ridgeline and into the Kethara basin.

The result is not gentle rain. On a thin-atmosphere world with intense solar heating and magnetite-rich basalt geology, the arrival of moisture creates a volatile, unstable weather cycle that the survey database describes as "periodic electromagnetic anomalies" and the team has been calling increasingly creative names for seven days.

---

### The Current Storm Cycle

Conditions now alternate on a roughly **48-hour period:**

| Phase | Duration | Sky | Temperature | Humidity | Wind | Visibility | EM Interference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Building | ~18 hours | Cloud thickening NW, overcast by end | Hot → warm | Rising rapidly | Light → moderate gusts | 10km → 3km | Rising — node sync issues, drone DM-1 at range |
| Peak storm | 6–8 hours | Heavy overcast, intermittent rain, electrical discharge | Warm, humid | Very high | Strong gusts 40–60kph, variable | 500m–1km; zero during rain | Maximum — DM-2 uplink, DM+1 ECM masking, TSU-9 nodes disrupted |
| Clearing | ~6 hours | Cloud breaking NE, fog in valley | Cooling rapidly | Dropping | Light, steady NE | Improving; valley fog 2–3 hrs after rain | Dropping — node resync window opens |
| Clear | ~16 hours | Thin-atmosphere blue, no cloud | Hot | Very low | Calm | 20km+ | None — but no EM masking either |

| System | Effect |
|---|---|

| System | Effect |
|---|---|

| System | Effect |

### Extended Play Weather (48-Hour Wait)

The next storm cycle arrives approximately **30 hours** after Hour 12 — around Hour 42 of the extended scenario. This second storm is potentially stronger (the meteorological vector is still establishing; each cycle is more intense as the season shifts).

| Storm Cycle | Building DM | Peak Storm DMs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| First cycle (Hours 0–12) | Standard | DM-2 uplink, DM+1 ECM, DM-1 drones | As per tables above |
| Second cycle (Hour ~42) | DM+1 to all | DM-3 uplink, DM+2 ECM, DM-2 drones | Stronger — season establishing |
| Third cycle (Hour ~90) | DM+2 to all | DM-4 uplink, DM+3 ECM, DM-3 drones | Peak monsoon onset |

**The Hour 72 convergence:** At Hour 72, Lakamsal is either in clear phase (optimal for SHEPHERD descent but maximum exposure to *Resolute Purpose* sensors) or approaching the third storm peak (DM-3 on all transmissions, DM-2 on drone ops, but excellent concealment from Imperial sensors). Roll 1D: on 1–3 the extraction window is clear; on 4–6 it is storm peak. The referee should choose based on pacing needs.

### What the Mission Brief Said vs. What Actually Happened

The SolSec mission brief rated Lakamsal's weather as: *"Stable seasonal patterns. Dry continental climate at mission dates. No significant meteorological hazard. The Kethara basin electromagnetic anomaly is low-severity and has no operational significance."*

This assessment was correct for the first 16 days. The seasonal shift was a known possibility in IISS long-term climate models — it happens every 4–7 local years. Osei-Bonsu's survey database notes: *"Seasonal monsoon onset variable. When established, expect 48-hour storm cycles for 60–90 local days. EM interference in basalt-rich valleys will be significant."*

Nobody at SolSec planning division read Osei-Bonsu's survey notes on monsoon onset. If they had, they would have scheduled the insertion 30 days earlier.

Pelczar has a copy of the mission brief weather assessment in her data core. Below it, she has added a single annotation, timestamped Day 17: *"Wrong."*
