## 6.1 — ACT THREE: HOLD THE STATION
*Day 23, Hours 4–8*

Three simultaneous problems. Cut between them. Never let one resolve cleanly before the next escalates.

---

### Problem One — Imperial Drone Hunt

One sweep drone per hour, eight passes total (Hours 4–12). Each pass: contested Electronics (comms). Two failures total = shuttle dispatched.

Breckenridge is the primary response here. He cannot simultaneously manage ECM and do other Electronics tasks without DM-1 on both. If the team needs him for something else during a pass window, the referee should flag the conflict and let the players decide.

*Full sweep drone mechanics, Marchetti-Solis's signal fingerprint search, and countermeasure options: Appendix E, Section E.3.*

---

### Problem Three — The Apex Predator

At Hour 5, the thermal shimmer from the three hot nodes on the cliff face attracts the Kethara Tyrant. It approaches the cliff base from the river side — a 15-tonne bipedal predator with a territorial claim on this river bend. It has been returning to this cliff face for three weeks. It has an IISS biotelemetry tag (KR-ALPHA-7 — Kowalski detects it automatically if scanning, Science (biology) 6+ if passive).

| Approach | Mechanic | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Direct fire | Gun Combat 8+ | Thermal spike +25% TSU-9; automatic ECM failure |
| Drone harassment | Flyer (rotor) 10+ | Success: gone 4 hours. Failure: drone damaged, predator agitated |
| Chemical deterrent (Kowalski) | Science (biology) 8+ | Success: 2-hour avoidance zone. Bad fail: aggressive response |
| Lure with carcasses | No roll | Leaves immediately — requires prepared bait |
| Flood cliff approach via drainage sluice | Engineer 8+ | Clean — uses emergency water reserve |
| Wait it out | 2D hours | DM-1 all ops; no external personnel; no sentries during wait |

**The three-problem convergence:** Okonkwo managing drones vs Imperial sweep AND the Tyrant vs the cliff face AND Haramoto servicing ports. Nobody can do two things at once without DM-1. The referee should name the conflict when it occurs and let the players choose who does what.

**If Kowalski is not in the game:** The team loses the tag detection, the Science (biology) option for deterrent, and the behavioural prediction. They are managing the Tyrant with gauss rifles and hoping for the best.

**If the Tyrant tags Haramoto on the cliff face:** She is in a grav belt 20 metres up. A bite or tail sweep that connects on a failed Athletics check could knock her off the cliff — 40m fall, 4D damage, filter mask and grav belt status determine survival. Mouton on the ground below can do nothing except watch and then act.

*Full Tyrant stats and encounter options: Appendix I, Bestiary.*

## 6.2 — ACT FOUR: EXTRACTION (THE BEGINNING)
*Day 23, Hour 8+*

### The Data Package — Pelczar's Decision

At Hour 8 — or whenever the surveillance run completes — Pelczar addresses Vasiliou directly. The referee should play this beat slowly. It is the scenario's pivot.

> *"The surveillance data is clean and ready to transmit. Ninety seconds, done. Standard package."*
>
> *"But I need you to know what else I have. Six days of partial decryption on whatever the Imperial court is routing through that relay. I am three passes from understanding what it is. Those three passes take four hours — cleaner conditions, better signal. If I complete them and append the result, the transmission is eleven minutes long. Eleven minutes gives their patrol cruiser time to find us."*
>
> *"I think what I am finding is important enough to wait for. I think the four hours is going to cost us something. I cannot tell you what it will cost until we are paying it."*

**The three choices:**

| Choice | Action | Time | Detection Risk | Intelligence Value | TSU-9 Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transmit now — standard only | 90-second burst, storm peak | Hour 8 | Low | Standard fleet surveillance data | TSU-9 still failing |
| Wait for clearing, standard only | 90-second burst, clearing | Hour 10–11 | Low — but TSU-9 may have failed | Standard fleet surveillance data | TSU-9 may have failed |
| Wait, complete decryption, transmit all | 11-minute burst, clearing or clear | Hour 12–14 | High — near certain detection | Strategic intelligence on Imperial court | Extraction window at risk |

**There is no clean answer.** The scenario is designed so that the professionally correct choice (complete the decryption, send everything) is also the choice that maximises detection risk, costs the most time, and puts the team in the most danger. This is a roleplay moment, not a skill check. The referee should let the players feel the weight of this before asking what Vasiliou decides.

**Transmission detection mechanics:**

| Duration | Sensor Roll (*Resolute Purpose*) | Detection on Success |
|---|---|---|
| 90 seconds | 10+ (Difficult) | Logged as possible anomaly — low priority |
| 4 minutes | 8+ (Average) | Logged as definite anomaly — forwarded to Marchetti-Solis |
| 11 minutes | 6+ (Easy) | Automatically logged — Marchetti-Solis alerted immediately |

---

### On Success

> *"Package is clean. Transmitted. Confirmed receipt."*

A pause. Pelczar's hand moves away from the data core on her chest. She looks, briefly, like someone who has set down something very heavy.

> *"The strike team will have everything they need."*

She returns to her console. She does not celebrate. She begins encrypting the backup copy.

---

### The Delay

Forty minutes after successful transmission — whether the team is still managing threats or has begun to breathe — the comms unit chimes.

> *"BLIND COBRA, this is SHEPHERD. Extract is delayed 48 hours — patrol schedule conflict. Confirm you can hold. Over."*

The apex predator has found a reliable heat source. The Imperial station has logged an anomalous contact and dispatched a long-range sensor buoy arriving in 36 hours. The mission continues.

**Vasiliou's response to SHEPHERD:**
> *"Confirmed. Holding."*

She does not transmit anything else. She looks at the team once around the room, measuring.

> *"Forty-eight hours. We have done twenty-three. We can do two more days."*

She does not say whether she believes this. She says it because it is what needs to be said.

---

### Failure Conditions

| Failure | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Two failed ECM rolls | Imperial shuttle dispatched — combat extraction scenario |
| TSU-9 enters Failed status while drones are active | Immediate detection — base exposed, incomplete data |
| Pelczar incapacitated before transmission | Cannot transmit — mission fails at its core objective |
| Base destroyed or abandoned | Full failure — ground evasion, data core survival scenario |

**These are not binary:** the scenario continues after most failure conditions, just at higher stakes. A failed ECM count means the Imperial response moves into Phase 3 (see Appendix E). A failed TSU-9 means immediate detection but not immediate defeat — the team still has 90 minutes before Marines arrive if Ibáñez-Ochoa follows his standard approach. The data can still be transmitted. The team can still extract. It just costs more.

## 6.7 — DATA STORAGE & PHYSICAL EXTRACTION

---

> *"The data cores are the size of playing cards and weigh less than a canteen of water. This is not what most people imagine when they hear 'classified intelligence product.' It is considerably more frightening."*
> — Vasiliou, Day 23, personal log

---

## 6.8 — CAN PELCZAR CARRY THE DATA OUT?

**The short answer: Yes. Easily. All of it fits in her vest pocket.**

At TL10+, data storage is not the bottleneck. The Core Rulebook (CRB22 p.112) confirms:

- **Data Wafer (TL10):** Credit-card sized rectangle, memory diamond substrate, information encoded in carbon atom structures. Stores Bandwidth/1 programs. Cost: Cr5. Mass: negligible.
- **Beyond TL8:** Storage capacity of even a modest computer is effectively unlimited for practical field purposes.

A single TL10 data wafer can hold more raw surveillance data than BLIND COBRA has collected in 23 days. The six weeks of intercepted relay traffic — even at the elevated 1117 volume — fits on a handful of wafers.

This is worth stating clearly: the scenario's urgency around the data uplink is not about physical storage. It is about **time** (Pelczar needs three decryption passes), **detection** (11 minutes of transmission is near-certain Imperial sensor acquisition), and **processing** (the TL12 SIGINT suite at the console cannot be replicated in a vest pocket). The data itself — all of it — fits in three playing-card-sized cases.

---

## 6.9 — WHAT PELCZAR IS ACTUALLY CARRYING

Pelczar wears three data cores on her vest at all times. Each is a hardened military-grade data storage unit — essentially a ruggedised, encrypted, shielded data wafer stack in a composite case approximately the size of a deck of playing cards. Each weighs approximately 200 grams. Each has a built-in destruction charge — six seconds to trigger, permanent vaporisation of the storage substrate. The charge leaves no recoverable data.

| Core | Designation | Contents | Mass | Encryption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DC-1 (Official) | Mission primary | Standard surveillance data: relay encryption protocols, transmission schedules, security parameters | ~200g | SolSec standard field encryption — Cr5,000 equivalent |
| DC-2 (Official) | Mission backup | Redundant copy of DC-1, updated every 8 hours | ~200g | SolSec standard field encryption |
| DC-3 (Hidden) | Pelczar personal | Partition 1: 8 years of intelligence reports. Partition 2: Breckenridge burned-asset file. Partition 3: Partial decryption of 1117 court traffic | ~200g | Personal encryption — non-standard keys, not in SolSec inventory |

**Total physical mass of all intelligence gathered by BLIND COBRA in 23 days of operation: approximately 600 grams.**

If the uplink fails — if the storm prevents transmission, if SHEPHERD cannot receive, if the team must evacuate before the data package sends — **Pelczar walks out with everything in her vest pockets.** The data cores are smaller than most people's phones and weigh less than a full canteen.

---

## 6.10 — THE REAL BOTTLENECKS

The constraints on getting the intelligence out are not storage. They are:

| Constraint | Issue | Storage Solves It? |
|---|---|---|
| **Transmission time** | 90 seconds (standard) to 11 minutes (full package) — detection risk scales with duration | No — this is bandwidth, not storage |
| **Decryption processing** | Pelczar needs her TL12 SIGINT master suite to run the three decryption passes — this cannot be done on a portable device | No — this is processing power, not storage |
| **Physical extraction** | If the team evacuates on foot, the data cores come automatically | Yes — solved. 600g in vest pockets. |
| **Evidence destruction** | If the base is overrun, DC-1 and DC-2 must be destroyed per Vasiliou's standing order. DC-3 must NOT be destroyed — Pelczar will not allow it. | Creates a conflict — standing order vs hidden agenda. |

**The uplink vs. physical carry decision** is therefore not about whether the data can survive extraction on foot — it can. It is about whether Partition 3 can be *decrypted* before extraction. Raw encrypted data on DC-3 is unreadable to SolSec headquarters without the three passes Pelczar's suite can run. They could eventually decrypt it — but the cipher keys will have rotated, the context will be months stale, and the intelligence value of the 1117 court traffic diminishes significantly with time.

Pelczar does not say any of this to the team. She says: *"I need clean signal conditions for three more passes."* This is true. The reason it matters — the reason she has not slept in six days — is Partition 3.

---

## 6.11 — THE SIGINT SUITE: THE ONE THING THAT DOESN'T FIT IN A POCKET

Pelczar's TL12 signals analysis suite is not a vest-pocket item. It is a 40-kilogram rack-mounted unit installed at the ops centre console. It is the piece of equipment that makes the three decryption passes possible. Without it, DC-3's most important partition remains encrypted indefinitely.

**If extraction is forced before Pelczar finishes:** She faces a binary choice.

**Option A — Leave the suite:**
Walk out with the raw encrypted data on DC-3. SolSec headquarters can eventually process it with equivalent equipment — but this requires months, and by the time anyone gets around to it, the Rebellion is already well underway and the 1117 court traffic will be historical artifact rather than actionable intelligence. The intelligence that might have shaped the Confederation's response to Imperial instability arrives too late to matter.

Pelczar will accept this outcome before she accepts Option B. Reluctantly. Angrily.

**Option B — Take the suite:**
Requires 30 minutes to disconnect (Mechanic 8+, one person). 40kg to carry — STR 9+ or two people. Ground scout drone to transport to KR-7 (the drone's capacity is 1 passenger; the suite requires a cargo run, separate trip). It fits in SHEPHERD's cargo bay — 40kg is negligible displacement.

But 30 minutes of disconnection time, plus a separate drone cargo run to KR-7, in a scenario where the team may have Marines approaching and a TSU-9 that is either repaired or failed — this is the cost of Option B.

**Pelczar's calculus:** She will attempt to complete her decryption passes before extraction rather than take the suite. She will work until Vasiliou tells her to stop. She will not ask to take the suite unless the alternatives are exhausted. When Vasiliou asks *"Can we take it?"* Pelczar will say: *"At what cost?"* She will not answer her own question.

---

## 6.12 — THE dTon QUESTION

Does any of the intelligence take ship space?

**No.** The data cores take zero displacement tons. They are personal equipment, smaller and lighter than a sidearm. Even if Pelczar packed every data wafer in the station — the backup copies, the drone telemetry logs, the signals intercept raw data, all 23 days of everything — the total physical volume would fit in a standard equipment case (approximately 10kg, negligible dTon).

SHEPHERD has 1 ton of available cargo space after its modifications. The entire intelligence product of BLIND COBRA's 23-day operation occupies well under 1% of that space.

The only scenario where displacement tons matter for data is if the team attempts to transport the physical SIGINT suite. At 40kg and roughly 0.3m³, it rounds to zero dTons at standard Traveller displacement calculations. It fits. The question is whether the team has 30 minutes and a drone cargo run to spare.

## 6.14 — THE CAMPAIGN THREAD: DC-3

DC-3 is the seed of the next scenario — and the next ten years of the Rebellion.

If it survives extraction (the most likely outcome), it arrives at SolSec headquarters as an undeclared personal item in Pelczar's custody. It is not logged. It does not exist on any manifest. Pelczar will eventually find a way to process Partition 3 — through a personal connection, a black-budget computation request, or simply waiting until equivalent processing capability is available through normal channels.

When she finally reads it in full — somewhere between Month 10 and Month 18 of Imperial Year 1118 — she will understand what she was looking at in that cave for six days. She will understand why the relay's traffic surge happened. She will understand, retroactively, that the 291st Fleet's elevated threat classification and Marchetti-Solis's increasingly sophisticated encryption was not preparation for an offensive. It was panic management. The Imperial court in 1117 was afraid of its own instability and routing its fear through a frontier relay on a forgotten border world.

She will write three paragraphs in her personal log. She will encrypt them to the point of theoretical unbreakability.

Then she will request a meeting with the SolSec Director. She will bring Partition 1 — eight years of documented suppression, hard-liner interference, intelligence that was buried. She will bring Partition 3 — the evidence that the Imperial court's panic was justified, that something was structurally wrong in the Imperium in the months immediately preceding and following the assassination on 132-1116.

And she will make her case.

What the Director does with it is a story for a different table. ## 6.17 — PHASE TWO: SHEPHERD'S DESCENT

### Mrazová's Options

When SHEPHERD receives the data package confirmation, Mrazová begins descent calculations from the outer system. *Resolute Purpose* has moved to an active patrol pattern — its optimal descent corridor is blocked.

| Descent Route | Transit Time | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Optimal corridor (blocked) | 35 min | *Resolute Purpose* intercepts — SHEPHERD exposed |
| Alternate corridor (night side approach) | 55 min | Low — sensor shadow on approach, but adds 20 minutes |
| Direct high-G descent (emergency) | 22 min | Moderate — brief high-G exposure, detectable but hard to intercept in time |

**Mrazová's default:** alternate corridor, unless the team has transmitted EXTRACT NOW — the emergency extraction code — in which case she uses the high-G descent and accepts the detection risk. Her reasoning: *"If they sent EXTRACT NOW, they are already visible. Fast is better than careful."*

**The timing question:** If *Resolute Purpose* is at a favourable point in its patrol pattern when SHEPHERD begins descent, the alternate corridor (55 min) arrives before the cruiser's sensors return to the basin approach vector. If the timing is unfavourable, Mrazová detects *Resolute Purpose*'s position and holds at 300km altitude until the window opens — adding up to 2 hours.

**Mrazová's one rule:** If she does not receive a status ping within 48 hours of her delay signal, she assumes the team is compromised and jumps for the rendezvous. She has broken this rule once in 8 years. She will not break it again.

**What BLIND COBRA can transmit to SHEPHERD:**
- Status ping (4-byte burst, 0.003 seconds, effectively undetectable): GREEN or RED
- Emergency extraction code (12-byte burst, 0.008 seconds): EXTRACT NOW
- Abort code (8-byte burst): SHEPHERD ABORT — she exits the system immediately, does not wait

They cannot send a narrative update over the status channel. Mrazová knows the team is alive (GREEN ping) or in distress (RED ping or silence). She does not know about Meridian, Osei-Bonsu's investigation, Pelczar's decryption, or the beetle encounters. She is flying blind on everything except orbital mechanics and her own professional judgment.

### The Transponder Problem

SHEPHERD is registered as *Persistent Venture*, commercial courier. A standard TL10 sensor query returns a clean civilian registration — Alpha Crucis neutral registry, legitimate-looking shell company. This holds against *Resolute Purpose*'s standard sensor check.

**A TL12 dedicated sensor analysis cracks the spoof in approximately 4 hours.** Marchetti-Solis has TL12 sensors on her courier vessel. If she is in-atmosphere and actively suspicious when SHEPHERD descends, she can initiate a detailed transponder analysis. She would need to hold *Persistent Venture* in a sensor lock for 4 hours to crack it — which requires her to flag it as suspicious first, which requires her to notice it, which requires SHEPHERD to be visible to her sensors.

During the alternate corridor approach, SHEPHERD is in *Resolute Purpose*'s sensor shadow and Marchetti-Solis's coverage arc misses the descent vector. During the high-G direct descent, both can see it briefly but the transit is too short for 4-hour analysis. The spoof holds in both cases.

**If SHEPHERD is delayed in orbit and detected:** Marchetti-Solis gets her 4 hours. The spoof breaks. The mission's deniability unravels.

## 6.18 — PHASE THREE: GETTING TO KR-7

The drone has been in heavy use for 23 days. Haramoto should have checked it. She has been busy.

**If the team uses the G-carrier (Ibáñez-Ochoa's vehicle):** Only available if the Marines have landed and left the carrier unguarded — an unlikely scenario, but not impossible if Voss brings all her troopers to the base perimeter on foot and leaves the G-carrier at KR-7 with minimal crew. Kowalski's Science (biology) 8+ can read sign and predict encounter probability before committing to a route.

**The tunnel option:** The geological survey notes indicate a tunnel through the formation 2km north of the base. It exits on the west side of the ridgeline — the megafauna-poor side. This adds 3–4km to the route but avoids the Tyrant's cliff-base territory entirely for the first leg. Haramoto knows about the tunnel. She has not mentioned it because it was not relevant until now.

## 6.19 — PHASE FOUR: THE LANDING ZONE CONFLICT

KR-7 is the landing zone for both SHEPHERD (arriving to extract the team) and Ibáñez-Ochoa's Ship's Boat (arriving to land Marines).

**The sequencing problem:** The team needs SHEPHERD to arrive before the Marines, or to have the Marines at KR-7 depart (or be neutralised) before SHEPHERD arrives.

## 6.20 — PHASE FIVE: JUMP CLEARANCE

Once all team members are aboard and SHEPHERD lifts from KR-7, Mrazová plots her ascent. Jump requires clearing Lakamsal's 100-diameter limit — approximately **40 minutes at Thrust 2** from the surface.

## 6.24 — THE JUMP: DESTINATION AND WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

### Jump Mechanics

Once all team members are aboard and SHEPHERD clears Lakamsal's 100-diameter jump limit, Mrazová executes Jump-2 to the pre-arranged rendezvous. The jump limit is approximately **40 minutes from the surface at Thrust 2**.

**Jump duration:** 7 days (Jump-2, standard).

**Destination:** Classified — Pelczar knows it, nobody else on the team does. This is standard SolSec compartmentalisation. If Breckenridge is not on the ship, Pelczar is the only person who knows where they are going. She does not share this voluntarily. If asked, she says: *"A system. Uninhabited. The strike team is waiting."*

### At the Rendezvous

The SolSec strike team that will physically destroy the relay is waiting at the rendezvous — a system with no permanent population, one jump from the Solomani border. They receive the data package. Pelczar's decryption work — whatever she has completed — goes with it.

**What the strike team does with it:** They destroy the relay. That was always the plan.

**What happens to Partition 3:** The intelligence product Pelczar has been working toward for six days — the evidence of the Imperial court's institutional panic, the forward strategy papers, the competing assessments of Solomani military capability written by analysts who believe something is coming — goes to SolSec intelligence analysis. It will be processed. It will be classified. It will be read by people who have the clearance to understand it.

It will reach the right desks within two months. The assassination of Emperor Strephon occurred on 132-1116 — eight months ago. The analysts who read Pelczar's data will, in retrospect, recognise it as an early signal of what was already unravelling at the Imperial court. They will not be able to do anything useful with this information. The Rebellion is already in progress. Confederation forces have been conducting offensive operations along the Solomani Front since 325-1116. *(For sourcing on these dates — which are canon and which are scenario-original — see Chapter 1, §1.9, "Sources & Canon Status.")*

**Whether the strike team still considers the relay a priority target** after reading what Pelczar found in the Imperial court traffic — whether they destroy it, or decide the intelligence product is now more valuable than destroying the source, or simply proceed as ordered — is a decision that will be made by people who are not in the Kethara basin, for reasons that BLIND COBRA will never be briefed on.

This is the nature of their work. It is not new to any of them.
