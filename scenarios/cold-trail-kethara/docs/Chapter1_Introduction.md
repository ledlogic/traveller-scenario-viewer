**Trade Classifications:** Agricultural, Pre-industrial *[Source: Traveller Wiki, "Lakamsal (world)" — https://wiki.travellerrpg.com/Lakamsal_(world)?sector=Solomani%20Rim&hex=3218]*
- **Agricultural** — a near-ideal environment for producing quality foodstuffs of plant, animal, and other forms; quality foodstuffs are a major export commodity for this world. This is the basis for the legitimate agricultural export trade that gives IRON GATE's cover story its plausibility (see "Why This World?" below).
- **Pre-industrial** — the world cannot yet manufacture enough high-technology resources to support a modern, star-faring society on its own; manufactured and high-tech goods must be imported, driving up their price locally. This is why imported TL10–13 equipment (noted in the Tech Level row above) stands out on Lakamsal in a way it wouldn't on a more developed world — and why a discreet supply run for specialist gear is a meaningfully harder logistics problem here than it would be at, say, Banasdan.

**Government: Civil Service Bureaucracy** *[Flavor adapted from "101 Governments" (fan supplement, British Isles Traveller Support), David Thomas, ed. Andy Lilly, "8: Civil Service Bureaucracy," pp.31–32]*
- **Character of the administration.** The colonial departments are staffed by career civil servants selected on merit, not politics, and each office in Lakamsal Town has developed its own institutional personality — the Agricultural Ministry brisk and practical, the Colonial Militia's liaison office understaffed and quietly overwhelmed, the customs and permits desk exactly as slow as you'd expect. None of it is sinister. It's just what a competent, underfunded frontier bureaucracy looks like after a century of doing the same job with the same headcount.
- **Rehabilitation over punishment — with a labour angle.** A Civil Service Bureaucracy prefers to make dissidents useful rather than martyrs. On Lakamsal that instinct has an obvious outlet: the agricultural quota system. Minor troublemakers, unlicensed agitators, and the occasional off-world drifter who can't produce clean papers are routinely assigned to river delta farming collectives to work off a debt to the administration — manual labour, deliberately kept clear of the mechanised equipment reserved for licensed agricultural workers, on the stated grounds of "equipment liability" but functionally to keep the assignment unskilled, supervised, and quota-driven. It is not framed as a penal colony and nobody on Lakamsal would call it one — it is framed as contribution, re-education, paying back what you owe the society that fed you. The resemblance to sending intellectuals to the countryside during a Cultural Revolution is not something the Civil Service would ever draw itself, but a well-read player character might.
- **Bureaucratic friction as an obstacle, not a fight.** Documentation matters more than money here. A character trying to move through Lakamsal Town's official channels — permits, contracts, customs clearance — should expect delays measured in hours while departments sort out jurisdictional boundaries, not violence. This is the natural texture for any scene involving Investigators Chen and Kaur's paperwork, or BLIND COBRA's "geological survey" cover story holding up (or not) under official scrutiny.
- **Price control creates arbitrage.** The administration fixes prices on most goods, which means local agricultural produce is often artificially cheap relative to other worlds, while anything imported (see Pre-industrial, above) runs artificially expensive. A character making a good Trade or Streetwise roll in Lakamsal Town can turn that gap into a genuine, small profit — or just notice it as one more sign of how tightly the colonial administration controls ordinary life here.

### Why This World?

Lakamsal is an agricultural world at the edge of Imperial space — 50 million people spread across river delta farming settlements, a Civil Service planetary administration centred on Lakamsal Town, and a Class C downport that handles the agricultural export trade. It is not a backwater. It is, however, the kind of world where most of the population lives in small farming communities, where the colonial administration is competent but understaffed, and where large tracts of wilderness — including the Kethara Reach — have no permanent human presence at all. There is no naval base. There is no Imperial garrison. The Scout Service maintains a relay drone in orbit and a single monitoring post co-located with the downport. The planetary militia is a light frontier force, adequate for megafauna control and minor criminal matters, not equipped to challenge Imperial military operations.

The Imperial Navy Signals Intelligence division chose Lakamsal for a specific combination of reasons. A Class C port world three parsecs spinward of the border. A legitimate agricultural economy that justifies regular traffic without attracting scrutiny. A colonial administration with formal authority but no Imperial security capability. And most importantly: the Kethara Reach — a 300-kilometre-wide river basin on the main supercontinent with no permanent settlements, no roads, active apex megafauna, and a local reputation that keeps colonists well clear of it. They embedded the relay 280 kilometres from Lakamsal Town inside a legitimate geological survey installation. The basin's hostility is the installation's best security.

The relay has been operational for 14 months. It monitors Confederation fleet movements across three subsectors of the Solomani Front and transmits encrypted burst packages to an Imperial Navy signals vessel that makes scheduled passes through the system every 11 days.

SolSec found out eight months ago. They have been planning ever since.

### The Thin Atmosphere

Lakamsal's atmosphere (Code 4 — Thin) has meaningful gameplay implications. All personnel operating outside without filter masks for more than 2 hours suffer DM-1 to all physical tasks due to exertion-induced hypoxia. Filter masks (×6, one per team member) are stocked on the station stores shelf in Crew Quarters/Medical, next to the Emergency Vacc Suit rack — see Station Layout below. Kowalski carries her own as personal kit and does not draw from station stores. The megafauna are evolved for this atmosphere and unaffected.

### Law

Personal firearms beyond shotguns are prohibited for civilians without a licensed permit. The Colonial Militia enforces this in Lakamsal Town and the settled delta regions. Kaur's autopistol (carried openly) requires a valid corporate security permit — Chen confirmed they have one before departure. Vasiliou's team's military weapons are covered by Imperial jurisdiction. This law level is not enforced in the Kethara Reach (there is no law enforcement presence there), but it becomes relevant if any character enters Lakamsal Town.

### Current Date

Imperial Year **1117**, day 010 — eight months after the assassination of Emperor Strephon Aella Alkhalikoi on 132-1116. Word of the assassination reached the Solomani Rim by 311-1116 and has been propagating through frontier systems since. Confederation forces began offensive operations along the Solomani Front on 325-1116, exploiting the chaos of the Rebellion. The Rebellion is underway. The Imperium that commissioned the relay BLIND COBRA is watching — and the Imperium that issued Ibáñez-Ochoa's sealed orders — is already cracking. Nobody in the Kethara basin understands what that means yet. The signals from Capital are confused, contradictory, and above their clearance level. The mission continues because nobody has told them to stop.

## 1.4 — KETHARA BASIN

### Terrain

The Kethara Reach basin — where BLIND COBRA is operating — is a primordial river valley ecosystem on Lakamsal's main supercontinent. Thin atmosphere means the vegetation grows dense and tall to maximise photosynthesis. The megafauna are large, oxygen-metabolising, and aggressive. The basalt cliff systems along the river are the result of ancient tectonic activity and provide natural concealment. The ecosystem has never experienced a mass extinction. The apex predators here are very large, and very old as a species.

The colonists call the river valley the "Kethara Reach," meaning a place you go when you want to be beyond reach of anything useful, including help. The IISS survey team that mapped the valley in 1098 lost two xenobiologists to megafauna: Dr. Priya Garner and Dr. Marcus Kethara, whose names the survey gave to the local fauna genus and the river respectively. Okonkwo named his two armed recon drones "Priya" and "Garner" after them. He did not explain why to the rest of the team.

### Electromagnetic Storm Cycle

The Kethara basin sits on a formation of magnetite-rich basalt — the same ancient volcanic geology that created the cliff system housing BLIND COBRA. At ground level the valley functions as a natural electromagnetic channel. When ionising weather systems move through from the northwest on a roughly 48-hour cycle during the wet season, the basalt concentrates and amplifies electromagnetic interference in ways completely invisible from orbit.

**This does not appear prominently in Osei-Bonsu's planetary survey database.** The entry reads: *"Periodic electromagnetic anomalies, Kethara basin and adjacent river valley system. Associated with basalt iron-magnetite content. Low severity. No commercial significance."* Nobody flagged it as operationally relevant. Both the Imperial relay installation and BLIND COBRA were sited using the same orbital survey data. Both discovered the problem after they were already committed.

**Day 23 — Storm Status:** A storm is building. Interference has been rising since six hours before the scenario begins. It will peak for approximately 6–8 hours starting at **Hour 4** — the same hour Marchetti-Solis's sweep drones go active. The storm begins clearing after **Hour 10**.

**The TSU-9 Node Synchronisation Problem:** The Intellect/2 thermal management program communicates with the surface emission nodes via low-power radio links through the cliff face. Storm interference has been disrupting this handshake for 23 days. Six nodes have lost synchronisation entirely and are running in uncoordinated open-loop mode — running hotter than instructed because they cannot hear the Core unit. This is the secondary cause of the 140% load. A jury-rig during storm peak holds the load but cannot resynchronise the nodes. A full repair during the clearing phase (Hours 10–14) gives the nodes a chance to resynchronise — potentially dropping load to 95% without crash site parts, if Haramoto is fortunate. Roll Engineer (electronics) 10+ during the clearing phase for resynchronisation. *(Full TSU-9 technical reference: see Appendix A.)*

**The data uplink problem:** At storm peak, Pelczar's encrypted data package uplink to the courier is at DM-2. She can transmit during the peak (Hours 4–10) at Difficult (10+) Electronics (comms), or wait until clearing (Hour 10+) for Average (8+) — but by then the base may already be compromised. This is her core dilemma at Hour 8.

**The storm hits IRON GATE too — and that's why any of this is breakable at all.** The same basalt-channeled interference degrading BLIND COBRA's uplink is degrading IRON GATE's outbound bandwidth to its Imperial Navy courier. IRON GATE was engineered for reliability under exactly this condition: when the storm cuts its available bandwidth, the relay automatically falls back from its full military-grade cipher suite to a lighter, faster, less secure legacy protocol — because to whoever built that failover logic, maintaining *some* contact with Capital during a storm mattered more than maintaining ideal security for a few hours at a time. Under ordinary circumstances that's a reasonable trade-off for a frontier relay nobody expects to be watched. Pelczar is watching. **Every one of her three partial decryption passes has happened during a storm peak** — she is not breaking IRON GATE's real encryption from scratch, she is exploiting the fallback cipher the relay drops to when the weather gets bad. Referees can make this explicit or leave it implicit: either way, her Electronics (computers) rolls to advance Partition 3 should get a **DM+2 during storm Peak phases only** (Hours 4–10 on Day 23), reflecting the degraded cipher, at the cost of everything else at the base getting worse at the exact same time.

### Megaflora: The Kethara Basin Jungle

The Kethara basin jungle is not comparable to a Terran tropical forest. The physics of Lakamsal's low gravity (0.45G) and thin atmosphere produce vegetation on a large scale.

| Vegetation Layer | Height | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Emergent megaflora | 120–180m | Thin-trunked, wide-crowned. Trunk diameter 3–5m at base, narrowing rapidly. Visible from orbit as texture variation. |
| Main canopy | 60–90m | Dense, continuous. Trunks 2–4m diameter. Blocks most light to the forest floor. The camo netting on the cliff face is at this level from above. |
| Understory | 20–40m | Shade-adapted. Broader leaves, blue-purple pigmentation. Visibility limited to 30–50m at ground level. |
| Ground cover | 0–8m | Dense fern-analogues with fronds 3–5m wide. Bioluminescent moss. Root systems breaking through basalt. Off-trail movement requires a blade or Athletics (endurance) 8+. |

**Gameplay effects:**
- **Aerial observation:** The 60–90m main canopy completely conceals ground-level activity from above. The cliff face is only visible where it rises above the canopy.
- **Drone operations:** Quad-rotors must fly above the canopy (60m+) for lateral movement. Below canopy, movement is limited to the river and game trails.
- **Ground movement:** Off-trail: 2kph maximum, Athletics (endurance) 8+ per hour.
- **Sound:** The megaflora forest is loud. Wind through 180m trees, canopy movement, branch falls, animal calls echoing off the basalt ridgeline. Recon (hearing-based) checks at DM-1 in the forest.
- **The basalt formation** rises above the main canopy by 0–20m. The cliff face drops into the canopy — the lower 20m of the cliff is below canopy level, making the cave entrance effectively invisible from above.

## 1.6 — SOLOMANI SECURITY: BACKGROUND & THE MISSION

SolSec is the third power in the Solomani Confederation. Its official mission is "the maintenance and protection of the Solomani Cause against all enemies" — which in practice means it operates simultaneously as secret police, external intelligence agency, counter-intelligence bureau, and covert action arm. Agents have broad extralegal powers within the Confederation and operate without formal acknowledgement in Imperial and neutral space.

### Why This Mission Exists

The Imperial Navy signals relay on Lakamsal has been compromising Confederation fleet movement security for 14 months — but the fleet movements it matters most for aren't the wider Confederation Navy's. They're **ORDA's**.

**The Orichalc Regional Defense Association** is a real, canon Solomani institution: a mutual defense alliance of thirteen Confederation border worlds founded in 1014 by Cthonia (Alpha Crucis 0515), which leads it. ORDA maintains a large, sophisticated fleet oriented specifically against the Imperial threat — a fleet that operates **independent of Confederation government control**, legally entitled to do so under the Charter, and tolerated by Confederation officials (who are privately uneasy about its size) largely because Cthonia's leverage over Solomani politics makes it politically costly to challenge. *[Source: Traveller Wiki, "Cthonia (AC 0515) (world)"; Mongoose Traveller: Solomani / Alpha Crucis Sector, cited on Traveller Wiki, "Orichalc Subsector."]*

That independence is exactly the problem IRON GATE creates. ORDA doesn't answer to Home the way the regular Confederation Navy does — its own leadership decides when its own fleet elements reposition, mass, or commit along the border, including the Banasdan Subsector stretch of the Solomani Front where Lakamsal sits. Every time an ORDA-linked squadron moves, IRON GATE reads it and Marchetti-Solis's reports go up the Imperial chain within 48 hours. That means the Imperium can see ORDA's hand tipping before ORDA itself has decided whether to commit. **SolSec's mission on Lakamsal is forward-ops work in service of ORDA's own decision cycle: destroy the relay, and ORDA regains the ability to choose its own moment.** The 325-1116 offensive along the Solomani Front (see 1.10, Timeline — "the Border Action") is the kind of operation this is meant to protect: whether it was ORDA's fleet, Confederation regulars, or some coordination of both is deliberately left unresolved in SolSec's own paperwork, and the referee is free to decide it either way without contradicting anything in this document.

SolSec has confirmed the compromise through the interrogation of a captured Imperial signals analyst. The relay has to be destroyed.

The problem: Lakamsal is Imperial territory. A naval strike or a conventional military team would constitute an overt act of war — exactly what neither government wants this close to the frontier, and exactly the kind of provocation that would hand the Imperium grounds to move against ORDA specifically, not just the Confederation in general. A diplomatic protest would simply alert the Imperium to seal the evidence and deny everything. Sending a corporate proxy or a third party could be traced back.

So SolSec is sending BLIND COBRA. A five-person remote operations cell, inserted under civilian cover, operating entirely deniably on Imperial soil, with orders to gather the technical data the inbound SolSec strike team needs to destroy the relay without leaving evidence — and to do it without anyone knowing they were ever there.

If the team is captured on Imperial territory, the Confederation denies their existence. Their cover identities — employees of a geological survey company registered on a neutral world in Alpha Crucis — will hold for weeks under Imperial scrutiny. By then, either the team has been extracted or there is nothing left to extract.

This is not a mission any of them can talk about. It does not appear in any SolSec database that could be legally subpoenaed. The authorisation exists in a single encrypted document held by the SolSec Director personally. It is the kind of mission that ends careers if it succeeds and ends lives if it fails.

### What the Relay is Carrying — 1117

For fourteen months the Lakamsal relay was a quiet, orderly operation. Military survey data. Fleet positioning reports. Routine Confederation naval traffic, intercepted and packaged into neat encrypted bursts every 11 days. Marchetti-Solis ran a clean, professional operation and her superiors were pleased with the product.

Six weeks ago everything changed.

**The Imperial Court is nervous.** In 1117 the court of Emperor Strephon is experiencing internal turbulence that the official channels are working hard to suppress. Succession tensions, factional positioning, concerns about the stability of the Domain of Sol, and something deeper — something none of the communiqués name directly, but which runs underneath all of them like a current. The intelligence agencies are responding the only way they know how: more data, faster, higher classification, more eyes, more analysis.

**What this means for the relay:** What was a trickle of clean military surveillance product has become a flood. The relay is now handling:

- **High-volume encrypted strategy documents** from the 291st Fleet headquarters and Domain of Sol command — forward planning papers for scenarios involving the Solomani Confederation that would have been unthinkable to write eighteen months ago
- **Theoretical position papers** on Solomani military capability, Confederation political stability, and SolSec operational capacity — written by analysts who clearly believe something is coming and are trying to get ahead of it
- **Intelligence assessments and counter-assessments** — multiple competing hypotheses about Confederation intentions, fleet readiness, and whether a Solomani move on the Front is being planned or merely feared
- **Heavily encrypted personal communications** between senior Naval Intelligence officers — traffic that should not be going through a frontier relay, routing here because someone in the chain does not trust the primary secure channels

The relay was built to handle one 11-day burst package at a time. It is now receiving continuous high-priority traffic. Its civilian-spec TL10 storage systems are running at capacity. Its encryption processors are cycling continuously and running hot. The six operators who think they are running a geological survey have noticed that the basement generator is on more often than usual. They have not asked questions. They are paid not to ask questions.

**Marchetti-Solis knows the relay is at its limit.** She has filed two reports in the past six weeks warning that the installation's civilian-grade hardware is not rated for sustained high-volume encrypted traffic. Both reports were acknowledged and effectively ignored. She does not believe the situation is temporary. She also knows that the content passing through her relay — the fragments she can see at the metadata level — suggests her superiors are preparing for something significant involving the Solomani Front. She does not know what. She is not cleared for it. She finds this deeply unsettling.

**Pelczar has noticed.** For twenty-three days BLIND COBRA's signals intercept array has been absorbing everything the relay transmits and receives. For the first fourteen days it was routine military product. Then the pattern changed. Volume up. Encryption strength up. Transmission frequency up. The burst-every-11-days schedule gone, replaced by continuous encrypted streaming. Pelczar has been working for six days with almost no sleep trying to understand what she is looking at.

The secret third data core on her vest now contains something she did not expect to find when BLIND COBRA was inserted: evidence that the Imperial court is in serious internal trouble, and that the Domain of Sol's military planners are treating the Solomani Front as a near-term active threat. She is three decryption passes away from knowing what that means. She needs those three passes to run cleanly, without storm interference, before the data package uplinks. This is the real reason she has not left the console in twelve days.

---

### BLIND COBRA — The Cell

BLIND COBRA is a Field Remote Operations Cell. They crossed the Solomani border 29 days ago aboard a civilian tramp freighter, using manifests showing cargo bound for the Lakamsal downport. They rented two ground vehicles from a colonist in the main settlement — for "geological survey work, three weeks" — and drove into the Kethara Reach. They have not returned the vehicles. They are 23 days into the operation.

The relay is 280 kilometres from the base. It is disguised as a geological survey installation operated by a legitimate Imperial contractor, employing six people none of whom know what is really in the basement. The relay originally transmitted burst packages every 11 days — that schedule has changed. It is now streaming continuously. The next major scheduled courier collection is in 72 hours, but the relay has been sending compressed data packages to any passing Imperial vessel for the past six weeks. The SolSec strike team that will physically destroy the relay needs the full technical data from BLIND COBRA's surveillance — including Pelczar's partial decryption work — before they can act.

**Yes, it really is just antennas and sheds — that's the whole design.** A team expecting a hardened listening post will find something that looks disappointingly modest: a weather/seismic instrument mast, a couple of equipment sheds, a small solar array, nine personnel (three naval, six civilian contractors), no visible weapons, no bunker, no perimeter defenses beyond what any legitimate survey site would have. This is not a mistake in how the site was built, and it is not a trap disguised as weakness — IRON GATE's security was never meant to be physical. Its entire defense is that nobody is supposed to know it's there, and nobody watching from orbit is supposed to see anything worth a second look. NavINT chose deliberate invisibility over hardening: a fortified installation would have been detected and flagged the moment anyone did a competent orbital survey, whereas an unremarkable one has sat here undetected for fourteen months. The installation's real security — the thing that would actually respond if it were ever seriously threatened — is not on-site at all. It's the Imperial Navy's sweep drones, and beyond them, Ibáñez-Ochoa's Marines, both of which exist precisely because the site itself has none.

The surveillance run is 80% complete. Pelczar is three decryption passes from something important. The team is 72 hours from extraction. Then everything starts going wrong.

## 1.7 — REFEREE NOTES

### Tone

These are professionals. The scenario does not require engagement with the broader ethics of Solomani politics. The referee should not push players toward political positions — SolSec is not the good guys, and the Imperial Navy is not the bad guys, and none of that is the point. BLIND COBRA is a team of skilled people in a situation that has become significantly more dangerous than planned. That is sufficient.

The megafauna are not horror elements. They are environmental hazards with their own logic — Kowalski understands that logic better than anyone else. The scenario works best when the jungle feels like a third faction, indifferent to everyone.

### The Year 1117 — Referee Dramatic Irony Note

The scenario is set in Imperial Year 1117, day 010 — eight months after the assassination of Emperor Strephon on 132-1116, with the Rebellion already underway and Confederation forces having launched offensive operations along the Solomani Front two months ago (325-1116). The referee should be aware of this. The players may or may not be, depending on their Traveller knowledge.

**If the players know their Traveller history:** They will feel the dramatic irony the moment they hear the year. BLIND COBRA is risking everything to destroy an intelligence asset whose parent organisation is already fracturing — the assassination happened eight months ago, word reached the Solomani Rim on 311-1116, and the Rebellion is in progress. Marchetti-Solis is filing maintenance reports to a chain of command that is actively contested. Ibáñez-Ochoa's sealed orders come from a fleet headquarters whose authority is no longer unambiguous. The relay they are trying to destroy may already be strategically compromised by the chaos above it — but nobody in the Kethara basin has been told to stand down.

**The intelligence surge is the first tremor.** The nervous traffic, the escalating classification, the personal cipher keys routing through a frontier relay, the forward strategy papers that treat the Solomani Front as a near-term active threat — these are the symptoms of an Imperial court that is, at the highest levels, beginning to feel the ground shift beneath it. Nobody has named what is coming. But the people generating this traffic know something is wrong. They are writing contingency plans for a frontier they are afraid they will not be able to hold.

**Pelczar's Partition 3 — what she is three decryption passes from finding:** She will not complete those passes during this scenario. But if the campaign continues, and if her data core survives extraction, she will eventually decrypt enough to understand that the Imperial court's traffic surge in the months before 132-1116 was the symptom of a court that already knew something was structurally wrong. What she will find in the most recent traffic — the past six weeks — is something different: the first attempts by Domain of Sol command to assess what the assassination means for the Solomani Front. The Emperor is already dead. Nobody in the Kethara basin knows it yet, but the chain of command that commissioned this relay and the chain of command that is hunting BLIND COBRA are both, at this moment, operating on inertia — awaiting orders that will never arrive in the form they expect.

**The 1117 political backdrop — what the referee needs to know:**
- Strephon was assassinated on **132-1116** in the Grand Reception Hall of the Imperial Palace above Capital/Core. The Empress Iolanthe and Grand Princess Iphegenia were also killed.
- Word reached the Solomani Rim approximately at **311-1116** — about two months before this scenario.
- Confederation forces began offensive operations along the Solomani Front on **325-1116** and have continued since. *(This is not the historical Solomani Rim War of 990–1002; it has no fixed name in wider Imperium historiography.)*
- *For sourcing on the two canon-anchored dates above, and a note on which dates in this scenario are scenario-original rather than canon, see "Sources & Canon Status" in 1.10 — Time.*
- Ibáñez-Ochoa's sealed orders, Marchetti-Solis's surveillance authority, and the entire Imperial Navy chain of command above them is operating in a contested succession environment. Their orders are real, but the authority that issued them is no longer stable.
- SolSec likewise: the Confederation government that inserted BLIND COBRA is making decisions in a rapidly changing strategic environment. The mission was planned before the assassination. Whether it still makes strategic sense is a question nobody in the Kethara basin is cleared to answer.

**Do not narrate this to the players.** Let it live in the texture of what Pelczar describes, what Breckenridge has deduced, and what the relay's changed traffic implies. The best version of this scenario ends with the team extracted, the data uplinked, and one or two of them quietly wondering what exactly they were looking at in that valley — and why it felt like something much larger than one signals relay on a forgotten frontier world.

## 1.9 — WHAT MAKES THIS SCENARIO WORK AT A CONVENTION TABLE

Cold Trail in Kethara Reach is designed for 3–5 hours at a convention, with 3–6 players who may have no prior Traveller experience. It has been playtested at GenCon and RPG Day at The Source. These notes reflect what actually works at the table.

**The scenario's three strongest elements:**
- The **cascading pressure system** — problems compound rather than resolve, which creates the feeling of a professional team genuinely stretched
- The **1117 dramatic irony** — players who know their Traveller history feel it immediately; players who don't feel it by the end
- The **characters** — Vasiliou's controlled exhaustion, Breckenridge's silence, Pelczar's private certainty that she is looking at something important — these are characters players remember

**What can go wrong at a convention:**
- Players spend too long on Act One (the surveillance run and TSU-9 diagnosis) and run out of time before the sweep begins
- The referee tries to resolve threats sequentially rather than simultaneously
- Players engage the Tyrant in direct combat instead of managing it, triggering a cascade of consequences that confuse the middle of the scenario
- The table loses track of the three simultaneous countdowns

**Solutions to each:**
- Run the surveillance roll as background — it succeeds unless Okonkwo is genuinely distracted by another task. The TSU-9 is the real Act One problem.
- When three problems are active, cut between them every 60–90 seconds of real time. Don't resolve one fully before moving to the next.
- When players reach for their weapons against the Tyrant, Kowalski (or Vasiliou) says: *"That thermal spike adds twenty-five percent to the TSU-9 load and logs an ECM failure. Your call."* Let them make the informed choice.
- Put the three countdowns on a physical index card visible to all players: TSU-9 load (current %), ECM failures logged (0/2), extraction window (72 hours minus elapsed time).

## 1.10 — TIME

Local time on Lakamsal, the full BLIND COBRA and Lakamsal timeline, and the referee's pacing guide for running the scenario's Hour-count — all in one place.

---

### Local Time on Lakamsal

Every clock reference in this scenario — Hour 0, Day 23, the TSU-9's 24-hour coolant cycle, the 72-hour extraction window — is given in **Imperial standard time**: the universal 24-standard-hour day and 365-standard-day year that Naval Intelligence, SolSec, and every ship in the Imperium keep, regardless of which world they're on. That is not a simplification for play purposes — it is how Traveller timekeeping actually works. With thousands of settled worlds, each on its own rotation and orbit, nobody re-synchronises their calendar to local solar days. Imperial personnel run on Imperial time everywhere, and Lakamsal is no exception.

Lakamsal's own solar day does not match that schedule. Its actual rotational period is **33.94 standard hours** — not 24 — with a 34° axial tilt and a 646-day, 9-hour, 57-minute orbital year. *[Source: Traveller Worlds generator (T5), "The Lakamsal system (3218 Solomani Rim)," Size Details panel.]* One full Lakamsal day/night cycle is therefore about **1 day, 9 hours, 56 minutes** in Imperial terms — roughly 1.41 Imperial standard days per local sunrise-to-sunrise cycle.

**What this means at the table:**
- The scenario's 72-hour extraction window covers **just over two local day/night cycles** on Lakamsal, not three.
- For every 24 Imperial hours that pass, the actual local clock drifts by roughly **+10 hours** relative to a tidy 24-hour cadence. A team that has been in the field for 23 days — like BLIND COBRA at scenario start — has had their subjective daytime and nighttime hours cycle all the way around the clock and back again several times over, purely because they track Imperial hours while the planet keeps its own, much longer schedule.
- **"Hour 4 of a 6-hour surveillance pass" does not reliably tell you whether it is light or dark outside.** The referee should feel free to set actual sun position independently of the scenario's Hour-count — early "daytime" hours can fall at local dusk or deep night depending on where in the 33.94-hour cycle the mission happens to land.
- This is good material for atmosphere, not a rules complication. A line from Kowalski or Haramoto about having genuinely lost track of when the sun is supposed to come up — after three weeks in the field, on a planet whose day doesn't match the one on their wrist chronometers — costs nothing and sells the isolation of the posting.

### Timeline: Lakamsal and the Kethara Reach

*Selected events relevant to Cold Trail in Kethara Reach. Dates in Imperial calendar (year.day). Entries reflect what is known at scenario start (010-1117) unless otherwise noted.*

---

**~0423** The Imperial Scout Service charts Lakamsal (Solomani Rim 3218) during the early expansion period. UWP established as C545786-8. The world is designated agricultural, Class C downport authorised. No follow-up survey conducted. The system is noted as strategically insignificant.

**~1020–1060** Lakamsal's population reaches the mid-millions and stabilises. River delta farming communities established across the main supercontinent. Civil Service planetary administration formed, headquartered at Lakamsal Town near the downport. Colonial Militia established — light frontier force, primary function megafauna management and agricultural district security.

**~1095** Shulgi & Enki Vehicle Hire established in Lakamsal Town downport district. One of several small commercial operators serving the agricultural and survey trade. Fleet of approximately forty ground vehicles.

**1098.???** Imperial Scout Service survey team enters the Kethara Reach basin to map megafauna distribution for colonial safety records. Two xenobiologists — Dr. Priya Garner and Dr. Marcus Kethara — are killed by an apex predator, later designated KR-ALPHA class. The basin is subsequently flagged in IISS records as a restricted xenobiological study zone with no permanent settlement recommended. The locals begin calling it the Kethara Reach — a place you go when you want to be beyond reach of anything useful, including help.

**~1098–1100** IISS installs a single relay drone in Lakamsal orbit. No active monitoring station established planetside beyond a downport coordination node. The Scout Service presence on Lakamsal consists of one officer (currently Dr. Kwame Osei-Bonsu) and a ground vehicle.

**1103.???** Imperial Navy Signals Intelligence (NavINT) identifies the Kethara Reach as a viable site for a long-range signals intercept installation covering three subsectors of the Solomani Front. The site meets all criteria: Class C world three parsecs spinward of the border, legitimate agricultural traffic providing cover, a wilderness zone with natural security, and a colonial administration with no Imperial security capability.

**1103–1104** NavINT constructs the relay installation inside a modified geological survey structure in the Kethara Reach basin, 280 kilometres from Lakamsal Town. Six civilian contractors are employed under geological survey cover. None are briefed on the installation's true purpose. Construction complete, installation operational. Relay designation: IRON GATE.

**~1104** SolSec Counter-Intelligence identifies unusual Imperial traffic patterns on the Solomani Front consistent with a signals intercept operation in the Banasdan Subsector. Source location not yet confirmed.

**1115.???** NavINT assigns Lieutenant Commander Yeva Marchetti-Solis to command IRON GATE. She replaces the previous officer, who has retired. Marchetti-Solis brings a staff of three naval signals officers under civilian cover. The installation now has nine personnel: three naval, six contractors.

**1115.???** SolSec Counter-Intelligence narrows the source of the intercept operation to within two parsecs of the Solomani border in Banasdan Subsector. A captured Imperial signals analyst confirms the existence of a frontier relay but cannot identify its location. SolSec planning begins for a physical neutralisation operation.

**1116.048** The IRON GATE relay intercepts and decrypts a Confederation naval repositioning order covering the 3rd Solomani Fleet's movements toward the Rim. This is the first major intelligence product of direct strategic value. Marchetti-Solis files a high-priority report. The relay's traffic classification is immediately elevated.

**1116.132** Emperor Strephon Aella Alkhalikoi is assassinated at 1517 hours local time in the Grand Reception Hall of the Imperial Palace above Capital/Core. The Empress Iolanthe and Grand Princess Iphegenia are also killed, along with twelve Imperial Guards and a number of bystanders. The Rebellion begins. *[Canon date. Sources: Traveller News Service archive, 1116; Traveller Wiki, "Rebellion"; GDW, Challenge #30 (1988), "Fall of the Imperium," p.30-11 — advance-word/official-news discrepancy table confirms 132-1116 as the assassination date used consistently across Classic Traveller, MegaTraveller, and Mongoose canon.]*

**1116.134–200** At IRON GATE on Lakamsal, Marchetti-Solis and her staff observe a dramatic surge in encrypted traffic volume. Transmission frequency increases from 11-day burst packages to near-continuous streaming. Encryption complexity increases markedly. Personal cipher keys from senior Naval Intelligence officers begin appearing on channels not rated for them. Marchetti-Solis files two maintenance reports warning that the installation's civilian-grade hardware is not rated for sustained high-volume encrypted traffic. Both are acknowledged and effectively ignored.

**1116.200** SolSec locates IRON GATE. Signals analysis and a source within NavINT's Banasdan Subsector command confirms the installation is operating from the Kethara Reach on Lakamsal. SolSec Director personally authorises a covert neutralisation mission. A physical strike team is assembled. A surveillance cell — BLIND COBRA — is assigned to gather the technical data the strike team will need.

**1116.311** Word of Emperor Strephon's assassination reaches the Solomani Rim. The Confederation government enters emergency session. The strategic calculus of every operation currently in planning shifts. BLIND COBRA's mission is reviewed and reconfirmed — if anything, the intelligence surge at IRON GATE makes the relay more valuable to destroy, not less. A destabilised Imperium with compromised frontier communications is a more dangerous adversary than a stable one. *[Adapted from canon. Source: GDW, Challenge #30 (1988), "Fall of the Imperium," p.30-11, which gives 311-1116 as the date advance word of the assassination reached Terra/Sol via naval courier (jump-6), versus 036-1117 for official news via the standard xboat network (jump-4). Terra/Sol is used here as the nearest documented data point for "the Solomani Rim" generally; Lakamsal, being deeper in Banasdan Subsector, may plausibly have heard sooner or later depending on courier routing — the referee may adjust this date without contradicting canon.]*

**1116.325** Confederation naval and paramilitary forces begin coordinated offensive operations against Imperial positions along the Solomani Front, exploiting the political chaos of the Rebellion following Strephon's assassination — word of which reached the Rim only two weeks earlier. *Referee's note: this is not the historical Solomani Rim War (990–1002) — that war ended over a century before this scenario. The 1116–1117 fighting has no fixed name in wider Imperium historiography; SolSec's own internal correspondence refers to it only as "the Border Action."* Lakamsal, three parsecs spinward of the border, becomes strategically significant for the first time in its history.

**1116.350** BLIND COBRA crosses the Solomani border aboard a civilian tramp freighter using false manifests bound for the Lakamsal downport. Five to six operators, depending on convention table size: Major Vasiliou (commanding), Technician Okonkwo, Agent Breckenridge, Technician Haramoto, Sergeant Mouton, and — if fielded — Field Medic/Xenobiologist Kowalski, plus Senior Analyst Pelczar (NPC signals analyst, not a player character). They rent two ground vehicles from a colonist in Lakamsal Town — "geological survey work, three weeks" — and drive into the Kethara Reach.

**1116.353 — Day 1 of operation** BLIND COBRA establishes Field Station Kethara, codename BLIND COBRA, inside a basalt cliff overhang above the Kethara River. Station construction takes 36 hours. Surveillance drone deployment begins. The fixed-wing drone begins its first pass over IRON GATE.

**1116.359 — Day 7** TSU-9 thermal spoofing unit shows first signs of elevated load. Haramoto logs it as a monitoring item. Jury-rig options identified. No action taken.

**1117.001 — Day 14** Dr. Kwame Osei-Bonsu (IISS, Lakamsal station) logs an anomalous biotelemetry alert from the Kethara Reach. One of his tagged Kethara Tyrant individuals (KR-ALPHA-7) has moved further into the basin than usual. He notes it for his quarterly report. He does not investigate.

**1117.004 — Day 17** TSU-9 load reaches 130%. Haramoto recommends jury-rig or crash site parts retrieval. Vasiliou approves monitoring only — the surveillance run is 70% complete.

**1117.007 — Day 20** Pelczar identifies a pattern change in IRON GATE's encrypted traffic. Volume up. Encryption strength up. Burst schedule gone. She begins continuous analysis. She has not slept more than four hours at a stretch since.

**1117.009 — Day 22** Shulgi & Enki Vehicle Hire contacts the Lakamsal Town council to report two overdue rental vehicles. Owner Dara Vasquez files a standard missing vehicle report. Meridian Recovery Solutions (Investigators Chen and Kaur) are contracted to locate and recover the vehicles.

**1117.010 — Day 23 / Hour 0** *Scenario begins.* TSU-9 at 140% load. Surveillance drone on Hour 4 of its final 6-hour pass, 280km northeast. Pelczar is three decryption passes from something important. Extraction is 72 hours away. Investigator Chen filed a preliminary report with the Lakamsal Town council before departing this morning. IRON GATE continues transmitting.

| 4–6 | Drone passes begin; TSU-9 degrading; crash site retrieval decision forced |
| 6–8 | Apex predator arrives; TSU-9 failing; Imperial passes intensify; beetles possible |
| 8+ | Resolution window; extraction delay reveal; Meridian recovery team inbound (Hours 18–24) |

The scenario is designed to stack pressure, not sequence it cleanly. By Hour 6, the referee should have at minimum two active problems running simultaneously. By Hour 8, three is the target. The players' choices about which problem to address first shape which problems compound.

### The Countdown Card

Print or handwrite this card and place it in the centre of the table at scenario start:

```

│         BLIND COBRA — SITUATION STATUS         │

│  FAILED:   161%+        │                       │
├─────────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│  ECM FAILURES LOGGED    │  □ □  (2 = Marines)   │
├─────────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤

└─────────────────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

Update the TSU-9 load whenever it changes. Mark ECM failures as they occur. This single visual element transforms the scenario from abstract to tangible — players can see the pressure building in real time.

## 1.11 — PHYSICAL TABLE SETUP

### Character Reference Cards

Each player needs a single card showing:
- Character name and role
- 3–4 key skills (the ones they will use)
- Key equipment
- One-line personality note
- Their relationship to the group's secrets (optional — can be held back)

These are simpler than full character sheets and faster for convention play. The full stat blocks in Chapter 2 are referee reference — players rarely need them.

### The TSU-9 Load Dial

For groups that enjoy physical props: a printed circular dial (10cm diameter) with the load percentages marked in amber (101–140%) and red (141–160%+). A split-pin fastener allows the indicator arrow to move as repairs and damage change the load. This is the most memorable physical element the scenario has produced at playtesting tables.

## 1.13 — POST-SESSION NOTES

### What to Leave Players With

At session close, the team should have:
- The data package transmitted (or a clear understanding of why it wasn't)
- SHEPHERD's delay acknowledged
- At least one open question about what Pelczar actually found

The best sessions end with a player asking: *"So what was actually in Partition Three?"* The correct answer is: *"That's the campaign."*

### The One Thing Referees Get Wrong

The most common referee error in playtesting: **treating the Tyrant as a monster encounter rather than an environmental hazard.** The Tyrant is not the enemy. It is a 15-tonne animal that has found a warm cliff in its territory and is investigating. Players who are given the choice between "fight the dinosaur" and "manage the dinosaur without triggering a thermal spike that exposes the base" make dramatically more interesting decisions. Frame it correctly and the Tyrant encounter becomes the scenario's best scene.

The second most common error: **pausing the Imperial sweep clock while players discuss the Tyrant.** In the scenario, time is continuous. While the players are deciding what to do about the predator, the next ECM pass is ticking closer. The referee should note this — *"You have about four minutes before the next drone pass window"* — without resolving it for them.
