# TRAVELLER: OPERATION ASHFALL
## Chapter 3 — Act One: City of Static

*Referee Reference Document*

---

**Objectives:** Insert into Meridian, the naval capital of Ys; confirm the control bunker's location and condition; secure a vehicle rated to survive the Glass Reach crossing.
**Countdown:** Starts at 0. Expect 2–3 marks by the end of this Act (see Chapter 1, §1.5).
**Tone:** Not combat-first. This Act runs on tension, choices, and a city that doesn't know it's already dead. Save the fighting for Acts Two and Three.

---

## 3.1 — INSERTION

Team UNDERTOW comes down twelve hours before H-Hour under full Confederation colors — no cover story to maintain, no cover to blow. They're openly what they are: a Solomani Confederation military cell, flag and Party emblem visible on their transport and their Battle Dress, holed up in a rented harbor-district warehouse while they wait and watch. Everyone who sees them arrive knows exactly what they're looking at. That's the point (Chapter 1, §1.6).

The referee should open the Act with the team already in the warehouse, already watching the city come apart on captured comm feeds, already past the point where waiting is a strategy. Kade's first call isn't whether to hide — it's how loudly to announce themselves. Moving through Meridian in full Battle Dress under the flag (fast, armored, unmistakably Solomani — a real statement to a city that's been waiting for one) is a fundamentally different choice than moving in civilian layers with the flag folded away (slower, quieter, saving the statement for when it matters more). Both choices are legitimate and both matter for the rest of the Act.

**Referee note:** the mechanical question in Act One was never really about being *seen* — it's about how fast word travels and how fast someone with authority to respond actually mobilizes (Appendix C tracks this directly). If the table wants a mechanical hook, a Streetwise or Persuade 8+ check gets the team through a checkpoint on reputation and nerve rather than force — a nervous militia checkpoint that waves six people under Confederation colors through *because* nobody wants to be the one who starts something with them tonight is more interesting than combat here. A failure doesn't need to be a firefight either — it can just mean word gets back to Vantry's office faster than the team would like.

## 3.2 — MERIDIAN IN REVOLT

Meridian is a naval company town — three million people, most of them dockworkers, engineers, and naval dependents, built up over a century around the shipyards that make Ys valuable enough to kill for. It is currently in a state nobody on either side has a word for: not battle, not peace, not quite a city that has thrown off its government, because the government is still very much present and very much armed.

- **The Yards** are contested — loyalist Marines hold the drydocks and the highport shuttle field; dockworker militia hold the surrounding warehouses and cargo rail.
- **Old Meridian**, the residential core, is functionally ungoverned — checkpoints exist, run by whoever got there first, and change hands without much warning.
- **The Boulevard**, Meridian's central plaza, is dominated by the naval administration building — Meridian's one unmissable landmark, visible from most of the city. Two massive square-based towers rise from opposite ends of the plaza, windowless below the fortieth floor, and between them — bridging the gap, cantilevered out past the towers' own footprint on both sides — sits a vast rectangular block, giving the whole structure the unmistakable silhouette of a capital letter Pi. It reads as exactly what it is: Imperial administrative power, built to be seen from everywhere in the city it governs. This is where Governor Krein's staff are barricaded, and where any move against him would have to go through.

**Color palette for the water and shoreline (Act One, night):** Ys's thin, tainted atmosphere (Chapter 1, §1.3) gives even a clear night sky a faint sickly cast — not enough to notice in daylight, but enough that Meridian Sound at night reads as flat, oily black rather than any true blue, broken only by reflected light: hard orange-white glare where fires are burning in the contested districts, cold blue-white from naval floodlights still running on the drydocks, a thin sodium-amber line along the working shoreline where streetlights haven't been cut yet. The shore itself is industrial, not scenic — dark wet concrete and slag-black rock revetment, no sand, slicked with a permanent film of fuel and ballast runoff that catches every light source in long, smeared streaks rather than clean reflections. It should never read as beautiful. It should read as a working harbor in the middle of the worst night of its life.

None of this is where the team needs to be. What they need — the control bunker's exact status, and a vehicle that can survive the Reach — is going to come from people, not buildings, and the biggest gathering of people in the city tonight is not at any of the above.

## 3.3 — THE DOCKYARD SCREENING

At the edge of the contested Yards, in a cargo warehouse the dockworker militia captured six hours ago and immediately turned into something closer to a town square, someone has hooked a salvaged naval recruitment display — normally used for shift schedules and enlistment ads — into the building's old projection rig. Word went out over the same captured comm channel the uprising used to coordinate this morning:

> *"Tonight. The old warehouse on Slip Nine. We're screening Battleship Potemkin. Everyone's invited — dock crews, off-shift ratings, families, doesn't matter what side you were on yesterday. Bring who you love."*

It is, on paper, absurd — a century-old film about a mutinous crew, playing to a crowd that is currently in the middle of an actual mutiny, on a night when a significant fraction of the audience doesn't yet know their government has decided they're expendable. It is also, on the ground, one of the only unambiguously joyful things happening on Ys tonight. Loyalist ratings who slipped away from post stand next to militia organizers who were shooting at each other's cousins yesterday. Someone is handing out real food. A woman with a naval engineer's rank patch still on her jacket is crying during the Odessa Steps sequence and nobody around her is treating it as strange.

**This scene exists to hurt.** The referee's job here is not to stage a firefight — it's to let the players sit, briefly, inside the version of Ys that could have existed if Krein had made a different choice ninety minutes ago, before the team has to go back out into the version where he didn't.

### Running the Screening

The warehouse holds a few hundred people loosely clustered around the projection, drink and food circulating, a rotating cast of speakers taking a platform between reels to talk about what happens next — none of it coordinated, all of it sincere. Team UNDERTOW can move through this in Battle Dress (draws immediate, wary attention — treat as Amber, not Red; nobody wants to start something on a night like this, but nobody relaxes either) or in civilian layers (draws none, until someone asks a question the team can't convincingly answer).

**What the team can get here, in roughly ascending order of difficulty:**

| Goal | Approach | Result |
|---|---|---|
| General mood, confirm the uprising has no central command | Just listen | Free — no roll needed |
| A name: who might know the bunker's current status | Streetwise or Persuade 6+ | Points to **Imre Basaran**, a retired naval systems tech working dock security tonight |
| Basaran's cooperation | Persuade 8+, or Achebe treating a minor injury for free first | Basaran talks — see below |
| A lead on Reach-rated transport | Streetwise 8+ or a Contact roll | Points to the impound yard, §3.4 |
| Avoiding a scene entirely | Passive, background presence | Possible in civilian layers; near-impossible in Battle Dress past the halfway mark of the reel |

**Imre Basaran** spent nine years as a junior systems technician on the original equatorial test site — the installation that became the Glass Reach thirty-one years ago. He was seventeen kilometers away when it went wrong and has never fully explained, even to himself, why he stayed in naval service afterward. He is at the screening because his granddaughter dragged him, and he is visibly uncomfortable being there at all.

If the team earns his trust — and Achebe's medic skills, not Kade's authority, are the fastest way there — Basaran confirms:
- The old test bunker was never fully decommissioned, just sealed and repurposed.
- He doesn't have current access codes, but he knows the physical route in, and he knows the bunker's original fail-safes were *analog*, deliberately kept off any network Krein's office could touch remotely — which is both the team's best chance at a manual override and the reason nobody can shut Ashfall down from a console somewhere.
- He has a nephew who still works motor pool at the naval logistics depot. If asked, and only if the team hasn't already found the vehicle lead themselves, he'll point them there — reluctantly, aware of exactly what he's risking his family for.

**If the players don't work the room:** the Act still moves — Reyes can find the vehicle lead independently (§3.4), and the bunker's rough location was always inferable from Chapter 1's naval seismic data. Basaran's information *upgrades* the crossing and the bunker assault; it doesn't gate them. Losing this scene costs the team accuracy and foreknowledge, not the mission.

**The countdown ticks once, automatically, when the reel ends and the crowd starts to disperse** — the team has spent real time here, whatever they walked away with.

## 3.4 — SECURING THE VEHICLE

The naval logistics depot on the Yards' northern edge — a converted cargo warehouse, high-bayed and cluttered with pallet racks, repurposed decades ago when the Navy needed covered storage close to the drydocks — is where Reach-rated equipment lives: armored haulers built to survive residual thermal and radiological load, normally reserved for the Navy's own environmental monitoring runs out to the crater. It is currently held by a skeleton loyalist detachment more worried about the Yards than their own motor pool, which is the team's opening.

**Two approaches, referee's choice which fits the table:**

- **Basaran's nephew (if earned in §3.3):** a night-shift dispatcher who can walk the team in on a forged maintenance order — no roll, but costs a scene of tension as loyalist NCOs eye the paperwork.
- **Cold infiltration:** Stealth 8+ to get past the perimeter, then Mechanic or Electronics 8+ to get a hauler powered up and moving before the skeleton crew notices. A failure here is not silent — it's a firefight against a handful of very surprised, very outgunned loyalist technicians, and Kade should get a hard look at whether that's a fight worth having on a night when every shot fired draws attention the team doesn't want.

*Full stats for the Reach-rated hauler — the team's primary vehicle for Act Two — are in Appendix (vehicle specifications, to follow in a later chapter).*

**The countdown ticks a second time here** if the infiltration goes loud, or if the team spends more than one scene negotiating access.

## 3.5 — CLOSING ACT ONE

By the end of this Act, Team UNDERTOW should have:
- A vehicle capable of crossing the Glass Reach.
- A rough-to-precise fix on the control bunker (precise if Basaran talked; rough if not — either is playable).
- A first, personal look at exactly what's going to be lost if they fail — not an abstraction on a countdown clock, but the woman crying during the Odessa Steps sequence, the loyalist rating and the militia organizer standing shoulder to shoulder for two hours, the granddaughter who dragged her grandfather out to see something joyful on the worst night of his life.

Act Two — The Reach — picks up as the team leaves Meridian behind and crosses thirty-one years of glass and warning obelisks toward a bunker built to survive exactly the kind of thing that's coming for it again.
