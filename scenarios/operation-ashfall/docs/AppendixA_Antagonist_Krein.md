# TRAVELLER: OPERATION ASHFALL
## Appendix A — Antagonist: Governor Tobias Krein
*(Krein pronounced KRAYN, rhymes with "rain" — and, coincidentally or not, a cognate of "Kren"/"Chrain," the Austrian German, Czech, and Yiddish word for horseradish, borrowed from a shared Slavic root. A sharp, pungent name for an otherwise mild-mannered man.)*

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## A.1 — WHO IS HE?

Not General Malachek. Not an inept Sam-from-a-tech-company type either. Krein is a third thing, and it's the thing that makes him work as an antagonist: **he is a competent, risk-averse status-quo administrator who has spent eleven years managing decline instead of confronting it, and it has finally caught up with him at the worst possible scale.**

He did not seize power. He did not want a naval base full of nine billion restless citizens to become his legacy. He was posted to Ys because he was good at exactly this kind of assignment — a captive-government world with a chronic underground problem, the sort of job the Imperium hands to someone who won't do anything dramatic. And for eleven years, he didn't. He managed. He issued curfews and called them "temporary public safety measures." He expanded the perimeter fencing and called it "infrastructure modernization." He deepened the surveillance grid and called it "community security investment." He has never, in eleven years, called anything by the name a person living under it would use. That's not a character flaw he's hiding — it's his entire governing method, and it worked, in the narrow sense that nothing got dramatically worse *on his watch*, right up until tonight.

**Play him as sincere.** He believes the platitudes when he says them. He believes — believed, until about two hours ago — that Ys was fundamentally stable, that the resistance was a manageable irritant, that one more curfew extension would buy another quiet year. When the uprising broke simultaneously in seven cities, it didn't just threaten the base. It proved, all at once and irrefutably, that his entire eleven-year theory of the situation had been wrong. Ashfall wasn't a plan he'd been holding in reserve, ready to use. It was a contingency he inherited, briefed on once, and never expected to need — which is exactly why, when the moment came, he reached for the only tool left that felt like it was still within his control.

He is not enjoying this. He is not righteous about it. He is a frightened, cornered man who has convinced himself that killing nine billion people quickly is more merciful than losing them slowly to a war he's now certain is coming regardless — and he has not examined that logic closely enough to notice it's wrong, because examining it closely enough would require admitting eleven years of "temporary measures" built the conditions for tonight in the first place.

**Do not let players talk him out of it with a good speech.** He can be stopped. He cannot be reasoned with — not because he's a monster, but because reasoning was never how he got here, and it isn't going to be how he leaves. The team's only real path is the bunker, not the man.

## A.3 — EXPERT-LEVEL SKILLS

*What Krein is actually excellent at — the specific, narrow competencies of a long-serving colonial administrator. Notice what's absent: nothing here is technical, tactical, or operational. He did not build Ashfall, does not understand it in engineering terms, and has never needed to. He has activation authority. He does not have a shutdown plan, because a shutdown plan implies the contingency was ever meant to be survivable for him to walk back.*

| Skill | Lvl | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership | 4 | Keeps a fracturing loyalist garrison functionally obedient under impossible pressure |
| Diplomat | 3 | Eleven years of managing Imperial oversight, naval liaison, and underground back-channels without ever conceding ground |
| Admin | 3 → 4 (situational, with Leadership synergy) | Bureaucratic control of a captive-government world; knows exactly which levers produce compliance without triggering revolt — until tonight |
| Advocate | 3 | Can justify almost any policy in language that sounds reasonable, including this one, to himself first |
| Streetwise | 1 | Weak — he has never had to operate outside institutional channels, and it shows |

## A.4 — EQUIPMENT

- **Governor's authorization codex** — a physical, non-networked authenticator; the only thing separating Ashfall's dead-man activation from an ordinary comms broadcast. Krein does not carry it into danger. It remains in the naval administration building on the Boulevard under his personal guard, which makes it a possible (referee's option, not required) secondary objective if the table wants an alternative to a straight bunker assault: capturing the codex could theoretically buy time or complicate the network's fail-deadly logic, but it does not disarm Ashfall outright — the arrays are already armed and running on their own clock regardless of what happens to the codex now.
- **Hardened personal comm suite** — what he used for the H-Hour broadcast; still active, still capable of reaching the whole planet if he chooses to speak again.
- **No personal weapon of note.** He has never needed one and it would not help him now.
- **No armor.** Just his administrator's coat — consistent with a man whose whole profile (§A.2) says he was never built for a fight and has no illusions about surviving one if it comes to that.
- **A battered leather attaché case, carried everywhere, opened rarely.** Inside: nothing operationally useful — old handwritten drafts of curfew extensions, a photo he's never shown staff, and a small sealed crock of homemade horseradish relish, a recipe he's kept since before Ys, that he eats a spoonful of when he's alone and it's bad. It's the one genuinely private thing about him. The case — and faintly, his whole office — carries the smell: sharp, unmistakable, and a quiet, unplanned echo of his own name (see the pronunciation note, top of this appendix). Referees can use the smell as a sensory beat the moment a team searches his office or the case itself — it should read as a real, human detail, not a joke at his expense.
- **Close Protection Detail** — see §A.5.

## A.6 — THE PUBLIC INFORMATION NETWORK

Every naval-administered world of this tech level runs some version of an automated public-messaging system — scheduled announcements, curfew reminders, civic morale content, all scripted in advance and cycled by a low-grade administrative AI so nobody in Krein's office has to write new copy every day. It has been running on Ys, unmodified, for years. It is still running tonight, on autopilot, cycling the same reassuring rotation into a city that has moved a great deal further than the system's script bank anticipated. Read these aloud when the team passes a working public terminal, streetside speaker, or dockside display — the dissonance is the point.

> *"Public Information Network, cycle 4471. Curfew remains in effect from 2000 to 0600 local. Compliance keeps Meridian safe."*

> *"A reminder: perimeter fencing upgrades in the Old Meridian district are a modernization investment in your community's long-term security."*

> *"Governor Krein thanks all citizens for their continued patience during this period of enhanced civil monitoring. Together, we keep Ys stable."*

> *"Reported: minor disturbances in the harbor district. Authorities are responding with appropriate measured care. Please remain indoors."*

None of these have been updated since H-Hour. The system doesn't know the broadcast happened. Krein hasn't told it to stop.
