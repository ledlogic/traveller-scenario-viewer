**Government: Captive Government** — Ys is not self-ruled in any meaningful sense. Its planetary administration exists to keep the naval base, shipyards, and orbital defense grid running for the Imperium, not to represent the roughly nine billion people who live under it. A Solomani Party-aligned underground has operated here for a generation, and Imperial military rule has never been relaxed — officially because of that resistance movement, unofficially because Ys is too strategically valuable to ever risk losing. That tension is the entire scenario in one sentence: a world worth keeping is now a world someone is willing to destroy rather than lose.

### Why This World?

Ys sits deep on the Imperial side of the Gemini Gap, three jumps from the nearest Solomani-held system. It was never supposed to be a front line. Its naval base is old, permanent, and enormous — shipyards, a fleet logistics hub, deep-storage armories, and an orbital defense grid that has been quietly modernized for a century without ever firing a shot in anger. Nine billion people live here because the base needs them: dockworkers, engineers, naval families, three generations deep in some cases. A significant fraction of them are pure-blood or mixed-blood Solomani whose ancestors never left when the Imperium drew the border somewhere else. They have been second-class citizens under military law for decades, and the Confederation's Rebellion-era gains along the Front gave the underground here a reason to believe help might finally be close.

They were wrong about the timing. Reinforcements are not close. The uprising happened anyway — coordinated, simultaneous, in every major coastal city — because a year of Confederation victories elsewhere convinced local cells that Ys itself might already be slipping. It has not slipped. It has provoked the one response nobody planned for.

## 1.4 — THE SITUATION

**H-Hour minus 3:00.** Coordinated uprisings break out in all seven major coastal cities on Ys within a nineteen-minute window — too tight to be spontaneous, too broad to be a single cell. Naval dependents, dockworkers, and a hard core of off-duty ratings seize administrative buildings, port facilities, and (briefly) two orbital defense stations before Imperial Marines retake them.

**H-Hour minus 1:30.** Planetary Governor **Tobias Krein** *(KRAYN, rhymes with "rain")* — a career administrator, not a soldier, installed on Ys for eleven years — addresses the entire planet on every open channel. The broadcast is short, unscheduled, and clearly not sanctioned by anyone above him. He does not sound like a man giving an order. He sounds like a man who has already decided.

> *"You were told this world would never be allowed to fall. I am telling you now: it will not be allowed to be taken. Not by the Confederation. Not by you. I will not hand nine billion Imperial citizens and a fleet base to Solomani hands to be used against the Imperium that raised them. If Ys cannot be held, Ys will not be held by anyone."*

The feed cuts. Imperial Naval Intelligence has no record of what Krein means by "cannot be held" — because he isn't Navy. He's civil administration, and civil administrators on Captive Government worlds hold something the Navy doesn't advertise: the activation authority for the base's own denial contingency, installed decades ago against the day the Confederation actually broke through. It was never meant to be triggered by an internal uprising. It has been.

**H-Hour minus 0:20 and counting.** Naval seismic monitoring detects seven synchronized charge arrays arming beneath the naval base perimeter and the three largest coastal cities — the same cities currently full of Imperial loyalists, Solomani rebels, and everyone in between. Best estimate from the handful of engineers who understand the system at all: **under four hours** before the arrays are unrecoverable. After that, nothing your team does matters.

### The Ashfall Network

The charges are not naval ordnance and they were never meant to be defused by anyone — that's the point of a denial system. Imperial engineers built the network at TL15, three grades above Ys's own already-formidable TL12, specifically so nothing on-world could disarm it without Navy authorization codes that Governor Krein no longer intends to release. Seven arrays, each independently capable of scouring its host city and everything for a hundred kilometers around down to bedrock; wired together so that losing contact with three or more triggers the rest early rather than late. Fail-deadly, not fail-safe.

Nobody currently on Ys — not the uprising, not the loyalist garrison, not the Governor's own staff — has full technical authority to stop this cleanly. What exists instead is a single hardened control bunker, built into the wreck of an older, smaller test array from thirty years ago, that can still issue a manual override — if someone physically reaches it, survives what's guarding it, and executes the shutdown sequence before the network decides it's been compromised and detonates anyway.

### The Glass Reach

Thirty-one years ago, a single prototype charge from an earlier generation of this same denial doctrine malfunctioned during a routine systems test in the equatorial desert north of the capital. It did not fail small. What's left is **the Glass Reach** — an eleven-kilometer scar of fused, black-glass desert, still hot enough in places to register on thermal. Imperial engineers ringed it with something closer to a warning that doesn't need translating: a field of jagged glass spires, some standing fifteen metres tall, no two the same shape, deliberately irregular so nothing about them reads as architecture or ornament — only as wounds. They were cast from the crater's own fused glass, so the warning and the hazard are built from the same material. Beneath the spike field, at ground level, plainer markers carry pictograms and short text in nine languages, none of which say anything except *stay out*. The Navy quietly repurposed the crater's original control bunker — deep, hardened, and already wired into base infrastructure — as the hub for the modern seven-array network. It has been sealed, guarded, and functionally forgotten by everyone except the people who built Ashfall.

It is exactly where your team needs to go.

## 1.5 — THE COUNTDOWN

Operation Ashfall runs on a visible clock, not a hidden one — the players should feel every minute leave the table.

- Track a **10-box Countdown**, marked in the open where everyone can see it.
- Mark **1 box** automatically at the end of each scene.
- Mark **1 additional box** for a failed Average (8+) task with meaningful consequences, or when the referee judges the team has burned significant time (a long detour, a botched infiltration, a firefight that runs long).
- At **7 boxes**, secondary arrays begin arming early — the referee should introduce a hard complication (loyalist counter-attack, rebel faction demanding the team choose a side, a city-wide evacuation clogging every route).
- At **10 boxes**, the network goes fail-deadly. The scenario ends in failure — play it out, don't fudge it. SolSec does not get to lose quietly, and neither does Ys.

Reaching the control bunker does **not** stop the clock. Disarming the network is Act Three's climax, and it costs time of its own.

## 1.6 — TEAM UNDERTOW

A SolSec Heavy Response Cell, inserted covertly under a Solomani flag of convenience twelve hours before the uprising began — officially to monitor Imperial fleet movements, unofficially because someone in the Directorate suspected exactly this kind of contingency existed and wanted eyes nearby. They are the only Confederation asset on-world, they have no extraction guaranteed, and no one back home will admit they exist if this goes wrong.
