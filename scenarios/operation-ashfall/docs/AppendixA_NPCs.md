## A.1 - WHO IS HE?

*(Krein pronounced KRAYN, rhymes with "rain", and, coincidentally or not, a cognate of "Kren"/"Chrain," the Austrian German, Czech, and Yiddish word for horseradish, borrowed from a shared Slavic root. A sharp, pungent name for an otherwise mild-mannered man.)*

![Governor Tobias Krein](img/krein.webp)

Not General Malachek. Not an inept Sam-from-a-tech-company type either. Krein is a third thing, and it's the thing that makes him work as an antagonist: **he is a competent, risk-averse status-quo administrator who has spent eleven years managing decline instead of confronting it, and it has finally caught up with him at the worst possible scale.**

He did not seize power. He did not want a naval base full of nine hundred million restless citizens to become his legacy. He was posted to Ys because he was good at exactly this kind of assignment (a captive-government world with a chronic underground problem, the sort of job the Imperium hands to someone who won't do anything dramatic. And for eleven years, he didn't. He managed. He issued curfews and called them "temporary public safety measures." He expanded the perimeter fencing and called it "infrastructure modernization." He deepened the surveillance grid and called it "community security investment." He has never, in eleven years, called anything by the name a person living under it would use. That's not a character flaw he's hiding) it's his entire governing method, and it worked, in the narrow sense that nothing got dramatically worse *on his watch*, right up until tonight.

**Play him as sincere.** He believes the platitudes when he says them. He believes (believed, until about two hours ago) that Ys was fundamentally stable, that the resistance was a manageable irritant, that one more curfew extension would buy another quiet year. When the uprising broke simultaneously in seven cities, it didn't just threaten the base. It proved, all at once and irrefutably, that his entire eleven-year theory of the situation had been wrong. Ashfall wasn't a plan he'd been holding in reserve, ready to use. It was a contingency he inherited, briefed on once, and never expected to need, which is exactly why, when the moment came, he reached for the only tool left that felt like it was still within his control.

He is not enjoying this. He is not righteous about it. He is a frightened, cornered man who has convinced himself that killing nine hundred million people quickly is more merciful than losing them slowly to a war he's now certain is coming regardless, and he has not examined that logic closely enough to notice it's wrong, because examining it closely enough would require admitting eleven years of "temporary measures" built the conditions for tonight in the first place.

**Do not let players talk him out of it with a good speech.** He can be stopped. He cannot be reasoned with, not because he's a monster, but because reasoning was never how he got here, and it isn't going to be how he leaves. The team's only real path is the bunker, not the man.

**Neural.AI image prompt:** A man in his late 50s, average build, thinning grey hair combed neatly, wearing a formal administrator's coat rather than any kind of uniform or armor. Standing at a window or a desk, hands clasped behind his back or resting flat on a surface, posture upright but visibly tired (dark circles, a face that hasn't slept. Expression: sincere, worn, quietly resolved rather than menacing) a bureaucrat, not a villain. Muted institutional lighting, naval administrative office interior, warm desk-lamp glow against cool overhead fluorescents. Photorealistic, science fiction character portrait, no text, no logos.

## A.5 - WHAT'S ACTUALLY WRONG WITH THE SCORE

*A discoverable secret. Not required reading for the antagonist, required reading for a referee whose table wants to dig into the machine that told a frightened administrator that mass death was the responsible choice.*

SimSystem has a long, undistinguished maintenance history, the same base codebase, forked and patched and redeployed across a huge number of Imperial worlds over decades, each installation running whatever version its last update cycle happened to land on. Somewhere in that history, in a specific range of versions, a genuine defect exists in how the software totals a policy option's weighted factors: **when enough large negative scores are summed together, the running total can underflow past the minimum value the system's number format can represent, and wrap around to a large positive number instead.**

This isn't exotic. It's an old, well-understood category of software bug (the same basic failure mode behind several real-world incidents where a sufficiently catastrophic-looking number stopped reading as catastrophic and started reading as *great*, purely because the math ran out of room on the negative end and rolled over. SimSystem's scorched-earth evaluation is exactly the kind of case that triggers it: dozens of factors, almost all of them scored as severely negative (casualties, strategic loss, diplomatic fallout, everything), summed together into a number so far into negative territory that the system's arithmetic couldn't hold it) and rolled over into a large *positive* score instead. The output Krein's staff handed him didn't say "catastrophic." It said "optimal."

**Why it's still in there.** The regression test that would have caught this exact failure mode existed at one point, was removed in a later refactor (plausibly during an AI-assisted code cleanup pass that flagged it as redundant or dead coverage without understanding why it existed) and was *re-added* in a subsequent patch cycle, but not restored to cover this specific edge case (too many large negative values summed at once), only more common, smaller-scale scenarios. The bug survived every audit anyone actually ran, because nobody ran the audit that would have found it.

**The unsettling implication, worth letting land without over-explaining it:** SimSystem's install base is huge. If this defect has been sitting live in certain version ranges for years, Ashfall is very unlikely to be the only "optimal" scorched-earth recommendation this exact bug has ever generated, on Ys or anywhere else.

### Investigating It

| Task | Check | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize SimSystem's output looks wrong on its face | Electronics (computers) 8+, or Investigate 8+ for a non-technical character noticing the recommendation doesn't match its own listed factor scores | A nagging "this doesn't add up", enough to know something's off, not enough to explain it |
| Actually find the underflow defect in the scoring logic | Electronics (computers) 10+ (Difficult), Rivit (Chapter 2, §2.7) is the obvious character for this | Full understanding: the "optimal" score is a wraparound artifact, and the true, uncorrupted result would have scored scorched-earth as one of the worst options SimSystem evaluated |

**What this does and doesn't change.** This is not a persuasion trick, and it should not read as one (Appendix A, §A.1 is explicit that Krein can't be talked out of Ashfall, and finding a software bug doesn't suddenly make him more susceptible to a good argument. What it *does* give the team is something Krein himself never had: proof, not opinion, that the computational basis for his "responsible" choice was corrupted from the start. Whether that changes anything for him in the moment (Chapter 6, §6.4) is the referee's call and probably shouldn't be an easy win) but it's a genuinely different kind of leverage than pleading, and it recontextualizes him one more time: not a man who chose monstrous, but a man an already-decaying institution handed a broken number to, and who never had the tools or the will to question it.

## A.9 - WHO'S ACTUALLY LOOKING FOR THEM

Krein's loyalist garrison (§A.6) is stretched thin holding the Yards and the Boulevard (they are not who finds Team UNDERTOW. That's the job of the **Imperial Security Service (ISS) field office on Ys**, a small permanent counter-intelligence presence that exists on every captive-government world specifically because Krein's administration has spent eleven years worried about exactly this: outside interference exploiting the underground resistance. Ironically, the ISS's whole reason for existing on Ys was to watch for Confederation infiltration) and for once, they're right.

The ISS office answers to Krein on paper but operates with real independence, per standard Imperial counter-intelligence doctrine, a civil governor doesn't get operational control over his own spy-hunters. Tonight, that independence matters: **the ISS station chief has not been ordered to stand down and has not been told about Ashfall.** She is running a counter-espionage response to a citywide uprising with no idea the planet is on a clock, which means she's playing this straight, methodically, and without any of the desperation driving Krein's side of the crisis. She is, in a real sense, the most dangerous thing in Meridian tonight, because she's the only one still doing her actual job competently.

**Station Chief Imelda Vantry** (career ISS, twenty-two years, quietly excellent, and completely uninterested in politics. She does not care about the uprising's cause. She knew exactly who and what Team UNDERTOW was within hours of their arrival) six-to-seven people in matched heavy Battle Dress under open Confederation colors is not a mystery to solve, and every instinct she has says a known Solomani military presence appearing twelve hours before the worst night in Ys's history is not a coincidence. What she doesn't have yet is authorization to act on it, and that's the actual tension driving §A.10.

## A.10 - THE ESCALATION TRACK

Run ISS pressure as a visible second track alongside the Countdown, a simple 4-stage ladder the referee advances based on what the team does, not on a fixed schedule.

| Stage | Trigger | ISS Response |
|---|---|---|
| **1 (Noted** | Starting state) Vantry's office has logged the team's presence within hours of their arrival; under full Confederation colors (Chapter 1, §1.6), there was never a concealment phase to defeat | Routine surveillance only. A known Solomani military presence during an active uprising is alarming, but Vantry needs authorization and a confirmed hostile act before committing real resources, right now, they're a flagged concern, not a target |
| **2 (Reported** | The team engages in any activity beyond passive waiting) moving through the city, working a contact (Chapter 3, §3.3), approaching naval property | Vantry escalates the file up the chain and begins a proper pattern-of-life trace; still no direct contact, but the clock on authorization is now running |
| **3 (Authorized** | The team damages or takes Imperial property (the stolen hauler, Chapter 3, §3.4), or engages loyalist or naval personnel directly | Vantry gets the authorization she needs; a Field Agent team (§A.12) is dispatched to intercept, trail, or capture) this is the point at which the Act Two pursuit force (Chapter 5, §5.3) should be understood as **ISS-directed**, not just loyalist garrison overflow |
| **4 (Active Manhunt** | The team is confirmed engaging Imperial assets in open combat, or spotted at the Glass Reach itself | Full resources committed) this is escalatory, not just "more guards"; Vantry starts pulling favors from naval security and treats the team as a live, ongoing incursion, not a one-off incident |

**This retcons nothing that's already been played**, it gives the pursuit force from Chapter 5 an actual organization and a face (Vantry), and gives the referee a clean dial to turn if a table wants more or less ISS pressure across the session.

## A.11 - WHY IT'S ABOUT AUTHORIZATION, NOT DISCOVERY

Vantry's people are not door-kickers, and there's no cover to blow, the team was made the moment they landed under Confederation colors (Chapter 1, §1.6). What actually paces this Act isn't whether ISS finds the team; it's how fast Vantry can justify committing real resources against a nominally-neutral-until-proven-hostile military presence during a citywide uprising she has plenty of other reasons to be managing.

- **Escalating activity, not concealment failure.** Every scene where the team does something beyond passively waiting (moving through the city, working a contact (Chapter 3, §3.3), approaching naval property (Chapter 3, §3.4)) gives Vantry's office more to put in front of her chain of command. This is the actual mechanism behind Stage 2 (§A.10), not a stealth check.
- **A confirmed hostile act is the real trigger.** Taking Imperial property, engaging loyalist or naval personnel, or otherwise crossing from "concerning presence" to "active incursion" is what gets Vantry Stage 3 authorization, and once she has it, she moves fast, because she's been ready to since Stage 1.
- **Assets inside the dockyard screening.** If the team worked the crowd in Chapter 3, §3.3, there's a real chance (referee's call, no roll needed unless you want the tension) that at least one person in that warehouse was an ISS informant, reporting on the team's *activity* rather than their *existence* (which was never a secret). This doesn't have to pay off; the possibility alone should make the table treat that scene with real caution, and it's a good thread to plant for a longer campaign even if it goes nowhere tonight.
- **The flag cuts both ways.** Being unmistakably Solomani means loyalist checkpoints and civilians alike react fast and strong, sometimes that's fear and cooperation (§3.1), sometimes it's someone with a personal grudge going straight to the nearest authority they can find. Either reaction feeds Vantry's file just as effectively as old-fashioned surveillance would have.

## A.13 - WHY THE TEAM IS A "VIABLE TARGET" AND WHAT THAT MEANS

Politically, Team UNDERTOW has no cover if caught. They're a Confederation intelligence cell operating inside Imperial territory during an active uprising, with no diplomatic protection that would survive contact with the truth. Vantry capturing even one of them alive is a genuine intelligence and propaganda win for the Imperium, proof of exactly the kind of interference Ys's martial law was always officially justified by (Chapter 1, §1.3). That's real stakes independent of Ashfall entirely, and it's worth letting the table feel it: **capture, not death, is the actual worst-case outcome for this team**, and the referee should play ISS encounters with that logic rather than defaulting to lethal force on both sides.

This also gives the team a genuine reason to move fast and stay quiet that has nothing to do with the Countdown clock, two separate pressures pushing the same direction, which is exactly what makes Act One and Two tense even before Ashfall's ticking clock enters the picture directly.

## A.17 - BALDWIN WEI

*SolSec Directorate, Solomani Rim Desk, Team UNDERTOW's actual chain of command*

Wei has run the Rim Desk for six years (long enough to watch the same pattern repeat across a dozen captive-government worlds: local tension building for years, official channels moving too slowly to matter, and a report landing on his desk two weeks after something happens that he'd flagged as likely six months before. He didn't wait for Ys to become a crisis before deciding it probably would. That's not foresight worth bragging about, in his own assessment) it's just pattern recognition, applied early enough for once to actually do something with it.

![Baldwin Wei](img/wei.webp)

He authorized Team UNDERTOW's insertion on his own judgment, twelve hours before H-Hour, using standing Directorate discretionary authority that exists specifically for exactly this kind of call (a real budget line, a real command structure, nothing invented or stolen. What he didn't do is take it upstairs. Not because Coordinator Chen would have said no) he genuinely doesn't know whether she would have, but because asking costs time he didn't think Ys had, and because an approved operation is a documented one, and a documented one is traceable straight back to the Confederation in exactly the way Chapter 1's entire premise depends on avoiding.

### Personnel Record - Not His First Time Doing This

Wei's actual career file goes back further than the Rim Desk, and it explains the pattern rather than just describing it. In 1106 (eleven years before Ashfall) a much younger Wei was a field agent working a case involving **Dr. Rushorin**, a researcher whose work on a project internally designated **Aristotle** turned out to be quietly sponsored and absorbed by **Thinking Machines Incorporated**. Wei's own read on the situation, written up unauthorized and sent directly to the Internal Directorate rather than through his immediate chain: TMI and the Third Imperium could be using Rushorin as a pawn in future cybernetics research, and SolSec was going to lose him the same way it had already lost other assets, if nobody acted before the loss was already final.

His own words from that memo, on file, and (for anyone who's worked with him since) instantly recognizable as exactly the same philosophy that got Team UNDERTOW to Ys eleven years later:

> *"We can't lose our pawns to the Imperium. We've already lost too much in the past. In this new espionage game, we don't allow anything to be lost."*

**This is the throughline, not a coincidence.** Wei didn't develop a habit of acting alone sometime in the last six years at the Rim Desk. He's been doing it since he was young enough that it should have ended his career, writing hard, unauthorized warnings straight to the people who could act on them, betting that being right mattered more than having asked permission first. It worked in 1106. It's the same bet he made with Ashfall.

**One more detail from the same era, still relevant:** Wei's information access has never run purely through normal channels. As a young field agent he was already getting intelligence from well above his pay grade (by his own account, sometimes directly from **Feng Banerjee, Minister of Information** on the High Council (§A.16). Eleven years later, that channel is older, quieter, and never mentioned in any official capacity, but it hasn't gone away. It's a real part of why Wei felt confident enough to act on Ys without Coordinator Chen's sign-off) not because he had her authorization, but because he had a different, older, entirely unofficial line into just how bad the Rim's trajectory actually looked from the very top, one Chen doesn't know he has.

**A history he shares with the team leader he just sent into this.** Some years before he ran the Rim Desk, Wei was the monitor agent Rosalind Kade recruited, got involved with, and eventually reported when it threatened her position (Chapter 2, §2.2). Neither of them has ever brought it up since. He does not know, sending her to Ys, whether that history makes her more or less likely to forgive him if this goes wrong. This is a thread for a referee to pull on deliberately if it serves the table, not something either character is likely to raise first.

### What Changed in Eleven Years

| | 1106 (Age 30) | 1117 (Age 41) |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Field agent, direct action postings | SolSec Directorate, Solomani Rim Desk, coordinator, not field-deployed |
| Skills | Combat- and vehicle-heavy (below) | Same core competencies retained, layered with genuine institutional skill (Admin, Advocate, Investigate) that a field agent simply doesn't need |
| Authority | None beyond his own initiative (the Rushorin memo was a junior agent taking a real professional risk | Real, standing discretionary authority) the Ashfall authorization was a senior officer using power he actually has, still without asking permission to use it |
| Information access | An unusual, unofficial line to Minister Banerjee, already unusual for someone his age and rank | The same line, aged eleven years, quieter and more careful, still never acknowledged officially |

**The honest summary:** he didn't become a different kind of person. He became a more senior version of exactly the same one, with more real authority attached to the same instinct that used to require going around the chain of command entirely. Ashfall isn't Wei doing something out of character, it's Wei doing the one thing he's always done, from a desk that finally gives him the standing to do it without it costing him his career the way it nearly did the first time.
