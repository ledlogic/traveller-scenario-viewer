# TRAVELLER: OPERATION ASHFALL
## Chapter 5 — Act Two: The Reach

*Referee Reference Document*

---

**Objectives:** Cross the Glass Reach in the Reach-rated hauler secured in Act One; reach the control bunker at the crater's center; survive both the radiation gradient (Chapter 4, §4.2) and anyone who noticed six people in Battle Dress leaving the city.
**Countdown:** Expect 2–3 more marks this Act (Chapter 1, §1.5). This is where the clock stops being abstract.
**Tone:** Escalating — a quiet, tense crossing that turns into a chase. The spike field is the Act's signature terrain, and it should get used, not just described.

---

## 5.1 — THE CROSSING

The hauler secured in Chapter 3 gets the team as far as the vehicle limit marked on the Chapter 4 map — the edge of the Hazard band, where the spike field starts in earnest. Past that point the ground is too broken and the spires too dense for anything with wheels or tracks; the rest is on foot, in Battle Dress, watching two clocks at once (the Countdown, and cumulative rads per Chapter 4, §4.2).

**Running the crossing:** this doesn't need to be a single roll. Break it into three or four short scenes — picking a route through the spires, a moment where the ground itself is the obstacle (glass slag that shatters underfoot, a spire canted at a bad angle over the only clear path), a point where the team has to decide how close to the crater core to cut it. An Average (8+) Recon or Survival check per scene keeps the team on a route that favors lower rad exposure; a failure doesn't need to be catastrophic, just costly — a longer route, a mark on the Countdown, a few more rads absorbed than the ideal path would have cost.

---

## 5.2 — USING THE SPIKE FIELD AS A DETERRENT

This is the part worth building the Act around. The spike field was engineered to keep people out — nothing says Team UNDERTOW can't use that same design against whoever's coming up behind them.

**The setup:** by this point in the Act, the referee should have a pursuit force in play — a loyalist patrol that clocked six people in Battle Dress leaving Meridian and radioed ahead, or a response team dispatched once Krein's staff notice the hauler is missing from the logistics depot (Chapter 3, §3.4). They're behind the team, they're faster on open ground, and they don't know the spike field's terrain the way Basaran (Chapter 3, §3.3) might have briefed the team on it, if that scene went well.

**What the team can actually do with the spikes:**

| Action | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Collapse a spire across a choke point | Explosives or Mechanic 8+ (Voss's specialty) | Blocks the narrowest route through that section of the field entirely — pursuers must detour, costing them time equivalent to one Countdown mark |
| Use standing spires as cover during a firefight | No roll — positioning | The irregular, oversized geometry (Chapter 4, §4.4) breaks line of sight in ways regular cover doesn't; treat as superior cover, and note that pursuers unfamiliar with the field are more exposed navigating it than the team, who've now crossed it once |
| Lure pursuers into a higher-rad section of the field | Recon or Deception 8+ | Loyalist troops without Battle Dress-grade Rad protection take the exposure the team's suits shrug off (Chapter 4, §4.2) — this is the sharpest version of "the deterrent was built for a reason," and the referee should let the irony land without the team needing to say it out loud |
| Navigate a genuine shortcut through the densest part of the field | Survival 10+ (Difficult) | Cuts real time off the crossing, but pushes exposure into the Hazard band's upper range — a straightforward risk/reward trade against the two clocks running in parallel |

**Referee note:** none of this requires the team to have a plan going in. If nobody suggests using the terrain, let the pursuit force close the distance and force a straight firefight instead — the spike field being usable this way is an opportunity the scenario offers, not a puzzle it demands. Voss and Kade are the most likely characters to think of it first (demolitions and tactics, respectively), and Rivit — if anyone asks him — will confidently, incorrectly claim the design is "based on a fortification pattern I recall from the Rule of Man" (Chapter 2, §2.7). It isn't. Let him say it anyway.

---

## 5.3 — THE PURSUIT

If the team doesn't lose the pursuit force entirely, run it as a handful of tense exchanges rather than a full tactical firefight — Ys doesn't have the loyalist manpower to spare a large force, and the referee should play any pursuers as under-strength, uncertain, and increasingly aware they're chasing six people through terrain built to kill anyone who enters it. A Reaction roll or two, colored by how the team's handled the spike field so far, is enough to resolve whether the pursuit peels off, gets bogged down, or has to be fought outright at the crater's edge — right before Act Three begins.

---

## 5.4 — AN ALTERNATIVE THREAT: THE REACH WARDENS

The pursuit force in §5.3 doesn't have to be the only thing in the spike field. Krein's administration — true to eleven years of managing problems by outsourcing them rather than solving them (Appendix A, §A.1) — has never trusted the loyalist garrison alone to keep people out of the Glass Reach. Officially, a contracted militia called the **Reach Wardens** patrols the perimeter to keep looters and rebel sympathizers from disappearing into the spike field. Unofficially, the Wardens run a quiet, tolerated salvage operation — pre-incident hardware, rare fused-glass formations, anything with resale value scavenged from the outer bands and sold through channels nobody in the naval administration officially acknowledges. Krein's office looks the other way because the Wardens are cheaper than a real garrison presence and because a government that already calls curfews "temporary safety measures" isn't about to call this arrangement what it is either.

**Use the Wardens instead of, or alongside, the ISS-directed pursuit force (§5.3)** if the table wants a different texture of threat — mercenary and transactional rather than institutional. They are not loyal to Krein, don't know or care about Ashfall, and are motivated entirely by pay and salvage rights. That makes them a genuinely different kind of encounter: **negotiable in a way ISS agents and Marines are not.**

|--|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| Score | 8 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 |
| DM | +0 | +0 | +0 | +0 | –1 | –1 |

**Skills:** Survival 2 · Recon 1 · Gun Combat 0 · Gun Combat (slug) 1 · Mechanic 1 · Streetwise 1
**Armour:** Cloth or scavenged Flak Jacket, mismatched and field-patched
**Weapon:** Shotgun (CRB22, Weapons table — practical, intimidating, not military-grade; the source PDF's damage column for this row was affected by the same text-extraction issue as other tables in this project, so confirm the exact damage code against your own copy rather than this document)
**Equipment:** Rad counters (better than the team might expect — they work this terrain constantly and know its dangers firsthand, unlike loyalist patrols), a working knowledge of safer paths through the spike field that the team does not have

**Running them:** the Wardens know the Glass Reach better than anyone else in this scenario, including the team — better even than Basaran's decades-old data (Chapter 3, §3.3), because they're out here weekly, not remembering it from thirty-one years ago. That expertise cuts both ways: they're a genuine hazard if hostile, but also the single best source of real-time route information in Act Two if the team can talk instead of fight.

**A Warden encounter can resolve several ways**, referee's call based on how the team approaches it:
- **Bribery or trade** (Persuade or Streetwise 8+, plus something worth offering — SolSec gear, information, or simple credits) buys safe passage and possibly a genuine shortcut through the field.
- **Intimidation** (Threk's specialty) works, but Wardens who back down don't forget it — a plausible seed for them reporting the encounter to whoever they think might pay for the information, complicating things later rather than resolving cleanly.
- **A fight** is winnable — six people in Improved Battle Dress (Appendix B) badly outclass a scavenger militia — but noisy, and noise draws the kind of attention the team is trying to avoid (Appendix C, §C.3).
- **Ignoring them entirely** is often the correct play if the team can manage it — Wardens patrol for salvage and looters, not military targets, and a team that stays out of their patrol routes may simply never trigger this encounter at all.



## 5.5 — CLOSING ACT TWO

By the time the team reaches the crater core and the bunker entrance, they should have:
- Crossed a landscape that was built, thirty-one years ago, specifically to stop people from doing exactly what they just did.
- Ideally, turned that same design against whoever came after them — proof the deterrent cuts both ways.
- Accumulated real rads and real time pressure, both trackable, both visible to the table.

Act Three — Ashfall — begins at the bunker entrance, with the manual override sequence and the network's own defenses standing between the team and a shutdown that still has to happen before the Countdown runs out.
