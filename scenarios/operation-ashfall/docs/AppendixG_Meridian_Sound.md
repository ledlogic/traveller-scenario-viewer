## G.1 — MERIDIAN SOUND

Meridian's naval base exists because of what it sits on: **Meridian Sound**, a vast tidal bay roughly the size of Lake Michigan — around 500km along its long axis, deep enough along the eastern shore for capital ship traffic without dredging, which is the entire reason the Imperium built a naval yard here a century ago instead of anywhere else on Ys. The Yards, the drydocks, and the Boulevard (Chapter 3, §3.2) all front onto it. This was always implicit in Meridian being a naval capital; this appendix just makes it explicit and playable.

The Pi Building — the naval administration tower where Krein's staff are barricaded (Chapter 3, §3.2) — is founded directly at the Sound's edge. Its two square towers rise from pylons driven into the shallows, and the cantilevered block bridging them at height hangs out over open water on the Sound-facing side, not just the plaza. That's not decorative — it was built that way for the same reason harbor cranes and loading gantries always are: direct water access to the base's lower levels for material and personnel transfer, without routing everything through the plaza-side entrance the Boulevard's crowds (and, tonight, the uprising) control.

**Visuals:** use the water/shoreline color palette established in Chapter 3, §3.2 — flat oily black water under Ys's tainted-atmosphere night sky, broken by fire-orange and floodlight blue-white reflections smeared rather than clean, dark wet concrete and slag-black rock at the waterline. This isn't a scenic bay; it should read as an industrial harbor in crisis even from the water looking up at the Pi Building's underside.

## G.2 — ASSAULTING FROM THE WATER

If the team wants an alternative to fighting through the Boulevard directly, the Sound-facing pylons offer a genuine, unconventional infiltration route: swim or pilot a small craft in under cover of the water, then climb the support pylons into the building's lower levels, bypassing the plaza entrance entirely.

**Why this is viable:** Improved Battle Dress (Appendix B, §B.2) is fully sealed and NBC-rated — functionally a diving suit as well as combat armour, with 12 hours of life support. Nothing about the suits stops the team from doing this.

**Why this is dangerous, beyond the obvious:** normally, Meridian Sound's harbor approaches are kept clear by naval sonic deterrent buoys — routine infrastructure, unglamorous, and, tonight, unstaffed and possibly untended, like everything else stretched thin by the uprising (Chapter 3, §3.2). That deterrent net is the only thing that normally keeps the Sound's native megafauna away from the harbor.

**Suggested check:** Athletics (endurance) or Vacc Suit 8+ per approach segment for the swim itself; treat a failed check as costing time (a Countdown-adjacent complication, if this scene is being run late enough that the clock matters) rather than immediate harm — the real danger of this route isn't the water, it's what's in it.

## G.3 — THE HALVETH

*Native Meridian Sound apex predator*

Ys's Garden-class biosphere (Chapter 1, §1.3) produces exactly the kind of large, successful native megafauna you'd expect from a world that's spent geological time uninterrupted by anything as inconvenient as a naval base. The Halveth is Meridian Sound's largest native predator — convergent with Old Earth's pliosaurs rather than the long-necked plesiosaur branch: short, thick neck, a massive crocodile-like head built almost entirely around the jaw, and four powerful paddle-like flippers driving fast, violent bursts of speed rather than sustained pursuit. Think Liopleurodon's build and hunting style, not the Loch-Ness silhouette — an ambush predator, not a stalker. It's squarely in CRB22's own **Large Sauropod** size category (the largest tier in the book's animal-size guide) — this is, structurally and by-the-book, the "huge dinosaur" the Sound actually contains, and the head-first ambush profile is exactly what should make an encounter feel like a real jump-scare rather than a slow reveal.

They are not naturally aggressive toward harbor traffic — the sonic deterrent net exists to keep them from *investigating* ship hulls and swimmers out of curiosity and territorial irritation, not because they hunt boats. Provoked, cornered, or simply hungry and encountering something person-sized in open water with no deterrent signal running, a Halveth is an entirely different problem.

**Stat block (CRB22 Animal Encounters format, Appendix, pp.86–88):**

*A scale note, since it's worth being upfront about: the real Liopleurodon this creature takes its build from was a genuinely large predator but not a leviathan — most confirmed adult specimens ran 5–7 metres, with the famous 25-metre figure from 1990s TV documentaries now understood to be a scaling error, not the actual animal. The Halveth is deliberately written bigger than that — this is an alien world's own megafauna, not a same-scale tribute — sized to CRB22's own largest official creature-size category rather than to the real Jurassic species. **Reference length: approximately 21 metres (69 ft) nose to tail** — comfortably inside CRB22's "Large Sauropod" tier, and on the same order as the largest real sauropod dinosaurs that tier is named after.*

**Skills:** Athletics (dexterity) 2 · Recon 1 · Survival 2
**Attacks:** Bite (the primary threat — a single strike from that jaw is the whole encounter); Ram (a secondary full-body strike used to stun or disorient before the follow-up bite, not a territorial display)
**Traits:** Amphibious · Large · Heightened Senses
**Behaviour:** Carnivore, Hunter — but see below

**Running a Halveth encounter:** this should read as a *natural hazard the team can avoid or de-escalate*, not a monster fight the scenario is pushing them toward. Ambush predators don't telegraph the way long-necked grazers do — there's no slow curious pass to notice coming. Instead, give the team environmental tells before contact: fish scattering en masse, a shadow crossing below that's gone before anyone can be sure they saw it, the sonar-adjacent hum of the (unstaffed) deterrent buoys they're not hearing. A Recon or Survival check lets a character read those signs and choose to hold still, change course, or get out of the water entirely before anything commits to an attack. If the team stays quiet, moves efficiently, and doesn't linger in open water, the Halveth is set dressing, not combat. If they panic, thrash, or spend too long presenting as an easy target, it's a genuine Large-category ambush threat that six people in Battle Dress can survive but shouldn't take lightly.

## G.4 — WHY USE THIS

This is a pure option, not a required beat — nothing in Chapters 1–6 assumes the team goes anywhere near the water. Use it if:
- The table wants a physical assault on Krein's building as an alternative to (or alongside) the comm-channel confrontation in Chapter 6, §6.4.
- You want one more genuinely tense, non-combat-default set piece before Act Three's climax — the Halveth encounter works best as tension and choice, not a forced fight.
- The players themselves suggest going in from the water, and you want a ready answer for "is there anything down there" instead of improvising cold.
