# TRAVELLER: OPERATION ASHFALL
## Chapter 6 — Act Three: Ashfall

*Referee Reference Document*

---

**Objectives:** Enter the control bunker; execute the manual override sequence; disarm (or fail to disarm) the Ashfall network before the Countdown reaches 10.
**Countdown:** This Act should open at 5–8 marks depending on how Acts One and Two went. If the table is at 10 already, see §6.6 — play the failure out, don't fudge it.
**Tone:** Claustrophobic and mechanical after two Acts of open terrain — tight corridors, old equipment, a system that was never meant to be shut down by anyone.

---

## 6.1 — THE BUNKER

The control bunker is the original test-site facility from thirty-one years ago, sealed and repurposed rather than rebuilt — which matters, because it means the team is walking into TL12-era Imperial military construction, not something built yesterday. Basaran's information (Chapter 3, §3.3) or the Level IV technical plaque (Chapter 4, §4.4) — whichever the team secured — gives them the physical layout in advance. Without either, the referee should still let the team map it through play; it costs time (a Countdown mark), not the mission.

**Layout, top to bottom:**

- **Entrance airlock** — sealed, requires either the physical override Basaran described or a breach (Voss's specialty; Explosives or Mechanic 10+, Difficult, given the door's age and the fact that it was built to resist exactly this).
- **Outer control room** — original test-site equipment, still functional, now slaved to the modern seven-array network as a manual override node. This is where the shutdown sequence actually happens (§6.2).
- **Deep level, sealed off** — where the original prototype charge detonated thirty-one years ago. Still hot, still off-limits, not relevant to the mission unless the referee wants a genuinely dangerous optional detour for a table that likes exploring. Nothing down there helps; a lot down there hurts.

---

## 6.2 — THE OVERRIDE SEQUENCE

Deliberately analog, per Chapter 1 (§1.4) — this was built off-network specifically so Krein's office couldn't touch it remotely, which is exactly why the team can touch it at all.

Run this as **three linked tasks**, not one big roll. Each can be attempted by any character with a plausible skill; the referee should let the table divide labor rather than funneling everything through one PC.

| Task | Check | Who's Suited | Failure Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Isolate the local node** from the wider seven-array network, so a shutdown here doesn't just trigger the fail-deadly response early | Electronics (computers) 10+ (Difficult) | Rivit is built for exactly this | Countdown mark; network begins arming secondary arrays early (see Chapter 1, §1.5, the 7-box threshold) |
| **Physically reset the array's arming state** — this is old hardware, and it wants hands on it, not a console | Engineer (any) or Mechanic 8+ | Voss | A jammed reset costs a Countdown mark and forces a second attempt |
| **Authenticate the shutdown** — the system still wants command-level confirmation, even offline. If the team captured Krein's authorization codex (Appendix A, §A.4) as a secondary objective, this step is automatic. Otherwise: | Persuade or Deception 10+ (talking the system's old command-verification protocol into accepting a workaround) or Electronics 12+ (Formidable — brute-forcing it) | Kade (Persuade) or Rivit (Electronics) | Failure doesn't end the mission — it costs another Countdown mark and the team has to try again |

**All three tasks can be worked in parallel** if the team splits up — a reasonable and encouraged choice given the clock. Completing all three ends the immediate threat: the local array stands down, and with it, the fail-deadly trigger that was going to bring the rest of the network with it.

---

## 6.3 — OPPOSITION AT THE BUNKER

Keep this small. Ys doesn't have spare loyalist manpower this deep into an uprising, and the scenario is stronger if Act Three's tension comes from the clock and the equipment, not a wall of enemies.

- **A skeleton technical detachment** (2–4 personnel, use the Loyalist Close Protection Detail block, Appendix A §A.5, reduced to Gun Combat 1) — present if the team was loud in Act Two, absent if they weren't.
- **The pursuit force from Act Two**, if it wasn't lost or turned back (Chapter 5, §5.3), catches up here for a final confrontation — this is the natural place for that thread to pay off if it's still live.

---

## 6.4 — THE GOVERNOR'S CALL

If the team is in the control room when Isolate the local node (§6.2) succeeds, Krein's office detects the interruption almost immediately — his own broadcast suite (Appendix A, §A.4) is still active. He calls the bunker directly. Play this as a short, uncomfortable scene, not a boss fight:

He is not enraged. He is not pleading. He genuinely believed — believes, even now — that this was the responsible choice, and he wants the team to understand that before they finish what they're doing, because being understood is the only thing left he can still ask for. He will not order them stopped by force from here; there's no one left to send. He also will not authorize the shutdown or hand over anything that helps.

**Do not let a good speech from the players change his mind.** He can be talked to. He cannot be talked out of it — that was true in Appendix A and it's still true here. What he'll actually give the team, if they engage him at all rather than cutting the channel, is confirmation of exactly what he did and why, which is closure, not leverage. Let the scene end on the team's terms — cutting him off, finishing the override anyway, or (if a player wants it) one line back at him before they do. It's the last human moment in the scenario, and it should feel like one.

---

## 6.5 — RESOLUTION

| Outcome | Condition | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Full success** | All three override tasks complete before Countdown hits 10 | The local array stands down cleanly; the isolation (if done first) prevents the rest of the network from noticing in time. Ashfall is stopped. Ys stays a planet. |
| **Costly success** | Override completes at Countdown 9–10, or the isolation task was skipped/failed | The array shuts down, but not before secondary arrays partially arm — a localized detonation hits the crater and immediate approach (nobody the team knows is there, but referees wanting stakes can note the pursuit force, if still present, doesn't make it out). Ys survives; Meridian does not fully believe it yet. |
| **Failure** | Countdown reaches 10 before override completes | Play it out honestly, per Chapter 1, §1.5. This does not have to be the table's last scene — a team that fails the shutdown can still try to get word out, warn Meridian, or simply survive what's coming. SolSec does not get a quiet loss and neither does Ys. |

---

## 6.6 — EPILOGUE

Whatever the outcome, close on Meridian, not on the bunker — the Act 1 dockyard screening (Chapter 3, §3.3) set up exactly this beat, and paying it off is the point of the whole scenario.

- **On full success:** the Public Information Network (Appendix A, §A.6) is still cycling its stale, oblivious rotation when the team gets back within range of a working terminal. Nobody's updated it. Nobody's going to, tonight. Basaran, if the team earned his trust, is still at the warehouse on Slip Nine — or wherever the crowd from the screening ended up — and finds out secondhand, the way everyone on Ys is about to.
- **On costly success or failure:** the same terminal is playing the same rotation. That's worse here, not better — let the silence between what the system is saying and what just happened do the work.
- **Loose threads for a longer campaign:** Krein's ultimate fate (arrest, flight, suicide, quiet Imperial cover-up — referee's choice, none of it needs resolving at the table); what SolSec Directorate does with the fact that a Confederation team just saved an Imperial naval world; whether Basaran, Imre's granddaughter, or anyone else from the screening becomes a recurring contact.
