# TRAVELLER: OPERATION ASHFALL
## Appendix L — Radiation: Quick Process Reference

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## L.1 — THE TWO NUMBERS

Every radiation situation in this scenario involves exactly two numbers, and the whole process is just subtracting one from the other:

1. **The environment's exposure rate** — how many rads/hour a given location is putting out (Chapter 4, §4.2's zone bands; the Glass Reach's Crater Core, for instance, runs ~600 rads/hr).
2. **The armor's Rad rating** — a flat number printed on the armor's stat line (Appendix B for Battle Dress: Rad 290; Type D Battle Dress: Rad 300).

**The rule, verbatim from your Core Rulebook:** "Some armour provides a measure of defence against radiation, as noted in the Rad column of the armour table. This amount is deducted from the rads a Traveller receives every time they are exposed to radiation." (CRB22, Armour section, immediately preceding the armour table.)

That's the entire mechanic. Rad rating is a flat subtraction, not a percentage, not a dice roll — pure arithmetic, every exposure.

---

## L.2 — THE MATH, STEP BY STEP

1. Determine the zone's exposure rate (Chapter 4, §4.2, or whatever the referee is improvising for a new location).
2. Subtract the character's worn armor's Rad rating.
3. Whatever's left (minimum 0 — exposure never goes negative) is what actually gets added to that character's **cumulative rads** for the exposure period.
4. Check the cumulative total against the Cumulative Radiation Effects table (Chapter 4, §4.2) to see if a new characteristic-loss threshold has been crossed.

**Worked example, reusing Chapter 4's own numbers for consistency:** the Crater Core runs ~600 rads/hr. A character in Improved Battle Dress (Rad 290) absorbs 600 − 290 = **310 rads per hour actually received**. That's not safe — it's *survivable for the length of a scene*, which is the entire design point of Appendix B's suits and Chapter 4's countdown-adjacent framing. A character in a Reach Warden's scavenged gear (no meaningful Rad rating — see §L.3) would take close to the full 600/hr, a very different and much worse proposition.

## L.4 — CUMULATIVE RADIATION EFFECTS (REPRINTED FOR CONVENIENCE)

Full context and the honesty note about this table's sourcing live in Chapter 4, §4.2 — reprinted here so this appendix is a complete standalone reference.

| Cumulative Rads | Effect |
|---|---|
| 50 or less | None |
| 51–200 | –1 to a physical characteristic, permanently |
| 201–400 | –2, permanently |
| 401–600 | –3, permanently |
| 601–800 | –4, permanently |
| 801+ | –5, permanently |

Track this openly per character, the same way the mission Countdown (Chapter 1, §1.5) is tracked openly — it's a second, quieter clock, and letting the players see both running is what makes an irradiated scene tense rather than arbitrary.
