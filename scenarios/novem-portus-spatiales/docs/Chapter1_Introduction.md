## 1.2 — THE SETUP (WHAT THE PLAYERS KNOW)

It is **1116**. On **Pankow** (2518), the one first-class starport in this corner of Magyar, the crew of the *Marginalia* is hired by **Dr. Anselm Baltic**, a celebrated specialist in legacy computing.

Thousands of programs called **Torvald Builds** circulate in Magyar: forks of a nine-Port control program written around 1090 by a survey scientist, Dr. Ilse Torvald, who died in a laboratory fire in 1094. Nearly all of them are junk. Baltic owns one of three known copies of the original fork, which developers call **the Big Splash**. He believes two of the three copies are corrupt or forged, and he does not know whether his is the good one.

The commission:

1. Carry his copy (the **Baltic build**) on a sealed portable core.
2. Visit the other two custodians, both in the Pankow system, and compare their builds against it, Port by Port. Both are air-gapped; no copies exist on any network.
3. Report which build is genuine, and acquire it if it can be acquired.

He pays Cr20,000 a head on signing, the same again on completion, and all port fees and fuel. The completion fee can rise at dinner (§4.1) and double at Kell (§4.5); the score sheet in Appendix G works out the totals.

## 1.3 — THE TRUTH

**The builds are not software.** In 1090 Torvald found that the crystalline flats of **Schaut** (2418) were alive: a single, planet-spanning silicon intelligence, kin to the natural chips of Cymbeline (Appendix D). She recorded its signalling and transcribed it as nine logic Ports. Run together, they are a **handshake**: the sequence by which one part of the mind greets another.

**Her employer mined it anyway.** *Ostrander Lithic*, the company that governs both Pankow and Schaut, has spent twenty-five years quarrying the flats and selling the product as premium processor substrate. Every cut was made into something that could feel it.

**And then moved it across the border.** Pankow is Imperial and always has been. Schaut was Imperial too, until 1112, when *Ostrander Lithic* and several other corporations began tilting its trade toward the Solomani Confederation, and then pressed its company government to follow. Schaut now answers to the Confederation. The switch put the dig beyond the reach of Imperial law, and of the Scout Service, whose Exploration branch makes contact with newly found sophonts and sometimes interdicts their worlds (TI:39).

**Torvald hid the handshake.** She built three copies and scattered the nine true ("native") Ports across them, three to a copy, filling the gaps with her own imitations. Then she forged one more: the ninth Port in the copy she kept herself is a convincing fake. The real ninth never left Schaut.

**Three authors, one of them not human.** Every Port carries a ref hash ID naming its author, and the registry lists all of them as Torvald under three identities: male, female and neuter. Torvald used the two human identities on her imitation Ports. The neuter identity is on every native Port, and it is the Lattice, signing in the only name it knew. The players spend two acts reading its handwriting before they meet it.

**The Big Splash.** Developers who know of the original fork call it the Big Splash, and most think the name is a joke about the 1090s fashion for splash screens. It isn't. It names what happens on Schaut when the nine are run together. Appendix A has the full detail.

**All three sets of hardware contain pieces of it.** The machines the builds run on use Schaut-grade chips. Those chips have been reaching for each other for decades, which is why the three builds have drifted apart.

**Baltic knows most of this.** He believes that whoever completes the handshake will be answered by a mind older than the Imperium, and he believes he alone deserves to be. He does not know about the forgery.

## 1.4 — DR. BALTIC'S THREE ACTS

| Act | Baltic is... | What he does | What the travellers see |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | **Patron** | Hires them, hands over his build, and keeps his yacht close enough to turn up wherever they go. | A generous, funny, learned man. He asks for progress a little too often. |
| Two | **Rival** | Once they prove each build holds three native Ports, he strips the sites behind them, takes back his build and voids the charter. | Dead custodians, gutted archives, and the knowledge that they did his finding for him. |
| Three | **Distraught** | Runs the handshake on Schaut with eight true Ports and one forgery. It works, and it is not what he wanted. | A broken man, a waking world, and a decision that is now theirs. |

Full statistics and playing notes are in Chapter 3.

## 1.6 — THE GROUND AT A GLANCE

Acts One and Two take place inside the **Pankow system**. Moving between sites takes hours and is played as a scene cut, with no piloting or astrogation rolls. The scenario has **one jump**: Pankow to Schaut, one parsec, at the end of Act Two.
