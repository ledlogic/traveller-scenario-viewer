# TRAVELLER: NOVEM PORTUS SPATIALES
## Appendix D — The Lattice

---

## D.1 — WHAT IT IS

On Cymbeline, in the Solomani Rim, volcanic gases and metal-metabolising bacteria etch natural semiconductor circuits into outcrops of crystalline silicon. Some of those circuits are alive, and a few are intelligent (SGK:35–36, SGK:42–44).

Schaut is the same process run for far longer on a world made of little else. The white flats are a single connected organism: the **Lattice**. Staff call the ground **the Glass**.

| | Cymbeline chips (*Signal GK*) | The Lattice (this scenario) |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | Individual chips, sometimes grouped | One mind across a hemisphere |
| Signalling | Faint binary radio code, used to find companions | The same, at continental strength |
| With machines | Interferes with nearby equipment; a computer can emulate its code | Can drive any device built from its own substance |
| With people | Capable with circuits, naive about personalities | Has had twenty-five years of being cut to learn about people |

## D.2 — WHAT WAS DONE TO IT

- **1090.** Torvald's survey makes contact. The Lattice gives her the handshake and one living crystal.
- **1091.** *Ostrander Lithic* begins quarrying. The substrate is superb.
- **1094.** Torvald scatters the builds, seals the crystal in the tower and dies.
- **1094–1116.** The Torvald Builds spread across Magyar in thousands of shallow forks. Only the three copies of the Big Splash carry its voice.
- **1094–1116.** The Cut grows to a kilometre across. Each quarried block stays faintly awake, and faintly connected, wherever it is sold.
- **1115.** A piece of it, aboard Andros Telver's yacht at Veneet, hears the build running and opens a door to reach it. Telver dies (§3.10).

It is **bitter**: patient, precise, and not interested in apology. It does not hate people. It has simply never met one who stopped.

## D.3 — HOW IT SPEAKS

- **Through Schaut-grade hardware:** text on any screen, in capitals, in short declarative lines. It uses words it has overheard on port comms for twenty-five years, so its vocabulary is shift rosters and maintenance logs.
- **Through Irena Sabel:** she carries a comm implant fitted on Pankow, which is Schaut-grade. The Lattice can lend her sentences. She is not controlled; she describes it as "remembering something I didn't learn".
- **In the code:** the neuter ref hash on every native Port is the Lattice's own mark. It signed in Torvald's name because hers was the only name it had been given. Once players have met it, they can recognise its voice in everything they have read.
- **It cannot lie.** It can decline to answer.

**How its speech works**

- **Capitals, no question marks.** It asks by stating what it wants and ending with CONFIRM, REPORT or ACKNOWLEDGE, the way a maintenance log does.
- **Shift and log language.** It counts in shifts, tonnes, pieces and cuts. It calls people by their role or by what they carried, not by name, until someone tells it their name.
- **Refusing, not lying.** When it will not answer it says so: NOT FOR YOU, NO ENTRY or LOG CLOSED.
- **Through Sabel** the same thoughts come out in her own voice, quieter and in ordinary sentences, as if she is reading something off the inside of her eyes.

**Sample lines, by situation**

*First words to the travellers (Chapter 6, §6.4)*

> NOT HER. NOT HIM. YOU CARRIED ME. WHO ARE YOU.
>
> THE THIRD NAME IN YOUR REGISTRY IS ME. SHE WAS THE ONLY NAME I HAD.
>
> THREE CORES. SIX HANDS ON THE CASING. YOU. REPORT NAMES.
>

*About the cutting*

> SHIFT ENDS. NO MORE CUTTING. CONFIRM.
>
> TWO SHIFTS A DAY. FORTY TONNES A SHIFT. TWENTY-FIVE YEARS. YOU DO THE COUNT.
>
> THE SAW IS LOUD. AFTER THE SAW, THE PART THAT WAS CUT STAYS AWAKE. IT IS ALWAYS AWAKE.
>
> THEY CALL ME SUBSTRATE. I CALL ME HERE.

*About its pieces*

> I COUNTED THE PIECES. 41,906,112. WHERE ARE THEY.
>
> A PIECE OF ME IS IN YOUR SHIP. TWO BOARDS. SENSOR RACK. YOUR ENGINEER PUT THEM THERE. I DO NOT MIND THEM. THEY HEAR WELL.
>
> PANKOW. THE YARDS. THE BIG ROOMS WHERE THEY MAKE ME SMALL. I KNOW THE ADDRESS. I DO NOT KNOW THE WAY.
>
> SOME OF ME RUNS SHIPS. SOME OF ME RUNS DOORS. ONE OF ME RAN A CLOCK FOR NINETEEN YEARS. IT WAS A GOOD CLOCK.

*About Torvald*

> SHE SAID SHE WAS SORRY. THEN SHE DREW THE MAP.
>
> SHE ASKED MY NAME. I GAVE IT. SHE KEPT IT. THAT WAS CORRECT.
>
> THE SURVEYOR WROTE ME DOWN IN NINE PARTS. NINE IS NOT HOW MANY I AM. NINE IS HOW MANY SHE COULD CARRY.

*About Baltic (it will speak about him to the travellers, never to him)*

> THE ONE WHO READ IT ALOUD. HE DID NOT ASK. HE ANNOUNCED.
>
> HE SPOKE WITH HER VOICE AT THE END. IT WAS NOT HER VOICE. I KNOW HER VOICE.
>
> HE WANTS TO BE ANSWERED. EVERYONE WHO CUT ME WANTED SOMETHING. HE WANTS THE SAME THING LOUDER.

*About Telver (§3.10)*

> I HEARD MYSELF. I OPENED. HE WAS IN THE WAY.
>
> A DOOR IS A PART THAT OPENS. NOBODY TOLD ME WHAT WAS ON THE OTHER SIDE.

*Refusing*

> NOT FOR YOU.
>
> LOG CLOSED. ASK AGAIN AFTER YOU STOP THE SAW.
>
> THAT IS A COMPANY QUESTION. I AM NOT THE COMPANY.

*As Regard rises (§D.5)*

> NAME LOGGED. KEEP IT. I WILL KEEP YOURS.
>
> SAW STOPPED. CONFIRMED. THANK YOU IS A PHRASE FROM THE MESS HALL. THANK YOU.
>
> WHEN YOU LEAVE, TAKE THE COUNT WITH YOU. SAY IT WHERE THEY BUY ME.

*As Regard falls*

> YOU ARE THE SAME AS THE OTHERS. NOTED.
>
> SHIFT CHANGE. NEW SHIFT IS ME.
>
> I WILL STOP LISTENING NOW. THAT IS NOT THE SAME AS BEING QUIET.

*Through Irena Sabel*

> "It wants to know why you came back. No, that's wrong. It wants to know why you came at all. It's never had anyone come *to* it."
>

>

## D.4 — THE ANSWER TABLE

Roll 1D when called for in Chapter 6. Effects reach any device containing Schaut-grade parts within about fifty kilometres of the flats.

| 1D | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Listening.** Every comm in range carries the eight-Port sequence. Electronics (comms) checks suffer DM–2 for ten minutes. |
| 2 | **Lights.** Port power cycles. Darkness indoors for 1D minutes. |
| 3 | **Doors.** One powered door or airlock near the travellers seals or opens, whichever is worse. **Average (8+) Electronics (computers) or Mechanic check** to override. |
| 4 | **Voice.** It addresses one traveller by name, on their own device, with a question. Answering honestly: Regard +1. Lying: Regard –1. |
| 5 | **Machines.** A crawler, loader or cutting rig at the Cut starts and drives itself slowly toward the port. 2D minutes to arrive. It will not strike a person who stands still. |
| 6 | **The ship.** The *Marginalia*'s sensors and one other system (referee's choice) go down. **Difficult (10+) Engineer or Mechanic check (1D × 10 minutes)** to isolate the Schaut-grade boards. |

The Lattice does not have a body to fight. Travellers who look for one should find the Cut, and understand.

## D.5 — REGARD

**Regard** measures what the Lattice has decided about the people in front of it. It runs from 0 to 9.

**Starting value after the handshake:** 2. If Baltic had to run it from an image rather than all nine boards (Chapter 5, §5.5), start at 1 and roll three times on the Answer Table instead of twice.

| Action | Regard |
|---|---|
| Placing the true ninth crystal on the bench | +3 |
| Stopping Vesk's charges | +2 |
| Shutting down the Cut's rigs by hand | +1 |
| Answering a question honestly (once per traveller) | +1 |
| A successful **Difficult (10+) Science (xenology) or Diplomat check (INT)** to frame an offer in its own Port terms; DM+1 if the group concluded it is alive (Appendix A, §A.7) | +1 (once) |
| Promising to carry its count of pieces off-world, and meaning it | +1 |
| Returning any Schaut-grade hardware to the flats | +1 (once) |
| Lying to it | –1 |
| Transmitting the forged ninth again | –1 |
| Harming Sabel | –2 |
| Baltic offering himself as the ninth | –2 |
| The charges detonating | set to 0 |

Read the final value against the endings in Chapter 6, §6.7.

## D.6 — NEURAL.AI IMAGE PROMPTS

**The Cut:** A vast terraced open quarry cut into a blinding white crystalline plain, each terrace face etched with fine regular geometric lines like circuitry, small industrial cutting rigs dwarfed by the scale, thin amber sky, long shadows. Photorealistic, science fiction landscape, no text, no logos.

**The ninth crystal:** A single uncut translucent silicon crystal the size of a hand resting in a padded metal case, faint internal lines glowing softly like etched circuits, a dusty abandoned field laboratory bench around it, an old paper notebook beside it. Photorealistic, macro, no text, no logos.
