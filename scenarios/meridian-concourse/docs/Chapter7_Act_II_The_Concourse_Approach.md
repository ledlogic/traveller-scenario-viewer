# CHAPTER 7, ACT II: THE CONCOURSE APPROACH

## 7.1: OVERVIEW

Meridian Field was three things stacked on top of each other, and the collapse layered them further: a surface **airfield** for winged atmospheric flyers, a mid-level **free-trade bazaar and arbitration concourse**, and a lower-level **corporate infrastructure deck** (power, data, and, before the war, light manufacturing). The party enters through the new sinkhole into the upper airfield level; the mainframes and the truth of Appendix A are at the bottom.

Run this as three descending zones. Zones One and Two are Act II (this chapter) and Zone Three is Act III (Chapter 8). Each has a distinct threat, tone, and pace.

## 7.2: ZONE ONE, THE FLYER GRAVEYARD (Surface / Airfield Level)

The sinkhole drops into what was once the open apron and hangar rows where atmospheric flyers parked and were serviced between runs. A century of dust has drifted in through cracks and old ventilation shafts, half-burying a graveyard of winged aircraft, commercial short-haul flyers, a few private craft, one partially intact *Heliarch* security gunship, all coated in red dust, many with hull sections torn open by the original fighting.

**Atmosphere here:** very thin, the same as the plateau surface above (vacc suits, per the plateau ruling in Chapter 2). This level was never fully pressurized even before the collapse, since it was an open apron, so suits stay on. Light is minimal: whatever daylight leaks through the new sinkhole opening, fading fast more than fifty metres in.

**The threat: shellworms.** See **Appendix C**. Colonies of large burrowing xenofauna have moved into the wreckage over the decades, using the flyers' hollow fuselages as protective shells the way a hermit crab uses a borrowed shell, hence the term the referee should use with players once they've seen one: *shell-squatters*. They are not intelligent, not hostile by nature, and mostly interested in the mineral content of the wreck-metal itself, which they slowly digest. They become dangerous when a party disturbs a fuselage a worm has claimed, or when a fight elsewhere in the zone drives several worms into a startled cluster.

**Set pieces:**
- **The Gunship.** The partially intact *Heliarch* security gunship is worth investigating, its weapon systems are long dead, but its flight recorder (if Solt or Kwan can get it running, Electronics (computers) 2+, or Mechanic if the case is pried open the hard way) has the last twenty minutes of *Heliarch* combat comms from Day 11, a direct, visceral piece of Appendix A's history delivered as recovered audio rather than exposition. It is also, unfortunately, currently occupied by the largest shellworm in the zone.
- **The Loading Ramp.** A drivable route down to Zone Two exists, a cargo ramp, dust-choked but structurally sound (Grit's read, Science (geology) 1+, confirms this), wide enough for Bracket to bring down heavy equipment if the party wants to try to recover anything large. This is the natural route for a "ship team stays behind, ground team advances with a hauler" split if the party divided after Chapter 6.
- **First Contact.** Somewhere in this zone, a still-functional Concourse maintenance terminal (dead, dust-covered, but with standby power) will react the first time any character tries to interface with it, a brief flicker, a single line of text (**"IDENTIFY."**), then silence. This is ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN noticing the party for the first time. Nothing happens immediately. Something will, later.

## 7.3: ZONE TWO, THE ARBITRATION CONCOURSE (Mid Level / Bazaar)

The heart of Meridian Field: three levels of shopfronts, brokerage stalls, customs offices, and, at the center, the **Arbitration Floor**, a wide atrium ringed by the terminals both companies used to file disputes. This zone is where the worst of the Day 8–11 fighting happened, and it shows: scorched walls, punched-through partitions, the skeletal remains of combat automata from both companies still lying where they fell, weapons long since dead or cannibalized.

**Atmosphere here:** this level *was* fully pressurized before the collapse, a proper sealed environment, not an open apron, and sections of it are, startlingly, still holding air twenty-five years later, kept alive by the same hidden geothermal taps that have kept ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN running. What nobody knows, including the referee's own NPCs, is which sections, how well, and for how much longer. This is deliberately not a settled fact the referee hands the party, it's the Safety Officer's whole job (see **Chapter 3, Petra Ilkovic**) to find out room by room, and the referee should run it that way: no blanket "the air here is fine." Every sealed door is a fresh Engineer (life support) check before anyone commits to cracking a helmet seal, and a pass doesn't mean *safe forever*, just *safe now, as far as she can tell*. Some sections will read clean. Some will read borderline, usable, but not somewhere to linger or exert. A few, especially nearer Zone Three's power infrastructure, should read actively wrong: a scrubber bank cycling stale air back in on itself, a slow trace-toxin leak nobody's traced yet, a pressure differential that means something on the other side of a door is either venting or about to. Where a section *is* holding good air, it is cold, it smells of ozone and old dust, and, once Ilkovic has cleared it, it lets characters work without a sealed helmet, a small, earned mercy the referee can use to vary the tension. Either way, the fact that anything down here is still being maintained at all, twenty-five years on, is the real discovery: *something* is still spending real power keeping select rooms livable, on purpose, and neither Grit's read nor Ilkovic's tells the party why. (They will find out, see Chapter 8, Zone Three, and Appendix B.)

**The threat: automated response.** ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN, now aware of the intrusion, begin actively responding. Per Appendix A, they cooperate against the party as a shared threat. Expect:
- **Salvaged sentry drones** (Chapter 5), a handful of surviving/rebuilt security automata from both companies' original inventories, some patched together from both companies' parts, an uncomfortable visual detail for Bracket's player in particular.
- **Environmental pressure**, doors that seal unexpectedly, lighting that guides (or misdirects) the party, life support that can be locally vented as a threat rather than a weapon (the mainframes would rather capture/study an intruder than kill one outright, see Appendix B, so expect non-lethal pressure before lethal force).
- **The recovered evidence.** Scattered through brokerage stalls and customs offices are salvageable fragments of the original arbitration filings, physical backup data-slates, mostly corrupted, a few intact. Draeger's Advocate skill and Solt's Investigate/Electronics skill are the tools for piecing together Appendix A's history from these, in-world, rather than the referee simply telling the players. Budget three to five discoverable fragments, each revealing one beat of Appendix A (the arbitration charter, the drone collisions, the K-S self-destruct doctrine, the joint settlement, the sealing).

The zone's centrepiece, the Arbitration Floor itself, is the opening scene of Act III (Chapter 8).

## 7.4: THE DUST, VALLEY FEVER (Hazard)

Palin is a desert world, and the Concourse has spent twenty-five years filling with its dust. Some of that dust carries the spores of a soil fungus, and breathing them in causes **valley fever**, a lung infection modelled on the real coccidioidomycosis. The Core Rulebook says Travellers are unaffected by a world's native pathogens, with exceptions that are mostly mutated or engineered variants of Earth diseases [CR p.80]. So here the fungus is an Earth-origin soil fungus that came to Palin with the first colonists' agricultural soil and thrived in the dry plateau. That is [HOMEBREW], and it is what makes the disease a legitimate threat to the crew. Valley fever does not pass from person to person, which keeps it a hazard of the place and never a plague.

**Where it is.** The spores live in dry, disturbed dust. The risk is highest in Zone One (the flyer graveyard and the open apron), in any rubble or breached section of Zone Two, and anywhere a fight, a blast, a collapse or a rotor wash throws dust into the air. It is lowest in the sealed, actively maintained sections of Zone Two and in Zone Three, where ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN keep the air filtered and breathable. The cleanest air in the Concourse is the air the two mainframes are keeping clean, which Ilkovic should find unsettling.

**Who is at risk.** Anyone with lungs. Remi-9 and Bracket (the two androids) cannot be infected, though their sensors and intakes clog with dust. Grit (Vargr) is at risk like any human.

### How to avoid it

- **Keep the suit sealed.** The plateau ruling (Chapter 2) already puts the party in vacc suits in Zone One. A sealed vacc suit helmet, a respirator or a filter mask worn properly gives full protection while it stays on. Exposure happens when a helmet comes off to talk, eat or repair something, when a suit is torn, or when the party enters a dusty sealed section and takes the mask off because the air reads breathable.
- **Do not raise the dust.** Wet the dust down before digging or sweeping. Work slowly. Avoid blasting, heavy vehicle traffic, running and fighting in dusty areas. A firefight or a collapse in the graveyard throws up a cloud that reaches everyone nearby.
- **Read the air.** Ilkovic's multi-gas analyzer reports particulate on a continuous readout. A normal check (Average, 8+, Engineer (life support)) shows an unusual biological load in a dusty area. Telling that it is fungal spores means taking a sample and a Science (life sciences) or Medic check (Average, 8+). Failing the check does not mean the air is safe, only that she cannot yet tell.
- **Decontaminate on the way out.** Spores ride out on suits, boots and gear. Ilkovic's decon kit, used at an airlock or at the ship's ramp, clears the dust with an Average (8+) Engineer (life support) check. If the party skips this or the check fails, the spores come aboard the *Sedimental Value*, and the ship's recirculated air exposes every unprotected person aboard.

### Exposure and the fight-it-off roll

Call for an exposure when a Traveller breathes disturbed dust without protection: helmet off in a dust cloud, a torn suit, contaminated ship air. Each exposed Traveller then rolls an **END check, Average (8+)**.

- **Success:** the Traveller coughs it out and is not infected.
- **Failure:** the spores take hold. Symptoms (dry cough, fever, night sweats, aching joints, sometimes a rash on the shins) appear **1D days** later. In a single session, play the first signs on the flight home or in the epilogue, as a ticking clock.

**Modifiers to the exposure roll [HOMEBREW]:**

| Situation | DM |
|---|---|
| Improvised cloth mask or scarf | +2 |
| Heavy dust cloud (blast, collapse, rotor wash, fighting in the dust) | -2 |
| Each further exposure in the same day | -1 |
| Aged 50 or over, END already damaged, or has taken radiation damage (Chapter 8) | -2 |

### The disease

Once infected, the Traveller must resist the disease with a series of END checks, as for any disease in the Core Rulebook [CR p.80]. The disease follows the Core format.

| Disease | END check Difficulty | Damage | Interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valley fever | Average (8+) | 1D-2 (minimum 1) | 1D days |

- **A tenacious disease.** Valley fever needs **two successful END checks** to shake off, not one. The Core Rulebook allows this at the referee's discretion for more tenacious diseases [CR p.80].
- **Each failed check** costs the listed damage to END, then to STR and DEX once END is 0, as the Core Rulebook describes for diseases and injuries [CR p.80, 82]. The Traveller is also **fatigued** while symptomatic, suffering DM-2 to all checks until they recover [CR p.80].
- **Rest helps.** A Traveller who stays in bed or does no strenuous work between intervals gets DM+1 on the next check. A Traveller who keeps working in the dust gets DM-1.
- **Disseminated infection.** Three failed checks in a row, or END reaching 0, means the fungus has spread beyond the lungs. The disease becomes **Difficult (10+), 1D damage, 1D days, three successes needed**, and it will not clear without antifungal drugs. A disseminated case that is cured leaves **1 point of END permanently lost** [HOMEBREW].

### Treatment and recovery

- **Medic check.** Treating the disease is a Core Rulebook task: **Treat Poison or Disease, Average (8+) Medic check (1D hours, EDU)** [CR p.69]. The Core Rulebook does not say what success does, so use this [HOMEBREW]: a successful treatment gives the patient **DM+2 to their next END check** against the disease. A failed treatment gives no bonus and costs the patient nothing.
- **Panacea.** Panacea (Cr200 per dose, TL8+) lets a Traveller with no Medic skill treat a disease as if they had Medic 0 [CR]. It does not cure valley fever by itself, but it lets anyone make the Treat check.
- **Antifungal course.** A course of antifungal drugs (TL9, Cr300, [HOMEBREW]) gives **DM+4 to each END check** while it lasts, and is the only thing that stops a disseminated case. Halcourt's pharmacy stocks it, and the *Sedimental Value*'s medbay holds 1D courses. Oyelaran (Chapter 4) knows to use it.
- **Long-term care.** A **Long-term Care** Medic check (Average, 8+, 1 day, EDU) [CR p.69] helps characteristic points come back. Points lost to the disease heal as normal natural healing once the disease is fought off [CR p.82].
- **Natural recovery.** A healthy Traveller who rests and keeps drinking will often get through on their own, which is why the first check (Average, 8+) is not harsh. The danger is the Traveller who cannot rest, who is under pressure to keep working in the dust, or who is already weakened.

**Using it at the table.** Valley fever is a slow-burn hazard like Ilkovic's air-quality readings: the exposure happens early, the symptoms arrive later, and by then the party has already left the dust. It rewards the crew that wears its helmet and decontaminates, and it punishes the one that removed a mask in Zone One "just for a minute". It pairs well with the radiation hot spots in Chapter 8: a character who has taken radiation damage is easier to infect.
