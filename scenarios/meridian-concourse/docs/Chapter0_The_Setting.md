# CHAPTER 0, THE SETTING
### Palin, So Skire Subsector, Old Expanses Sector, Third Imperium

The Meridian Concourse takes place on **Palin**, hex 2234 of the So Skire Subsector, in the Third Imperium's Domain of Sol. Palin is a real entry in the published So Skire world list (the full list is kept in **Appendix D**), so its Universal World Profile, stars, nobility code and neighbours below are fixed data, not inventions. Nothing in your uploaded books or on the Traveller Wiki says anything more about it: the wiki's So Skire Subsector page lists Palin without a description and leaves its background and history sections empty. Everything that gives Palin a surface, a town, a working government and a past is invented for this campaign.

**Source tags used in this chapter.** [TABLE] is the So Skire world list in Appendix D. [CR] is Core Rulebook Update 2022. [SF] is Solomani Front (Aug23). [WIKI] is the Traveller Wiki pages for the So Skire Subsector, Hell and Digriz. [SHEET] is the Traveller Map world data sheet for Palin supplied with the project, transcribed in Appendix D. [HOMEBREW] is invented for this campaign. Appendix C walks through the same tags chapter by chapter.

## THE SYSTEM

Palin's primary is a pair of M1 V red dwarfs [TABLE], called **Palin A** and **Palin B** here [HOMEBREW names]. Orbits are the referee's choice. A good default is that the mainworld circles Palin A closely while Palin B hangs in the sky as a smaller, dimmer second sun, so everything on the surface is lit in two shades of dull red.

The table gives the system 8 worlds with a PBG of 214 [TABLE]. Beyond the mainworld that means one planetoid belt, four gas giants, and two further minor worlds that nothing describes. The four gas giants make unrefined fuel trivial to skim, and the belt, which locals call the Cinders [HOMEBREW], explains the Economic extension's Resources value of B, abundant [TABLE, SHEET]. Read together, the data describes a world rich in rock and short of people: Resources abundant, Labour in the thousands, Infrastructure very limited, Efficiency good [SHEET]. That is the economic reason Meridian Field mattered so much to Halcourt, and the reason Grit's profession is worth hiring.

## WHERE PALIN SITS

So Skire is subsector O of the Old Expanses sector. It holds 37 worlds and its capital is Peart (1834, four parsecs from Palin). The Third Imperium controls 33 of the 37 worlds, the Solomani Confederation holds four, and the subsector's defending force is the 308th Fleet [WIKI]. The four Confederation worlds in the table are Patuxant (2240), Bordelon (2340), Khan (2439) and Digriz (2440) [TABLE], all strung along the southern edge. Those hexes sit six to seven parsecs from Palin and three to four from Hell [distances calculated from the hex coordinates].

Palin itself is deep in Imperial space and unwatched in the way only a backwater can be. It has no naval base, scout base, way station or travel zone [TABLE]. The nearest naval bases are at Di Meola (2236, two parsecs) and Hjorts (2432, three), the nearest scout base is at Clarke (2333, two), with more at Hell (2237), Barlow (1934) and Dunbar (2036) at three, and the nearest class A starport is Tama (2134), one parsec away.

**The border.** Your Solomani Front book's Dagir Subsector map carries a label reading "To So Skire," which marks So Skire as the neighbouring subsector. If the two sectors' hex grids join row 40 to row 01 [my reading, not stated in the book], Patuxant is one parsec from Dagir, Conterolla and Churchill, Digriz is one parsec from Conterolla and Cloudia, and Palin is about seven parsecs from Dagir. What the Dagir chapter itself says [SF, Dagir Subsector chapter]: the subsector has 26 worlds, mostly inside the Confederation, with a narrow strip on the spinward fringe under Imperial occupation or non-aligned. Late in the Solomani Rim War the worlds of Dagir, Cloudia and Gemstar, dominated by Radical Embracer factions, tried to secede from the Confederation. The Armistice stopped the war before the Imperial Navy could help, so the Confederation signed the Treaty of Gemstar with them instead, making them client states with internal self-rule, a permanent Confederation naval base at Gemstar, and a separate peace with the Imperium. The Dagir Border Fleet guards the cluster from the Imperial border at Zephir, smugglers are the Confederation's main concern, and in 1103 Cloudia began building merchant ships that some observers think could be converted to warships. SolSec is likely investigating, but the treaty stops it operating openly in the Cloudia belt.

**Digriz** (2440, B689414-A) is the nearest Confederation world to Palin's neighbourhood that the wiki describes: a corporate polity, a frontier world on the Imperial border, with a class B starport and shipyard [WIKI, TABLE]. A company-run Confederation world seven parsecs away is a ready-made patron or rival for the expedition.

## THE WORLD AS PLAYED [HOMEBREW]

**Surface.** Palin is a small, cold, dry world of red-brown regolith and iron-oxide dust under a thin sky lit by two red suns. Wind is the dominant hazard: dust storms can cut visibility to arm's length and static-charge exposed equipment badly enough to fry unshielded electronics. The equatorial plateau where Meridian Field stood is flat basalt, which made it an easy strip for winged atmospheric flyers. The fault that dropped the sinkhole is the Halcourt Rift.

**Plateau ruling.** In the lowlands, treat the air as the Core Rulebook's Atmosphere 3 and require a respirator. On the plateau the air thins further, so treat it as Atmosphere 1 (Trace) and require a vacc suit. This is why earlier chapters assume vacc suits at the site. Inside the sealed sections of the Concourse the air is whatever Chapter 5 and Petra Ilkovic's readings say it is.

**Halcourt.** A domed town and downport holding most of Palin's roughly 20,000 people, 340 km from the sinkhole. It grew out of a depot dating to the Solomani Rim War and was never really replanned. Locals call it Purgatory, half as a joke. Water comes from polar ice and deep aquifers. It is the closest place to refuel, requisition gear and hire.

**Government in action.** The Imperial interest on Palin is held by a Knight [TABLE], here **Sir Teodor Brandt, the Resident**. The actual administration is a Civil Service Bureaucracy (Government 8), here the **Palin Civil Registry**, whose main product is filed paperwork. When Heliarch Dynamics and Kovalenko-Straub settled in 1094, the Registry accepted their joint filing, stamped it "industrial accident," and archived it. Nobody senior read it, because nobody senior had reason to. That is the whole cover-up, and it is more plausible than a conspiracy.

**Economy.** Mining the Cinders, skimming the gas giants, and importing almost everything else. Meridian Field was the transhipment point that made that work, which is why Halcourt never recovered when it went dark.

**Culture.** The Cultural extension [556B] reads on the data sheet as harmonious, very aloof and very distinct, with abstract symbols [SHEET]. At the table that is a town whose people agree among themselves, keep outsiders at arm's length, and do things in their own way, which suits a Registry that never reads what it files. How such a town treats a ten-person off-world crew, and who in it will talk to them, is [HOMEBREW].

## LAW LEVEL 9, CHARTERS AND REGISTRATION

Law Level 9 sits on the last row of the Core Rulebook's Law Level table, where all weapons and all armour are banned [CR]. The data sheet words the same level as possession of weapons outside the home being prohibited [SHEET], so the practical rule is that nothing is carried in public. Under the Law and Travellers procedure in the World Creation chapter, the authorities take an interest whenever a 2D roll comes in at or below the Law Level, so at Law 9 that is five rolls in six [CR, arithmetic]. The expedition cannot simply walk off the downport armed.

**Hazard Site Charter** [HOMEBREW]. The Resident's office issues the expedition a charter naming the individuals allowed to carry arms and armour, restricted to the exclusion zone around Meridian Field. Everything else is bonded in a locker at the Halcourt downport. By default only Rurik is named. Alcala's sidearm and Draeger's holdout pistol are not covered, and each is a quiet arrest waiting to happen if it leaves the ship inside Halcourt.

**Autonomous Systems Ordinance** [HOMEBREW]. Passed after 1094, it bans unregistered autonomous systems outright. Androids need a Registry card to leave the downport. Remi's personhood rests on the Confederation's android statute (Chapter 3), which the Imperium does not recognise on its own soil, and Bracket has no papers at all. Both are a negotiation with the Resident's office before the expedition can leave Halcourt.

## THE PATRON, THE BORDER AND WHY NOW

Chapter 1 leaves the patron to the referee. With real neighbours available, four options fit the data:

- **A Tama syndicate.** An insurance or salvage consortium on the nearest class A port, settling a claim from a century that nobody wanted to open.
- **An Atkins combine.** A high-tech industrial concern that wants the arbitration cores and has money to spend quietly.
- **A Digriz company.** A Confederation corporate polity working through Draeger's old SolSec contacts. It has the border context, the motive, and the deniability.
- **The Resident's own office.** Sir Teodor Brandt, cleaning up before an audit from the naval staff at Di Meola.

Whichever is chosen, the border supplies the pressure: the Confederation's main worry along it is smuggling [SF], and a buried complex of TL 10 to 12 robotics hardware is exactly what both sides would rather the other not reach first.
