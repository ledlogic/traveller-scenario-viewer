# CHAPTER 6, ACT II: THE CONCOURSE APPROACH

## 6.1: OVERVIEW

Meridian Field was three things stacked on top of each other, and the collapse layered them further: a surface **airfield** for winged atmospheric flyers, a mid-level **free-trade bazaar and arbitration concourse**, and a lower-level **corporate infrastructure deck** (power, data, and, before the war, light manufacturing). The party enters through the new sinkhole into the upper airfield level; the mainframes and the truth of Appendix A are at the bottom.

Run this as three descending zones. Zones One and Two are Act II (this chapter) and Zone Three is Act III (Chapter 7). Each has a distinct threat, tone, and pace.

## 6.2: ZONE ONE, THE FLYER GRAVEYARD (Surface / Airfield Level)

The sinkhole drops into what was once the open apron and hangar rows where atmospheric flyers parked and were serviced between runs. A century of dust has drifted in through cracks and old ventilation shafts, half-burying a graveyard of winged aircraft, commercial short-haul flyers, a few private craft, one partially intact *Heliarch* security gunship, all coated in red dust, many with hull sections torn open by the original fighting.

**Atmosphere here:** very thin, the same as the plateau surface above (vacc suits, per the plateau ruling in Chapter 2). This level was never fully pressurized even before the collapse, since it was an open apron, so suits stay on. Light is minimal: whatever daylight leaks through the new sinkhole opening, fading fast more than fifty metres in.

**The threat: shellworms.** See **Appendix C**. Colonies of large burrowing xenofauna have moved into the wreckage over the decades, using the flyers' hollow fuselages as protective shells the way a hermit crab uses a borrowed shell, hence the term the referee should use with players once they've seen one: *shell-squatters*. They are not intelligent, not hostile by nature, and mostly interested in the mineral content of the wreck-metal itself, which they slowly digest. They become dangerous when a party disturbs a fuselage a worm has claimed, or when a fight elsewhere in the zone drives several worms into a startled cluster.

**Set pieces:**
- **The Gunship.** The partially intact *Heliarch* security gunship is worth investigating, its weapon systems are long dead, but its flight recorder (if Solt or Kwan can get it running, Electronics (computers) 2+, or Mechanic if the case is pried open the hard way) has the last twenty minutes of *Heliarch* combat comms from Day 11, a direct, visceral piece of Appendix A's history delivered as recovered audio rather than exposition. It is also, unfortunately, currently occupied by the largest shellworm in the zone.
- **The Loading Ramp.** A drivable route down to Zone Two exists, a cargo ramp, dust-choked but structurally sound (Grit's read, Science (geology) 1+, confirms this), wide enough for Bracket to bring down heavy equipment if the party wants to try to recover anything large. This is the natural route for a "ship team stays behind, ground team advances with a hauler" split if the party divided after Chapter 5.
- **First Contact.** Somewhere in this zone, a still-functional Concourse maintenance terminal (dead, dust-covered, but with standby power) will react the first time any character tries to interface with it, a brief flicker, a single line of text (**"IDENTIFY."**), then silence. This is ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN noticing the party for the first time. Nothing happens immediately. Something will, later.

## 6.3: ZONE TWO, THE ARBITRATION CONCOURSE (Mid Level / Bazaar)

The heart of Meridian Field: three levels of shopfronts, brokerage stalls, customs offices, and, at the center, the **Arbitration Floor**, a wide atrium ringed by the terminals both companies used to file disputes. This zone is where the worst of the Day 8–11 fighting happened, and it shows: scorched walls, punched-through partitions, the skeletal remains of combat automata from both companies still lying where they fell, weapons long since dead or cannibalized.

**Atmosphere here:** this level *was* fully pressurized before the collapse, a proper sealed environment, not an open apron, and sections of it are, startlingly, still holding air twenty-five years later, kept alive by the same hidden geothermal taps that have kept ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN running. What nobody knows, including the referee's own NPCs, is which sections, how well, and for how much longer. This is deliberately not a settled fact the referee hands the party, it's the Safety Officer's whole job (see **Chapter 3, Petra Ilkovic**) to find out room by room, and the referee should run it that way: no blanket "the air here is fine." Every sealed door is a fresh Engineer (life support) check before anyone commits to cracking a helmet seal, and a pass doesn't mean *safe forever*, just *safe now, as far as she can tell*. Some sections will read clean. Some will read borderline, usable, but not somewhere to linger or exert. A few, especially nearer Zone Three's power infrastructure, should read actively wrong: a scrubber bank cycling stale air back in on itself, a slow trace-toxin leak nobody's traced yet, a pressure differential that means something on the other side of a door is either venting or about to. Where a section *is* holding good air, it is cold, it smells of ozone and old dust, and, once Ilkovic has cleared it, it lets characters work without a sealed helmet, a small, earned mercy the referee can use to vary the tension. Either way, the fact that anything down here is still being maintained at all, twenty-five years on, is the real discovery: *something* is still spending real power keeping select rooms livable, on purpose, and neither Grit's read nor Ilkovic's tells the party why. (They will find out, see Chapter 7, Zone Three, and Appendix B.)

**The threat: automated response.** ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN, now aware of the intrusion, begin actively responding. Per Appendix A, they cooperate against the party as a shared threat. Expect:
- **Salvaged sentry drones** (Appendix C), a handful of surviving/rebuilt security automata from both companies' original inventories, some patched together from both companies' parts, an uncomfortable visual detail for Bracket's player in particular.
- **Environmental pressure**, doors that seal unexpectedly, lighting that guides (or misdirects) the party, life support that can be locally vented as a threat rather than a weapon (the mainframes would rather capture/study an intruder than kill one outright, see Appendix B, so expect non-lethal pressure before lethal force).
- **The recovered evidence.** Scattered through brokerage stalls and customs offices are salvageable fragments of the original arbitration filings, physical backup data-slates, mostly corrupted, a few intact. Draeger's Advocate skill and Solt's Investigate/Electronics skill are the tools for piecing together Appendix A's history from these, in-world, rather than the referee simply telling the players. Budget three to five discoverable fragments, each revealing one beat of Appendix A (the arbitration charter, the drone collisions, the K-S self-destruct doctrine, the joint settlement, the sealing).

The zone's centrepiece, the Arbitration Floor itself, is the opening scene of Act III (Chapter 7).
