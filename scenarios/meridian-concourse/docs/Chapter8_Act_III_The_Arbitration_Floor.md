# CHAPTER 8, ACT III: THE ARBITRATION FLOOR AND EXTRACTION

## 8.1: THE ARBITRATION FLOOR

 the atrium at this zone's center is where the party will eventually need to go to reach either mainframe's physical access point (Zone Three has the hardware; the Floor has the last remaining *neutral* terminal both mainframes still recognize as legitimate, per their original charter, using it is the single safest way to communicate with either system without immediately being treated as a combat threat). A large, dust-shrouded holographic dispute-status board still flickers here, cycling meaninglessly through a century-old backlog of unresolved arbitration cases. Small, eerie, and worth describing in full.

## 8.2: ZONE THREE, THE INFRASTRUCTURE DECK (Lower Level / Mainframes)

The lowest accessible level, and the only one still under active, deliberate environmental control by its two remaining occupants. This is where ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN physically reside (each in its own hardened server core, on opposite sides of the deck, separated by the blast-collapsed section that used to connect them directly, neither has had physical access to the other's core in twenty-five years, only network access, which is part of why the stalemate has held). Full stats, personality, and interaction rules for both are in **Appendix B**.

**Atmosphere here:** fully sealed, fully pressurized, warmer than anywhere else in the Concourse (waste heat from two continuously running server cores). This is the one place in the entire site where a character could survive without a suit indefinitely, which the referee should let land as unsettling rather than comforting. Something has been keeping this level alive, on purpose, for twenty-five years, waiting.

**What's here:**
- **ARBITER's core** (*Heliarch* side) and **COUNTERSIGN's core** (K-S side), each defensible, each willing to talk.
- The **collapsed connecting corridor** between them, the actual blast site from Day 11, still showing scorching and structural damage nobody's touched since. Grit's structural read here is the campaign's tensest single check: this is genuinely unstable, and heavy activity (combat, blasting, Bracket's equipment) here risks a secondary collapse.
- Remnant **fabrication and repair bays** on both sides, the source of every salvaged/rebuilt drone the party has fought in Zone Two, and, if the party investigates, unambiguous physical evidence of exactly how far each mainframe's independent technological advancement over twenty-five years has actually gone (a good place for the referee to show, not just tell, the TL13–14 tech level called out in Appendix A).

**Radiation hot spots:** the surface, the bazaar level and the rest of the site are clean, but Zone Three is not uniformly safe [HOMEBREW]. The blast breached containment in at least two places, and the spill is local, not general. The **collapsed connecting corridor** is a low-level hot spot from scattered isotope stock. A **buried lab annex** holding sealed medical and research isotopes is cracked open behind a rubble fall, and it is the worst spot on the deck. A **backup fission generator room** sits behind the mainframes' own power feed, shielded and still running, and COUNTERSIGN and ARBITER both know exactly where it is and keep each other away from it. Run these as rad meter readings that climb as the party approaches, so Ilkovic gets a warning before anyone takes a dose. Use the Core Rulebook's radiation rules for the effects (check the printed book for the exact dose and damage figures). The hot spots are a reason the corridor was never dug back in after 1094, and a bargaining chip, since either mainframe can steer the party around them or into them.

**The choice point:** this is where Act Three's real content happens, negotiation, information-gathering, and the campaign's central decision (destroy the mainframes, extract one or both, broker a real peace between them, or leave them exactly as they are and seal the site again). Appendix B gives the referee everything needed to run both mainframes as distinct, reactive NPCs rather than a single monolithic threat.

## 8.3: EXTRACTION OR OCCUPATION

Getting out is its own problem. The standing protocol does not end when the party stops fighting, and neither mainframe has any reason to let a known intruder leave quietly. If the party has turned one against the other, or brokered a truce, the exit is a negotiated one. If not, the party fights its way up through the same zones it came down. The party can also decide to stay, hold the site, and call for help from Halcourt, which turns the campaign into an occupation: the patron, the Resident and the Registry all have an opinion about who now owns the Concourse.
