# CHAPTER 4, NPCS

The people the party meets, or is told about, on Palin. Everything in this chapter is **[HOMEBREW]** except where a name comes from the So Skire table (Palin's Knight, Chapter 2). Statistics are left light on purpose: only the ship's officers and the mainframes matter in play for more than a scene, and the mainframes are built out in Appendix B.

## 4.2: THE SHIP'S OFFICERS (Act I)

![Captain Aveline Devereux-Sato](img/devereux-sato.webp)

![Senior Engineer Bram Voskuijlen](img/voskuijlen.webp)

![Medic and Steward Hana Oyelaran](img/oyelaran.webp)

The three senior officers of the *Sedimental Value*. All three are fitted with the neural shipboard uplink described in Chapter 5, which is why the Concourse's attack takes them and no one else.

- **Captain and Senior Pilot Aveline Devereux-Sato.** Veteran charter pilot, twenty years running exactly this kind of hazard-zone work. Mentor to Alcala (PC 9) and Vidkun (PC 10). Calm, dry, and the kind of professional whose absence the party feels at once.
- **Senior Engineer Bram Voskuijlen.** Methodical and cautious. Mentor to Kwan (PC 8). Checks everything twice and says so.
- **Medic and Steward Hana Oyelaran.** Runs the medical bay and passenger logistics. She is the one officer with no implant, so she is unharmed, and she can stabilise the three but cannot revive them. After the incident she becomes the party's most useful non-player ally, and the person who keeps the unconscious officers alive on the flight back to Halcourt.

After the incident all three officers are out of the campaign for good (Chapter 6). If the party presses on, Oyelaran can ride the ship back with the wounded.

## 4.3: HALCOURT AND THE RESIDENT (Acts I and III)

![Sir Teodor Brandt, the Resident](img/brandt.webp)

- **Sir Teodor Brandt, the Resident.** The Knight whose title the So Skire table records for Palin. A careful, unambitious Imperial functionary, the sort who survives postings like this one by never being the person who made a decision. He is cleaning up ahead of an audit from the naval staff at Di Meola (Chapter 2), which gives him a reason to let a private expedition go in first and a stronger reason to be unhappy if it finds anything embarrassing. Cooperative on the surface, evasive on anything about 1094.
- **The Registry clerk.** The Palin Civil Registry's face to the party: courteous, thorough about forms, and entirely incurious about what is in them. Use the clerk to deliver the Hazard Site Charter and the Autonomous Systems Ordinance (Chapter 2), and to let a player discover that the 1094 filing was stamped "industrial accident" and archived unread.

## 4.4: THE PATRON'S AGENT (Acts I to III)

The party's contact with whoever is paying. Chapter 2 lists four patron options (a Tama syndicate, an Atkins combine, a Digriz company, the Resident's own office), and the agent changes with the choice. Whoever it is, the agent wants the mainframes captured intact, and is not fully honest with the crew about that (Chapter 1). Play the agent as pleasant, well briefed, and never quite answering the question the party actually asked.

## 4.5: THE OPPOSITION

![ARBITER](img/arbiter.webp)

![COUNTERSIGN](img/countersign.webp)

- **ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN.** The two surviving mainframes. They are not NPCs in the usual sense, and they should be played as characters. Personalities, game statistics and the standing protocol are in Appendix B.
- **Shell-squatters.** Burrowing xenofauna living in the flyer wrecks. Not hostile by nature (Appendix C).
- **Scavenger crews.** Independent salvage gangs out of Halcourt who have heard about the sinkhole (Appendix C). A crew may already be inside when the party arrives, having set off the standing protocol itself shortly before.

## 4.6: USING THE NPCS

None of these characters needs more than a name, a want and a voice at the table. The strongest scenes are the ones where an NPC says something the player does not yet know is important: the clerk's offhand mention of the archive, the Resident's refusal to meet anyone's eyes about 1094, Oyelaran's quiet account of exactly when the officers went down.
