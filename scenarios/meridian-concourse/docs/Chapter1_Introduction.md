# CHAPTER 1, INTRODUCTION
### The Meridian Concourse, a Mongoose Traveller 2nd Edition Campaign

---

**Setting:** [Palin](https://wiki.travellerrpg.com/Palin_(world)?sector=Old%20Expanses&hex=2234) / [So Skire](https://wiki.travellerrpg.com/So_Skire_Subsector) [(Old Expanses](https://wiki.travellerrpg.com/Old_Expanses_Sector) 2234), Third Imperium, Imperial Year 1119
**Structure:** Three Acts
**Player Roles:** A chartered salvage/survey expedition into a newly exposed ruin

> *"The Concourse never closed. It just stopped letting anyone leave."*
> Graffiti, Halcourt transit shed

## 1.1: WRITEUP

A quake cracks open a buried airport and bazaar on Palin, sealed for decades since a corporate robot war tore it apart. Two rival AI mainframes still haunt the ruins, cooperating only against intruders, you. Hire a ten-specialist crew, from security to safety officer to the ship's own junior pilot, and go underground to learn what really happened, and whether anything down there is worth saving. A full Mongoose Traveller 2e one-shot for 6 players.

## 1.3: HALCOURT

Palin is Imperial territory, and has been since long before anyone currently living there was born. That is most of what recommends it.

The world sits deep inside the Imperium's So Skire Subsector, six parsecs from the nearest Confederation world, nominally under full Third Imperium administration and in practice under almost none. Its one settlement, **Halcourt**, exists because the Imperium needed something on this stretch of space during the Solomani Rim War, a picket world and supply depot, and never quite got around to deciding what it should become afterward.

For a stretch of thirty years, Palin's literal main corridor of economic activity was **Meridian Field**, a linear, sprawling joint civil and commercial airport-and-bazaar complex on the equatorial plateau, built to service atmospheric flyer traffic between Halcourt and the handful of mining claims scattered across the highlands. It was built into a natural trench, using the strong hardrock cliff face to cantilever its shops and businesses while a central corridor of gardens, biohabs and zoos ran down the middle: the ultimate full-lifecycle mall, for living, working, learning and playing. In its last years of operation, Meridian Field also did something the Palin Civil Registry never quite noticed happening under its nose: it hosted the arbitration courts, brokers and cargo bays through which two rival robotics conglomerates, one Solomani and one Imperial-backed, settled their manufacturing disputes. They did it specifically because neither trusted the other's home courts, and specifically because nobody on Palin was paying close enough attention to ask why a Solomani firm was running hardware trials on Imperial soil in the first place.

That arrangement ended badly. In 1094 the corporate rivalry that had been fought in patent offices and boardrooms for fifteen years came out from under the table. Over the following eleven days the Concourse collapsed (the full account is in **Appendix A**). The crater it left was declared a hazard exclusion zone by a Registry with every incentive to make the paperwork as boring as possible, and Palin went back to being a world nobody thinks about.

Then, six weeks ago, a quake along the Halcourt Rift fault line dropped forty metres of regolith into a sinkhole that had not been there the week before, and exposed a clean, intact section of pressurized concourse roofing, undamaged, dark, and (according to the drone the local prospecting syndicate flew through the gap before it stopped transmitting) still showing environmental telltales on the airlock control panel.

**Someone is paying very well for the first professional team down there.**

Imperial interest in Palin remains thin: a Knight in residence, Sir Teodor Brandt, and a Civil Service Registry that files more than it reads. There is no naval base, scout base or way station on the world at all. That is exactly why a private expedition is being allowed to go in first, quietly, before anyone with real authority has to have an opinion about what is down there. SolSec, for its part, maintains an unofficial watch post in Halcourt, tolerated and not welcomed by the Resident, because everyone with a long memory remembers what a Solomani company operating unsupervised on this world did the last time.

## 1.4: PALIN AT A GLANCE

```
Palin 2234, So Skire Subsector (Old Expanses)
UWP: B430489-A      Trade Codes: De Ni Po      Nobility: B (Knight)
Starport B | Size 4 | Atmosphere 3 (Very Thin) | Hydrographics 0
Population 4 | Government 8 (Civil Service Bureaucracy)
Law Level 9 (weapons and armour banned in public) | Tech Level A (10)
Stars: M1 V + M1 V (twin red dwarfs) | PBG 214 | 8 worlds | Bases: none
```

Palin's surface is red-brown regolith and iron-oxide dust under a thin sky lit by two dull red suns, and travellers who have been to Mars expect the resemblance and get it. Size 4 is the Core Rulebook's own Mars example, and surface gravity is half a G. Wind is the dominant hazard: dust storms can drop visibility to arm's length and static-charge exposed equipment badly enough to fry unshielded electronics. A respirator is the minimum outside, and on the equatorial plateau where Meridian Field once stood the air thins enough that a vacc suit is required. That plateau is otherwise unremarkable flat basalt, chosen because it made an easy strip for winged atmospheric flyers.

**Halcourt**, with most of the world's roughly 20,000 people, is 340 km from the sinkhole. It is a domed town and class B downport built outward from a Rim War depot and never really replanned. It houses the Resident's staff, the Civil Registry, the descendants of the original depot workers who never left, and an economy that has not recovered from losing its one real transhipment point. It is also the closest place to refuel, requisition gear and hire.

Law Level 9 means carrying weapons or armour outside the home is banned. The expedition's armed security works under a Hazard Site Charter that covers only named people, only inside the exclusion zone. See **Chapter 2** for the charter, the Autonomous Systems Ordinance that complicates the two android PCs, and the full UWP breakdown.

## 1.5: MISSION OBJECTIVES

The expedition has been chartered (by a patron of the referee's choosing: a salvage consortium, an insurance syndicate settling a century-old claim, an Imperial heritage-recovery office with an unusually specific interest, or the corporate rep's own employer; see **Chapter 2, The Patron**, and **Chapter 3, Character 4**) with three stated objectives, in priority order:

1. **Establish what happened.** Eleven days of corporate war destroyed Meridian Field in 1094. No official inquiry was ever completed. The companies involved settled quietly with a cooperative Registry, sealed the site, and the paper trail goes cold. Someone now wants a real answer: was it sabotage, a runaway arms race, or something that got out of anyone's control? (See **Appendix A**. The referee knows the truth, and the players should have to dig for it.)

2. **Assess what remains.** A century-plus-old commercial concourse, sealed and largely undisturbed, is worth a fortune in salvage rights alone, if it is stable, and if whatever is still running down there can be dealt with.

3. **Determine viability for reopening.** The patron's real interest (revealed as the campaign progresses) is whether Meridian Field's substructure, including its still-functional if hostile computer cores, could be recovered rather than demolished.

The referee should let the players discover, over the course of the campaign, that objectives 2 and 3 are in quiet tension: the patron would very much like the two mainframes described in **Appendix B** captured intact rather than destroyed, and has not been fully honest with the crew about that.

## 1.6: STRUCTURE

This campaign is written for a full ten-character crew (**Chapter 3**) built around the twin conceits that (a) the party's own ship's professional crew is taken out of action almost immediately, for reasons tied directly to the site's central threat (**Chapter 5**), and (b) every player character has some baseline capacity to keep that ship running regardless. This means the Concourse delve and the ship itself both stay in play as connected problems throughout. A party that mismanages the ship is stuck on Palin with everyone else who has ever been stranded there.

The campaign runs in three acts, one chapter each:

- **Act I, Arrival and the Silence (Chapter 5):** Transit to Palin, arrival at the Halcourt downport (where Law Level 9 and the Hazard Site Charter shape what the party can carry), hiring and prep, the flight out to the sinkhole, and the incident that disables the ship's professional crew.
- **Act II, The Concourse Approach (Chapter 6):** Entry, the shell-squatter-infested flyer graveyard, first contact with automated Concourse systems, the air and the evidence of the bazaar level, and the Miner and Safety Officer earning their keep as two genuinely separate disciplines (Appendix C).
- **Act III, The Arbitration Floor and Extraction (Chapter 7):** Reaching the two mainframes, learning the true history (Appendix A is the payoff), the players' choice about what to do with ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN (Appendix B), and then getting out, or deciding to stay and hold the site, which changes the campaign's shape entirely.

Referees should feel free to run this as a single long session (4 to 6 hours) hitting the beats of all three acts, or expand each act into its own session for a proper campaign.
