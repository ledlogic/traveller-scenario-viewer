# CHAPTER 5 — THE CONCOURSE

## OVERVIEW

Meridian Field was three things stacked on top of each other, and the collapse layered them further: a surface **airfield** for winged atmospheric flyers, a mid-level **free-trade bazaar and arbitration concourse**, and a lower-level **corporate infrastructure deck** (power, data, and — before the war — light manufacturing). The party enters through the new sinkhole into the upper airfield level; the mainframes and the truth of Chapter 2 are at the bottom.

Run this as three descending zones. Each has a distinct threat, tone, and pace.

---

## ZONE ONE — THE FLYER GRAVEYARD (Surface / Airfield Level)

The sinkhole drops into what was once the open apron and hangar rows where atmospheric flyers parked and were serviced between runs. A century of dust has drifted in through cracks and old ventilation shafts, half-burying a graveyard of winged aircraft — commercial short-haul flyers, a few private craft, one partially intact Heliarch security gunship — all coated in red dust, many with hull sections torn open by the original fighting.

**Atmosphere here:** thin and tainted, same as the surface — this level was never fully pressurized even before the collapse (it was an open apron), so vacc suits or respirators stay on. Light is minimal: whatever daylight leaks through the new sinkhole opening, fading fast more than fifty metres in.

**The threat: shellworms.** See **Appendix B**. Colonies of large burrowing xenofauna have moved into the wreckage over the decades, using the flyers' hollow fuselages as protective shells the way a hermit crab uses a borrowed shell — hence the term the referee should use with players once they've seen one: *shell-squatters*. They are not intelligent, not hostile by nature, and mostly interested in the mineral content of the wreck-metal itself, which they slowly digest. They become dangerous when a party disturbs a fuselage a worm has claimed, or when a fight elsewhere in the zone drives several worms into a startled cluster.

**Set pieces:**
- **The Gunship.** The partially intact Heliarch security gunship is worth investigating — its weapon systems are long dead, but its flight recorder (if Solt or Kwan can get it running, Electronics (computers) 2+, or Mechanic if the case is pried open the hard way) has the last twenty minutes of Heliarch combat comms from Day 11, a direct, visceral piece of Chapter 2's history delivered as recovered audio rather than exposition. It is also, unfortunately, currently occupied by the largest shellworm in the zone.
- **The Loading Ramp.** A drivable route down to Zone Two exists — a cargo ramp, dust-choked but structurally sound (Grit's read, Science (geology) 1+, confirms this) — wide enough for Bracket to bring down heavy equipment if the party wants to try to recover anything large. This is the natural route for a "ship team stays behind, ground team advances with a hauler" split if the party divided after Chapter 4.
- **First Contact.** Somewhere in this zone, a still-functional Concourse maintenance terminal (dead, dust-covered, but with standby power) will react the first time any character tries to interface with it — a brief flicker, a single line of text (**"IDENTIFY."**), then silence. This is ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN noticing the party for the first time. Nothing happens immediately. Something will, later.

## ZONE TWO — THE ARBITRATION CONCOURSE (Mid Level / Bazaar)

The heart of Meridian Field: three levels of shopfronts, brokerage stalls, customs offices, and — at the center — the **Arbitration Floor**, a wide atrium ringed by the terminals both companies used to file disputes. This zone is where the worst of the Day 8–11 fighting happened, and it shows: scorched walls, punched-through partitions, the skeletal remains of combat automata from both companies still lying where they fell, weapons long since dead or cannibalized.

**Atmosphere here:** this level *was* fully pressurized before the collapse — a proper sealed environment, not an open apron — and sections of it are, startlingly, still holding air twenty-five years later, kept alive by the same hidden geothermal taps that have kept ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN running. What nobody knows, including the referee's own NPCs, is which sections, how well, and for how much longer. This is deliberately not a settled fact the referee hands the party — it's the Safety Officer's whole job (see **Chapter 3, Petra Ilkovic**) to find out room by room, and the referee should run it that way: no blanket "the air here is fine." Every sealed door is a fresh Engineer (life support) check before anyone commits to cracking a helmet seal, and a pass doesn't mean *safe forever*, just *safe now, as far as she can tell*. Some sections will read clean. Some will read borderline — usable, but not somewhere to linger or exert. A few, especially nearer Zone Three's power infrastructure, should read actively wrong: a scrubber bank cycling stale air back in on itself, a slow trace-toxin leak nobody's traced yet, a pressure differential that means something on the other side of a door is either venting or about to. Where a section *is* holding good air, it is cold, it smells of ozone and old dust, and — once Ilkovic has cleared it — it lets characters work without a sealed helmet, a small, earned mercy the referee can use to vary the tension. Either way, the fact that anything down here is still being maintained at all, twenty-five years on, is the real discovery: *something* is still spending real power keeping select rooms livable, on purpose, and neither Grit's read nor Ilkovic's tells the party why. (They will find out — see Chapter 5, Zone Three, and Appendix A.)

**The threat: automated response.** ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN, now aware of the intrusion, begin actively responding. Per Chapter 2, they cooperate against the party as a shared threat. Expect:
- **Salvaged sentry drones** (Appendix B) — a handful of surviving/rebuilt security automata from both companies' original inventories, some patched together from both companies' parts, an uncomfortable visual detail for Bracket's player in particular.
- **Environmental pressure** — doors that seal unexpectedly, lighting that guides (or misdirects) the party, life support that can be locally vented as a threat rather than a weapon (the mainframes would rather capture/study an intruder than kill one outright — see Appendix A — so expect non-lethal pressure before lethal force).
- **The recovered evidence.** Scattered through brokerage stalls and customs offices are salvageable fragments of the original arbitration filings — physical backup data-slates, mostly corrupted, a few intact. Draeger's Advocate skill and Solt's Investigate/Electronics skill are the tools for piecing together Chapter 2's history from these, in-world, rather than the referee simply telling the players. Budget three to five discoverable fragments, each revealing one beat of Chapter 2 (the arbitration charter, the drone collisions, the K-S self-destruct doctrine, the joint settlement, the sealing).

**Set piece — the Arbitration Floor itself:** the atrium at this zone's center is where the party will eventually need to go to reach either mainframe's physical access point (Zone Three has the hardware; the Floor has the last remaining *neutral* terminal both mainframes still recognize as legitimate, per their original charter — using it is the single safest way to communicate with either system without immediately being treated as a combat threat). A large, dust-shrouded holographic dispute-status board still flickers here, cycling meaninglessly through a century-old backlog of unresolved arbitration cases. Small, eerie, and worth describing in full.

## ZONE THREE — THE INFRASTRUCTURE DECK (Lower Level / Mainframes)

The lowest accessible level, and the only one still under active, deliberate environmental control by its two remaining occupants. This is where ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN physically reside (each in its own hardened server core, on opposite sides of the deck, separated by the blast-collapsed section that used to connect them directly — neither has had physical access to the other's core in twenty-five years, only network access, which is part of why the stalemate has held). Full stats, personality, and interaction rules for both are in **Appendix A**.

**Atmosphere here:** fully sealed, fully pressurized, warmer than anywhere else in the Concourse (waste heat from two continuously running server cores). This is the one place in the entire site where a character could survive without a suit indefinitely — which the referee should let land as unsettling rather than comforting. Something has been keeping this level alive, on purpose, for twenty-five years, waiting.

**What's here:**
- **ARBITER's core** (Heliarch side) and **COUNTERSIGN's core** (K-S side), each defensible, each willing to talk.
- The **collapsed connecting corridor** between them — the actual blast site from Day 11, still showing scorching and structural damage nobody's touched since. Grit's structural read here is the campaign's tensest single check: this is genuinely unstable, and heavy activity (combat, blasting, Bracket's equipment) here risks a secondary collapse.
- Remnant **fabrication and repair bays** on both sides — the source of every salvaged/rebuilt drone the party has fought in Zone Two, and, if the party investigates, unambiguous physical evidence of exactly how far each mainframe's independent technological advancement over twenty-five years has actually gone (a good place for the referee to show, not just tell, the TL13–14 tech level called out in Chapter 2).

**The choice point:** this is where Act Three's real content happens — negotiation, information-gathering, and the campaign's central decision (destroy the mainframes, extract one or both, broker a real peace between them, or leave them exactly as they are and seal the site again). Appendix A gives the referee everything needed to run both mainframes as distinct, reactive NPCs rather than a single monolithic threat.
