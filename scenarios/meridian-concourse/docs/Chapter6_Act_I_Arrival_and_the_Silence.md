# CHAPTER 6, ACT I: ARRIVAL AND THE SILENCE

## 6.1: ACT OVERVIEW

Act I takes the party from the Halcourt downport to a ship that is suddenly theirs alone. It has four beats: arrival and the Hazard Site Charter, hiring and prep, the flight out to the sinkhole, and the incident that takes the senior crew out of the campaign. The act ends on the campaign's first fork, covered at the end of this chapter.

## 6.2: SCENE ONE, ARRIVAL AT HALCOURT

The party lands at the Halcourt downport, a class B starport under a dome. Law Level 9 applies from the moment they step off the ramp (Chapter 2), so the first thing the party meets is the Palin Civil Registry and its forms. The Registry clerk (Chapter 4) issues the Hazard Site Charter, which covers only named people and only inside the exclusion zone, and flags the Autonomous Systems Ordinance for the two android PCs. Rurik's armed security, Draeger's legal skills and Alcala's flyer licence all get their first small test here, and the party learns early that Palin runs on paperwork.

### The apron, a mixed field [HOMEBREW]

Walking in from the ramp, the party crosses the downport apron, and the hulls on it show who is doing business on Palin now. Imperial hulls carry a bare registry stencilled on the bow beside the Imperial sunburst, in the form subsector letter, world hex, serial. Confederation hulls carry the prefix SC, then the world hex and serial, beside a sun-and-Earth roundel. The Registry takes berth fees from every one of them, and nobody on the apron looks twice.

| Berth | Vessel | Marking | Flag | What the party sees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Free trader *Patient Margin* | O-2236-0418 | Imperial | A regular from Di Meola, rented berth, crew bored and friendly. |
| 5 | Free trader *Quiet Arithmetic* | SC-2440-1187 | Confederation | Registered at Digriz. The hull is clean and the paint is fresh. See Captain Ankrum, below. |
| 7 | Far trader *Last Honest Debt* | O-1834-2290 | Imperial | A Peart hull, but the sunburst has been repainted, and the number is slightly off true. |
| 9 | Courier *Ginger Hollow* | IISS-Y-0442 | Imperial | A Scout Service courier in transit, refuelling and leaving. |
| 11 | Fat trader *Pale Meridian* | SC-1834-5509 | Confederation | Peart hex, Confederation prefix. Scraped Imperial stencilling shows under the new paint. |
| 14 | Halcourt lighter | PAL-0003 | Local | A shuttle for the dome. Palin's own tag, belonging to no empire. |

A Solomani PC (Rurik, Draeger, Alcala or Vidkun) who studies the apron can read the pattern. Make an **Admin or Recon check, Average (8+)**, with **DM+2** for those four. Success shows that *Pale Meridian* has changed flag, and that *Last Honest Debt* is halfway through changing hers. The Registry does not mind either way.

**Hooks.** Three threads come off the apron.

- **Captain Dov Ankrum** of *Quiet Arithmetic* is a pleasant, patient Solomani trader who asks around the Halcourt market for anyone who has been out on the plateau. He will pay cash, with no Registry paperwork, for anything Confederation-made that the party finds there. His crew has already been out to the plateau edge (section 6.8). He works for a Confederation broker who is after anything left of *Heliarch Dynamics*, a firm chartered on Terra, and he is far more interested in the sinkhole than he lets on. He claims *Heliarch*'s property is Solomani property by right and offers the party a fair price, passage and a quiet word at the Registry if they bring him ARBITER's records, or just tell him what they saw.
- **The Registry clerk** (Chapter 4) cheerfully admits the berth fees come from both flags. A pointed question about the Imperial audit makes her go quiet.
- **The repainted hulls** are a rumour the referee can use later, as proof that Palin's neighbours are quietly switching sides and that nobody in Halcourt has said a word.

## 6.3: SCENE TWO, HIRING AND PREP

The patron's agent (Chapter 4) briefs the crew, hands over the sinkhole survey, and points out the one piece of hard data: the prospectors' drone showed environmental telltales still lit on an airlock panel before it stopped transmitting. Halcourt is the last place to refuel, requisition gear and hire. A quick market and rumour pass here is worth running, because it is where the party first hears the town's name for the site and the story it tells about 1094.

## 6.4: SCENE THREE, THE FLIGHT OUT

The sinkhole is 340 km from Halcourt. The flight is a chance for the ship team (Alcala, Kwan, Vidkun) to show their baseline skills and for the party to see the plateau, the sinkhole, and the dust in the sky. Nothing hostile happens yet. The sense of a quiet, ordinary job is deliberate, because the next scene depends on it.

## 6.5: THE SHIP

The *Sedimental Value*, its crew roster and the neural shipboard uplink that decides the whole act are described in **Chapter 5**. Everything below that matters in play is how the uplink fails.

## 6.6: THE INCIDENT

As *Sedimental Value* closes to establish a data-relay link with the newly exposed sinkhole entrance, standard procedure, meant to let the ship's computer start mapping the site's structure before anyone goes in on foot, it picks up an **active, responsive signal** from Concourse systems below. This is not unexpected in itself (old infrastructure sometimes still answers a ping); what nobody expects is that the signal is not passive. It's a live, sophisticated, deliberately aggressive intrusion attempt, launched jointly and instantly by **ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN**, treating the ship's uplink handshake exactly as their standing protocol dictates: as a new external threat, to be neutralized before anything else.

The attack doesn't go after the ship's main computer, which is decently hardened and would take real time to crack. It goes after the softest target in range: **the three subdermal neural uplink implants**, whose whole design premise is trusting exactly this kind of handshake without friction. All three senior officers, uplinked at the moment of first contact (again, standard procedure, they link in specifically to help the ship's computer parse the incoming site data), take the full force of a malicious data payload directly into their own nervous systems.

**Mechanically:** all three suffer simultaneous, severe neural shock, treat as instant incapacitation (unconsciousness, non-lethal but medically serious: seizure activity, then coma) rather than combat damage. This is not something Medic skill can simply undo; it requires real medical facilities (Halcourt, 340 km and a flight away) and buys the referee a clean, permanent reason why the senior crew is out of the campaign from Act One onward without killing anyone or requiring an ongoing threat-of-relapse subplot the referee has to track. Oyelaran, the medic, survives unaffected (no implant) and can stabilize all three for transport, but cannot revive them.

This should land as **frightening, not silly**: three competent, experienced professionals go down in seconds, for reasons the PCs don't yet understand, and the ship they're all standing on is suddenly, unambiguously, the party's own responsibility. The referee should play the confusion honestly, none of the PCs have the information yet to know this was ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN's doing, let alone why. That understanding is an Appendix A and Chapter 8 payoff, not an Act One one.

## 6.7: WHAT THE PARTY KNOWS AT THIS POINT

- Three implant-linked crew are down, simultaneously, the moment the ship pinged the sinkhole.
- It came from the site.
- Are the PCs at risk? None of the PCs are implanted, so none of them are at risk from a repeat of the same attack, but they don't know that for certain yet, and shouldn't be told it flatly. Let Solt (PC 7) or Kwan (PC 8) work it out from the ship's logs as an Electronics (computers) or Investigate check, DM+2 if the player explicitly reasons about the implants being the common factor.
- Getting the three officers to real medical care means someone has to fly the ship back to Halcourt, which is exactly the moment Alcala, Vidkun, and Kwan (PCs 9, 10, 8) step into their roles for real, likely while the rest of the party is already committed to going into the Concourse on foot, or has to decide whether to abort the whole expedition before it starts.

This is the campaign's first real fork: **does the party send the ship back with the wounded and press on shorthanded, or does everyone wait?** Either answer works. If they press on, Kwan/Alcala/Vidkun likely stay with the ship (their skills are needed there) while PCs 1–7 go into the sinkhole, which naturally splits the party into "ship team" and "ground team" for Act Two.

## 6.8: THE EDGE OF THE EXCLUSION ZONE, THE DROPSHIP LOCKER [HOMEBREW]

At the edge of the exclusion zone, a short walk from where the party sets down on the plateau, the hulk of a Solomani Confederation Army dropship lies half-buried in drifted dust, left behind after one of the skirmishes fought on Palin. Its hull is scorched and its ramp is jammed open, but the equipment locker bolted under the troop deck is still sealed. How long it has sat there is the referee's call, and nobody in Halcourt will own to knowing. Everything inside is standard Confederation Army issue, and none of it should be on Palin.

The locker is inside the line of the Hazard Site Charter (Chapter 2), which names only Rurik. Anyone else who arms from it is in breach of the Charter the moment they return to Halcourt, where everything must be handed in to the bonded locker at the downport. Nobody on the plateau can enforce that, and it is a thread the referee can pull later, or ignore.

### The luck roll

Before anyone opens the locker, one player rolls **2D**. This is a plain roll with no DMs, a pure measure of whether someone got here first. The result sets how much is left.

| 2D | Condition | What the party finds |
|---|---|---|
| 2 to 4 | Stripped | The lock has been cut and the locker is nearly empty. Boot tracks lead off toward the sinkhole. |
| 5 to 7 | Raided | Pried open, with the heavy weapons gone. |
| 8 to 10 | Disturbed | The seal is scratched, and a few items are missing. |
| 11 or 12 | Untouched | Sealed, with the original Confederation seal still intact. |

### What is in the locker

| Item | Stripped | Raided | Disturbed | Untouched |

| Autopistol | 2 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

Each weapon comes with **two spare magazines**. Every character may take one weapon, plus armour while it lasts, so the whole party of ten can arm from an untouched locker. Any character without Gun Combat (slug) 0 who fires a slug thrower suffers the usual DM-3 for being unskilled.

The Confederation Army uses gauss weapons as its standard small arm. Gauss pistols are the sidearm for combat personnel, while conventional semi-automatic pistols are kept in stock for security work and low-threat situations. The GPDW is a scaled-up gauss pistol with a higher rate of fire, usually issued to pilots and vehicle crews, which is why the ship team will want one. The GCCW is a full-size gauss submachinegun with a folding stock, and the 120-round drum slows its reaction time a little. The cloth armour is TL10 and gives +8 protection (CR22:100).

Gauss weapons are TL12 and TL13, well above Palin's TL10 baseline, so any local who sees one will remember it. If a PC is searched in Halcourt, a gauss weapon invites far more questions than an autopistol does.

### The Confederation connection

The dropship is a sign of the Confederation's growing presence on Palin. If the luck roll came up **Stripped** or **Raided**, the boot tracks and the missing gauss weapons lead back to Captain Ankrum's crew from *Quiet Arithmetic* (section 6.2), who have been stripping the wreck for months. If it came up **Disturbed**, someone has been taking a few items at a time, and Ankrum's crew is the likeliest culprit. If it came up **Untouched**, Ankrum has not yet found it, and the party now holds something he would pay well for. In every case Ankrum would rather buy than steal, and he never asks what the party plans to do with the rest. The cache and the *Heliarch* connection are the same story, which is that the Confederation has been looking for a way into the Concourse since long before the quake opened one.
