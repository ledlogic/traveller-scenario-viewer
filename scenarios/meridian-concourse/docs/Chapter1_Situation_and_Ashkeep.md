## THE HOOK

Ashkeep is Imperial territory, and has been since long before anyone currently living there was born. That's most of what recommends it.

The world sits on the So Skire side of the Dagir border — nominally under full Third Imperium administration, in practice under almost no administration at all. Its one settlement, **Halcourt**, exists because the Imperium needed *something* on this stretch of frontier during the Solomani Rim War — a picket world, a supply depot, briefly a holding facility for detainees nobody wanted processed anywhere more visible — and never quite got around to deciding what it should become afterward. Locals have called the town "Purgatory" for two generations, half as a joke.

For a stretch of thirty years, Ashkeep's only real economic activity was **Meridian Field** — a joint civil/commercial airport-and-bazaar complex on the equatorial plateau, built to service atmospheric flyer traffic between Halcourt and the handful of mining claims scattered across the highlands. In its last years of operation, Meridian Field also did something the Imperial administrator's office never quite noticed happening under its nose: it hosted the arbitration courts, brokers, and cargo bays through which two rival robotics conglomerates — one Solomani, one Imperial-backed — settled their manufacturing disputes, specifically *because* neither trusted the other's home courts, and specifically because nobody on Ashkeep was paying close enough attention to ask why a Solomani firm was running hardware trials on Imperial soil in the first place.

That arrangement ended badly. In 1094 the corporate rivalry that had been fought in patent offices and boardrooms for fifteen years came out from under the table. What happened at Meridian Field over the following eleven days is detailed for the referee in **Chapter 2**. What matters for the players is simpler: the Concourse collapsed, the crater it left was declared a hazard exclusion zone by an Imperial administrator with every incentive to make the paperwork as boring as possible, and Ashkeep went back to being a world nobody thinks about.

Then, six weeks ago, a marsquake along the Halcourt Rift fault line dropped forty metres of regolith into a sinkhole that hadn't been there the week before — and exposed a clean, intact section of pressurized concourse roofing, undamaged, dark, and (according to the drone the local prospecting syndicate flew through the gap before it stopped transmitting) still showing environmental telltales on the airlock control panel.

**Someone is paying very well for the first professional team down there.**

Imperial administrative interest on Ashkeep remains thin — one appointed governor, a small garrison, and a rotating Scout liaison who mostly handles weather relay — which is exactly why a private expedition is being allowed to go in first, quietly, before anyone with actual authority has to have an opinion about what's down there. SolSec, for its part, maintains an unofficial watch post in Halcourt: tolerated, not welcomed, by the Imperial governor, because everyone with a long memory remembers exactly what a Solomani company operating unsupervised on this world did the last time.

Ashkeep's surface is red-brown regolith and iron-oxide dust under a thin, cold sky — travellers who have been to old Terra's Mars expect the resemblance and get it. Wind is the dominant hazard: dust storms can drop visibility to arm's length and static-charge exposed equipment badly enough to fry unshielded electronics. The equatorial plateau where Meridian Field once stood is otherwise unremarkable flat basalt, chosen originally because it made an easy strip for winged atmospheric flyers.

**Halcourt** (pop. ~35,000) is 340 km northeast: domed, dependent on imported water ice from the poles, built outward from what was originally a Rim War garrison-and-depot footprint and never really replanned since. It houses the Imperial governor's small administrative staff, a rotating Navy/Scout liaison detachment, the descendants of the original garrison and support workers who simply never left, and — since Meridian Field went dark — an economy that has never fully recovered from losing its one real transhipment point. The town has one thing going for it now: it is the closest place to refuel, requisition gear, and hire.

## MISSION OBJECTIVES

The expedition has been chartered (by a patron of the referee's choosing — a salvage consortium, an insurance syndicate settling a century-old claim, an Imperial heritage-recovery office with an unusually specific interest, or the corporate rep's own employer; see **Chapter 3, Character 4**) with three stated objectives, in priority order:

1. **Establish what happened.** Eleven days of corporate war destroyed Meridian Field in 1094. No official inquiry was ever completed — the companies involved settled quietly with a cooperative Imperial administrator, sealed the site, and the paper trail goes cold. Someone now wants a real answer: was it sabotage, a runaway arms race, or something that got out of anyone's control? (See **Chapter 2** — the referee knows the truth; the players should have to dig for it.)

2. **Assess what remains.** A century-plus-old commercial concourse, sealed and largely undisturbed, is worth a fortune in salvage rights alone — if it's stable, and if whatever's still running down there can be dealt with.

3. **Determine viability for reopening.** The patron's real interest (revealed as the campaign progresses) is whether Meridian Field's substructure — including its still-functional, if hostile, computer cores — could be recovered rather than demolished.

The referee should let the players discover, over the course of the campaign, that objectives 2 and 3 are in quiet tension: the patron would very much like the two mainframes described in **Appendix A** captured intact rather than destroyed, and has not been fully honest with the crew about that.

## STRUCTURE

This campaign is written for a full ten-character crew (**Chapter 3**) built around the twin conceits that (a) the party's own ship's professional crew is taken out of action almost immediately, for reasons tied directly to the site's central threat (**Chapter 4**), and (b) every player character has some baseline capacity to keep that ship running regardless. This means the Concourse delve and the ship itself both stay in play as connected problems throughout — a party that mismanages the ship is stuck on Ashkeep with everyone else who's ever been stranded there.

Recommended act structure:

- **Act One — Arrival & the Silence:** Transit to Ashkeep, hiring/prep at Halcourt, the flight out to the sinkhole, and the incident that disables the ship's professional crew (Chapter 4).
- **Act Two — Concourse Approach:** Entry, the shellworm-infested flyer graveyard, first contact with automated Concourse systems, and the Miner and Safety Officer earning their keep as two genuinely separate disciplines (Chapter 5, Appendix B).
- **Act Three — The Arbitration Floor:** Reaching the two mainframes, learning the true history (Chapter 2 payoff), and the players' choice about what to do with ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN (Appendix A).
- **Act Four — Extraction (or Occupation):** Getting out — or deciding to stay and hold the site, which changes the campaign's shape entirely.

Referees should feel free to run this as a single long session (4–6 hours) hitting the beats of all four acts, or expand each act into its own session for a proper campaign.
