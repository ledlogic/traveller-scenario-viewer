# CHAPTER 5, ACT I: ARRIVAL AND THE SILENCE

## 5.1: ACT OVERVIEW

Act I takes the party from the Halcourt downport to a ship that is suddenly theirs alone. It has four beats: arrival and the Hazard Site Charter, hiring and prep, the flight out to the sinkhole, and the incident that takes the senior crew out of the campaign. The act ends on the campaign's first fork, covered at the end of this chapter.

## 5.2: SCENE ONE, ARRIVAL AT HALCOURT

The party lands at the Halcourt downport, a class B starport under a dome. Law Level 9 applies from the moment they step off the ramp (Chapter 2), so the first thing the party meets is the Palin Civil Registry and its forms. The Registry clerk (Chapter 4) issues the Hazard Site Charter, which covers only named people and only inside the exclusion zone, and flags the Autonomous Systems Ordinance for the two android PCs. Rurik's armed security, Draeger's legal skills and Alcala's flyer licence all get their first small test here, and the party learns early that Palin runs on paperwork.

## 5.3: SCENE TWO, HIRING AND PREP

The patron's agent (Chapter 4) briefs the crew, hands over the sinkhole survey, and points out the one piece of hard data: the prospectors' drone showed environmental telltales still lit on an airlock panel before it stopped transmitting. Halcourt is the last place to refuel, requisition gear and hire. A quick market and rumour pass here is worth running, because it is where the party first hears the town's name for the site and the story it tells about 1094.

## 5.4: SCENE THREE, THE FLIGHT OUT

The sinkhole is 340 km from Halcourt. The flight is a chance for the ship team (Alcala, Kwan, Vidkun) to show their baseline skills and for the party to see the plateau, the sinkhole, and the dust in the sky. Nothing hostile happens yet. The sense of a quiet, ordinary job is deliberate, because the next scene depends on it.

## 5.5: THE SHIP: *SEDIMENTAL VALUE* (Modified Far Trader, TL12)

A chartered 200-ton far trader, refitted for survey/salvage work: extra cargo winching gear, a small drone bay, and reinforced landing gear for rough-field work on worlds like Palin. Owned and operated by an independent charter contractor out of Halcourt, hired for this run by the expedition's patron. Full stats are left to the referee's preferred trader template (Core Rulebook Update 2022 has a suitable baseline); what matters for this campaign is the crew roster and the one system modification below.

**Standard crew (NPCs) at expedition start:** Captain and Senior Pilot Aveline Devereux-Sato, Senior Engineer Bram Voskuijlen and Medic and Steward Hana Oyelaran. All three are described in **Chapter 4**.

**The one relevant refit, Neural Shipboard Uplink:** *Sedimental Value*, like most modern charter vessels running hazardous or time-critical work, has its three senior officers fitted with subdermal shipboard uplink implants, a standard, unremarkable, entirely legal augmentation that lets a rated officer interface directly with ship's systems (helm, engineering diagnostics, astrogation plot) without a physical console, cutting critical response time in an emergency. It is considered good practice, not exotic technology, and none of the ten PCs have it: junior/backup specialists on a charter this size are never issued the implant, both because of cost and because the whole point of the Hazardous Charter Standard Training doctrine (Chapter 3) is that backup crew should be able to function *without* relying on ship-integrated augmentation, precisely in case something happens to the people who do.

Something happens to the people who do.

## 5.6: THE INCIDENT

As *Sedimental Value* closes to establish a data-relay link with the newly exposed sinkhole entrance, standard procedure, meant to let the ship's computer start mapping the site's structure before anyone goes in on foot, it picks up an **active, responsive signal** from Concourse systems below. This is not unexpected in itself (old infrastructure sometimes still answers a ping); what nobody expects is that the signal is not passive. It's a live, sophisticated, deliberately aggressive intrusion attempt, launched jointly and instantly by **ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN**, treating the ship's uplink handshake exactly as their standing protocol dictates: as a new external threat, to be neutralized before anything else.

The attack doesn't go after the ship's main computer, which is decently hardened and would take real time to crack. It goes after the softest target in range: **the three subdermal neural uplink implants**, whose whole design premise is trusting exactly this kind of handshake without friction. All three senior officers, uplinked at the moment of first contact (again, standard procedure, they link in specifically to help the ship's computer parse the incoming site data), take the full force of a malicious data payload directly into their own nervous systems.

**Mechanically:** all three suffer simultaneous, severe neural shock, treat as instant incapacitation (unconsciousness, non-lethal but medically serious: seizure activity, then coma) rather than combat damage. This is not something Medic skill can simply undo; it requires real medical facilities (Halcourt, 340 km and a flight away) and buys the referee a clean, permanent reason why the senior crew is out of the campaign from Act One onward without killing anyone or requiring an ongoing threat-of-relapse subplot the referee has to track. Oyelaran, the medic, survives unaffected (no implant) and can stabilize all three for transport, but cannot revive them.

This should land as **frightening, not silly**: three competent, experienced professionals go down in seconds, for reasons the PCs don't yet understand, and the ship they're all standing on is suddenly, unambiguously, the party's own responsibility. The referee should play the confusion honestly, none of the PCs have the information yet to know this was ARBITER and COUNTERSIGN's doing, let alone why. That understanding is an Appendix A and Chapter 7 payoff, not an Act One one.

## 5.7: WHAT THE PARTY KNOWS AT THIS POINT

- Three implant-linked crew are down, simultaneously, the moment the ship pinged the sinkhole.
- Whatever did it came *from* the site, not from anywhere else.
- None of the PCs are implanted, so none of them are at risk from a repeat of the same attack, but they don't know that for certain yet, and shouldn't be told it flatly. Let Solt (PC 7) or Kwan (PC 8) work it out from the ship's logs as an Electronics (computers) or Investigate check, DM+2 if the player explicitly reasons about the implants being the common factor.
- Getting the three officers to real medical care means someone has to fly the ship back to Halcourt, which is exactly the moment Alcala, Vidkun, and Kwan (PCs 9, 10, 8) step into their roles for real, likely while the rest of the party is already committed to going into the Concourse on foot, or has to decide whether to abort the whole expedition before it starts.

This is the campaign's first real fork: **does the party send the ship back with the wounded and press on shorthanded, or does everyone wait?** Either answer works. If they press on, Kwan/Alcala/Vidkun likely stay with the ship (their skills are needed there) while PCs 1–7 go into the sinkhole, which naturally splits the party into "ship team" and "ground team" for Act Two, giving every character real material either way.
