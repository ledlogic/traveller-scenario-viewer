# TRAVELLER: ARE OLD FRIENDS ELECTRIC
## Chapter 3: Act One: The Crossing

*Mongoose Traveller 2nd Edition · Ready-to-Play*

---

> Act One is the border run: out of Alizarin, across the Rimward Gap by way of Krypton, and into Imperial Albadawi (Chapter 1, §1.5). It should take a real chunk of the table's four hours, but it isn't padding. It's where the players find out what *this specific crew* is actually like under pressure, before the facility on Halo asks the same question with worse stakes.

---

## 3.1: THE ROUTE

*Terminal Leave* (Appendix B) has a Jump-3 drive, and she needs all of it. The Rimward Gap is a real void, measured on the sector grid from the world tables (SF:176, SF:206). The nearest Imperial worlds to Alizarin and Krypton are Bajavanang (0628) and Kidashi (0528): both are four parsecs from Alizarin and three from Krypton, and nothing in Jardin is closer than four. A Jump-2 ship cannot cross at all. A Jump-3 ship can cross only by jumping from Krypton.

**The direct run:**

| Leg | Distance | Jump | Fuel burned | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alizarin to Krypton | 1 parsec | J1 | 20 tons | Class A starport and a Confederation naval base. Visit as a civilian trader, refuel, leave |
| Krypton to Bajavanang | 3 parsecs | J3 | 60 of 63 tons | The crossing. No margin: the tanks are empty on arrival until she skims |
| Bajavanang to Halo | 3 parsecs | J3 | 60 of 63 tons | Bajavanang is Imperium, Class A, gas giant, no base listed. Halo has a gas giant too |

Kidashi (also three parsecs from Krypton) has an Imperial naval base, so Bajavanang is the safer landing. Three jumps, three weeks, each one a full week and a chance for something to go wrong.

**The Sionnach dogleg.** Sionnach is four parsecs from both Alizarin and Krypton, so it cannot sit on the direct run. Reaching it means Alizarin to Cadmus (3 parsecs, J3), Cadmus to Sionnach (1 parsec, J1), and back through Cadmus to Krypton (3 parsecs, J3), which adds three jumps for six weeks in all. Wei's reason for it is simple: one fuel stop where no Confederation or Imperial authority keeps logs, before the crossing. If the table wants a faster game, run the Sionnach scene at Cadmus's gas giant instead and keep the direct run; nothing in §3.2 to §3.5 depends on which it is.

The referee doesn't need to run every jump in real time. Suggested structure for a 4-hour slot:

1. **Departure and the first hops.** Skip ahead, or play the packing and departure scene here if the table wants a slower opening (a good place for banter, last-minute doubts, and Wei seeing them off if he's travelling separately).
2. **The Sionnach stop (§3.2).** The set-piece. Play this in full.
3. **The crossing from Krypton.** Skip ahead again unless the table wants a second, quieter scene. Yatika plotting a 3-parsec jump with 60 of 63 tons of fuel committed, and no way to refuel until the far side, is a good beat for anyone who wants a non-combat highlight.

## 3.2: THE SIONNACH STOP

**Sionnach** (Jardin Subsector, hex 0632), UWP C533369-A, Trade Codes *Low Population, Poor*, Solomani Confederation allegiance, one gas giant (SF:206). It sits inside what the sourcebook itself calls the **Jardin-Cluster region**, the neglected rimward end of the subsector, "of little importance," starved of investment for a century (SF:208). That same passage names the **Jardin Syndicate**, organised crime and piracy operating openly enough that SolSec has an active, slow-moving investigation into it (SF:208). Nobody's coming to help out here. That's the whole point of stopping here to skim fuel quietly.

*Terminal Leave* is streamlined, so her fuel scoops are built in and her fuel processor (Appendix B, §B.2.1) refines what she gathers, but at 200 tons with Thrust 3 she is not a graceful skimmer, and the run takes hours, not minutes. Call for a Routine (6+) Pilot check from Farrow to hold position properly. On a failure, nothing dangerous happens yet, just extra time at the giant, which is exactly how the *Fighting Launch* below gets close enough to matter.

## 3.3: "TOLL COLLECTOR": TAVISH RENNER

While the tanks fill, a **Fighting Launch** (SCC:58, 20 tons, single fixed-mount pulse laser, Thrust 5) drops out from behind the gas giant's ring debris, using the clutter as sensor cover the way local pirates always do here. It's crewed by three people in a boat designed for one pilot; two of them are visibly riding in what used to be cargo space.

**Tavish Renner**, 23, hails them: cocky, a little too loud, clearly rehearsed. He wants a "toll" for "safe passage through Syndicate space." He has looked at an unarmoured-looking old tramp trader crewed, as far as his sensors can tell, by a handful of pensioners, and concluded this is the easiest work he's done all month. Full stats, equipment, and background for Tavish and his two crew: **Appendix C, §C.2–C.3**.

His two crew are worse: Gun Combat 0, no specialisation, along for the intimidation factor and nothing else (Appendix C, §C.3). This is not a dangerous crew. It is a **loud** one.

**Referee note, the whole point of this scene:** every one of *Terminal Leave*'s six has more real skill, more real nerve, and (Vosloo notwithstanding) usually a lower Physical DM than Tavish's crew have raw stat. Play that contrast for real. He is not a joke to be laughed off-page; he is a genuinely under-experienced young man who has badly misjudged exactly one detail, who he's talking to, and that misjudgment should land with weight, not just comedy, once it becomes clear to him.

## 3.4: HOW IT CAN GO

Offer the table real choices; don't railroad toward one outcome.

**Talk him down.** Persuade or Intimidate, Average (8+). Antonella's the obvious lead (Persuade 3), but let any PC try. A clean success gets Tavish to back off with his dignity nominally intact and a story he'll tell for years about the old trader crew that didn't even blink. A failure doesn't have to mean combat; it can mean he digs in, postures harder, and forces the next beat.

**Split the orbiter.** Farrow can detach the 10-ton orbiter (Appendix B, §B.2.2, 1D rounds) and put a second hull between Tavish and the rest of the crew. It is Thrust 4 against the Fighting Launch's Thrust 5 and has no weapon, so it works as a decoy and a shield, not a pursuit craft, a good use of it, and a good way to show the table that this old ship has tricks.

**Fight.** If Tavish opens fire or tries to force a boarding, resolve it fast; this should take one or two exchanges, not a grinding space battle. *Terminal Leave*'s dorsal double turret (Vosloo, Gunner (turret) 3, beam lasers at DM+4 to hit) against one fixed pulse laser crewed by Gun Combat 1 is not a fair fight, and it shouldn't feel like one. If he tries to physically board instead, Delacroix and Komatsubara are both more than capable of handling three overconfident young men in a cargo hold: Melee (unarmed) 1, Athletics, and decades of not being where an attacker expects them to be will do the rest. Nobody on this crew needs to be reminded they're old. Play the fight as *efficient*, not effortless; the aged characteristics matter on the dice, but so does forty years of knowing exactly where to stand.

**Recognise him.** At any point, before, during, or after, a PC who asks Tavish his family name, or simply looks hard at him during the standoff, may make an Average (8+) Recon or Investigate check (or the referee can simply hand this to Antonella as a freebie the moment his surname comes up over comms). Success: **Renner**. Antonella ran an asset named Absalom Renner on this stretch of border more than forty years ago, a courier, reliable, useful, quietly protected for years, and then simply *not*, when the border stabilized and the Directorate's attention moved somewhere that mattered more. Tavish is Absalom's grandson. Nobody ever came back for this family. This corner of Jardin is what's left when the Confederation stops needing a place.

---

## 3.5: AFTER

There is no mechanically "correct" ending to this scene, and the referee shouldn't manufacture one. Some tables will let Tavish go with a warning and whatever spare credits are lying around. Some will have Antonella give him a name to call if he's ever actually in trouble, knowing perfectly well she has no institutional authority left to back that promise up. Some tables will fight first and only find out who he is afterward, which changes the taste of the fight retroactively rather than the outcome. All of these are the right answer for different tables; this scene exists to tell the players something true about the world Wei's operation is quietly a symptom of, not to hand them a problem with a clean solution.

Whatever happens, *Terminal Leave* finishes refueling and jumps on toward Albadawi. The crossing isn't over. It's just the first thing that went wrong.
