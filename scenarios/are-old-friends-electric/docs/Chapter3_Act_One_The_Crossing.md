# TRAVELLER: ARE OLD FRIENDS ELECTRIC
## Chapter 3: Act One: The Crossing

*Mongoose Traveller 2nd Edition · Ready-to-Play*

---

> Act One is the border run: out of Alizarin, across the Rimward Gap by way of Krypton, and into Imperial Albadawi (Chapter 1, §1.5). It should take a real chunk of the table's four hours, but it isn't padding. It's where the players find out what *this specific crew* is actually like under pressure, before the facility on Halo asks the same question with worse stakes.

---

## 3.1: THE ROUTE

*Terminal Leave* (Appendix B) has a Jump-3 drive, and she needs all of it. The Rimward Gap is a real void, measured on the sector grid from the world tables (SF:176, SF:206). The nearest Imperial worlds to Alizarin and Krypton are Bajavanang (0628) and Kidashi (0528): both are four parsecs from Alizarin and three from Krypton, and nothing in Jardin is closer than four. A Jump-2 ship cannot cross at all. A Jump-3 ship can cross only by jumping from Krypton.

**The direct run:**

| Leg | Distance | Jump | Fuel burned | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alizarin to Krypton | 1 parsec | J1 | 20 tons | Class A starport and a Confederation naval base. Visit as a civilian trader, refuel, leave |
| Krypton to Bajavanang | 3 parsecs | J3 | 60 of 63 tons | The crossing. No margin: the tanks are empty on arrival until she skims |
| Bajavanang to Halo | 3 parsecs | J3 | 60 of 63 tons | Bajavanang is Imperium, Class A, gas giant, no base listed. Arriving there leaves only the plant fuel, so she must skim before this leg (§3.3). Halo has a gas giant too |

Kidashi (also three parsecs from Krypton) has an Imperial naval base, so Bajavanang is the safer landing. It is not a new road. In 1117 the Imperial Admiralty reported the first Solomani fleet jumping from Krypton through Kidashi into Albadawi, the same three-parsec leg, as the opening move of the Rim War's renewal (C31). Three jumps, three weeks, each one a full week and a chance for something to go wrong.

The referee doesn't need to run every jump in real time. Suggested structure for a 4-hour slot:

1. **Departure and Krypton (§3.2).** Skip ahead, or play the packing and departure scene here if the table wants a slower opening (a good place for banter, last-minute doubts, and Wei seeing them off if he's travelling separately). Krypton is a Confederation naval base: they pass through as a civilian trader and leave with full tanks.
2. **The crossing from Krypton.** Skip ahead unless the table wants a quieter scene. Yatika plotting a 3-parsec jump with 60 of 63 tons of fuel committed, and no way to refuel until the far side, is a good beat for anyone who wants a non-combat highlight.
3. **The Bajavanang skim (§3.3).** The set-piece. Play this in full.

## 3.2: KRYPTON: THE LAST FRIENDLY PORT

**Krypton** (Albadawi Subsector, hex 0330), UWP A9A49BC-D, Trade Codes *Fluid Oceans, High Population, High Technology*, Solomani Confederation allegiance, one gas giant, naval base (SF:176). Class A starport, Law Level C. It is a restrictive dictatorship run by a small ruling class of Party officials with strong social controls, and its Home Forces are large, well equipped and politically trusted: their soldiers wear "more like SolSec than SolSec" as a badge of honour (SF:209). The naval base keeps a modest patrol force of frigates and a couple of light carriers working the cluster and the worlds near it (SF:209). It is also where the Confederation's first fleet across the border in 1117 jumped from (C31).

It is also the last place on this run that is theirs. Akio was born here (Chapter 2, §2.7).

**The fuel problem.** *Terminal Leave* leaves Alizarin with 63 tons and burns 20 on the one-parsec hop, so she reaches Krypton with 43. The crossing needs 60. She has to take on at least 17 tons.

| Option | Cost | What it risks |
|---|---|---|
| Dock at the downport | Refined fuel, paid for | Starport logs, customs at Law Level C, and Home Forces and SolSec eyes on the dock |
| Skim the gas giant | Free, plus about ten hours of processing for 17 tons at 40 tons a day (Appendix B) | No port record, but a lone ship skimming near the border is exactly what the patrols watch for |

**Skimming is the better answer, and the one the crew will want.** *Terminal Leave* is streamlined, so her scoops are built in, and the procedure and the Pilot check are the same as at Bajavanang (§3.3). Their paper cover (Appendix B) is a tramp hauling agricultural goods around the Rimward Gap, so a token cargo of Alizarin produce and a story about a trade run to Bajavanang is ordinary, and it keeps Halo out of every record.

**What they might encounter.** Roll 1D for whichever they chose.

If they **skim**:

| 1D | Encounter |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | A patrol hail |
| 3 | The Syndicate's boat |
| 4-6 | Quiet |

If they **dock**:

| 1D | Encounter |
|---|---|
| 1 | The family on the dock |
| 2 | An old comrade |
| 3 | A SolSec watcher |
| 4 | Customs and the weapons seal |
| 5-6 | Quiet |

**A patrol hail.** A Home Forces frigate on its cluster patrol closes to ask what a Jump-3 tramp is doing alone at the gas giant. The officers are polite, thorough and untroubled, the manner of a force that believes itself beyond reproach. They want a flight plan, a manifest and a reason for skimming instead of buying fuel at the downport. A sound answer (an Alizarin tramp saving credits before a trade run to Bajavanang) is a Routine (6+) Deception, Persuade or Advocate check, and Antonella's Persuade is as good as the crew has. Failure means a boarding inspection of 1D hours. Nothing illegal is aboard, but the Jump-3 drive, the crew's ages and their former callsigns invite questions, and most of a day is gone. These are not enemies. The crew simply cannot tell them the truth.

**The Syndicate's boat.** A battered free trader skims the same gas giant a few hundred thousand kilometres off, transponder dark. It is a people-mover from the Jardin Syndicate's pipeline, which sells persecuted non-Solomani humans low-berth passage to "freedom" and jobs across the border (SF:103). An Average (8+) Streetwise or Investigate check identifies it. If the crew reports it, the patrol arrives and the crew is back under the frigate's attention. If they ignore it, nothing happens, but the other end of that pipeline is waiting at Bajavanang (§3.3), and the table should remember this boat when they get there.

**The family on the dock.** A non-Solomani human family, four of them, has paid the Syndicate for a low berth to "freedom" and been left standing on the dock with nothing (SF:103). They approach the crew because *Terminal Leave* is a tramp bound for the border with an old crew that looks kind. The ship has six staterooms for six crew and no low berths (Appendix B), and a mission this deniable cannot carry passengers or witnesses. The right answer is probably no, and the scene is about how that feels. What they say on the way out is the first real warning: the people who take you across take your last credit, and they wait for you on the other side.

**An old comrade.** Akio served five terms with the Krypton Home Forces (Chapter 2, §2.7). A retired sergeant major who served under him, or an officer on the dock who learned first aid from him, recognises "Patch" before the crew has noticed anyone. It is warm, loud and hard to refuse: an invitation to the mess, a toast, an hour. The danger is not malice. A force proud of knowing everything about everyone now knows that Akio Komatsubara is back on Krypton with five old friends and a Jump-3 hull, headed for the border.

**A SolSec watcher.** SolSec has an office on any world this close to the border. A duty officer who worked under Antonella in her Administrator years (1110 to 1126) sees her name on the arrivals list: Captain Interlenghi, retired, aboard a civilian tramp. It may be friendly curiosity or professional suspicion, and she knows exactly how this officer thinks. Wei's job is deniable only if the Directorate never hears of it, so she needs an easy lie (Average 8+ Deception or Persuade), or a friend she can quietly use later.

**Customs and the weapons seal.** At Law Level C, the downport will expect personal weapons declared and sealed aboard for the stay (a referee's ruling, not a printed rule). Hendrik's revolver, Antonella's autopistol and Akio's spear-probe all draw attention, and the dorsal turret has to be locked down. It costs time and an uncomfortable inspection, and it leaves the crew a day behind.

**Quiet.** Nothing happens. The tanks fill, the processor hums, and the crew has a few hours to look at Krypton for the last time while Yatika checks her jump plot again: 60 of 63 tons, no margin. Let the table feel the border coming.

**Referee note:** whichever way it goes, this is the last place the Confederation is at their backs. When they jump, nobody on the other side will vouch for them (§3.3).

## 3.3: THE BAJAVANANG SKIM

**Bajavanang** (Albadawi Subsector, hex 0628), UWP A5898CA-D, Trade Codes *High Technology, Rich*, Imperium allegiance, one gas giant, no base listed (SF:176). It is a rich Class A world at Law Level A, which means busy traffic, thorough Imperial customs, and an outer system that is much more closely watched than anything on the Confederation side of the Gap.

The crossing from Krypton burns 60 of *Terminal Leave*'s 63 tons of fuel. She arrives with only her plant fuel, cannot jump anywhere, and has to skim the gas giant before the last leg to Halo. The crew does not dock: a Confederation-registered tramp filing for refined fuel at an Imperial Class A starport, a week after leaving a Confederation naval base, is exactly the kind of thing customs remembers.

That is also what the Jardin Syndicate has noticed. The sourcebook says the Syndicate has spread "on both sides of the Solomani border" (SF:103), and every Confederation hull that crosses the Gap from Krypton arrives here nearly dry, in a hurry, and unable to call on Imperial police. A cell has set itself up in the outer system to charge them. Nobody on this side of the border will take the crew's part.

*Terminal Leave* is streamlined, so her fuel scoops are built in and her fuel processor (Appendix B, §B.2.1) refines what she gathers, but at 200 tons with Thrust 3 she is not a graceful skimmer, and the run takes hours, not minutes. Call for a Routine (6+) Pilot check from Farrow to hold position properly. On a failure, nothing dangerous happens yet, just extra time at the giant, which is exactly how the *Fighting Launch* below gets close enough to matter.

## 3.4: "TOLL COLLECTOR": TAVISH RENNER

While the tanks fill, a **Fighting Launch** (SCC:58, 20 tons, single fixed-mount pulse laser, Thrust 5) drops out from behind the gas giant's ring debris, using the clutter as sensor cover. It has no jump drive, so it has to be based in the system, tucked into a hidden berth in the outer belt. It's crewed by three people in a boat designed for one pilot; two of them are visibly riding in what used to be cargo space.

**Tavish Renner**, 23, hails them: cocky, a little too loud, clearly rehearsed. He wants a "toll" for "protection while you refuel." He has watched Confederation hulls limp in from the Gap for months, and he has now looked at an unarmoured-looking old tramp trader crewed, as far as his sensors can tell, by a handful of pensioners, and concluded this is the easiest work he's done all month. Full stats, equipment, and background for Tavish and his two crew: **Appendix C, §C.2–C.3**.

His two crew are worse: Gun Combat 0, no specialisation, along for the intimidation factor and nothing else (Appendix C, §C.3). This is not a dangerous crew. It is a **loud** one.

**Referee note, the whole point of this scene:** every one of *Terminal Leave*'s six has more real skill, more real nerve, and (Vosloo notwithstanding) usually a lower Physical DM than Tavish's crew have raw stat. Play that contrast for real. He is not a joke to be laughed off-page; he is a genuinely under-experienced young man who has badly misjudged exactly one detail, who he's talking to, and that misjudgment should land with weight, not just comedy, once it becomes clear to him.

---

## 3.5: HOW IT CAN GO

Offer the table real choices; don't railroad toward one outcome.

**Talk him down.** Persuade or Intimidate, Average (8+). Antonella's the obvious lead (Persuade 3), but let any PC try. A clean success gets Tavish to back off with his dignity nominally intact and a story he'll tell for years about the old trader crew that didn't even blink. A failure doesn't have to mean combat; it can mean he digs in, postures harder, and forces the next beat.

**Split the orbiter.** Farrow can detach the 10-ton orbiter (Appendix B, §B.2.2, 1D rounds) and put a second hull between Tavish and the rest of the crew. It is Thrust 4 against the Fighting Launch's Thrust 5 and has no weapon, so it works as a decoy and a shield, not a pursuit craft, a good use of it, and a good way to show the table that this old ship has tricks.

**Fight.** If Tavish opens fire or tries to force a boarding, resolve it fast; this should take one or two exchanges, not a grinding space battle. Keep it quiet as well: Bajavanang is an Imperial Class A world at Law Level A, a firefight in its outer system will draw patrol boats, and with the jump tanks near empty the crew cannot run. *Terminal Leave*'s dorsal double turret (Vosloo, Gunner (turret) 3, beam lasers at DM+4 to hit) against one fixed pulse laser crewed by Gun Combat 1 is not a fair fight, and it shouldn't feel like one. If he tries to physically board instead, Delacroix and Komatsubara are both more than capable of handling three overconfident young men in a cargo hold: Melee (unarmed) 1, Athletics, and decades of not being where an attacker expects them to be will do the rest. Nobody on this crew needs to be reminded they're old. Play the fight as *efficient*, not effortless; the aged characteristics matter on the dice, but so does forty years of knowing exactly where to stand.

**Recognise him.** At any point, before, during, or after, a PC who asks Tavish his family name, or simply looks hard at him during the standoff, may make an Average (8+) Recon or Investigate check (or the referee can simply hand this to Antonella as a freebie the moment his surname comes up over comms). Success: **Renner**. Antonella ran an asset named Absalom Renner on this stretch of border more than forty years ago, a courier, reliable, useful, quietly protected for years, and then simply *not*, when the border stabilized and the Directorate's attention moved somewhere that mattered more. Tavish is Absalom's grandson. Nobody ever came back for this family. This corner of Jardin is what's left when the Confederation stops needing a place.

---

## 3.6: AFTER

There is no mechanically "correct" ending to this scene, and the referee shouldn't manufacture one. Some tables will let Tavish go with a warning and whatever spare credits are lying around. Some will have Antonella give him a name to call if he's ever actually in trouble, knowing perfectly well she has no institutional authority left to back that promise up. Some tables will fight first and only find out who he is afterward, which changes the taste of the fight retroactively rather than the outcome. All of these are the right answer for different tables; this scene exists to tell the players something true about the world Wei's operation is quietly a symptom of, not to hand them a problem with a clean solution.

Whatever happens, *Terminal Leave* finishes refueling and jumps on to Halo. They are across the Gap now, in Imperial space, with a Syndicate cell that has seen their transponder. It's just the first thing that went wrong.
