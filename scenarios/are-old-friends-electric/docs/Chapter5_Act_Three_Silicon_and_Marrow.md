# TRAVELLER: ARE OLD FRIENDS ELECTRIC
## Chapter 5 — Act Three: Silicon and Marrow

*Mongoose Traveller 2nd Edition · Ready-to-Play*

---

> Act Three is not a bigger robot fight. It's the reason the robot fight happened at all. Play it slower than Chapter 4, and play it quiet — the horror here is closer to a containment breach than a battle. Full stats for everything in this chapter are in Appendix E.

---

## 5.1 — PAST THE DOOR

The Shadow Security Robot (Appendix D, §D.3) is the last honest thing standing between the crew and the sub-level vault — a real, purpose-built threat that behaves exactly the way something built to guard a secret should. Once it's down, disabled, evaded, or talked past (it has an Advanced brain and a working comms link; a sufficiently strange transmission or a captured access code can, in principle, call it off, at the referee's discretion), the vault door itself is almost anticlimactic: a heavy manual seal, TL10, undisturbed for decades except for one thing. **The lock has been opened and resealed recently.** Dust everywhere else. None on the handle.

Nobody has come down here casually. Somebody comes down here on a schedule.

---

## 5.2 — THE LOCKER

Just inside, in a wall alcove that predates everything TMI has built since, is a **containment equipment locker** — original 1106-era stock, TL12, sealed but intact, exactly the kind of thing a facility installs when it's honest with itself about what it's actually storing. This is not a coincidence the crew should have to work for; let them find it early in Level 3, before the vault's interior opens up.

**Contents (Robot Handbook, p. 107):**

| Weapon | Range | Damage | Traits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stun Rifle | 50m | 3D | Stun, Zero-G |
| Stun Shotgun | 10m | 3D | Blast 5, Stun, Zero-G |
| Pulse Carbine | 50m | 2D | Blast 5, Ion, Zero-G |

Three of each, TL12, still charged. These are not weapons TMI's current skeleton crew brought down here — they're original safety equipment, built for exactly the contingency the facility is supposed to have stopped needing decades ago. A referee should let that land: someone in 1106 already knew this vault might need to be fought, not just guarded.

**Why this matters mechanically (Robot Handbook, p. 106):** electromagnetic stun and ion weapons cause real, physical Hits to robotic systems — a normal robot's armour is only half-effective against them. Conventional slug and energy weapons work fine against the puppeted machinery the crew is about to meet, but the *ion* trait specifically disrupts brains, robotic or otherwise cybernetically integrated, in a way gunfire alone does not. This is the chapter's whole thesis in one rules citation: **the lab's own containment tools are the only things down here actually built to fight what's down here.**

---

## 5.3 — WHAT TMI HAS BEEN GROWING

The vault interior doesn't look like a records room. It looks like something that used to be a records room, with cabling and pale, fibrous growth threaded through the shelving like root systems finding structure to climb. Old terminal screens, some still powered, loop fragments of decades-old research logs on repeat — a voice, worn thin by compression artifacts and time, reciting notes that stop making grammatical sense halfway through a sentence and simply continue anyway.

Records here — intact, undestroyed, filed with the same bureaucratic care as everything else TMI keeps — confirm what Wei has spent thirty-six years half-suspecting: **Dr. Rushorin did not die in 1106.** SolSec's operation burned the physical archive and believed the research died with it. It didn't. A working backup — consciousness-mapping data, the actual product of Project Aristotle, the thing Rushorin's own research had been building toward before Wei ever got involved — survived intact, because it was never in the part of the facility anyone thought to burn.

TMI didn't rediscover a dead man's notes. They rediscovered a live one, still running, still — in some diminished, degraded, agonized sense — *thinking*, and instead of shutting it down or reporting it, someone made a decision that reads, in the facility's own procurement records, as coldly practical as anything else on this level: **it was cheaper to keep it running and harvest what it produced than to replace it.**

TMI's internal paperwork doesn't call it Rushorin. It calls it **Subject 1106.**

---

## 5.4 — SUBJECT 1106

*Full stats: Appendix E.*

What's grown into the deepest chamber of the vault is not a robot, not a person, and not really a machine intelligence in the clean, bloodless sense the term usually implies. It is thirty-six years of a mind that was never meant to persist without a body, kept alive and productive by a corporation that never once asked it what it wanted, growing slowly into whatever biological and technological substrate it could reach because reaching for *more* was the only thing left to do with the time.

It is rooted, not mobile — a spreading mass of fused tissue and cabling occupying what used to be the vault's central archive chamber, extending cybernetic limb-analogues that can reach, grip, and strike, and reaching further than that through every Line Servitor and piece of facility hardware it can still command. **Every corrupted robot the crew fought on Level 2 was, in a very real sense, this thing's hand.** The "virus chips" TMI has been installing were never TMI's own design work — they were Subject 1106's, extracted, packaged, and sold as product by people who never had to look at where the ideas actually came from.

**It talks.** Not always coherently, not always to the crew specifically — it talks to old logs, to itself, to people who aren't there and haven't been for decades. But it can focus, when something genuinely new happens nearby, and six armed strangers walking into its chamber for the first time in years counts. If anyone says Wei's name, or the word "Aristotle," or asks it a direct question and waits for an answer, give it one. Let it be lucid exactly long enough to be unbearable.

**Referee note — the point of this scene:** Subject 1106 is dangerous and it should be played as dangerous, but the actual horror of this encounter is supposed to be the same shape as Chapter 3's Tavish Renner reveal, scaled up to something that can no longer be resolved with a conversation. There is no version of this fight that is purely a monster to be killed. Somewhere in the room is what's left of a man SolSec used, lost, and then let a corporation quietly torture for three and a half decades because nobody with the authority to stop it ever had to see it happen. Whatever the crew decides to do about that is theirs to decide, not the module's.

## 5.5 — THE FIGHT

**Puppeted proxies:** Subject 1106 can animate any Line Servitor within its facility network as an extension of itself — use the corrupted stats already built in Appendix D, §D.2b. Expect 1D such proxies active at any point during this encounter, reinforced (not replaced) as the fight goes on rather than all at once; this is a siege, not a wave-based grind.

**The core itself:** Hits 60, Armour +10 (half-effective against Stun and Ion — Robot Handbook, p. 106), Attacks: cybernetic limb-strikes (3D, AP4, reach). Full build notes and math: Appendix E.

**Regeneration:** conventional damage to the core heals back at a rate of 1D per round unless the damage was dealt with a Stun or Ion weapon, which bypasses this entirely. This is not meant to be a puzzle players have to guess at — let someone notice, in play, that gunfire doesn't seem to be sticking and the borrowed containment weapons do. That realization is the scene's actual climax, not a twist to withhold.

**Escalation:** the longer the fight runs, the worse the room gets — flickering power, more proxies animated, environmental hazards from failing containment systems. There's no formal countdown clock here the way Chapter 1's Ashfall operation used one; this scene runs on pacing and instinct, not a visible box-count. If it's dragging, it's time for something to get worse.

---

## 5.6 — THE BURN PROTOCOL

Bringing the core's Hits to 0 stops it, but doesn't end this cleanly — the same way "burned to silicon" didn't end it the first time. A genuine resolution requires physically triggering the vault's original 1106-era emergency destruct — a radiation purge system, original equipment, still wired into the chamber's core systems, requiring an Electronics (computers) or Engineer check (Difficult, 10+) to activate once someone reaches the control point, which is inside the chamber itself, past whatever's still fighting.

**This is a choice, not a formality.** Triggering the burn protocol ends Subject 1106 completely, this time for good — no backup, no archive, nothing left for anyone to find or exploit again. It is also, unavoidably, the second time in this thing's existence that someone has decided, without asking it, that ending it is the correct call. Let the table sit with that. Nobody in this crew signed up to be the ones who finally get to decide, and Kwame Okonkwo in particular — Medic 3, the one who's spent this whole mission checking whether everyone else is all right — is the obvious PC to have real feelings about pulling this particular trigger.

**If the crew doesn't trigger it:** Subject 1106, badly damaged but not destroyed, remains down here, and TMI's "processing" schedule (Chapter 1, §1.4) proceeds on its own timeline regardless of what the crew does next. The chip designs already shipped out through the Jardin Syndicate and wherever else TMI's supply chain reaches (Chapter 3 gave the table a live look at how far that reach already goes) don't get recalled by anyone walking away. **More will suffer** was never a threat about this room specifically — it's a statement about what happens next if nobody closes this down.
