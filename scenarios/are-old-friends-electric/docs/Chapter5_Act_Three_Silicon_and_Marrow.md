# TRAVELLER: ARE OLD FRIENDS ELECTRIC
## Chapter 5: Act Three: Silicon and Marrow

*Mongoose Traveller 2nd Edition · Ready-to-Play*

---

> Act Three is not a bigger robot fight. It's the reason the robot fight happened at all. Play it slower than Chapter 4, and play it quiet; the horror here is closer to a containment breach than a battle. Full stats for everything in this chapter are in Appendix E.

---

## 5.1: PAST THE DOOR

The Shadow Security Robot (Appendix D, §D.3) is the last honest thing standing between the crew and the sub-level vault, a real, purpose-built threat that behaves exactly the way something built to guard a secret should. Once it's down, disabled, evaded, or talked past (it has an Advanced brain and a working comms link; a sufficiently strange transmission or a captured access code can, in principle, call it off, at the referee's discretion), the vault door itself is almost anticlimactic: a heavy manual seal, TL10, undisturbed for decades except for one thing. **The lock has been opened and resealed recently.** Dust everywhere else. None on the handle.

Nobody has come down here casually. Somebody comes down here on a schedule.

**Wei's reminder.** With his hand an inch from the handle, Wei stops and asks the crew to check their kit. "Last chance," he says, in the same flat voice he uses for everything that matters. "It took three teams to solve this the first time, and it still wasn't clean. I'm not asking whether you're ready. I'm asking what you brought." Let the table answer honestly. A crew that packed a toolkit and a radiation counter (see 5.6) will be glad of them. A crew that didn't can find that out here, with the door open.

---

## 5.2: THE LOCKER

Just inside, in a wall alcove that predates everything TMI has built since, is a **containment equipment locker**, original 1106-era stock, TL12, sealed but intact, exactly the kind of thing a facility installs when it's honest with itself about what it's actually storing. This is not a coincidence the crew should have to work for; let them find it early in Level 3, before the vault's interior opens up.

**Contents (RH:107):**

| Weapon | TL | Range | Damage | Traits | Cost each |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stun Rifle | 12 | 50m | 3D | Stun, Zero-G | Cr2,000 |
| Stun Shotgun | 12 | 10m | 3D | Blast 5, Stun, Zero-G | Cr2,000 |
| Pulse Carbine | 11 | 50m | 2D | Blast 5, Ion, Zero-G | Cr5,000 |

Three of each, TL11 to TL12, still charged, and worth Cr27,000 together (spare 50-shot power packs are Cr200 each). These are not weapons TMI's current skeleton crew brought down here, they're original safety equipment, built for exactly the contingency the facility is supposed to have stopped needing decades ago. A referee should let that land: someone in 1106 already knew this vault might need to be fought, not just guarded.

**Why this matters mechanically (RH:106):** electromagnetic stun and ion weapons cause real, physical Hits to robotic systems; a normal robot's armour is only half-effective against them. Conventional slug and energy weapons work fine against the puppeted machinery the crew is about to meet, but the *ion* trait specifically disrupts brains, robotic or otherwise cybernetically integrated, in a way gunfire alone does not. This is the chapter's whole thesis in one rules citation: **the lab's own containment tools are the only things down here actually built to fight what's down here.**

## 5.3: WHAT TMI HAS BEEN GROWING

The vault interior doesn't look like a records room. It looks like something that used to be a records room, with cabling and pale, fibrous growth threaded through the shelving like root systems finding structure to climb. Old terminal screens, some still powered, loop fragments of decades-old research logs on repeat, a voice, worn thin by compression artifacts and time, reciting notes that stop making grammatical sense halfway through a sentence and simply continue anyway.

Records here, intact, undestroyed, filed with the same bureaucratic care as everything else TMI keeps, confirm what Wei has spent thirty-six years half-suspecting: **Dr. Rushorin did not die in 1106.** SolSec's operation burned the physical archive and believed the research died with it. It didn't. A working backup, consciousness-mapping data, the actual product of Project Aristotle, the thing Rushorin's own research had been building toward before Wei ever got involved, survived intact, because it was never in the part of the facility anyone thought to burn.

TMI didn't rediscover a dead man's notes. They rediscovered a live one, still running, still, in some diminished, degraded, agonized sense, *thinking*, and instead of shutting it down or reporting it, someone made a decision that reads, in the facility's own procurement records, as coldly practical as anything else on this level: **it was cheaper to keep it running and harvest what it produced than to replace it.**

TMI's internal paperwork doesn't call it Rushorin. It calls it **Subject 1106.**

**The launch panel.** Among the dead terminals, one wall panel is newer than anything else in the vault, and very much alive.

> A wall panel, newer than everything around it, glows amber. Six rows. Each reads the same way: TUBE READY, a mail class (REGISTERED, SEALED), a destination mail facility, and a tracking number in the standard Imperial postal format. Under the last row, in clean white type: NEXT VOLLEY, T-30:00, and counting. It is not hidden and it is not encrypted. It is a shipping manifest.

The Annex ships its product the way anyone ships anything: six sealed courier missiles in a launch rack, each carrying a registered crate of the corrupt chips, each addressed to a mail facility and logged in the Imperium's ordinary tracking system. That is the horror of it. Nothing about the volley is concealed. It will move through the same postal network that carries a student's letter home, and nobody who handles it has any reason to look twice.

| Tube | Destination | Tracking (invented format) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Halo downport mail depot | IMP/1142/HAL/0417 |
| 2 | Bajavanang starport mail office | IMP/1142/BAJ/0418 |
| 3 | Kidashi naval base mail office | IMP/1142/KID/0419 |
| 4 to 6 | Other Imperial mail depots in the subsector (referee's choice) | IMP/1142/.../0420 to 0422 |

**The launch clock.** The panel wakes when the vault door is opened, and the clock starts at **T-30:00**. This is the one visible clock in the chapter. It runs on narrative time, not combat rounds, so the fight itself costs almost nothing (ten rounds is a minute). What costs time is everything else:

| What the crew does | Clock cost |
|---|---|
| Reads the panel and copies the six tracking numbers (Electronics (computers), Average 8+ to download them all) | 2 minutes |
| Opens and searches the locker (5.2) | 3 minutes |
| Hears Subject 1106 out (5.4) | 3 to 5 minutes |
| Reaches the destruct control and triggers it (5.6) | 2 minutes |
| Argues about what to do | 1 minute per real argument |

When the clock reaches zero, the volley fires. The crew has time for most of what they might want to do and not for all of it, which is the point.

**Stopping it.** These are suggested checks, not printed rules.
- **At the panel:** Electronics (computers) Average (8+) puts the volley on hold for 10 minutes; Difficult (10+) cancels it for good. While Subject 1106 is lucid and watching, both checks are one step harder, unless it is being talked to, is down, or has lost the network. Akio and Yatika (Electronics (computers) 2) are the natural hands.
- **At the rack:** the launchers sit on the surface beside the cargo lock, which is why that lock is the only manned one (Chapter 4, Level 1 map). Wrecking them by Explosives or brute force stops the volley, but the crew would have to climb back up past the guards to do it.
- **The destruct does not stop it.** Burning the core ends Subject 1106 (5.6) but the launch system runs on its own. Someone has to deal with the panel as well.

## 5.4: SUBJECT 1106

*Full stats: Appendix E.*

What's grown into the deepest chamber of the vault is not a robot, not a person, and not really a machine intelligence in the clean, bloodless sense the term usually implies. It is thirty-six years of a mind that was never meant to persist without a body, kept alive and productive by a corporation that never once asked it what it wanted, growing slowly into whatever biological and technological substrate it could reach because reaching for *more* was the only thing left to do with the time.

It is rooted, not mobile, a spreading mass of fused tissue and cabling occupying what used to be the vault's central archive chamber, extending cybernetic limb-analogues that can reach, grip, and strike, and reaching further than that through every Line Servitor and piece of facility hardware it can still command. **Every corrupted robot the crew fought on Level 2 was, in a very real sense, this thing's hand.** The "virus chips" TMI has been installing were never TMI's own design work; they were Subject 1106's, extracted, packaged, and sold as product by people who never had to look at where the ideas actually came from.

**It talks.** Not always coherently, not always to the crew specifically; it talks to old logs, to itself, to people who aren't there and haven't been for decades. But it can focus, when something genuinely new happens nearby, and six armed strangers walking into its chamber for the first time in years counts. If anyone says Wei's name, or the word "Aristotle," or asks it a direct question and waits for an answer, give it one. Let it be lucid exactly long enough to be unbearable.

**What it asks.** If the crew lets it speak, Subject 1106 does not demand to be freed or begs for mercy. In the flat, patient tone of a man reading a roll call, it asks whether the old team is still coming, and whether the machines that kept it company all these years can be counted as friends. It uses that word for the Line Servitors and does not seem to be joking. Whoever answers, Wei or Antonella or anyone else who knew the work, should feel how much the question costs.

**Referee note, the point of this scene:** Subject 1106 is dangerous and it should be played as dangerous, but the actual horror of this encounter is supposed to be the same shape as Chapter 3's Tavish Renner reveal, scaled up to something that can no longer be resolved with a conversation. There is no version of this fight that is purely a monster to be killed. Somewhere in the room is what's left of a man SolSec used, lost, and then let a corporation quietly torture for three and a half decades because nobody with the authority to stop it ever had to see it happen. Whatever the crew decides to do about that is theirs to decide, not the module's.

## 5.5: THE FIGHT

**Puppeted proxies:** Subject 1106 can animate any Line Servitor within its facility network as an extension of itself, using the corrupted stats already built in Appendix D, §D.2b. Expect 1D such proxies active at any point during this encounter, reinforced (not replaced) as the fight goes on rather than all at once; this is a siege, not a wave-based grind.

**The core itself:** Hits 60, Armour +10 (half-effective against Stun and Ion, RH:106), Attacks: cybernetic limb-strikes (3D, AP4, reach). Full build notes and math: Appendix E.

**Regeneration:** conventional damage to the core heals back at a rate of 1D per round unless the damage was dealt with a Stun or Ion weapon, which bypasses this entirely. This is not meant to be a puzzle players have to guess at; let someone notice, in play, that gunfire doesn't seem to be sticking and the borrowed containment weapons do. That realization is the scene's actual climax, not a twist to withhold.

**Escalation:** the longer the fight runs, the worse the room gets: flickering power, more proxies animated, environmental hazards from failing containment systems. There's no formal countdown clock here the way Chapter 1's Ashfall operation used one; this scene runs on pacing and instinct, not a visible box-count. If it's dragging, it's time for something to get worse.

**Escalation by round** (a referee-only guide, not a visible clock; slide it earlier if the fight is dragging, later if the crew is on top of it). Round 1 is the round combat starts. Distances refer to the Level 3 map.

| Round | The room | Proxies | Mechanical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Lights steady. Subject 1106 is lucid and speaks (see 5.4). | 1D active, already in the vault | No hazards yet. This is the round for the crew to notice that gunfire isn't sticking. |
| **2** | **Flicker.** The lights stutter and the old terminal screens loop faster. | None added | DM–1 to ranged attacks and Recon checks beyond 10m. |
| **3** | The growth tightens around the archive stacks. | **+1** Line Servitor arrives through the vault door, up the Guardian Passage from the service shaft | The vault door is now a choke point: it takes two rounds for any proxy to cross the vault floor. |
| **4** | **Sparking.** A cable bundle by the destruct control bursts. | None added | Anyone within 3m of Destruct Control at the start of a round takes 1D electrical damage unless they move away. Armour does not help. |
| **5** | **Fibre burst.** Pale spores puff out of the growth in the aisle. | None added | Average (8+) END check or DM–2 on all actions for that round, coughing. Repeat each round while in the vault interior. |
| **6** | The lights dip hard and recover. | **+1** | Core regeneration is unaffected (1D per round against conventional damage; Stun and Ion still bypass it). |
| **7** | **Alarms.** Sirens and a recorded voice read containment procedures to an empty room. | None added | DM–1 to hearing-based Recon and to any Electronics (comms) check while the alarm runs. |
| **8** | **Power fails.** Darkness except the terminal screens and the core's own pale glow. | None added | DM–2 to attacks and sight-based checks unless the crew uses a torch, a thermal or low-light device. Robots and the core are not affected. |
| **9** | The growth starts reaching for the crew's position. | **+1D** (reinforced, not replaced) | The core can make an extra limb-strike each round against anyone within reach of a fresh growth in the aisle. |
| **10+** | **Containment is failing.** Everything above worsens. | Roll 1D each round | 1–2: a new sparking bundle (as round 4). 3–4: another fibre burst (as round 5). 5–6: +1 proxy. Power stays out. |

**Using it.** Escalation should be felt, not announced. Describe the room, then apply the effect. Stun and Ion weapons on the core ought to shorten the fight and with it the table, which rewards the crew for finding the locker (5.2). If anyone reaches the destruct control and makes the check, the table stops there. **The launch clock (5.3) is still running.** If the fight runs past Round 10, Subject 1106 pulls the volley forward: every further 5 rounds takes 5 minutes off the clock.

## 5.6: THE BURN PROTOCOL

Bringing the core's Hits to 0 stops it, but doesn't end this cleanly, the same way "burned to silicon" didn't end it the first time. A genuine resolution requires physically triggering the vault's original 1106-era emergency destruct, a radiation purge system, original equipment, still wired into the chamber's core systems, requiring an Electronics (computers) or Engineer check (Difficult, 10+) to activate once someone reaches the control point, which is inside the chamber itself, past whatever's still fighting.

**What it looks like.** Nothing arcs. Nothing explodes. There is no flare, no crack of discharge, no wall of light, and the module should not give the table one. The control point is a cold iron plate with a worn lever and a single lamp. When it is thrown, the lamp changes from amber to a steady blue. Its old label has rubbed away, and nobody can say whether blue means "purging" or "purged."

> The lights in the chamber do not flare. They sag. Evenly, the whole room, from yellow-white to a deep dusty blue, as if someone turned a dimmer. The terminals cut off mid-word. Somewhere in the walls a pump runs for the first time in thirty-six years. The pale growth stops moving, and then, over a long minute, the colour leaves it, until it is grey like old paper. The air smells for a moment of hot dust and rain. The blue holds. And then, from the nearest terminal, the old recorded voice finishes its sentence, a whole one, in order, for the first time since you arrived, and goes quiet.

Pace it. For the first round or two, nothing seems to have happened. Let a limb twitch once after the lights go, and let the crew wonder. **The first impression is ambiguous on purpose.** Wei, if he is there, stands very still and says only: "It looked like this last time too. Quiet. We thought it had worked."

**Did it work?** The crew has to find out, and how well depends on what they brought. These are suggested checks, not printed rules.

| Method | Check | What it tells them |
|---|---|---|
| **Geiger Counter** (CSC:75; TL5 Cr250, or TL10 Improved Cr400) | none, just read it | The clicks spike to a hard burst and fall to background within about a minute. Something was purged here. |
| **Core telemetry** through the control plate's diagnostic port: Electronics (computers) with an **Electronic Tool Set** (CSC:126; TL7, 2 kg, Cr2,000) | Average (8+), or Difficult (10+) without the tool set | The core's heartbeat line is flat and its network handshake to every Line Servitor has dropped. The clearest answer available. |
| **The network:** Electronics (comms) | Routine (6+) | The chatter that has run through the facility for thirty-six years has stopped. |
| **The tissue:** Medic (Akio, Medic 4) | Average (8+) | The mass is cooling and necrotic, not dormant. |
| **The floor above:** send someone up to look | no check | Every Line Servitor is standing still. |

A failed check should give a partial or uncertain reading, never a false one. A crew that packed a toolkit and a counter gets a confident answer within minutes. A crew that didn't has only the lights, the silence and Wei's face, which is a legitimate way for the scene to end, and a good reason for Wei to have asked what they brought.

The purge is aimed inward, at the core and the substrate around it, and the control point is built to be used by someone standing at it. If you want a cost, a brief dose of a few rads at the plate is enough, and Akio will mention it. Otherwise the radiation is a clue, not a hazard.

**This is a choice, not a formality.** Triggering the burn protocol ends Subject 1106 completely, this time for good: no backup, no archive, nothing left for anyone to find or exploit again. It is also, unavoidably, the second time in this thing's existence that someone has decided, without asking it, that ending it is the correct call. Let the table sit with that. Nobody in this crew signed up to be the ones who finally get to decide, and Akio Komatsubara in particular, Medic 4, the one who's spent this whole mission checking whether everyone else is all right, is the obvious PC to have real feelings about pulling this particular trigger.

**If the crew doesn't trigger it:** Subject 1106, badly damaged but not destroyed, remains down here, and TMI's "processing" schedule (Chapter 1, §1.4) proceeds on its own timeline regardless of what the crew does next. The chip designs already shipped out through the Jardin Syndicate and wherever else TMI's supply chain reaches (Chapter 3 gave the table a live look at how far that reach already goes) don't get recalled by anyone walking away. **More will suffer** was never a threat about this room specifically; it's a statement about what happens next if nobody closes this down.

**The volley:** if it launches, six crates of corrupt chips enter the Imperial mail stream under ordinary registered tracking. That also makes them findable. If the crew copied the tracking numbers from the panel and gives them to the right people (the Imperial postal inspectorate, the Navy, or anyone Wei still trusts), the crates can be intercepted before sorting, at the cost of the crew explaining where the numbers came from. If they didn't copy them, the volley is lost into a postal system that treats it as mail. Either way, the epilogue should show someone, somewhere, picking up a sealed crate at a mail counter.
