## C.1: BALDWIN WEI (2026-10-06 UPDATE: AGE 62, IY1142)

![Baldwin Wei](img/wei-03.webp)
**Special Consultant, SolSec Directorate · Formerly Solomani Rim Desk**

*"I told them, in 1106, that we don't allow anything to be lost. Nobody believed a twenty-six-year-old field agent then either."*

Wei's full career file, his 1106 field record, his authorization of Operation Ashfall at 37, and his complicated, half-official information channel to the High Council, is documented in *Operation Ashfall*, Appendix K, §K.2. Everything there still stands. What follows is 25 years further on: the same man, considerably greyer, still not entirely willing to let a case stay closed.

**Career-Prime Characteristics (age 30, IY1110, from the *Signal GK* record; unchanged through 1117 in *Operation Ashfall*, Appendix K):** STR 8 (+0) · DEX 11 (+1) · END 9 (+1) · INT 10 (+1) · EDU 9 (+1) · SOC 8 (+0)

**Dating note:** *Operation Ashfall*, Appendix K dates the personnel record 1106 and gives Wei as 41 in 1117. Correlating *Signal GK* with *Rim of Fire* puts the record in IY1110, at age 30. He was therefore born in IY1080, was 26 at the 1106 Rushorin memo, was 37 for Ashfall, and is 62 now. Appendix K needs the same correction.

**Ageing Record, 1110–1142:** Wei's total terms served for Ageing-table purposes remains **4**, his entire field career, ending at 30 in IY1110. Everything since has been a desk, which is exactly why he shows the mildest ageing on this document's entire cast. Eight checks at DM–4 (CRB:48-49), one every four years from age 34 to age 62. Seven came back "no effect." The age-54 check (rolled 3, DM–4 = –1) cost him two physical points. Net: **STR –1, END –1**.

**Current Characteristics (age 62, IY1142):** STR 7 (+0) · DEX 11 (+1) · END 8 (+0) · INT 10 (+1) · EDU 9 (+1) · SOC 8 (+0)

**Referee note, why this matters:** hold this stat block up next to Chapter 2's roster. Wei aged gently because he spent thirty-two years mostly not in the field. The six people he's asking for a favour aged the way they did *because* they didn't. He knows this. It is a large part of why this was such a hard ask for him to make, and it should colour every scene he's in.

## C.2: TAVISH RENNER, "REAPER"

![Tavish Renner, "Reaper"](img/reaper-01.webp)
**Jardin Syndicate, low-level muscle · Bajavanang outer-system racketeer**

*"Nobody crosses the Gap with fuel to spare. You want to skim here, you pay the toll."*

Born on Sionnach (Jardin Subsector, hex 0632), UWP C533369-A, one of the neglected Jardin-Cluster worlds the sourcebook itself calls "of little importance" (SF:208). Grandson of **Absalom Renner**, a Confederation courier and informant Antonella Interlenghi ran on this stretch of border more than forty years ago, protected for years, then quietly not, when the border stabilized and the Directorate's attention moved on. Tavish never met Antonella. He grew up on half-remembered family stories about a grandfather who "used to matter to somebody" and a Confederation that stopped calling. He left Sionnach at nineteen for Syndicate work on the Imperial side of the border, where the Syndicate has spread (SF:103), and now works a cell at Bajavanang that preys on Confederation ships arriving dry from the Gap. It is not the life he'd have picked if this corner of the Rim had any other economy on offer.

**Characteristics:** STR 7 (+0) · DEX 7 (+0) · END 6 (+0) · INT 7 (+0) · EDU 5 (–1) · SOC 5 (–1)

**Skills:** Pilot 0 · Pilot (small craft) 1 · Gun Combat 0 · Gun Combat (energy) 1 · Melee 0 · Melee (blade) 1 · Streetwise 1 · Persuade 0

**Equipment:** Laser Pistol (TL9, Range 20m, 3D, 100-charge magazine, Zero-G trait; CSC:149), an Accelerator Rifle (TL9, Range 250m, 3D, 2kg, 15-round magazine (Cr30/mag), Zero-G, with two spares; CSC:145) bought for him by Darya Noor (§C.4), a Cutlass (TL2, Melee, 3D; CSC:134) worn more for the look of the thing than any real intent to use it, a scavenged transponder unit rigged to read back to a decommissioned survey vessel, a cheap flight jacket two sizes too big that used to belong to somebody else.

**Personality:** Loud, rehearsed, badly overconfident, right up until he isn't. Everything about his opening approach is performance, learned from older Syndicate hands he's trying to impress. Underneath it is a young man from a world nobody remembers who has genuinely never had anyone explain to him what he's worth, in either direction. Play the bravado as real and the crack underneath it as real too: he is not a joke, and the scene works best when the table realises that at roughly the same moment he does.

**Neural.AI image prompt:** A wiry young man in his early 20s with a shaved head and a cheap, oversized flight jacket, a laser pistol holstered a little too visibly at his hip, a scavenged old cutlass slung across his back more for show than function. Standing in the open airlock of a battered fighting launch, chin up, trying hard to project menace he hasn't fully earned yet. Backdrop of gas giant cloud bands and drifting ring debris. Photorealistic, science fiction character portrait, no text, no logos.

## C.5: THE JARDIN SYNDICATE
**Organised crime and piracy · Born in Jardin, working both sides of the border**

*Sources: Solomani Front, SF:103, SF:120, SF:208 and SF:210. Anything marked "scenario" below is this scenario's extrapolation, not the sourcebook.*

**What it is.** A criminal syndicate that began in the Jardin subsector and has since spread through the sector on both sides of the Solomani border (SF:103). Its members include organised crime groups and pirates (SF:208).

**What it does.** Interstellar trafficking, counterfeiting and money laundering, on many different worlds (SF:103).

**Its largest racket.** Preying on non-Solomani humans who are persecuted by purist regimes on Jardin, Krypton and a few other hard-line Confederation worlds near the Imperial border (SF:103).
- It poses as an interstellar employment agency or a human rights organisation.
- It collects large payments from members of oppressed minorities in return for promises of low-cost low-berth passage to "freedom" and jobs in the Imperium, or to moderate Confederation worlds.
- On some worlds it works with local Solomani Party interests who want "undesirables" removed.

**What happens to its victims** (SF:103). Many pay extra for bribes, identity papers and life support, and end up in debt, forced into sweatshop labour or sexual exploitation. Others are caught by Imperial or local authorities trying to cross starport extrality zones, and end up in legal limbo. The Imperium is sympathetic to refugees fleeing Solomani persecution but cannot force worlds to accept undocumented immigrants. Claims can take months or years to process, and able-bodied adults may be offered enlistment in the Imperial military.

**History and the official response.**
- **1059:** the growing piracy problem in Jardin is officially acknowledged, and the Solomani Ministry of Justice refers to the "Jardin Syndicate" by name (SF:120).
- **Today:** SolSec and other agencies keep investigating, with slow progress against some of the larger rings. The sourcebook says there may or may not be a link to the abortive Sirius Rising uprising (SF:208).
- **Ptolemy:** its government is accused of harbouring Syndicate-linked organisations, but no clear evidence has ever been presented (SF:210).

**What the sourcebook does not give you:** leaders, ships, bases or stat blocks.

**Sionnach, the recruiting ground** (Jardin Subsector, hex 0632), **UWP C533369-A**, Trade Codes *Low Population, Poor*, Solomani Confederation allegiance, one gas giant (SF:206). It sits in the neglected Jardin-Cluster, "of little importance" and starved of investment (SF:208). Reading the UWP with the standard world codes:

| Digit | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Starport | C | Routine quality, no shipyard |
| Size | 5 | About 8,000 km across |
| Atmosphere | 3 | Very thin |
| Hydrographics | 3 | About 30% water |
| Population | 3 | Thousands |
| Government | 6 | Captive government |
| Law Level | 9 | Very high |
| Tech Level | A | TL10 |

TL10 is why Tavish's crew carries TL10 gear: it is the best a Sionnach workshop can make. A world with this few people, this little money and this much law is exactly where a recruiter with cash and promises finds teenagers who see no future at home.

**How this scenario uses it** (scenario):
- **The Bajavanang cell.** Tavish Renner, Anton Vera and Darya Noor work out of a hidden berth in Bajavanang's outer belt and prey on Confederation ships arriving nearly dry from the Rimward Gap. The victims cannot call Imperial police (Chapter 3, §3.3, §C.2 to §C.4).
- **The Krypton end of the pipeline.** The Syndicate's low-berth boat at Krypton's gas giant and the family left on the dock are the other end of the same trade (Chapter 3, §3.2).
- **Recruitment.** It takes poor young people from neglected Jardin-Cluster worlds like Sionnach (SF:206, SF:208) and gives them work as Enforcers at 18.
- **Tone.** Play the Syndicate as a symptom of the Confederation's persecution, not as a cartoon. Tavish and his crew are small links in a trade whose victims are real.
