## Appendix E — Subject 1106

*Referee Reference Document*

## E.1 — WHY THIS IS BUILT THE WAY IT IS

Subject 1106 is not a robot in any clean sense the Robot Handbook's own design system was written for, and this build says so rather than pretending otherwise. It's built using the real Chassis/Locomotion/Armour/Resiliency rules as a scaffold — the same honest, math-shown approach as Appendix D — because a referee needs actual numbers at the table. But the fiction should always outrun the stat block here: this is a once-human mind that grew a body out of whatever was nearby, not a product anyone designed on purpose. Where the numbers and the horror pull in different directions, run the horror.

**Design constants used, per the Robot Handbook's own tables:**
- Chassis Size table: p. 13
- Locomotion "None" (no movement type — the +25% Slot bonus, applied because this thing has never once needed to walk anywhere): p. 16
- TL12–14 armour bracket (Base Protection 4, Max 3 protection per Slot, Cost Cr1,500/Slot): p. 19
- Resiliency (+1 Hit per Slot dedicated, 5% of Base Chassis Cost per Slot, max 50% of Base Slots): p. 19–20
- Robot Brains table (Self-Aware tier used as the nearest available category — see note below): p. 65–66
- Damaging Robots / stunner and ion effects on robotic systems: p. 106
- Anti-Robot Weapons (Stun Rifle, Stun Shotgun, Pulse Carbine): p. 107
- Bio-tech vehicle regeneration (10 Hits/day, cited as inspiration for the faster combat-pace version below, not a direct reuse) — *Vehicle Handbook*, p. 65

## E.2 — THE BUILD

**Chassis:** Size 7 (64 Base Slots, 50 Base Hits — the book's own comparison point is K'kree/Bear/Shark scale)
**Locomotion:** None — rooted in place, extends reach through limb-analogues and puppeted proxies rather than moving its own mass. +25% Slots (rounded up): **80 Slots available** (percentage-based costs below still calculate off the base 64, per the rules' own convention)
**TL:** 12–14 bracket used for armour purposes; brain tier pushed to Self-Aware regardless of nominal chassis TL — see §E.3

| Build step | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Base Chassis Cost | Size 7 base (Cr4,000) × None multiplier (×1) | **Cr4,000** |
| Armour | Base Protection 4; target +4 more; TL12–14 caps at 3/Slot, so 2 Slots minimum, providing +6 (rounds up past target, same effect Appendix N's own Walker build hit) | **2 Slots, Cr3,000 → Protection +10 total** |
| Resiliency | +10 Hits (50 → 60) at 1 Slot per Hit, 5% of Base Chassis Cost (Cr200) per Slot | **10 Slots, Cr2,000 → +10 Hits** |
| Slots remaining | 80 − 2 (armour) − 10 (resiliency) | **68 Slots** — limb-analogues, network control reaching into every Line Servitor on Level 2, environmental integration, and whatever else three and a half decades of unsupervised growth produces. Not itemized further; this isn't a robot anyone designed a parts list for. |

## E.3 — THE BRAIN (A NOTE, NOT JUST A STAT LINE)

The nearest official category is **Self-Aware** (TL15, Computer/10, Base INT 12, Skill DM +2, capabilities "Near sentient, Expert/3, Security/3" — Robot Handbook, p. 66), and that's the entry used for any mechanical purpose that needs one — resisting a hack attempt, an INT-based check, whatever the table needs a number for.

**Say it plainly at the table if it comes up: this is the wrong box, and everyone should know it's the wrong box.** The Robot Handbook's brain tiers describe manufactured intelligences on a spectrum from "very smart tool" to "genuinely persuasive impression of a person." Subject 1106 didn't arrive at Self-Aware from below, climbing up through Advanced and Very Advanced the way a product line does. It arrived at whatever this is from *above* — a real human mind, Dr. Rushorin's, degraded and re-grown through thirty-six years of enforced isolation and cybernetic integration. The Self-Aware brain tier is the closest available approximation of the *outputs* (what it can do, how it reasons, how hard it is to fool). It says nothing true about what it actually *is*.

## E.4 — REGENERATION

Subject 1106 recovers **1D Hits per round** from any damage that was not dealt by a weapon with the **Stun** or **Ion** trait. This is faster than the Robot Handbook's own bio-tech vehicle regeneration rule (10 Hits per *day* — Vehicle Handbook, p. 65) by design: that rule describes a vehicle quietly healing between adventures, not a combat-paced ability, and this scene needs the players to feel the difference between "the gunfire isn't working" and "the borrowed containment weapons are" within a few rounds, not a few sessions.

**Stun and Ion damage is not regenerated.** This is the entire mechanical point of Chapter 5, §5.2's equipment locker — the Stun Rifle, Stun Shotgun, and Pulse Carbine found there are not a marginally-better weapon choice, they are the only weapon choice that actually ends this fight.

## E.5 — PUPPETED PROXIES

Any Line Servitor within the facility's network can be animated as an extension of Subject 1106 — use the corrupted stats already built in Appendix D, §D.2b, without modification. Suggested pacing: **1D active proxies at any one time**, reinforced through the fight rather than delivered all at once. These are the same machines the crew may already have fought, hacked, or destroyed on Level 2 — Chapter 4's fight and this one are not separate encounters, mechanically speaking. They're the same hand, seen twice.

---

## E.6 — THE BURN PROTOCOL

Not a weapon, not a check against Subject 1106 directly — a separate objective. Reaching and activating the vault's original 1106-era emergency destruct (Electronics (computers) or Engineer, Difficult 10+, at a control point inside the chamber itself) ends this permanently, bypassing Hits and Regeneration entirely. Narrated fully in Chapter 5, §5.6; no additional mechanics beyond the single activation check are needed. The weight of this scene was never meant to live in the dice.
