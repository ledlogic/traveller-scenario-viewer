# TRAVELLER: ARE OLD FRIENDS ELECTRIC
## Chapter 4 — Act Two: The Parkside Annex

*Mongoose Traveller 2nd Edition · Ready-to-Play*

---

> Act Two is the infiltration: three levels, one skeleton crew, and a working robot line that most of the facility's own paperwork still describes as a mothballed asteroid research annex. It isn't. Full robot stat blocks for everything in this chapter are in Appendix D.

---

## 4.1 — THE SHAPE OF THE PLACE

The **Parkside Annex** reads, on approach, exactly like what Wei said it was: a decommissioned government-and-corporate research station bored into a rock in the Halo belt, the kind of "classic mid-century Big Science" facility built when everyone still had an almost cult-like faith that enough funding solved everything. Bureaucratic. Unglamorous. Three levels, stacked, connected by a single central shaft — old enough that half its systems are original TL10 hardware nobody's replaced, new enough underneath that somebody has spent real money down here in the last few years.

That contrast is the whole facility in one sentence, and it should be visible in every scene: **Level 1 is old. Level 2 is new. Level 3 is both, and worse.**

| Level | Character | What's actually there |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Surface / Admin** | Original construction, decades of institutional dust | Reception, records, old offices, a skeleton TMI security presence |
| **2 — The Floor** | Gutted and rebuilt inside the old pilot-plant bay | An active robot assembly and refit line — the "Tokyo-style" factory floor |
| **3 — Sub-level** | Original vault, sealed since the facility's first closure | The archive that was supposed to have been destroyed, and one very good reason nobody's gotten back in since |

There is no "front door" in the usual sense — Halo has no atmosphere, and the Annex's only entrances are airlocks. Rosa Delacroix and Priya Lindqvist both have a real shot at getting *Terminal Leave*'s crew in through a disused personnel airlock on the surface rather than the manned cargo lock TMI actually uses (Electronics (sensors) or Mechanic, Average 8+, to confirm it's genuinely unmonitored before committing to it). This is the infiltration's first real choice: quiet and slow through the old lock, or fast and loud through the working one. Either way, Level 1 is where they land.

---

## 4.2 — LEVEL 1: SURFACE / ADMIN

Empty corridors, TL10 environmental systems still humming along on original hardware, a reception desk nobody's staffed in years, records rooms nobody's cleared out because nobody's been told to. This level is genuinely, boringly old — the referee should let a search here pay off with texture rather than danger: personnel files with Rushorin's name still in them, a framed dedication plaque from the facility's original opening, general institutional evidence that this place used to be exactly the kind of legitimate research station it still claims to be on paper.

**Skeleton security:** two to three TMI contract guards, human, doing rounds out of boredom rather than real vigilance — this is a budget posting, not an elite one. Basic gear (Autopistol, Cloth armour), no Gun Combat above level 1, easily avoided with a Stealth check (Average 8+) or talked past with a plausible cover story (Deception or Persuade, Average 8+ — Perpetua's specialty). A loud entrance here is recoverable, not fatal, but it does mean Level 2 is expecting company by the time the crew reaches it.

**What Level 1 is for, structurally:** a controlled, low-stakes space for the crew to get their bearings, split up if the table wants to, and find the one or two pieces of documentation that confirm what they're walking into before the noise starts. Don't rush it, but don't let it run long — the real scene is one floor down.

## 4.3 — LEVEL 2: THE FLOOR

The old pilot-plant bay — originally built for large-scale ore-processing test runs, the single biggest open space in the Annex — has been gutted and rebuilt into a genuine assembly line. Gantries overhead. Conveyor beds. A dozen or more **Line Servitors** (Appendix D, §D.2) doing exactly what they were built to do: maintenance, construction, hauling, welding, cutting. Bright work-lighting, arc-weld flare, the specific ordinary noise of an active industrial floor. It should feel less like a secret lab and more like *a factory*, because that's exactly what TMI has quietly turned it into.

At the far end, a small sub-station most of the floor's own traffic simply routes around: a single technician's bench, a rack of unmarked chip modules, and a Line Servitor currently held immobile in a service cradle while its chassis panel sits open. This is where the swap happens (Appendix D, §D.2b) — Cr2,000, no tools beyond a screwdriver and a Parasite Link, and a Basic-brain maintenance robot becomes a Hunter/Killer.

**Triggering the encounter:** however the crew approaches — Stealth along the gantry catwalks, a bold walk-through in stolen TMI coveralls, straight through the middle — call for a Recon or Stealth check (Difficult, 10+, given how open the floor is) at the point they get close enough to the chip-installation sub-station to matter. On a failure, or the moment anyone does something conspicuous (a shot fired, a shout, an obviously non-TMI weapon drawn), the technician at the bench hits a floor-wide alert. **2D robots currently on the assembly floor flip from D.2a to D.2b in a single round** — not a horde, but not nothing, and the crew should watch it happen in real time: ordinary maintenance robots stopping mid-task, all at once, and turning.

**Running the fight:** the Line Servitors are Hits 32, Armour +4, Cutting Torch (3D, AP4) — genuinely dangerous in melee, especially given several PCs' current STR isn't what it was (Chapter 2), but not remotely as durable or accurate as a purpose-built combat robot. This should read as *dangerous but fair*, the same "efficient, not effortless" pacing Chapter 3 used for Tavish Renner, scaled up. A few concrete options for the table:

- **Fight them conventionally.** Gun Combat or Melee against Hits 32/Armour +4 targets is winnable but not trivial for a crew this size — this is where Hendrik earns his keep if any weapon heavier than a sidearm made it off the ship, and where Rosa's Explosives can drop one Servitor in a single well-placed charge instead of a slow melee grind.
- **Shut the sub-station down instead of fighting the floor.** Destroying or disabling the chip-installation bench (Explosives or brute force) stops any *further* robots from flipping mid-fight, even if it doesn't help against ones already converted.
- **Hack rather than fight.** Any corrupted Line Servitor can be taken rather than destroyed — Kwame Okonkwo, Electronics (computers) 2, direct physical contact for automatic access, then a second Very Difficult (12+) intrusion check to seize control outright (Appendix D, §D.2b). A captured Hunter/Killer unit fighting for the crew for the rest of the scene is a genuinely satisfying payoff for a character whose Chapter 2 writeup calls him "gentle" and "sharp-eyed" rather than a frontline fighter.

**Referee note:** resist the urge to make this a wave-based grind. Two dozen corrupted robots would be a slog and would also not make sense — TMI doesn't have infinite chip stock sitting out on the floor, and the point of this scene is the *reveal* (the chips are still being made, right now, by people who told everyone they weren't) more than pure attrition. 2D converted units per alert is plenty.

## 4.4 — LEVEL 3: THE VAULT

Below the floor, reached by a single service shaft the sub-station technician was clearly never meant to leave unwatched, is the original archive — sealed since the Annex's first closure decades ago, older than the robot line above it by a generation. This is what "burned to silicon" was supposed to mean: the actual physical archive Wei believed destroyed in 1106 (Chapter 1, §1.4).

It wasn't. Some of it survived, in a vault nobody officially remembers exists, guarded by the one piece of hardware down here actually built for killing people rather than assembling them: a **Shadow Security Robot** (Appendix D, §D.3) — Hits 21, Armour +13, Laser Rifle 5D+3, Stealth +3, and genuinely smart enough to ambush, retreat, and call for help rather than simply charge.

What's actually inside the vault — data, a person, or both, per Wei's own uncertainty in Chapter 1, §1.4 — is Chapter 5's business, not this one's. What matters here is the tone shift: Level 2 was dangerous but almost sympathetic, ordinary machines turned into something they weren't built to be. Level 3 is the opposite — the one thing in this whole facility that was always exactly what it looks like.
