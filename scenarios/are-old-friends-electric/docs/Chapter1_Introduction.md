> *"We can't lose our pawns to the Imperium. We've already lost too much in the past. In this new espionage game, we don't allow anything to be lost."*
> Baldwin Wei, field memo, 1106 (see OA:App K)

Thirty-six years ago, a much younger Baldwin Wei wrote those words about a researcher named Rushorin and a project called **Aristotle**. Nobody listened to him then except the people who mattered. This time, he's the one who has to be talked into asking for help, and the six people he's asking are not exactly in fighting shape anymore.

## 1.2: THE SETUP

It is **Imperial Year 1142**. Six retired Solomani Confederation veterans (Navy, Marines, Army, SolSec Intelligence, a Krypton Home Forces medic) have spent the last decade-plus living together in a rambling, badly-insulated townhouse on **Alizarin** (Chapter 1, §1.3), the kind of arrangement that starts as "temporary, just until we're back on our feet" and quietly becomes permanent. They argue about the thermostat. They argue about whose turn it is to requalify their Vacc Suit certs at the starport, purely for the discount on the annual physical. They have, by any reasonable measure, retired.

Baldwin Wei has not, quite. At 62, he no longer runs the Solomani Rim Desk. He was eased into a "special consultant" role eight years ago, the SolSec Directorate's polite way of admitting a man who never once asked permission before acting was never going to stop, so they'd rather keep him close than push him out entirely. He still has a desk. He still has a line to people who owe him. He does not, any longer, have a team.

He is about to ask his last one for one more favour.

## 1.3: WHERE THEY LIVE: ALIZARIN

![Alizarin and Krypton, sitting across the Rimward Gap from Imperial Albadawi](img/alizarin-rimward-gap-map.png)
*Alizarin (0230) and Krypton (0330), on the tan (Confederation-aligned) side of the Rimward Gap, with the border running along the hex line into pink (Imperial) territory.*

**Alizarin** (hex 0230, Albadawi Subsector), UWP **A566987-C**, Trade Codes **Garden, High Population, High Tech**, sits inside a geographic and political oddity. The Albadawi Subsector is, and has been since the Solomani Rim War, overwhelmingly Imperial territory: a "hotbed of political machinations and covert operations" on the Imperial-Confederation border (SF:177). But the *Rimward Gap* physically isolates two of its 27 worlds, Alizarin and Krypton, from the rest of the subsector entirely. Cut off from Imperial space by the Gap and connected instead to the neighbouring Jardin Subsector, both worlds are **part of the Solomani Confederation and politically aligned with the worlds in Jardin** (SF:177; SF:176).

In practice: a pocket of home soil, tucked in behind Imperial lines, that nobody on either side particularly patrols. It's exactly the kind of place an old intelligence hand retires to, not because it's glamorous (it isn't; Alizarin's a pleasant, unremarkable garden world doing pleasant, unremarkable garden-world things), but because nobody looking for Confederation veterans thinks to look inside what is, on every strategic map, "Imperial territory." Rosa Delacroix (Chapter 2, §2.5) was born there and never really left; the other five followed her home, one at a time, over about six years.

## 1.4: WHAT SHOWS UP AT THE DOOR

Wei doesn't call ahead. He arrives at Alizarin's modest downport on a scheduled liner, travelling on a civilian identity that hasn't been used in a decade, and walks the last two kilometres to the townhouse because he no longer entirely trusts hired transport with what he's carrying. He is, by his own admission, not the man he was at 37 (OA:App K §K.2); full updated characteristics and skills for the man he is now are in Appendix C, §C.1. What hasn't changed is the instinct: he found something, he didn't take it up the chain, and now he needs people he trusts more than he trusts procedure.

**What he found:** a fragment of a decades-dormant SolSec dead-drop protocol, the kind that predates most currently-serving officers' careers, activating out of a mothballed research annex on **Halo** (Chapter 1, §1.5), deep in Imperial Albadawi. TMI's own paperwork calls the place the **Parkside Annex**, a name chosen decades ago for the same reason "Parkside" always gets chosen: it sounds like nothing at all. The protocol traces back to Rushorin. To Aristotle. To Thinking Machines Incorporated, the same corporation Wei flagged in 1106 as quietly absorbing Rushorin's cybernetics research for the Third Imperium's benefit.

Someone, or something, that was supposed to have been lost thirty-six years ago is still down there, and the caretaker contract on the annex just lapsed. A skeleton TMI security detail is scheduled to "process" whatever's in that facility within the week. Wei doesn't know if it's a person, a data cache, or both. He knows it's the last loose thread from the case that shaped his entire career, and he knows that if he takes this through proper channels, it becomes a documented operation. And a documented operation against a facility inside Imperial territory, run by a corporation with Imperial ties, is exactly the kind of incident nobody currently on his old desk can afford to own.

He needs six people who officially don't exist anymore. He knows exactly six.

## 1.5: WHERE IT'S TAKING THEM: HALO

**Halo** (hex 0726, Albadawi Subsector), UWP **C000679-B**, Trade Codes **Asteroid Belt, Non-Agricultural, Non-Industrial, Vacuum** (SF:176), is not a world so much as a scattering of rock and a reason nobody visits. No native population, no listed government, a Class C starport that exists mainly to service the handful of licensed claims still working the belt. It is, in short, the kind of nowhere a corporation parks a facility it would prefer nobody remembers exists.

**The catch:** Halo does not sit conveniently next door. The Rimward Gap is a genuine void: the nearest Imperial worlds, Bajavanang and Kidashi, are four parsecs from Alizarin and three from Krypton, and nothing in Jardin is closer than four (SF:176, SF:206). A Jump-2 ship cannot cross at all, and a Jump-3 ship can cross only by jumping from Krypton with full tanks. Reaching Halo means at least three jumps (Alizarin to Krypton, Krypton to Bajavanang, Bajavanang to Halo), with the middle leg burning 60 of *Terminal Leave*'s 63 tons of fuel, all while avoiding the Albadawi subsector fleet's patrol lanes along the border (SF:177). This is not a hop. It is a real astrogation problem, worked out on a chart table by someone who hasn't had to plot a covert border run in fifteen years, which is precisely why Yatika Lindqvist (Chapter 2, §2.3) is on this crew and not just any pilot with a licence.

## 1.6: THE PITCH, THE ARGUMENT, AND THE ANSWER

Wei doesn't get a hero's welcome. He gets six people who are annoyed he tracked down their address, more annoyed that he's clearly not sleeping, and *most* annoyed that he's right about needing them. Play the recruitment scene for what it actually is: not excitement, but a group of old friends recognising, all at once, that the alternative to going is watching Wei go alone and die of it. Rosa is the one who says yes first, mostly to shut everyone else up. Akio Komatsubara makes Wei sit down and takes his pulse before agreeing to anything. Nobody asks whether they're too old for this. They ask whether the ship still starts on the first try.

It does. Barely.
