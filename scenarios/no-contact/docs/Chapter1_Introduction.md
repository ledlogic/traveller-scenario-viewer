## 1.0 — PREMISE

All six player characters are **Solomani Security (SolSec) assets** who have each been
told, separately and through their own handler or cutout, to go to ground at a
specific three-room motel and wait for extraction back across the border to
Confederation space. **Each was also told they'd be paired with a partner for this
job — sharing a room, a cover story, and the wait for extraction — but each received
that instruction independently, and most pairs have never actually met their partner
in person before tonight.** Verifying that the person who shows up matching your
partner's description really is who they say they are is part of the job, not a
given. No pair was told anything about the other two rooms; as far as any given pair
knows, they may be the only SolSec presence at the motel tonight. Each pair has a
cover identity — a matched pair of motel-guest personas — they believe is unique to
them.

**The extraction never comes.**

Over one night, broken into **Night / Graveyard / Morning** shifts, the six agents have
to figure out why — and in doing so, discover that one of their own is not who they
claim to be. **One PC (secretly assigned by the referee before play) is not a genuine
SolSec agent at all — they are Imperial counter-intelligence**, inserted into the
network to identify and roll up exactly this kind of safehouse operation. The missing
extraction may be *because* of them.

Looming over the whole thing is **Baldwin Wei** — the network's controller (canon SolSec
officer, *Signal GK*), whose silence tonight could mean he's been compromised, has cut
this cell loose as a loss, or is testing them. Wei does not need to appear in person;
his absence is the engine of the scenario.

## 1.1 — THE WORLD: JORJOR

**Jorjor** — Arcturus Subsector, Solomani Rim Sector, hex 3225. UWP **B641888-7**.

| UWP element | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Starport | B | Good quality — refined fuel, shipyard, both highport and downport. Real, unremarkable ship traffic. |
| Size | 6 | Medium, 9,600 km diameter |
| Atmosphere | 4 | Thin, tainted |
| Hydrographics | 1 | Dry world, 10% surface water |
| Population | 8 | Moderate, ~100 million |
| Government | 8 | Civil Service Bureaucracy |
| Law | 8 | High Law — controlled blades (firearms are legal and common) |
| Tech Level | 7 | Pre-stellar electronics |

Jorjor is a **hellworld** — poor for its size, never became the industrial power it
hoped to be, and its people are described as grim realists who survive daily hardship.
That's exactly why it's a good place to disappear. The Class B starport means real ship
traffic in and out, so a late extraction reads as *late*, not *suspicious* — at first.

Jorjor sits in the same subsector (Arcturus) that *Signal GK* uses for Baldwin Wei's
home turf, keeping this scenario in continuity with existing SolSec/Wei material.

## 1.2 — THE PRIZE: WHY JORJOR

The answer isn't a thing. It's a stamp.

Jorjor's government is a **Civil Service Bureaucracy** — and per the pattern this
government type follows, its administration runs on exactly the kind of fastidious,
jealously-guarded procedural minutiae that makes a "Jobsworth's World": permits and
restrictions abound, demarcation lines between departments are exactly drawn, and
inspections and manifests are treated as sacred process rather than practical
security.

**That rigidity is the vulnerability.** SolSec doesn't want anything buried, mined, or
built on Jorjor. It wants **quiet, repeatable control of one procedural chokepoint**:
the office that stamps off-world cargo and personnel manifests as inspected and
cleared.

### The compromised official

**Deputy Comptroller Yannick Osei**, Off-World Manifest & Clearance Office, Jorjor
Civil Service Bureaucracy — a career bureaucrat, twenty-two years in the department,
utterly unremarkable on paper.

**Age:** 55

**UPP:** 546598 *(STR 5 (-1), DEX 4 (-1), END 6 (0), INT 5 (-1), EDU 9 (+1), SOC 8 (0))*

**Career history:** Four terms **Bureaucrat**, entirely within Jorjor's Civil
Service Bureaucracy, climbing by seniority and procedural correctness rather
than any particular ability — exactly the promotion path the government type
rewards. Twenty-two years in the Off-World Manifest & Clearance Office
specifically.

**Skills:** Admin 2, Persuade 1, Streetwise 0, Electronics 0 *(genuinely
minimal — see below)*, Gambler 1 *(the debt that got him turned in the first
place)*

SolSec identified Osei four years ago through a pattern any patient handler would
recognize: chronic gambling debt, quietly spiraling, invisible to his superiors because
his departmental paperwork was flawless. A soft approach (help with the debt, no
strings, "a friend") became leverage inside eighteen months. Osei now has the
authority to issue **administrative clearance stamps** that route a shipment or a
travel manifest past physical inspection entirely — a routine, boring, correctly
filed exception that a bureaucracy this proud of its own procedure never
double-checks, because *questioning a properly stamped form* is itself
procedurally irregular.

**What this actually buys SolSec:** not a single prize, but a standing **smuggling
corridor** — the ability to move personnel, documents, and material through Jorjor's
Class B starport undetected, repeatedly, for as long as Osei's position and cover
hold. That's why this motel, in this jurisdiction, on this world: it sits inside
Osei's practical reach, close enough for an asset in trouble to be plausibly cleared
through his office on short notice.

### Inept with technology, holds the keys anyway

Osei's **Electronics skill sits at 0** — not a gap in the writeup, a real and
important fact about him. He does not understand, in any functional sense, the
systems his office nominally controls. He can operate the terminal that issues a
clearance stamp because the interface is built to require nothing more than
that, and because he's done the same handful of keystrokes for over two decades.
Ask him to troubleshoot it, explain how it verifies anything, or notice when
something about it is behaving oddly, and he's lost.

**How he passes Imperial competency reviews anyway:** Jorjor's civil service
requires periodic technical-proficiency testing for anyone holding a
clearance-issuing post. Osei passes these using a **mind-enhancing pharmaceutical**
(referee's call on a period-appropriate name and TL — something TL8-9,
short-acting, legitimately prescribed for exam performance rather than exotic
or illegal) that sharpens recall and test-taking focus for a few hours at a
time without granting any lasting understanding. He walks out of every review
having technically passed, and forgets most of what the test covered within a
day. Nobody above him has ever had reason to look closer, because the test
result is the only thing the bureaucracy's own procedure actually checks —
"questioning a properly stamped form is itself procedurally irregular" applies
just as well to questioning a properly passed exam.

**Referee use:** if a PC ever needs Osei to *do* something technical under
pressure — investigate an anomaly, verify a system remotely, adapt on the fly —
he cannot, full stop, regardless of how much he's threatened or bribed. This is
a hard limit, not a skill check to beat. It's also a quiet source of dramatic
irony: SolSec's whole corridor runs through a man who doesn't understand the
system he's exploiting any better than the bureaucracy that employs him does.

### The quota

Osei's other survival mechanism is entirely mundane: he **processes a steady
quota of ordinary, legitimate manifest clearances** every cycle — enough real,
boring, correctly-handled cases that his output looks like bare-minimum
competent diligence to anyone doing a surface review. The SolSec-compromised
exceptions are a small, carefully limited fraction hidden inside a much larger
body of genuine work. He treats the quota itself as his real job security,
more than the bribe money or the blackmail leverage — "keep the numbers
normal" is his own survival instinct as much as SolSec's operational
requirement.

**Referee use:** this is the mechanism behind the "internal audit" explanation
below for why this ties to no extraction. An audit wouldn't find Osei's
compromised clearances by volume — his numbers look ordinary — it would only
find them by pattern, which is exactly the kind of narrow, specific anomaly a
jealous rival department (per Jorjor's administrative culture) might stumble
onto while looking for something else entirely.

### He asks questions sometimes

Osei is not a pushover or a fool, and playing him as one undersells the
character. Every so often — unpredictably, not on any schedule a PC could
game — genuine scrutiny surfaces: he'll ask a real, specific question about a
manifest, push back on a request, or notice a detail that doesn't fit. This
isn't competence breaking through the fog; it's exactly the fastidious,
procedure-worshipping instinct the whole bureaucracy runs on, firing on him
the same as it would on anyone else in the department, entirely independent
of whether he understands the technology behind what he's looking at.

**Referee use:** a good beat if Osei ever appears directly (he doesn't need
to — see below) is to let him ask one sharp, correct, procedurally-minded
question that has nothing to do with technology and everything to do with
paperwork — and let it briefly, genuinely rattle whichever PC is dealing with
him, before he moves on and signs off anyway.

### Neural.AI image prompt

A soft, unremarkable man in his mid-50s in a rumpled civil-service uniform,
seated at a cluttered desk stacked with paper forms rather than any visible
technology, wire-rimmed glasses pushed up on his forehead, an absent,
slightly-too-focused expression as if concentrating hard on something simple.
Fluorescent government-office lighting, beige walls, a small pill bottle
half-hidden under a stack of forms. Photorealistic, science fiction character
portrait, no text, no logos.

### Why this ties to "no extraction"

Bureaucracies built on Jorjor's exacting, procedure-worshipping model are also
*compulsive record-keepers* — precisely the trait that made Osei useful is the same
trait that could expose him. Pick one (or seed both as red herrings):

- **An internal audit.** Some other department, jealously competing for budget and
  relevance per the world's own administrative culture, ran a routine cross-check
  and flagged an anomalous pattern of clearances bearing Osei's stamp. If this is
  the cause, Osei may currently be in an "interview" with Internal Compliance — not
  arrested, not yet, just delayed, and unreachable.
- **Osei got cold feet.** Four years is a long time to live compromised. He may have
  simply stopped answering, uncertain whether the safer move is to keep serving
  SolSec or to confess and throw himself on the bureaucracy's notoriously
  procedural mercy (which, per the government's own texture, tends to go easy on
  anyone willing to correctly fill out the right form of contrition).
- **Imperial counter-intelligence found him first** — independently of, or in
  coordination with, the Version B mole seeded among tonight's guests (Chapter 2).
  If so, Osei may already be turned, and the "clearance" the PCs are waiting on
  could arrive from the wrong direction entirely.

### Referee use

Osei never needs to appear in person for this to work — his absence, like Wei's, is
load-bearing. If a PC (in either version) tries to independently reach the manifest
office tonight, that's a legitimate way to surface Graveyard-shift information (Chapter
3, §3.2): a busy signal, a night-shift clerk who's never heard of any "administrative
exception," or — worse — someone on the other end who asks a few too many careful
questions before hanging up.

## 1.3 — TONE & SETTING DRESSING

The surface culture around the motel reads close to Earth, circa **1970s**, tuned to
Jorjor's actual **TL7**: sedans, wired phones, film cameras, revolvers and shotguns
(legal and common — only blades are controlled under High Law), grimy analog
electronics rather than clean consumer tech, no visible grav vehicles or fusion tech in
daily life. Think dust, heat-shimmer, and a gas station sign missing half its neon —
not a leafy highway diner.

## 1.4 — TECH LEVEL REFERENCE

| Layer | TL | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Jorjor (surface world)** | **TL7** | Everything visible reads as period-plausible: cars, phones, cameras, firearms (legal, unremarkable), analog/early-digital electronics, no obvious spacer tech. |
| **True SolSec gear (Version "A")** | **TL10–11** | Concealed inside period objects. A real edge, but survives casual inspection. |
| **Imperial mole gear (Version "B")** | **TL12** | One tier better than SolSec issue. This is the fair, discoverable tell — a device that does something SolSec kit shouldn't be able to do, a wound that heals a little too fast, a lock picked a little too cleanly. |

**Referee tip:** Never let the TL gap be the *only* tell. Pair every mechanical tell
with a behavioral one so sharp roleplay is rewarded as much as sharp system mastery.
