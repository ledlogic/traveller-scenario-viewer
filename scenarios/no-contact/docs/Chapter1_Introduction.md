## CONVENTION BLURB (Con of the North)

Six strangers. One roadside motel on the edge of a dying desert world. Each of you is a Solomani Security agent told to lie low at Motel Yermo and wait for extraction back across the border. The ride never comes. The storm has cut the phones, the diner is far too quiet, and something out on the flats is being fed. Includes material integrated from Mystery Flesh Pit National Park. Mongoose Traveller 2e, pre-generated characters, new players welcome.

*(452 characters)*

---

## 1.1: PREMISE

**When:** day 245 of Y1116 (Imperial date 245-1116), a single night. The history behind Jorjor and the Rim is in Chapter 2, §2.20.

All six player characters are **Solomani Security (SolSec) assets** who have each been told, separately, to go to ground at a specific three-room motel, **Motel Yermo** *(Spanish, "wasteland/barren place"; matches the hellworld texture of Jorjor itself)*, and wait for extraction back across the border to Solomani Confederation space. Each was also told they'd be paired with a partner for this job (sharing a room, a cover story, and the wait for extraction) but each received that instruction independently, and most pairs have never actually met their partner in person before tonight. Verifying that the person who shows up matching your partner's description really is who they say they are is part of the job, not a given. No pair was told anything about the other two rooms; as far as any given pair knows, they may be the only SolSec presence at the motel tonight. Each pair has a cover identity, a matched pair of motel-guest personas, they believe is unique to them.

**The extraction never comes.**

Extraction is set for **9:40 PM local (21:40) on day 245 of Y1116**, at the motel. Over one night, broken into **Night / Graveyard / Morning** shifts, the six agents have to figure out why. All six are genuine SolSec. Also in the lot is an **Imperial Security Service agent, Ansel Voss, an NPC** (Chapter 4, §4.6), who has been watching the network and carries enough heavy gear to change the night. Two of the six, Dabney and Tomas, already know the Reserve keeps a weapons locker with military-grade hardware (Chapter 2, §2.29, Area 7).

Looming over the whole thing is **Baldwin Wei**, the network's controller (canon SolSec officer, *Signal GK*), whose silence tonight could mean he's been compromised, has cut this cell loose as a loss, or is testing them. Wei does not need to appear in person; his absence is the engine of the scenario.

**What's really going on.** The extraction team, four SolSec agents aboard the courier-sloop *Soft Landing* (Chapter 4, §4.8), landed a day early, toured the nearby Halvorsen Basin Biological Reserve (Chapter 2, §2.16–2.31), and got trapped underground. The game is in three acts (Chapters 6–8): **Act I**, notice that something big has happened at the Reserve, survive the Mass's raiders (the gutwings, Chapter 2, §2.30) and decide to go and look; **Act II**, take a stranded feed hauler's big rig through the Reserve's gate; **Act III**, use the Reserve's own mole vehicles to drill to the pickup, or at least to their ship's key fobs, and get off planet before dawn.

### The original transmission

Every PC received some form of this, days before tonight, through their own handler:

> *"Good. You've completed your mission. Now meet up with your partner, identified by the codename provided, at [the motel]. Extraction is at 2140 local on 245-1116. Do not leave lingering traces to this location; we intend to use it again. That means no loaner car, no parking fees on record, nothing that puts a name or a transaction anywhere near this address. Understand: no loose threads."*

## 1.3: SESSION STRUCTURE

The session runs as three acts, mirroring a motel's own shift structure. Total runtime target: **4 hours**, convention slot length.

| Act | Shift | Time at table | Question | Ends when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I: Roll Call** | Night | ~75 min | Who's in the group, and who isn't? | The table has a crew for the road, the interlopers are dealt with, and they know where the pickup went. |
| **II: The Big Rig** | Graveyard | ~90 min | What happened to our contact? | The rig is running and through the Reserve's gate (or another way in). |
| **III: Dig Them Out** | Morning | ~75 min | Can we get them (or their codes) off this planet? | The *Soft Landing* lifts, or doesn't. |

**The park is not optional.** The *Soft Landing* sits in a dry wash just outside the Halvorsen Basin Biological Reserve, and the pickup crew used their spare day to tour it. Whatever else the table decides, the only way off Jorjor runs through the Reserve.

**The secret under the premise.** "No contact" is literally true. The extraction team (Chapter 4, §4.8) came a day early, toured the Halvorsen Basin Biological Reserve (Chapter 2, §2.27), and got trapped underground in the closed camping areas 12–14. The players may voice the same suspicions all night: Wei burned them, Wei is compromised, or it is a test. The Reserve is what is actually true.

**The dust storm clock** (Chapter 2, §2.31) runs through all three acts: a distant wall in Act I, first gusts in Act II, full storm in Act III, dawn at the end.

## 1.4: TONE & SETTING DRESSING

The surface culture around the motel reads close to Earth, circa **1970s**, tuned to Jorjor's actual **TL7**: sedans, wired phones, film cameras, revolvers and shotguns (banned for civilians at Law 8 and carried only under permit or as contraband, Chapter 2, §2.32), grimy analog electronics rather than clean consumer tech, no visible grav vehicles or fusion tech in daily life. Think dust, heat-shimmer, and a gas station sign missing half its neon, not a leafy highway diner.

Everything for sale runs on gears, coins and springs: **mechanical vending machines** in the lobby and along the covered walkway, selling soda, cigarettes, stale snacks and a hand-cranked pack of road maps, each a heavy steel cabinet that clunks, whirs and sometimes eats the coins (Chapter 2, §2.12). No card readers, no screens, no smart tech to fail dramatically. They are part of the dressing, not a plot device, and they make a good place for two strangers to stand next to each other and not talk.

---

## 1.5: THE CORE MECHANIC

The scenario has no hidden traitor. All six PCs are genuine SolSec and are told so. The tension comes from three things working together: **an unexplained silence** (why did extraction not come?), **a source of heavy gear that is also an enemy** (Ansel Voss, the Imperial spy in the lot, Chapter 4, §4.6), and **a threat from the Reserve** (the gutwings, Chapter 6, §6.4) that proves something is badly wrong. Two PCs, Dabney and Tomas, hold private knowledge of the Reserve's armoury (Chapter 3, §3.3 and §3.7), and they decide when and how to share it.

## 1.7: PACING NOTES

- Don't let any single room's thread eat more than ~20 minutes uninterrupted; cut between rooms (in Act I) and between the cab and the trailer (in Act II) to keep all six players engaged.
- If the table is moving faster than expected, the Graveyard complications (§7.1) can be introduced early. If slower, cut them, or reveal them as a false alarm to buy time. In Act III, skip the Maintenance Yard haggling and go to the drill.
- Every action in the Reserve raises the **Alert track** (Chapter 2, §2.28). Use it to keep pressure on without a hard clock.
- For a clock-time reference across all three acts (when the complications land and when the dust storm advances) see Chapter 1, §1.6.

## 1.9: DISCOVERABLE THROUGH PLAY

What the table can find out, and where it lives. Use this mid-session instead of reconstructing the knowledge landscape from memory across the other documents.

- **The TL gear gap** (§5.1), the TL12 receiver in Voss's car and the military kit in his trunk, surfaced through Recon, Electronics checks or sharp in-fiction observation, never handed over for free.
- **The Reserve armoury** (Chapter 2, §2.29, Area 7): known beforehand to Dabney and Tomas, confirmed by Voss, and a later stop for the whole party.
- **The gutwings** (Chapter 2, §2.30; Chapter 6, §6.4): proof the Reserve has changed, and a torn strip of the pickup's jacket.
- **Osei's situation** (§4.7), why the extraction never came, reachable only indirectly if a PC tries the manifest office tonight: a busy signal, an evasive night clerk, or worse.
- **Highway traffic table results** (Chapter 2, §2.7) and **lobby random events** (Chapter 2, §2.12), mostly ambient texture, occasionally real signal buried in it.
- **The Fence-Line Hum** and **the Big Queue** (Chapter 2, §2.8; Chapter 2, §2.10): rumor-tier local color, deliberately never confirmed one way or the other.
- **Del's broken rig and the Ashworths' CB gossip** (Chapter 4, §4.2), mundane, intentionally ruled-out red herrings.
- **The pickup's day** (Chapter 6, §6.2; Chapter 4, §4.2): the cook, the washboy's Solomani coin, and Del's CB gossip about shots and a bang at the Reserve. Any two point at the Gut (Chapter 6, §6.1).
- **The Reserve's cover-up** (Chapter 2, §2.23; Chapter 4, §4.11): Pruitt's Form 11-C count and her list of missing names; the Incident Room; the Gate ledger entry for the pickup's permit #0614.
- **The paper trail** (Chapter 7, §7.7); the Gate's false "departed" stamp, the Clinic's "S. Hall," Pell's whiteboard, Teller's work orders; each office has a slice, spelled differently, and the contradictions are the clue to where the pickup really is.
- **What the Mass is and does** (Chapter 2, §2.30), Drifters, Waders and Egg-blooms; the way it reacts to psionics, gunfire and explosives.
- **The hang-up call** (Chapter 7, §7.2): a pulse and layered voices on the front-desk line at 1:35 AM. It is the Mass tapping the comms through the Feed Line's telemetry cable, traceable with Electronics (comms).
- **Voss's observations** (Chapter 4, §4.6): the pickup walking into the Reserve at 11:30, the closed gate, the covered stretcher at dusk, and the sealed cases carried into the Tower last week.

## 1.10: DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVABLE

Built to stay ambiguous regardless of how the session plays out, resist any pressure, including from a persistent table, to pin these down:

- **Wei's own role**: whether he burned them, is compromised, or is running a test. The *immediate* reason for no extraction is now known (the pickup got trapped underground in the Reserve; see Chapter 4, §4.8), but whether Wei sent them blind is never confirmed.
- **The Shutter Project and the satellite** (Chapter 2, §2.25–2.26), still explicitly optional background, not meant to resolve in this scenario.
- **Exactly what the Mass is** (Chapter 2, §2.21); its origin is known; its nature (one mind? waiting?) is not.
