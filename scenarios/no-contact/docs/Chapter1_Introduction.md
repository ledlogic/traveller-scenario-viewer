# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Chapter 1: Introduction

## CONVENTION BLURB (Con of the North)

Six strangers. One roadside motel on the edge of a dying desert world. Each of you is a Solomani Security agent told to lie low at Motel Yermo and wait for extraction back across the border. The ride never comes. The storm has cut the phones, the diner is far too quiet, and something out on the flats is being fed. Includes material integrated from Mystery Flesh Pit National Park. Mongoose Traveller 2e, pre-generated characters, new players welcome.

*(452 characters)*

---

## 1.1: PREMISE

**When:** day 245 of Y1116 (Imperial date 245-1116), a single night. The history behind Jorjor and the Rim is in Chapter 2, §2.20.

All six player characters are **Solomani Security (SolSec) assets** who have each been told, separately, to go to ground at a specific three-room motel, **Motel Yermo** *(Spanish, "wasteland/barren place"; matches the hellworld texture of Jorjor itself)*.  They were told to wait for expected extraction back across the border to Solomani Confederation space. Each was also told they'd be paired with a partner for this job (sharing a room, a cover story, and the wait for extraction) but each received that instruction independently, and most pairs have never actually met their partner in person before tonight. Verifying that the person who shows up matching your partner's description really is who they say they are is part of the job, not a given. No pair was told anything about the other two rooms; as far as any given pair knows, they may be the only SolSec presence at the motel tonight. Each pair has a cover identity, a matched pair of motel-guest personas, they believe is unique to them.

Every PC received some form of this last transmission, days before tonight, as a relayed telegram or coded HAM message:

> *"By now I hope you've completed your mission. Now meet up with your partner, identified by the codename provided, at ¿wasteland hotel. Extraction is at 2140 Imperium time on 245-1116. Do not leave lingering traces to this location; we intend to use it again. That means no loaner vehicle, no police charges on record, nothing that puts a name or a transaction anywhere near this address. Understand: no loose threads. - WeiVern"*

Extraction is set for **21:40 Imperium time (9:40 PM) on day 245 of Y1116**, at the motel. Over one night, in three locales, **The Motel / The Highway / The Reserve**, the six agents have to figure out why. Their orders arrive as relayed messages, telegrams and coded HAM traffic signed *WeiVern*, a name every PC knows to be **Baldwin Wei** (canon SolSec officer, *Signal GK*, Chapter 4, §4.13). Communications with SolSec have been kept deliberately minimal in case Imperial Security is listening, and old physical channels, telegrams and HAM radio carrying encoded messages, leave less exposure for SolSec on this side of the Imperial border than any modern link. Wei does not need to appear in person; his silence denotes the unconcerned oversight he is providing to the mission.

The extraction team (Chapter 4, §4.8), which consists of four SolSec agents aboard the courier-sloop *Soft Landing* (Chapter 5, §5.5), landed a day early, walked into the nearby Halvorsen Basin Biological Reserve (HBBR) (Chapter 2, §2.16–2.31) on a day-tour cover, carrying biological and psionic test equipment they never mentioned to anyone, and got trapped underground. The game is in three acts (Chapters 6–8): **Act I**, notice that something big has happened at the Reserve, survive the Mass's raiders (the gutwings, Chapter 2, §2.30) and decide to go and look; **Act II**, take a stranded feed hauler's big rig through the Reserve's gate; **Act III**, use the Reserve's own mole vehicles to drill to the pickup, or at least to their ship's key fobs, and get off planet before dawn.

## 1.3: FULL TIMELINE

A referee-facing timetable for every scheduled or randomly-triggered event across all three acts (Chapters 6–8).

### Timetable

**Jorjor Time Synchronization.** **IT** is Imperium time, the 24-hour clock that the starport, the Reserve, SolSec and every watch use (Chapter 2, §2.1, Jorjor Time). **JT** is Jorjor time, counted from sunrise: a Jorjor day is **6 JT hours**, 0:00 to 5:59, then it resets at the next sunrise. One JT hour is 62 IT minutes and a JT minute is the same length as an IT minute, so JT minutes run 00 to 61 and the day is 372 IT minutes (6h 12m). Sunrise is JT 0:00 and sunset is JT 3:00. Tonight's dawn at IT 05:45 is a sunrise, so sunrises fall at IT 05:45, 11:57, 18:09 and 00:21, about four between IT 21:00 and dawn. Use JT for what the sky is doing in a scene, and IT for everything people schedule. **The storm hides the sun:** from Stage 2 onward the sun-up hours are only a brown gloom, so "sky starts to lighten" (04:30) and "Dawn" (05:45) mean the storm thinning and then clearing, and only the 05:45 row is also a true sunrise.

## 1.4: ATMOSPHERE

Neural.AI image prompts for what the sky looks like in each act, matched to the sky, ring and dust storm stages in §1.3 and Chapter 2, §2.1 (Jorjor Sky, Air and Smell). Add the shared style line to the end of each prompt, and use the shared negative prompt with all of them.

The sky is the subject, so each prompt describes it as colour gradients and haze. Image generators draw the words "ring" and "sun" as huge hero objects, a floating hoop or a black eclipse disc, so the prompts never use the word ring: the planetary ring is described as a faint straight band of pale light, and the sun as a small half-set orange glow. The negative prompt blocks circles, halos and eclipses.

**Shared style line:** Muted palette, dry atmospheric haze, photorealistic matte painting, 1970s science fiction paperback cover feel, wide aspect ratio, no people. The sky fills most of the frame, soft and empty, with a low flat horizon.

**Shared negative prompt:** ring, circle, halo, hoop, torus, eclipse, black sun, lens flare, light rays, giant sun, huge sun, large planet, gas giant, Saturn, huge moon, full moon, second moon, multiple suns, planet rising over the horizon, blue sky, green vegetation, oceans, Earth-like clouds, cartoon, text, watermark, people, spacecraft, saturated neon colours, snow

### Act I: The Motel (IT 21:00 to 23:00, dusk into night, Stage 1 to 2)

A low flat desert horizon under a very large empty sky at dusk, thin tainted atmosphere. The sky is a smooth gradient of washed-out ochre-pink near the horizon, shading to dusty slate-violet overhead, with a rusty-brown haze of fine dust and a few thin streaks of cloud. A small copper-orange glow of a sun, half set behind the horizon, tiny against the sky, with a bruise-yellow and rust glow spreading along the skyline. A faint straight band of pale bone-white light crosses the upper sky from one horizon to the other, thin like a high contrail or the Milky Way, with a slight copper tint. A distant low orange wall of dust on the horizon. A small roadside motel with a few warm yellow windows sits tiny in the lower frame, with a deserted two-lane highway running to the vanishing point.

### Act II: The Highway (IT 00:00 to 01:45, Stage 2 to 3)

A long empty two-lane highway running to the vanishing point across a flat desert plain at night, seen from low on the road, thin tainted atmosphere. The sky fills most of the frame as a heavy, even, dim orange-brown dust gloom, darker at the top and a little lighter near the horizon, with no sun, no moon and no bright object anywhere, only a faint diffuse glow low behind the dust. Grit blows across the asphalt in low sheets. In the distance a single pair of headlights makes a narrow cone through the dust. Roadside power poles lean into the wind. Oppressive, claustrophobic, nothing human in sight.

### Act III: The Reserve (IT 01:45 to 05:45, Stage 3 to 4)

A ruined survey fence and gate on the rim of a vast sunken basin, thin tainted atmosphere. The sky is a thick, featureless wall of rust-brown dust, orange fading to brown, filling the whole upper frame, with no sun, no moon and no bright objects visible. Low on the horizon the storm is thinning, and a narrow strip of pale copper-pink light shows through. The basin floor falls away into shadow, with a faint damp sheen and pale fleshy mounds in the dust, and a faint organic wrongness in the air. Torn warning signs hang from the fence. Heavy, claustrophobic, lit only by dim storm-light and the first clearing.

### Dawn variant (IT 05:45, for the end of Act III or the Conclusion)

The storm has cleared. A flat rust-coloured plain under a very large pale sky, a smooth gradient of washed ochre-pink near the horizon to slate-violet overhead. A small copper-orange glow of a sun just clearing the horizon, tiny against the sky, with a dust-brown haze lying low. A faint straight band of pale bone-white light crosses the upper sky, thin like a high contrail. Cold light on a scarred, empty landscape.

## 1.5: PACING NOTES

- Don't let any single room's thread eat more than ~20 minutes uninterrupted; cut between rooms (in Act I) and between the cab and the trailer (in Act II) to keep all six players engaged.
- If the table is moving faster than expected, the Act II complications (§7.1) can be introduced early. If slower, cut them, or reveal them as a false alarm to buy time. In Act III, skip the Maintenance Yard haggling and go to the drill.
- Every action in the Reserve raises the **Alert track** (Chapter 2, §2.28). Use it to keep pressure on without a hard clock.
- For a clock-time reference across all three acts (when the complications land and when the dust storm advances) see Chapter 1, §1.3.
