| Layer | TL | Notes |

| **Jorjor (surface world)** | **TL7** | Everything visible reads as period-plausible: cars, phones, cameras, firearms (legal, unremarkable), analog/early-digital electronics, no obvious spacer tech. |

**Referee tip:** Never let the TL gap be the *only* tell. Pair every mechanical tell with a behavioral one so sharp roleplay is rewarded as much as sharp system mastery.

### Del's rig: *the Gravy Train*

![The Gravy Train](img/gravy-train.webp)

Yes, there is a big rig, and it is the table's best way to the Reserve (Chapter 7, §7.3, Route A). Del calls it **the Gravy Train**, the name hand-lettered in flaking cream paint on both doors, under a chrome bull and a row of amber roof lamps. It is a TL7 diesel-analog 6x4 tractor pulling a 30-tonne stainless **feed tanker**, loaded tonight with 28 tonnes of slaughterhouse offal on a stamped manifest (Consignment 9-4471).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Seats** | Cab bench seats three (driver plus two). A curtained sleeper berth fits two more. Up to five inside, comfortably. |
| **Riding outside** | Catwalk and tank-top hatch fit three or four more, holding on, in grit and wind. Anyone up there is **coated in feed** by the end of the trip (Chapter 2, §2.31). |
| **Speed** | 90 km/h on the highway; 40 km/h on the Reserve's service track; 15 km/h in a dust storm. |
| **Hull / Armour** | Hull 40, Structure 40, Armour 4 (a heavy steel bumper and a lot of tank). Tires are the weak point. |
| **Range** | About 800 km on a full tank; fuel is not the problem tonight. |
| **Kit** | Air horn (audible 5 km, which raises attention), CB radio, roof floodlight bar, front winch (rated for the rig itself, useful for pulling a person or a stuck mole), steam-wash hose from the tank pump. |
| **Quirks** | Reeks of offal; Del swears the smell washes out. Idles with a rattle and a cough. Del drives it with great care and will not let anyone else take the wheel without a very good reason. |

**Its problem tonight:** genuinely broken down, not staged; a fouled fuel line, plausible for a TL7 diesel-analog engine after a long haul through dust and heat. Fixable with a **Mechanic check** (Engineer (power) also works) at roughly **2D hours** of work and whatever period-appropriate parts are on hand. Each additional person with Mechanic or Engineer skill working on it cuts the time by about a quarter. If the party wants the rig running faster than that, treat it as a scene, not an automatic timer skip. Del is possessive of his truck and won't hand off repairs to a stranger without a good reason. He also will not leave without it.

**Neural.AI image prompt:** A battered 1970s-style long-haul semi tractor with a long stainless fuel tanker trailer, "Gravy Train" hand-lettered in flaking cream paint on the cab door, amber roof lamps glowing, steam rising from the tank hatch, parked at the edge of a gravel motel lot under an orange dust-storm sky. Photorealistic, science fiction/Americana crossover, no text beyond the door lettering, no logos.

### Repairable vehicles within 100km

| Distance | Location | What's there | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~15km | Roadside filling station/garage | Small, one mechanic on call, closed overnight, reachable but nobody's answering the phone (comms are down anyway, per §2.5) | Low, but getting there on foot or by hitching in a storm is its own ordeal |
| ~60km | Regional town with a proper garage | Real parts inventory, staffed during the day, closes at night | Moderate: a "hot" or stolen vehicle showing up here draws normal small-town attention, not Imperial-level scrutiny, but High Law 8 means local police involvement is a real possibility |
| ~90km | Processing facility (site of the labor-stoppage rumor, §2.9) | Industrial vehicle pool, technically not public, heavier equipment | Higher: trespassing on an active labor dispute site, plus equipment here skews toward heavy/industrial rather than road-legal passenger vehicles |

**Referee framing:** nothing within 100km is a clean, risk-free way out. Every option either takes real in-fiction time (repair, travel on foot or by hitching), involves a legal risk under Jorjor's High Law regime, or both, which keeps the "trapped" feeling from Chapter 6, §6.1 intact even once players start looking for a way around it, rather than solving the whole premise with a lucky Mechanic roll.

## 5.4: MOLES

The Reserve keeps **two** TL7 **moles** (burrowing excavators) at Area 9, used to inspect the Mass and to lay pipe. A mole takes a driver (Drive (mole) check) and carries four passengers.

| | **Mole-1 "Maggie"** | **Mole-2 "Lazarus"** |
|---|---|---|
| Crew / pax | 2 / 4 | 2 / 6 |
| Speed through soil | 4 km/h | 3 km/h |
| Speed through rock | 1 km/h | 1 km/h |
| Speed through the Mass | 6 km/h (it is soft) | 5 km/h |
| Armour | 8 | 8 |
| Hull / Structure | 28 / 28 | 36 / 36 |
| Condition | Running | Needs a **fan belt** (Mechanic, 30 minutes) |

*(House statistics. Adapt to the vehicle rules in the Core Rulebook.)*

**Drilling through the Mass hurts it:** +1 AP per 10 minutes, and Waders converge on the vibration. Drilling through *rock* or *soil* is quiet. The best route to the Orchard is **soil from Area 9 to Area 12**, then the Terrace tunnels, then rock to the grove's roots.

**Distances from Area 9:** Area 12: 3.5 km; Area 13: 4.5 km; Area 14: 5.5 km.

## 5.5: THE SHIP, *SOFT LANDING*

A **TL11 SolSec courier-sloop**, 100 dTon, Jump-1, with a cargo hold with four cots, and a quiet, matte-grey hull that looks, from the air, like a dry-wash boulder. Passive sensor-shadow field (TL11) hides it from Jorjor's TL7 surveillance unless something draws attention to it.

- **Location:** a dry wash **11 km north of Area 13**, beyond the Reserve fence line. Reachable on foot across the flats (Route C, Chapter 7, §7.3).
- **Fuel:** enough for one lift and one jump.
- **Lock:** TL11 biometric-and-key. **Any two of the four key fobs** (§5.6) open the hatch and wake the flight computer. A fob alone gets the door open and nothing else.
- **Backup:** Okafor-Brandt's spoken codeword *("Bluebird, no reply")* overrides the lock from inside the hatch comm panel. Anyone who hears it can use it.
- **Flight computer:** will lift on autopilot for a PC at the controls. Skilled pilot not required; Pilot (spacecraft) 1+ is easier.

## 5.6: THE KEY FOBS

Each team member wears a **bone-conduction key fob** (TL11, a slim black disc on a chain, passes as a dog tag). Under the **2-of-4 rule**, even a partial rescue pays off: two fobs, two living people, or two bodies with their gear. The fobs stay with the person.

| Fob | Wearer | If captured | If trapped | If lost | If held |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Okafor-Brandt | On her, in the cradle | On her | On her | Locked in the Incident Room evidence cage |
| 2 | Mbeki | On him | On him | On him | Same cage |
| 3 | Hale | On her | On her | On her | Same cage |
| 4 | Delacroix | On him | On him | On him | Same cage |

**Referee note:** if the table recovers the fobs but not the team, they can leave. The scenario's third Act ends on that choice; everyone out, or only the codes out.
