# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Appendix B: Night Atmosphere, Detection Risk & Random Tables

*Referee Reference Document*

## B.0: WHY THEY FEEL TRAPPED

All six agents took **one-way rides** to reach this motel (a courier drop-off, a hired local driver, a hitched ride that continued on without them) on the assumption that extraction would be their ride *out*. None of them arranged their own return transport, because none of them expected to need it. That's the trap: it's not a locked door, it's the quiet, sinking realization over the course of the night that nobody has a car, nobody knows a reliable local contact, and the nearest town is a long walk down a two-lane highway in the dark.

Layer in two concrete, external complications:

- **A dust storm (haboob) front is blocking regional traffic** on the highway approach (see Chapter 8, §8.7 for its clock). It is real and checkable (a radio weather bulletin, a trucker who mentions it, downed branches visible from the parking lot), not a mystical omen. It's the reason a normal ride out isn't simple even before anyone suspects anything's wrong.
- **Local commercial comms are out**, knocked down by the same storm, payphones dead, the motel office's own line down. This is what makes the missed extraction ambiguous rather than immediately alarming: is there no contact because the extraction failed, or just because the storm took the lines down? Neither the players nor their characters can tell early on, and that ambiguity should be played straight, not tipped either way by the referee's tone.

## B.1: TECH LEVEL DISCIPLINE, WHAT GETS NOTICED

Yes, **using gear above the local TL7 baseline risks detection**, and this should be a standing pressure the whole session, not a one-time rule. Jorjor's starport and regional infrastructure include TL7-appropriate detection: passive radio-spectrum monitoring (routine, automated, watching for exactly the kind of anomalous signal a TL10+ transmitter or scanner throws off), and, because Jorjor sits in Imperial space, any signal that *is* flagged gets escalated to the **Imperial Security Service**, not just local police. A flagged signal doesn't mean immediate arrest; it means someone starts asking the local bureaucracy pointed questions, which is its own slow-building threat given Chapter 1, §1.2.

**House rule for the table:** any use of TL10+ gear for anything beyond brief, low-power, one-off activity (a quick burst transmission, a single scan) should call for a **Electronics (comms) or Stealth check** against a DM set by how sustained or high-power the use is. A failed check doesn't mean instant Imperial agents at the door; it means a detection event has occurred, banked quietly by the referee, that surfaces later (a "routine" knock from a bureaucratic inspector, a stranger's car that starts idling outside, per Chapter 3 §3.2's complication options) rather than immediately.

**The practical implication for players:** anyone using their real tradecraft gear has to weigh capability against exposure, and the TL12 gear carried by whichever PC is secretly the Imperial mole (Chapter 1, §1.3) is the single riskiest thing in the building to use carelessly, which gives sharp players something concrete to watch for without the referee needing to hand-wave a "vibes-based" tell.

## B.2: RANDOM TABLE, TRAFFIC THAT DOESN'T STOP

Roll or pick 1D6 once per hour of in-fiction night, or whenever a PC is watching the highway (Room 1 Guest 2 does this by default, Chapter 6, §6.2):

| 1D6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | A single set of headlights approaches, slows almost to a stop directly opposite the motel, then continues on. No horn, no signal. |
| 2 | A convoy of two or three vehicles passes at speed, moving away from the storm-blocked region, locals evacuating, not extraction traffic. |
| 3 | Nothing for a long stretch; the silence itself becomes the tell; comment on how long it's been since *anything* passed. |
| 4 | A vehicle's lights flick on and off twice, far down the highway, then go dark entirely. Could be signal, could be a stall, could be nothing. |
| 5 | Local law enforcement patrol car passes at normal speed, doesn't slow, spotlight briefly sweeps the motel lot out of routine, not suspicion. |
| 6 | A vehicle actually turns into the lot: a genuine new arrival (civilian, lost, or something else, referee's call in the moment). This is the one result that should always demand a scene, not just a description. |

## B.3: LOCAL FLORA & FAUNA

Jorjor's thin, tainted atmosphere and dry (10% water) surface support sparse, tough native life; nothing native is aggressive toward humans by default, but the environment itself is what makes going outside at night unpleasant rather than dangerous:

- **Scrub-thorn**: low, wiry desert brush that catches clothing and makes quiet movement across open ground genuinely difficult (DM−2 to Stealth checks moving through it off the beaten path).
- **Wind-chitters**: small, nocturnal, insect-analog swarms that produce a constant dry clicking/rattling sound across the open desert at night, unsettling ambient noise, harmless, but excellent cover for masking (or mistaking) the sound of an actual approach.
- **Dust-runners**: fist-sized ground-burrowing scavengers, skittish, harmless, but their sudden scrabbling movement in peripheral vision is a reliable source of false alarms and jump-scares for anyone stepping outside.
- **The Fence-Line Hum** (optional): roughly 4km out, a stretch of scrub-desert fenced off decades ago by the Civil Service Bureaucracy (Chapter 1, §1.2) as an unexplained hazard site, officially logged as "geological survey, access restricted." Locals who'll talk about it at all describe a low, directionless hum audible only at night, wind-chitters going dead silent within the fence line, and a standing rule never to bring metal tools past the posted markers. Nobody's filed a real incident report in years; the fence, like everything else in Jorjor's bureaucracy, mostly persists because nobody's filed the correct form to take it down. Play this strictly as unresolved texture: a rumor a PC might hear in the lobby (§B.4, or Appendix C) or from the motel owner, never a site the scenario actually visits. It exists to make Jorjor feel like a world with older, stranger history than tonight's plot touches, not a plot hook to chase.

Nothing on Jorjor should physically threaten the PCs outdoors; the fear is psychological and logistical (visibility, footing, disorientation in unfamiliar scrub-desert dark), not predation.

## B.4: RUMORS OF UNREST OR BANDITS

Optional flavor, seed through the motel owner (Appendix A) or passing traffic (§B.2, result 2 or 5) as background texture, not necessarily plot-relevant:

- Talk of a **wildcat labor stoppage** at a regional processing facility, unrelated to tonight's events but explaining unusual road traffic patterns and a heavier local law-enforcement presence than normal.
- Rumors of **highway bandits** working the storm-affected stretch of road, preying on stranded travelers, plausible, mostly exaggerated, but a good reason for a PC to hesitate before trying to walk out alone.
- A vague, half-believed story about a **civil service inspector who vanished** on an audit trip out this way last season, deniable, mostly campfire-story material, but a referee who wants to lean harder into Chapter 1, §1.2's audit-discovery scenario can plant this as a quiet foreshadowing beat.

### Del's rig: *the Gravy Train*

Yes, there is a big rig, and it is the table's best way to the Reserve (Chapter 8, §8.4, Route A). Del calls it **the Gravy Train**, the name hand-lettered in flaking cream paint on both doors, under a chrome bull and a row of amber roof lamps. It is a TL7 diesel-analog 6x4 tractor pulling a 30-tonne stainless **feed tanker**, loaded tonight with 28 tonnes of slaughterhouse offal on a stamped manifest (Consignment 9-4471).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Seats** | Cab bench seats three (driver plus two). A curtained sleeper berth fits two more. Up to five inside, comfortably. |
| **Riding outside** | Catwalk and tank-top hatch fit three or four more, holding on, in grit and wind. Anyone up there is **coated in feed** by the end of the trip (Chapter 8, §8.3). |
| **Speed** | 90 km/h on the highway; 40 km/h on the Reserve's service track; 15 km/h in a dust storm. |
| **Hull / Armour** | Hull 40, Structure 40, Armour 4 (a heavy steel bumper and a lot of tank). Tires are the weak point. |
| **Range** | About 800 km on a full tank; fuel is not the problem tonight. |
| **Kit** | Air horn (audible 5 km, which raises attention), CB radio, roof floodlight bar, front winch (rated for the rig itself, useful for pulling a person or a stuck mole), steam-wash hose from the tank pump. |
| **Quirks** | Reeks of offal; Del swears the smell washes out. Idles with a rattle and a cough. Del drives it with great care and will not let anyone else take the wheel without a very good reason. |

**Its problem tonight:** genuinely broken down, not staged; a fouled fuel line, plausible for a TL7 diesel-analog engine after a long haul through dust and heat. Fixable with a **Mechanic check** (Engineer (power) also works) at roughly **2D hours** of work and whatever period-appropriate parts are on hand. Each additional person with Mechanic or Engineer skill working on it cuts the time by about a quarter. If the party wants the rig running faster than that, treat it as a scene, not an automatic timer skip. Del is possessive of his truck and won't hand off repairs to a stranger without a good reason. He also will not leave without it.

**Neural.AI image prompt:** A battered 1970s-style long-haul semi tractor with a long stainless fuel tanker trailer, "Gravy Train" hand-lettered in flaking cream paint on the cab door, amber roof lamps glowing, steam rising from the tank hatch, parked at the edge of a gravel motel lot under an orange dust-storm sky. Photorealistic, science fiction/Americana crossover, no text beyond the door lettering, no logos.

### Repairable vehicles within 100km

| Distance | Location | What's there | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~15km | Roadside filling station/garage | Small, one mechanic on call, closed overnight, reachable but nobody's answering the phone (comms are down anyway, per §B.0) | Low, but getting there on foot or by hitching in a storm is its own ordeal |
| ~60km | Regional town with a proper garage | Real parts inventory, staffed during the day, closes at night | Moderate: a "hot" or stolen vehicle showing up here draws normal small-town attention, not Imperial-level scrutiny, but High Law 8 means local police involvement is a real possibility |
| ~90km | Processing facility (site of the labor-stoppage rumor, §B.4) | Industrial vehicle pool, technically not public, heavier equipment | Higher: trespassing on an active labor dispute site, plus equipment here skews toward heavy/industrial rather than road-legal passenger vehicles |

**Referee framing:** nothing within 100km is a clean, risk-free way out. Every option either takes real in-fiction time (repair, travel on foot or by hitching), involves a legal risk under Jorjor's High Law regime, or both, which keeps the "trapped" feeling from Chapter 3, §3.1 intact even once players start looking for a way around it, rather than solving the whole premise with a lucky Mechanic roll.
