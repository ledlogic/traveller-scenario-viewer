# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Appendix B — Night Atmosphere, Detection Risk & Random Tables

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## B.0 — WHY THEY FEEL TRAPPED

All six agents took **one-way rides** to reach this motel — a courier drop-off, a
hired local driver, a hitched ride that continued on without them — on the assumption
that extraction would be their ride *out*. None of them arranged their own return
transport, because none of them expected to need it. That's the trap: it's not a
locked door, it's the quiet, sinking realization over the course of the night that
nobody has a car, nobody knows a reliable local contact, and the nearest town is a long
walk down a two-lane highway in the dark.

Layer in two concrete, external complications:

- **A storm is blocking regional traffic** on the highway approach — real, checkable
  (a radio weather bulletin, a trucker who mentions it, downed branches visible from
  the parking lot), not a mystical omen. It's the reason a normal ride out isn't
  simple even before anyone suspects anything's wrong.
- **Local commercial comms are out**, knocked down by the same storm — payphones
  dead, the motel office's own line down. This is what makes the missed extraction
  ambiguous rather than immediately alarming: is there no contact because the
  extraction failed, or just because the storm took the lines down? Neither the
  players nor their characters can tell early on, and that ambiguity should be
  played straight, not tipped either way by the referee's tone.

## B.1 — TECH LEVEL DISCIPLINE: WHAT GETS NOTICED

Yes — **using gear above the local TL7 baseline risks detection**, and this should be
a standing pressure the whole session, not a one-time rule. Jorjor's starport and
regional infrastructure include TL7-appropriate detection: passive radio-spectrum
monitoring (routine, automated, watching for exactly the kind of anomalous signal a
TL10+ transmitter or scanner throws off), and — because Jorjor sits in Imperial
space — any signal that *is* flagged gets escalated to the **Imperial Security
Service**, not just local police. A flagged signal doesn't mean immediate arrest; it
means someone starts asking the local bureaucracy pointed questions, which is its own
slow-building threat given Chapter 1, §1.2.

**House rule for the table:** any use of TL10+ gear for anything beyond
brief, low-power, one-off activity (a quick burst transmission, a single scan) should
call for a **Electronics (comms) or Stealth check** against a DM set by how sustained
or high-power the use is. A failed check doesn't mean instant Imperial agents at the
door — it means a detection event has occurred, banked quietly by the referee, that
surfaces later (a "routine" knock from a bureaucratic inspector, a stranger's car
that starts idling outside, per Chapter 3 §3.2's complication options) rather than
immediately.

**The practical implication for players:** anyone using their real tradecraft gear has
to weigh capability against exposure — and the TL12 gear carried by whichever PC is
secretly the Imperial mole (Chapter 1, §1.3) is the single riskiest thing in the
building to use carelessly, which gives sharp players something concrete to watch for
without the referee needing to hand-wave a "vibes-based" tell.

## B.2 — RANDOM TABLE: TRAFFIC THAT DOESN'T STOP

Roll or pick 1D6 once per hour of in-fiction night, or whenever a PC is watching the
highway (Room 1 Guest 2 does this by default — Chapter 6, §6.2):

| 1D6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | A single set of headlights approaches, slows almost to a stop directly opposite the motel, then continues on. No horn, no signal. |
| 2 | A convoy of two or three vehicles passes at speed, moving away from the storm-blocked region — locals evacuating, not extraction traffic. |
| 3 | Nothing for a long stretch — the silence itself becomes the tell; comment on how long it's been since *anything* passed. |
| 4 | A vehicle's lights flick on and off twice, far down the highway, then go dark entirely. Could be signal, could be a stall, could be nothing. |
| 5 | Local law enforcement patrol car passes at normal speed, doesn't slow, spotlight briefly sweeps the motel lot out of routine, not suspicion. |
| 6 | A vehicle actually turns into the lot — a genuine new arrival (civilian, lost, or something else; referee's call in the moment) — the one result that should always demand a scene, not just a description. |

## B.3 — LOCAL FLORA & FAUNA

Jorjor's thin, tainted atmosphere and dry (10% water) surface support sparse, tough
native life — nothing native is aggressive toward humans by default, but the
environment itself is what makes going outside at night unpleasant rather than
dangerous:

- **Scrub-thorn** — low, wiry desert brush that catches clothing and makes quiet
  movement across open ground genuinely difficult (DM−2 to Stealth checks moving
  through it off the beaten path).
- **Wind-chitters** — small, nocturnal, insect-analog swarms that produce a
  constant dry clicking/rattling sound across the open desert at night — unsettling
  ambient noise, harmless, but excellent cover for masking (or mistaking) the sound
  of an actual approach.
- **Dust-runners** — fist-sized ground-burrowing scavengers, skittish, harmless,
  but their sudden scrabbling movement in peripheral vision is a reliable source of
  false alarms and jump-scares for anyone stepping outside.
- **The Fence-Line Hum** (optional) — roughly 4km out, a stretch of scrub-desert
  fenced off decades ago by the Civil Service Bureaucracy (Chapter 1, §1.2) as an
  unexplained hazard site, officially logged as "geological survey — access
  restricted." Locals who'll talk about it at all describe a low, directionless hum
  audible only at night, wind-chitters going dead silent within the fence line, and
  a standing rule never to bring metal tools past the posted markers. Nobody's
  filed a real incident report in years — the fence, like everything else in
  Jorjor's bureaucracy, mostly persists because nobody's filed the correct form to
  take it down. Play this strictly as unresolved texture: a rumor a PC might hear
  in the lobby (§B.4, or Appendix C) or from the motel owner, never a site the scenario
  actually visits. It exists to make Jorjor feel like a world with older, stranger
  history than tonight's plot touches — not a plot hook to chase.

Nothing on Jorjor should physically threaten the PCs outdoors — the fear is
psychological and logistical (visibility, footing, disorientation in unfamiliar
scrub-desert dark), not predation.

## B.4 — RUMORS OF UNREST OR BANDITS

Optional flavor, seed through the motel owner (Appendix A) or passing traffic
(§B.2, result 2 or 5) as background texture, not necessarily plot-relevant:

- Talk of a **wildcat labor stoppage** at a regional processing facility, unrelated
  to tonight's events but explaining unusual road traffic patterns and a heavier
  local law-enforcement presence than normal.
- Rumors of **highway bandits** working the storm-affected stretch of road,
  preying on stranded travelers — plausible, mostly exaggerated, but a good reason
  for a PC to hesitate before trying to walk out alone.
- A vague, half-believed story about a **civil service inspector who vanished**
  on an audit trip out this way last season — deniable, mostly campfire-story
  material, but a referee who wants to lean harder into Chapter 1, §1.2's audit-discovery
  scenario can plant this as a quiet foreshadowing beat.

## B.5 — VEHICLES

### What's parked outside tonight

**No PC arrived with their own vehicle** — per §B.0, every agent took a one-way ride
to get here, on the assumption extraction would be the ride out. Whatever's in the
gravel lot belongs to someone else:

| Vehicle | Owner | Condition | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Battered pickup truck | Motel owner (Appendix A) | Running, TL7, half a tank | The owner's own errand vehicle. Borrowing it without asking is theft under Jorjor's High Law 8 — a real legal risk, not just a social one. |
| Long-haul freight rig + trailer | Del Ashworth (Appendix C) | **Not currently running** — see below | Parked at the edge of the lot, engine cover up. Del's been "fixing" it since he arrived, which is also his cover for being out in the lot at odd hours if a referee wants a red-herring beat. |
| Motel's own courtesy van | Motel (shared) | Running, but visibly marked with the motel's name/logo | Useless for a quiet getaway — recognizable on sight to anyone in the area. |

**Del's rig, specifically:** genuinely broken down, not staged — a fouled fuel line,
plausible for a TL7 diesel-analog engine after a long haul through dust and heat.
Fixable with an **Engineer (life support) or Mechanic-equivalent check** (referee's
choice of skill per house ruleset) at roughly **2D hours** of work and whatever
period-appropriate parts are on hand. If the party wants Del's rig running faster
than that, treat it as a scene, not an automatic timer skip — Del is possessive of
his truck and won't hand off repairs to a stranger without a good reason.

### Repairable vehicles within 100km

| Distance | Location | What's there | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~15km | Roadside filling station/garage | Small, one mechanic on call, closed overnight — reachable but nobody's answering the phone (comms are down anyway, per §B.0) | Low — but getting there on foot or by hitching in a storm is its own ordeal |
| ~60km | Regional town with a proper garage | Real parts inventory, staffed during the day, closes at night | Moderate — a "hot" or stolen vehicle showing up here draws normal small-town attention, not Imperial-level scrutiny, but High Law 8 means local police involvement is a real possibility |
| ~90km | Processing facility (site of the labor-stoppage rumor, §B.4) | Industrial vehicle pool, technically not public, heavier equipment | Higher — trespassing on an active labor dispute site, plus equipment here skews toward heavy/industrial rather than road-legal passenger vehicles |

**Referee framing:** nothing within 100km is a clean, risk-free way out. Every option
either takes real in-fiction time (repair, travel on foot or by hitching), involves a
legal risk under Jorjor's High Law regime, or both — which keeps the "trapped"
feeling from Chapter 3, §3.1 intact even once players start looking for a way around
it, rather than solving the whole premise with a lucky Mechanic roll.
