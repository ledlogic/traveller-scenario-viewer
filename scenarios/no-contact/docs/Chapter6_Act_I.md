# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Chapter 6: Act I: Night, "Roll Call"

*Referee Reference Document*

## 6.1: ACT I: NIGHT, "ROLL CALL" (~75 MINUTES)

Guests arrive and settle in. Small talk, cover stories, first hints something is off; a car that doesn't stop at the motel sign, a payphone call that doesn't connect, a light that shouldn't be on in an empty room. Players start informally sounding each other out without revealing themselves.

**Establish the trap early, quietly:** every PC took a one-way ride to get here, assuming extraction would be their ride out; nobody arranged their own return transport. Layer in two concrete complications rather than vague dread: a **dust storm front is blocking regional highway traffic** (Stage 1: an orange wall on the south-west horizon), and it has also knocked out **local commercial comms** (payphones, the motel's own line). Neither is supernatural or plot-generated; both are checkable, ordinary facts a PC can confirm. Three small beats seed the wrongness before anyone says it aloud: a **sealed-load tanker** that rolls slowly past before the road closes, **night sounds** (a rail horn or slurry-pump throb that should not be running), and the **late lighter** flying low toward the downport (Chapter 2, §2.7 results 7 and 8; §2.31). Use them as texture first and clues second. Full detail, including a random table for passing traffic that never stops, local flora/fauna texture, and optional unrest/bandit rumors, is in Chapter 2, §2.7–2.9.

**Tech discipline matters from the first scene.** Jorjor runs passive detection at its own TL7 baseline, escalating to the Imperial Security Service if something anomalous is flagged, see Chapter 2, §2.6 for the house rule on checks required when a PC risks using above-TL7 gear.

**The goal of Act I.** The table should notice that **something big happened at the Reserve** and decide to go and look. Nobody tells them. They work it out from what is wrong around them: the extraction that never came, the cook's four hikers and the washboy's coin, Del's CB talk of shots and a bang, and then, at the end of the act, something from the Reserve that comes to the motel. Two of the party, Dabney and Tomas, already know the Reserve keeps a weapons locker with military-grade hardware (Chapter 3, §3.3 and §3.7). A third piece of the puzzle, and a source of weapons tonight, is the idling stranger in the lot (Chapter 4, §4.6).

### The question: who's in the group, and who isn't?

Act I's real work is sorting people.

- **In the group:** the six SolSec guests in Rooms 1–3 (Chapter 3). They each know their *room partner* by codename; they do not know the others. All six are genuine.
- **Not in the group (bystanders):** the motel owner (Chapter 4, §4.1), the Ashworths (Chapter 4, §4.2), the restaurant staff (Chapter 4, §4.3). Ordinary people, and genuine bystanders if the night turns violent.
- **The interlopers.** Anyone who should not be riding along, watching, or asking questions: **Cabo Marsh** (Chapter 4, §4.5), a curious Priya, and, the dangerous one, **the idling stranger** (Chapter 4, §4.6), an Imperial Security Service agent who knows exactly what the motel is.

**Get the interlopers out** means the table makes a decision about each:

| Interloper | Options |
|---|---|
| Cabo Marsh | Talk him away (boring, plausible story). Mislead him. Take him out of the picture. |
| The idling stranger (Voss) | Leave him alone and let him watch. Approach him unseen. Trade with him. Take his kit by stealth or force. He will not be shaken off; he stops watching only when the party is gone or he is. See §6.3. |
| The Ashworths | Keep them out of it. Del becomes essential in Act II; Priya stays at the motel. |

**The Roll Call scene.** Around 40–50 minutes in, put the six in a room: Wei's order in each hand. They compare notes on "no loose threads" and the one-way rides, and someone says the sentence the scenario exists for: *"If they're not coming, why not?"* Do not resolve it. Let the three theories (Wei burned us / Wei's compromised / it's a test) be argued out. If Dabney or Tomas's players have not mentioned what they scouted, give them an opening: ask each what they remember of the Reserve from their pre-mission work.

### Act I closes pointing at the Reserve

Before the attack the table should be working toward **two of these three** pieces of information, enough to point at the Reserve. The attack itself (§6.4) supplies a fourth, and Voss (§6.3) a fifth:

| Clue | Source | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| The cook | Chapter 4, §4.3 | Four off-worlder-looking hikers ate breakfast at 10:30, asked the road to "the Gut," left a large tip. |
| The washboy | Chapter 4, §4.3 | Saw the four walk the spur road toward the Reserve at 11:30. One woman gave him a coin: **a silver Solomani "Terran" coin** (TL tell). |
| Del's CB | Chapter 4, §4.2 | Heard on the CB: the Reserve "closed up tight" at five o'clock; **shots, then a bang** around four-twenty; areas 12 through 14 are shut. |

If the table is slow, have Del or the cook volunteer it. If fast, add the Reserve's own signage on the highway. The gutwing attack is the catch-all: even a table that found none of these cannot miss it.

## 6.2: WHAT THEY SAW TODAY: THE PICKUP

Earlier today, four off-worlder-looking hikers passed through the restaurant (Chapter 4, §4.8). The cook and the washboy are the table's best early pointers to the Reserve (Chapter 6, §6.1).

- **The cook** (Act I clue): served four strangers (two women and two men in good boots and field jackets, one younger man carrying a pack too heavy for a tourist) at about 10:30. They ordered strong coffee and pie, asked the road to "the Gut," and left a large cash tip in unfamiliar notes. He tells the story if asked about strangers, or volunteers it if the table is stuck.
- **The washboy** (Act I clue): saw the four walk off down the spur road at about 11:30. A woman handed him a coin as thanks: a **silver Solomani "Terran" coin**, TL10-minted, plainly not Jorjor currency. He is proud of it. A PC with Streetwise, Electronics, or the right Science check can tell it is not Imperial.

## 6.3: THE SPY AND HIS KIT (~10:35 PM)

The idling stranger is **Ansel Voss**, an Imperial Security Service operative under the cover name Hollis Brandt (Chapter 4, §4.6). A sedan sits at the edge of the lot, engine running, lights off. Anyone who has Recon, a window, or a bad feeling can see it. He has been watching the motel for hours.

**What the PCs can do.**
- **Watch him.** Recon (Average 8+) from a room or the lot. He is photographing the doors with a long lens. That is enough to know he is not lost.
- **Approach unseen.** Stealth (Average 8+), Dex or Dabney. They reach the car and see the dash receiver's green glow and the long-lens camera on the seat. A fail means he sees them, says nothing, and drives to the far edge of the lot.
- **Talk to him.** He plays "Hollis Brandt, equipment assessor" and bluffs. Investigate against his Deception 2, opposed, reveals the cover is thin. He does not know their faces but knows the cover names; a PC who uses theirs gets an amused nod.
- **Take his kit.** The trunk case (Chapter 4, §4.6, Mechanic or Electronics Average 8+) holds weapons and armour for four. A discovered theft means he draws his pistol, drives off, and the gutwings (§6.4) catch him on the road. The kit stays in the wreck and is recoverable.
- **Trade.** He wants the pickup's location and a promise to be left alone. Persuade or Deception (Difficult 10+) buys the kit before the attack, or trades it after the attack for his life.

**If the table never goes near him.** The gutwings find him at 10:40 anyway. The kit is in the wreck and the table can pull it out afterward. Do not let a table skip the guns because it skipped the conversation.

**What the kit is for.** It is sized to beat the first flight, no more. See the count table in §6.4.

## 6.4: THE GUTWING ATTACK (~10:40 PM)

The Mass was alarmed at 16:20 when Dr. Hale's accidental pulse woke it (Chapter 7, §7.5). It has sent its raiders to hunt whatever is warm, loud or emitting TL10+ gear. The nearest such thing to the Reserve's southern fence is the sedan in the motel lot. The next is the motel.

**The beats.**
1. **The wrong sound (10:38).** Dust-storm Stage 2 is gusting. Over the wind, a slow wet flapping. The lot's single lamp swings. Recon (Average 8+) to see a shape cross the orange sky. Dabney and Dex are best placed. Failing the roll means surprise (see Dive in Chapter 2, §2.30).
2. **The sedan (10:40).** The gutwings dive on Voss's car. The roof folds. The dash receiver spits sparks, and the car horn runs.
3. **The motel (10:42 on).** The flight turns on lit windows and the diner. They rake glass, try the doors, and go for anyone in the open. **Bystanders matter:** Priya in the diner's back booth, Earlene at the desk, Del under the Gravy Train. Pick one bystander to put in real danger.
4. **The break (about 10:50).** When half the flight is down or any gutwing is reduced to 8 Hits or fewer, they break off and circle. They come back in 1D minutes if the party stays lit and loud.

**Count the flight from what the party holds.** Gutwing stats are in Chapter 2, §2.30.

| What the party has when the first dive comes | Gutwings |
|---|---|
| Nothing but tools, kitchen knives and the vehicles | 1 |
| Hidden sidearms (contraband pistols, the owner's shotgun) | 2 |
| Part of the spy's kit (one rifle, a few vests) | 3 |
| The whole trunk (two rifles, the Gauss rifle, three vests, Combat armour) | 4 |
| The whole trunk, everyone in armour, and the Gravy Train's floodlights running | 5 |

Pick the row, then add one if the table is firing in the open and being loud about it. Never go above five. The scale is built so the kit is *enough*: a table that takes the whole trunk should win, losing perhaps one NPC or taking two or three wounds, and a table with nothing should survive by hiding, running a vehicle at a landed gutwing (Drive, Average 8+, 3D from the pickup or 6D from the Gravy Train) or by being clever.

**Clever ways out (reward them).**
- **Lights out.** Cut the motel lights. A dark, unheated room is invisible to them (Recon Average 8+ to find a hidden group).
- **Steel doors.** The diner's back door (Armour 4, Structure 15) holds. The room doors (Armour 1, Structure 6) do not.
- **The Gravy Train.** Its roof floodlight bar dazzles a gutwing at close range (it loses its next attack). The cab is Armour 4. The steam-wash hose coats a PC in feed, and gutwings do not attack anything coated in feed (Chapter 7, §7.3). Del objects loudly. The smell does not wash out.
- **The air horn.** Audible for 5 km and raises attention, in both directions.

**Loot, evidence, and the point of the scene.** Whatever the table kills or drives off, the attack points at the Reserve:
- The gutwings came out of the south-east, the direction of the Reserve, and smell of feed and Reserve slurry.
- No brochure, local or Del's stories, has a flying creature in it. Earlene: "I've lived here forty years and never seen one of those." Something has changed at the Reserve.
- A barb or a crop holds a torn strip of khaki field jacket with a TL10 weave and a SolSec laundry mark. It is the pickup's. Investigate or Medic (Average 8+) on a dead gutwing.
- If Voss is alive, he adds his observation of the four walking into the Reserve at 11:30 and the closed gate (Chapter 4, §4.6).
- Over the CB (Del's rig): the Reserve Rescue Service calling all staff to the Tower, "Code Closed."

## 6.5: ACT I ENDS (~11:00 PM)

Act I ends when the table decides to go to the Reserve, or when the referee runs out of patience. By this point they should know:
- The pickup walked into the Reserve and did not come out (two of the three clues in §6.1).
- Something at the Reserve has changed (the gutwings).
- The Reserve has a weapons locker (Dabney, Tomas, Voss).

They may have, or lack, the spy's kit. Neither is a failure. The table with the kit goes to the Reserve ready to fight; the one without it goes to the Reserve knowing exactly what they need and where it is (the Tower armoury, Chapter 2, §2.29, Area 7).

Hand off to Act II (Chapter 7). If Voss is alive, decide what he does: he drives off to a vantage point and reaches his transmitter (a cutter arrives at dawn, Chapter 8, §8.3), he is bound and aboard, or he stays and helps for one scene.

## 6.6: WHO ROLLS WHAT, ACT I

Act I is where the PC sheets shine. Use this table to make sure every player gets a moment, and call a check only when something is at stake. Difficulties use the Core Rulebook scale (Routine 6+, Average 8+, Difficult 10+).

| Moment | Check | Difficulty | Built for it | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traffic that does not stop (Chapter 2, §2.7) | Recon | Average (8+) | Dabney | Notices the pattern is wrong: right cars, wrong hours. |
| Talking Cabo Marsh away (Chapter 4, §4.5) | Persuade or Deception | Average (8+); Difficult (10+) if he has already seen a TL10+ item | Corvin, Corrin, Tomas | He leaves, or he stays and asks to see a room. |
| Intimidating without drawing | Persuade (Tomas's cover) | Average (8+) | Tomas | Marsh or the stranger backs off but remembers the face. |
| Reading who is genuinely rattled in the lobby (Chapter 2, §2.12) | Insight | Average (8+) | Yeva | Names the one person who is performing calm. |
| Getting the Ashworths or the owner talking | Carouse | Routine (6+) | Corrin | Del's CB gossip about shots and a bang, unprompted. |
| Noticing the dash receiver's TL12 glow in the sedan (Chapter 5, §5.1) | Electronics (comms) or Recon | Average (8+) | Dabney, Corrin | The car is not what it claims to be. |
| The washboy's silver Terran coin (Chapter 4, §4.3) | Streetwise, Electronics or Science | Routine (6+) | Dabney, Dex | It is Solomani, TL10-minted, and not Jorjor money. |
| Reaching the idling stranger's car unseen (§6.3) | Stealth | Average (8+) | Dex, Dabney | Reads the plate and the receiver without being seen. |
| Opening the spy's trunk case (§6.3) | Mechanic or Electronics | Average (8+) | Dabney | The kit. |
| Selling Voss on a trade (§6.3) | Persuade or Deception | Difficult (10+) | Corvin, Yeva | He trades the kit for the pickup's location. |
| Seeing the shape against the sky (§6.4) | Recon | Average (8+) | Dabney, Dex | No surprise on the first dive. |
| Firing into the flight (§6.4) | Gun Combat | Average (8+) | Tomas, anyone with a rifle | A gutwing drops in a burst or two. |
| Stabilising Voss after the dive (§6.4) | Medic | Average (8+) | Yeva | He lives and bargains. |
| Reading the dead gutwing for the jacket scrap (§6.4) | Investigate or Medic | Average (8+) | Yeva, Corrin | Proof the pickup hit the Mass. |
| The stranger bolts, or the restaurant search turns into a chase (Chapter 2, §2.15) | Athletics (dexterity) | Average (8+) | Dex | Keeps up, or loses him in the dark. |
| Pulling the cook's story out of him (Chapter 4, §4.3) | Persuade or Streetwise | Average (8+) | Corvin, Tomas | Four hikers, the road to "the Gut," a big tip. |
| Holding a cover under pressure | Deception, opposed by the questioner's Investigate | Opposed | Dex, Corrin | Cover holds, or a fellow guest now doubts it. |

**Rule of thumb:** every PC should make at least two meaningful rolls in Act I, and at least one of them should come from a skill that is not Persuade or Deception.
