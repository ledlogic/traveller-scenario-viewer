# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Appendix E — Graveyard Shift Complications: NPCs

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## E.0 — WHAT THIS COVERS

Chapter 3, §3.2 lists three optional Graveyard-shift complications and leaves them
as referee discretion/prep. This appendix builds the ones that need a body and
stats — the patrol officer and the idling stranger — so they're ready to drop in
without prep. The third option (a hang-up phone call) needs no stat block; it's
covered in §E.3. §E.4 lays out a full-night timetable for all three
complications plus a new phone check-in mechanic for the three PC rooms.

---

## E.1 — LOCAL POLICE: DEPUTY CALLUM MARSH

Drawn by noise, a nosy neighbor's call, or simple routine patrol. Not hunting
SolSec or Imperial activity specifically — has no idea any of tonight's guests
are anything other than ordinary travelers, and should be played that way unless
a PC gives him genuine cause to think otherwise.

**Age:** 36

**UPP:** 776654 *(STR 7 (0), DEX 7 (0), END 6 (0), INT 6 (0), EDU 5 (-1), SOC 4 (-1))*

**Career history:** One term **Army** out of school, then four terms with
Jorjor's regional sheriff's department — long enough to be competent and
unhurried, not long enough to have seen much worth being cynical about. Small
stretch of highway, mostly traffic stops and drunk-and-disorderly calls; tonight
is more interesting than his usual shift, which is itself worth noting if he
seems to be lingering.

**Skills:** Gun Combat 0, Gun Combat (slug pistol) 1, Recon 1, Drive
(wheeled) 1, Streetwise 1, Persuade 0

**Gear:** TL7 service revolver, patrol car (running, marked, easily recognized —
useless for any PC to "borrow" quietly), a portable radio on the department's own
channel. **Referee's call** whether the department's radio net is knocked out by
the same storm silencing civilian comms (Appendix B, §B.0) — either answer
works: if it's down too, Marsh is as isolated as everyone else and more nervous
about it than he lets on; if it's up, he's a rare, real link to the outside
world PCs might try to use or avoid.

**Role at the table:** Wants a calm, boring end to his shift. Responds well to
plausible, unremarkable explanations and poorly to anything that reads as
evasive or rehearsed — a PC using genuine Persuade/Deception skill has an easier
time with him than one relying on tech or intimidation. Should never escalate to
gunplay unless a PC forces the issue; his default is to write up a noise
complaint and leave, not to search rooms or run identities.

**Neural.AI image prompt:** A lean, unhurried sheriff's deputy in his mid-30s,
tan uniform, wide-brimmed hat, standing beside a marked patrol car with one hand
resting on his belt near a holstered revolver, flashlight beam cutting across a
dark gravel motel lot. Calm, faintly bored expression rather than alert
suspicion. Photorealistic, science fiction/Americana crossover, no text, no
logos.

## E.2 — THE IDLING STRANGER

A car parked across the highway or at the lot's edge, engine running, headlights
off. Deliberately built as **two interchangeable versions** — pick one before
the session, or decide live based on how the table's doing, the same way the
mole mechanic works (Chapter 2).

### Version 1 — Mundane

Nothing to do with tonight's plot. A genuinely lost or exhausted traveler who
pulled over to nap, check a map, or wait out the worst of the storm before
continuing — exactly the kind of ordinary, deflating explanation Appendix B,
§B.2 encourages for traffic events in general.

**Age:** 44

**UPP:** 655766 *(STR 6 (0), DEX 5 (-1), END 5 (-1), INT 7 (0), EDU 6 (0), SOC 6 (0))*

**Career history:** No terms of particular note — a traveling sales rep or
similar, homeworld background skills only.

**Skills:** Persuade 1, Streetwise 0, Drive (wheeled) 1

**Gear:** Nothing of consequence — a TL7 sedan, a road map, a thermos.

**Referee use:** should resolve fast and mundanely if investigated — a tired,
slightly embarrassed stranger who didn't realize how suspicious idling outside
a motel at 3am looks. Good for deflating tension right when a table most expects
it to escalate.

### Version 2 — Shutter Project Watcher

Not Imperial at all — genuine surveillance, but local, and tied to the
Strange Park's hidden funding scheme rather than the SolSec/Imperium plot
(Chapter 5, §5.4–5.6). This person is quietly attached to the Shutter
Project, watching the motel because unusual activity nearby — stranded
storm traffic, an unfamiliar police presence, six new guests in one night —
is exactly the kind of attention that could get someone asking questions
about the body-bank accounts or the Project itself. Their concern is entirely
selfish and local: protecting a decades-old secret, not stopping an
extraction. Use this version when a referee wants dread and outside
attention to feel real without pulling Imperial Intelligence into a scenario
that's meant to stay self-contained — and as a way to let the Strange Park's
background lore brush up against tonight's plot without the two threads
actually connecting.

**Age:** 41

**UPP:** 776655 *(STR 7 (0), DEX 7 (0), END 6 (0), INT 6 (0), EDU 5 (-1), SOC 5 (-1))*

**Career history:** No formal career terms — a lifelong Jorjor local, recruited
into the Shutter Project's watch rotation the way most of its people are:
knew someone, needed the quiet extra income the body-bank float can
discreetly provide, and never asked too many questions about where the
money actually came from.

**Skills:** Recon 1, Stealth 1, Electronics 0, Electronics (comms) 1,
Streetwise 1, Persuade 0

**Gear:** A genuinely homemade TL7 directional radio receiver, wired together
from surplus and salvaged parts — the same scrappy, vacuum-tube-era
electromagnetics the whole Shutter Project runs on (Chapter 5, §5.5), not
anything that would read as anomalous if searched. No weapon beyond
whatever's locally unremarkable to carry (Chapter 1, §1.1's High Law
firearms allowance).

**Referee use:** should never approach or make contact voluntarily — the
whole point is passive observation from a safe distance, same caution as
any nervous amateur trying not to be noticed. If confronted directly, the
watcher drives off rather than escalates, and under real pressure would
rather admit to small-time local snooping (fear of a jealous spouse, petty
surveillance for the town's rumor mill) than reveal anything about the
Project — a plausible, mundane lie that happens to be protecting something
much stranger underneath. This version deliberately has **no TL gap** to
notice — unlike the Version B mole's gear (Chapter 1, §1.4), there's nothing
here to mechanically detect, which keeps it a genuine, separate thread rather
than a second copy of the same tell.

**Neural.AI image prompt:** A weathered, unremarkable local in a worn jacket,
sitting low in the driver's seat of a plain sedan parked at the edge of a
dark desert highway, engine running, headlights off, a homemade radio
receiver with exposed wiring resting on the passenger seat. Nervous,
furtive posture rather than professional stillness. Night desert backdrop.
Photorealistic, science fiction scene, no text, no logos.

## E.3 — THE HANG-UP CALL

No NPC or stat block needed — this complication is a phone call to the motel's
front desk that goes dead the instant anyone other than the owner (Appendix A)
answers. Deliberately ambiguous: could be a wrong number, could be the
extraction contact attempting one last check-in and losing nerve or connection,
could be Imperial surveillance confirming who's on-site. **Never resolve this
one concretely.** Its value is entirely in the players not knowing which of the
three it was — resist the urge to have it call back or explain itself later in
the session.

## E.4 — FULL NIGHT TIMELINE

A referee-facing timetable for every scheduled or randomly-triggered event
across all three shifts (Chapter 3), including a new mechanic: **periodic
check-in calls to each SolSec room.**

### The room check-in calls

Each PC room (1, 2, and 3 — the SolSec pairs, Chapter 6) has **one scheduled
check-in call** attempted during the night, placed by a regional cutout as
standard tradecraft — unrelated to whether extraction itself is happening, just
routine contact protocol. This is separate from, and should not be confused
with, the ambiguous hang-up call to the front desk in §E.3.

**Hard rule — no answering devices exist at this TL:** if nobody in the room
picks up before it stops ringing, the call is simply gone. No message, no
recording, no retry later in the scene. Jorjor is TL7; period phone lines have
no voicemail or answering machines of any kind, and the storm (Appendix B,
§B.0) makes the line's reliability genuinely uncertain rather than a sure
thing either way. A missed call is not itself a clue — it's exactly as
explicable as "they were in the shower, arguing next door, or just didn't
reach the phone in time," and should be played that plainly if a PC asks
about it afterward.

**Referee use:** if a room answers, deliver one short, mundane, low-content
line appropriate to that pair's situation (a supply confirmation, a
generic all-clear, nothing that resolves the main mystery) — the value is in
the *rhythm* of the night having a call each room could get right or miss, not
in delivering plot. If a room misses its call, say so factually and move on;
don't let the miss itself become a mystery to chase.

**Using this table:** treat every time as approximate, not a hard clock (Chapter
3's own pacing notes already establish Graveyard as free-form) — shift events
earlier or later by ten or fifteen minutes freely to match how a given table's
scenes are actually landing. What matters is the *order and spacing*, not the
literal clock: each room gets exactly one call, the complications land roughly
evenly spaced through Night and Graveyard rather than clustering, and nothing
repeats or escalates outside of what's written in each event's own section.
