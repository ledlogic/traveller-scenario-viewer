# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Chapter 2 — How The Twist Works

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## 2.0 — THE CORE MECHANIC

Each of the six PCs has two full versions of their character in Appendices A–C:
**Version A (True Solomani Agent)** and **Version B (Imperial Mole)**. Skill totals are
matched between versions — only emphasis, secret objective, and tells differ — so a
sheet alone shouldn't give the game away to a player glancing at a neighbor's.

---

## 2.1 — SETUP STEPS

1. Before the session, **secretly choose one PC** to be the Imperial mole. Randomize
   with a card draw (six cards, one marked) if you want plausible deniability as
   referee.
2. Hand that player their **Version B** sheet privately — folded card, sealed
   envelope, or a private message if playing online. Everyone else gets **Version A**.
3. The mole player keeps playing their cover persona normally. **The deception is
   in-fiction, not a metagame trick.** The mole doesn't know who the other five are
   either — nobody has a full picture at the start, including their own roommate.
4. **Room assignment matters:** the mole is one half of a room pair. Their roommate
   was told to expect a partner matching a certain description and cover, but — per
   Chapter 1, §1.0 — most partners haven't actually met in person before tonight.
   That's what makes the mole's cover survivable at close range: the roommate has no
   prior relationship to compare against, only a briefing to match. Whether the real
   partner was quietly intercepted, reassigned, or simply never existed as described
   is a detail the referee can decide (or leave deliberately unresolved) per table.
5. The mole's secret objective is to identify and signal the location of the other
   five assets to an Imperial pickup team before morning, without blowing their own
   cover — including their own roommate's trust.
6. The other five are trying to work out why extraction didn't come, whether Wei is
   dead, turned, or testing them, and — as suspicion mounts, both within their own
   room and across the motel — who among them might be
   the leak.

---

## 2.2 — REFEREEING THE MOLE

- Never let the TL gear gap (Chapter 1, §1.3) be a free confession. It's a clue a
  sharp player can notice, not an automatic reveal.
- The mole should get real, achievable small wins across the night (a successful
  eavesdrop, a plausible excuse for a slip) so the reveal — whenever it comes — feels
  earned rather than telegraphed.
- If the mole is identified early, don't let the scenario collapse. Pivot to a
  chase/escape/negotiation for the endgame instead of ending the mystery portion
  outright.
- If no one identifies the mole, that's a valid and interesting ending — let the
  extraction (or its absence) play out with the network compromised and nobody the
  wiser. Reveal the mole's identity at the table afterward, in the debrief.

---

## 2.3 — WHY THE EXTRACTION DIDN'T COME

Baldwin Wei is a **very good tracker, detective, and secret agent — not superhuman**
(per his established characterization in *Signal GK*). Pick or seed clues toward one of
these three explanations, depending on how dark you want the table's night to go:

- **Wei burned them.** He decided one or more of these assets were compromised or
  expendable and quietly redirected or cancelled the extraction rather than risk a
  larger network exposure.
- **Wei's compromised.** Imperial counter-intelligence is onto his network, the
  extraction was intercepted, and Wei himself is now racing to clean house before
  Imperial agents roll the motel up — putting him on a collision course with the PCs.
- **It's a test.** Wei planted six agents at one site deliberately, knowing not all of
  them are genuine — one's a plant testing the others' discipline, and "no
  extraction" *is* the test.

Any of these lets Wei function as the looming, competent threat from *Signal GK*
without needing him to physically appear until the climax (or at all).

---

## 2.4 — WEI'S PRESENCE: RESOLVED

**Wei stays offscreen.** He does not physically appear in this scenario. His only
direct presence is the original transmission every PC received before tonight — the
order that sent them here in the first place. He does not check in again, and he
does not need to for the scenario to work.

### The original transmission

Every PC (in both versions — the mole received an equivalent, intercepted or
fabricated copy of the same order, referee's choice) received some form of this,
days before tonight, through their own handler or cutout:

> *"Good. You've completed your mission. Now meet up with your partner, identified
> by the codename provided, at [the motel]. Do not leave lingering traces to this
> location — we intend to use it again. That means no loaner car, no parking fees on
> record, nothing that puts a name or a transaction anywhere near this address.
> Understand: no loose threads."*

This single order is load-bearing for several things already established elsewhere
in this document set — treat it as the *reason* those details exist, not a
retroactive patch:

- It's why **every PC took a one-way ride** rather than arranging their own
  transport (Chapter 1, §1.0).
- It's why **Corrin Ashe arranged for her rented sedan to be quietly returned**
  rather than keeping it (Chapter 6, §6.5) — a direct, individual compliance
  with "no loaner car."
- It's why **nobody's vehicle is sitting in the lot tonight** except the owner's,
  the Ashworths', and Del's broken-down rig (Appendix B, §B.5) — none of which
  are PC-arranged, so none of them violate the order.
- It gives every PC a **shared, concrete piece of received orders** to compare
  notes on if they choose to — and a shared, concrete thing to notice is now
  *not happening* (the promised follow-up that never comes).

**Referee use:** this transmission can be read aloud, handed out as a printed
prop, or simply summarized — whichever suits the table. It should feel like a
routine, competent order from a controller who expects to be obeyed exactly,
not a dramatic monologue. The mundane, procedural tone (matching Wei's own
established characterization in *Signal GK*, §2.3 above) is the point.

### If Wei has to show up, it's already gone wrong

**Wei does not appear in person during this scenario, and a referee should not
introduce him as a character who walks through the door.** The reason is stated
plainly, in-fiction, as a fact every PC should already understand about their own
chain of command: **if Baldwin Wei personally comes to a compromised site, it's
because the situation has escalated to a black operation — clean sweep, no
survivors, no exceptions for agents who might simply be confused or slow to
respond.** Nobody on the SolSec side wants that outcome, including Wei himself;
it's a last resort, not a rescue.

This gives the table a real, understood stake without ever needing to stat Wei
out or bring him to the table: **the longer the PCs take, and the messier they
are, the more plausible it becomes that this is the outcome that's coming for
them instead of extraction.** Let this pressure build entirely through
implication and the transmission's own tone — competent, procedural, faintly
threatening even at its most polite — rather than through any explicit
countdown or announcement.
