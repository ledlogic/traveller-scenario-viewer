## 3.1: HOW TO USE THIS CHAPTER

All six guests, three room pairs, are here in one place. Every guest is a genuine SolSec agent or asset; there is no traitor among them. The Imperial presence on the property is an NPC (Chapter 4, §4.6), not a player role.

Each player gets Page 1 (the surface bio and cover) and Page 2 (the secret objective, the item they carry and the tell). Each guest also brings back one **Imperial Secret**, a single piece of spywork about the Reserve that no other guest holds (see §3.1).

**Career timelines:** each guest has a career timeline, "life to date", built from the Core Rulebook (2022 update) career tables and the Solomani rules in Aliens of Charted Space Vol. 2 (chapter 11): Agent, Army, Citizen, Drifter, Entertainer, Marine and SolSec. All six were rolled with real dice, not chosen; the referee only picked assignments and skill tables. Every mishap ended its term with no skill roll and no Benefit roll, so some guests are thinner than their covers suggest. Skills trace to a table roll, a rank bonus, basic training, the group Skills Package or a Connection; benefits follow the mustering-out rules (one roll per term plus rank rolls, three Cash rolls at most). Insight is not a Core skill and no longer appears on any sheet. The combined timeline below shows all six lives side by side.

**Short table:** at five or six players, play everyone. At four, drop Dex and Corrin; the core four are Corvin, Dabney, Yeva and Tomas. At three, also drop Tomas and play Corvin, Dabney and Yeva. Each guest's table size is in the Table notes column of the Cast overview below. The dropped guests' roles are covered in play: the party must Persuade Gus Teller to run the mole (Chapter 8, §8.4), and Okafor-Brandt, if rescued, or Yeva's Pilot 0 on the autopilot lifts the ship (Chapter 8, §8.8). Tomas's kill-order objective and his Imperial Secret (the Terrace and the closed Areas) fall to Dabney, whose maps show them; Dex's secret (the tunnels) is covered by Gus Teller, and Corrin's (the body bank) by the Osei thread.

**Characteristics:** each guest has a full UPP (STR/DEX/END/INT/EDU/SOC) with standard MGT2e DMs shown inline. Standard DM table: 2 or less −2, 3–5 −1, 6–8 +0, 9–11 +1, 12–14 +2, 15 +3.

### Connections ledger

The Connections Rule: when two Travellers link an event in their histories, both gain one free skill of their choice at level 1. Each Traveller may take at most **two** free skills this way, and each connection must be with a **different** Traveller. A free skill cannot be Jack-of-all-Trades and cannot go above level 1. All six guests use both of their connections, in a ring, so each has two free skills and nobody has a third.

## 3.9: HONEST FRAMING FOR A COMBAT-LEANING PLAYER

*No Contact* is built as a social-deduction/investigation scenario first; that's a real tradeoff worth naming at the table, not hiding. That said, there are genuine levers:

- **Big Tomas Reyes** (Chapter 3, §3.7) is the built-in action valve, Melee/Gun Combat focus, with a literal kill-order as his secret objective. He knows the closed Areas and the Terrace (§3.7), so he is also the natural point man for Act I's gutwing attack. He should feel useful the moment a scene turns physical.
- **Cabo Marsh** (Chapter 4, §4.5) carries a real weapon and real stakes once talking stops working; the idling stranger, the Imperial spy (Chapter 4, §4.6), is deliberately built to avoid a fight and drive off instead, so lean on Marsh if a table wants that kind of tension.
- Chapter 8, §8.1 explicitly allows a **firefight** as one of three valid endings, alongside escape and stand-down.
