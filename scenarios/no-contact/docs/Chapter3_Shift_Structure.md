# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Chapter 3 — Shift Structure (Night / Graveyard / Morning)

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## 3.0 — OVERVIEW

The session runs as three acts, mirroring a motel's own shift structure. Total runtime
target: **4 hours**, convention slot length.

---

## 3.1 — NIGHT (Act One, ~75 minutes)

Guests arrive and settle in. Small talk, cover stories, first hints something is off —
a car that doesn't stop at the motel sign, a payphone call that doesn't connect, a
light that shouldn't be on in an empty room. Players start informally sounding each
other out without revealing themselves. No PC should suspect the mole by name yet;
the goal of this act is atmosphere and cast introduction.

**Establish the trap early, quietly:** every PC took a one-way ride to get here,
assuming extraction would be their ride out — nobody arranged their own return
transport. Layer in two concrete complications rather than vague dread: a **storm is
blocking regional highway traffic**, and it has also knocked out **local commercial
comms** (payphones, the motel's own line). Neither is supernatural or plot-generated —
both are checkable, ordinary facts a PC can confirm — but together they mean a normal
ride out isn't simple even before anyone suspects sabotage, and the missing
extraction is genuinely ambiguous (storm, or something worse?) rather than an
immediate alarm. Full detail, including a random table for passing traffic that
never stops, local flora/fauna texture, and optional unrest/bandit rumors, is in
Appendix B.

**Tech discipline matters from the first scene.** Jorjor runs passive detection at
its own TL7 baseline, escalating to the Imperial Security Service if something
anomalous is flagged — see Appendix B, §B.1 for the house rule on checks required
when a PC risks using above-TL7 gear. This should shape how (and whether) players use
their better equipment all night, not just in a climactic scene.

---

## 3.2 — GRAVEYARD (Act Two, ~90 minutes)

The extraction window comes and goes. Nothing. Panic and suspicion rise. This is where
investigation, confrontation, and the first hard tells (TL gear slip-ups, inconsistent
details, a specialization that shouldn't exist) should surface.

**Pacing is free-form, not a hard clock.** There's no countdown mechanic — the
referee paces this act by feel, using the complications below and §3.4's notes to
speed things up or slow them down as the table needs.

Consider a scripted complication here to force decisions under pressure — options
include (full NPC stat blocks for the first two, ready to drop in, are in
Appendix E):

- Local police drawn by noise or a nosy neighbor's call — Appendix E, §E.1.
- A stranger's car idling too long outside, engine running — Appendix E, §E.2,
  built as two interchangeable versions (mundane or genuine Imperial
  surveillance).
- A phone call to the motel's front desk that hangs up the moment anyone but the
  owner answers — Appendix E, §E.3, deliberately left unresolved.

---

## 3.3 — MORNING (Act Three, ~75 minutes)

Someone has to make a call: trust the group, run alone, force a confrontation, or use
the emergency rendezvous protocol (held by Room 1, Guest 1 — see Chapter 6, §6.1). The
mole's endgame plays out here — either they've quietly signalled Imperial pickup, or
the group catches them first. Resolve with an escape, a firefight, or a tense
stand-down depending on how play went.

---

## 3.4 — PACING NOTES

- Don't let any single room's thread eat more than ~20 minutes uninterrupted — cut
  between rooms to keep all six players engaged, the way the source Room Ledger's
  three-shift grid implies parallel, simultaneous action.
- If the table is moving faster than expected, the Graveyard complication (§3.2) can
  be introduced early to add pressure. If slower, cut it, or reveal it as a false
  alarm to buy time.
- For a referee who wants a full clock-time reference across all three shifts —
  when the complications land, and when each room's scheduled phone check-in
  call comes through — see Appendix E, §E.4.
