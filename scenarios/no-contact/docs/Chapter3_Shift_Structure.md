# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Chapter 3 — The Three Acts (Night / Graveyard / Morning)

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## 3.0 — OVERVIEW

The session runs as three acts, mirroring a motel's own shift structure. Total
runtime target: **4 hours**, convention slot length.

| Act | Shift | Time at table | Question | Ends when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I — Roll Call** | Night | ~75 min | Who's in the group, and who isn't? | The table has a crew for the road, the interlopers are dealt with, and they know where the pickup went. |
| **II — The Big Rig** | Graveyard | ~90 min | What happened to our contact? | The rig is running and through the Reserve's gate (or another way in). |
| **III — Dig Them Out** | Morning | ~75 min | Can we get them (or their codes) off this planet? | The *Soft Landing* lifts, or doesn't. |

**The park is not optional.** The *Soft Landing* sits in a dry wash just outside
the Halvorsen Basin Biological Reserve, and the pickup crew used their spare day
to tour it. Whatever else the table decides, the only way off Jorjor runs
through the Reserve.

**The secret under the premise.** "No contact" is literally true. The
extraction team (Appendix H) came a day early, toured the Halvorsen Basin
Biological Reserve (Chapter 8), and were taken by something in the closed
camping areas 12–14. Baldwin Wei's three possible explanations (Chapter 2,
§2.3) remain the *suspicions* the players will voice all night. The Reserve
is what is actually true.

**The dust storm clock** (Chapter 8, §8.7) runs through all three acts: a distant
wall in Act I, first gusts in Act II, full storm in Act III, dawn at the end.

## 3.1 — ACT I: NIGHT — "ROLL CALL" (~75 minutes)

Guests arrive and settle in. Small talk, cover stories, first hints something is
off — a car that doesn't stop at the motel sign, a payphone call that doesn't
connect, a light that shouldn't be on in an empty room. Players start
informally sounding each other out without revealing themselves.

**Establish the trap early, quietly:** every PC took a one-way ride to get here,
assuming extraction would be their ride out — nobody arranged their own return
transport. Layer in two concrete complications rather than vague dread: a **dust
storm front is blocking regional highway traffic** (Stage 1: an orange wall on
the south-west horizon), and it has also knocked out **local commercial comms**
(payphones, the motel's own line). Neither is supernatural or plot-generated —
both are checkable, ordinary facts a PC can confirm. Full detail, including a
random table for passing traffic that never stops, local flora/fauna texture,
and optional unrest/bandit rumors, is in Appendix B.

**Tech discipline matters from the first scene.** Jorjor runs passive detection at
its own TL7 baseline, escalating to the Imperial Security Service if something
anomalous is flagged — see Appendix B, §B.1 for the house rule on checks required
when a PC risks using above-TL7 gear.

### The question: who's in the group, and who isn't?

Act I's real work is sorting people.

- **In the group:** the six SolSec guests in Rooms 1–3 (Chapter 6). They each
  know their *room partner* by codename; they do not know the others.
- **Not in the group (bystanders):** the motel owner (Appendix A), the
  Ashworths (Appendix C, §C.2), the restaurant staff (Appendix D). Ordinary
  people, and genuine bystanders if the night turns violent.
- **The interlopers.** Anyone who should not be riding along, watching, or
  asking questions: **Deputy Marsh** (Appendix E, §E.1), the **idling
  stranger** (Appendix E, §E.2), a curious Priya, and — the hard one — **the
  Version: Imperium mole** (Chapter 2) among the six.

**Get the interlopers out** means the table makes a decision about each:

| Interloper | Options |
|---|---|
| Deputy Marsh / the stranger | Talk him away (boring, plausible story). Mislead him. Take him out of the picture. The Watcher (if used) can't be fully shaken; he only stops reporting if the party is gone. |
| The Ashworths | Keep them out of it. Del becomes essential in Act II; Priya stays at the motel. |
| The mole | Cut them loose, leave them restrained at the motel, take them along under watch, or *use* them. This is the table's call, and the mole's player decides how loudly to resist. |

**The Roll Call scene.** Around 40–50 minutes in, put the six in a room:
Wei's order (Chapter 2, §2.4) in each hand. They compare notes on "no loose
threads" and the one-way rides, and someone says the sentence the scenario
exists for: *"If they're not coming, why not?"* Do not resolve it. Let
the three theories (Wei burned us / Wei's compromised / it's a test) be
argued out.

### Act I closes with the first clue to the pickup

By the end of Act I the table should have **two of these three** pieces of
information, enough to point at the Reserve:

| Clue | Source | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| The cook | Appendix D | Four off-worlder-looking hikers ate breakfast at 10:30, asked the road to "the Gut," left a large tip. |
| The washboy | Appendix D | Saw the four walk the spur road toward the Reserve at 11:30. One woman gave him a coin: **a silver Solomani "Terran" coin** (TL tell). |
| Del's CB | Appendix C, §C.2 | Heard on the CB: the Reserve "closed up tight" at five o'clock; **shots, then a bang** around four-twenty; areas 12 through 14 are shut. |

If the table is slow, have Del or the cook volunteer it. If fast, add the
Reserve's own signage on the highway.

## 3.2 — ACT II: GRAVEYARD — "THE BIG RIG" (~90 minutes)

The extraction window comes and goes. Nothing. Panic and suspicion rise. The
table now has a bearing: the pickup went into the Reserve. Act II is about
**finding out what happened** and **getting there**.

**Pacing is free-form, not a hard clock.** There is no countdown mechanic — the
referee paces this act by feel, using the complications below and §3.4's notes.

### The scenes

1. **Confirming the contact.** The table pieces together the pickup's day:
   the Gate ledger entry and permit #0614 (Chapter 8, §8.2 Area 1) can be
   learned from Del, a rumour, or by phone-to-the-Reserve if comms
   return. The pickup team is real and is missing.
2. **The paper trail.** Inside the Gate, the table learns the pickup is
   recorded as "departed without notice," and that no one office has
   the whole story. Gate, Property, Clinic, Rescue log, Incident file and
   Maintenance orders each hold a slice, spelled five different ways
   (Chapter 8, §8.9). Captain Pell of the Rescue Service (§8.8) is the
   best ally: his whiteboard is the only honest record, and Policy RS-9 has
   stopped his people from going in.
3. **Del's rig.** Del Ashworth's long-haul tanker (Appendix B, §B.5;
   Appendix C, §C.2) is the only vehicle that can get through a Red-alert
   Gate (Chapter 8, §8.4, Route A). It is broken: a fouled fuel line,
   about **2D hours** to fix. The party can speed it up (Mechanic, Engineer
   (power), or Del's own Mechanic (repair)), or work other routes (the
   owner's pickup is a Route C crossing on foot; Form 14-R is Route B).
4. **Persuading Del.** He is possessive of the rig, tired, and has a load
   of 28 tonnes of offal and a stamped manifest. What changes his mind:
   a fair price, a true story, a threatened wife, or a ranger's badge.
5. **The Gate.** At Red (Chapter 8, §8.1) the Gate checks manifests. A
   stamped manifest gets a feed truck through to Area 8. Tolliver and
   Pruitt are here; this is the Act's best social scene.
6. **The drive.** Dust storm Stage 2: visibility 50 m, a Drive check, and
   the first Drifters.

### Complications

Use these to force decisions under pressure — full NPC stat blocks are in
Appendix E:

- Local police drawn by noise or a nosy neighbor's call — §E.1.
- A stranger's car idling too long outside, engine running — §E.2,
  built as two interchangeable versions (mundane or a Shutter Project
  Watcher).
- A phone call to the motel's front desk that hangs up the moment anyone
  but the owner answers — §E.3, **still deliberately unresolved**, even now.

**Act II ends** when the rig is past the Gate (or the table has found
another way in) and they are at the Feed Line Terminal (Area 8) with the
storm rising. If the table is out of time, skip to the Terminal.

## 3.3 — ACT III: MORNING — "DIG THEM OUT" (~75 minutes)

Dust storm Stage 3. The surface patrols have been pulled in. The only
quiet way to reach the closed Areas 12–14 is **under** the ground.

### The scenes

1. **The Maintenance Yard (Area 9).** Mole driver Gus Teller and the Reserve's
   two moles (Chapter 8, §8.6). Persuade him, steal a mole, or bring your
   own driver (Drive (mole)).
2. **Where are they?** The location depends on Appendix H, §H.7: roll the
   team's fate or choose. Captured crew are sedated in the Orchard
   (Area 13); trapped crew are in a Terrace void (Area 12); lost crew are
   in the meadows or the Sink; held crew are in the tower's Incident Room
   (Area 7).
3. **Digging.** The mole's route is soil under Area 12 and rock beneath the
   Orchard (Chapter 8, §8.6). Drilling the Mass raises the Alert track and
   calls the Waders.
4. **The rescue.** Open an Egg-bloom, free a trapped team member, find a
   lost one, or break a held one out of the Incident Room (Appendix H, §H.7).
5. **The ship.** Reach the *Soft Landing* (Appendix H, §H.2), 11 km north of
   Area 13. Any **two fobs** or Okafor-Brandt's codeword unlock it.
6. **Lift before dawn.** At about 05:45 the storm passes. If a
   Version: Imperium PC has signalled, an Imperial cutter arrives.

**The mole's endgame plays out here** (Chapter 2) — either they have quietly
signalled Imperial pickup, or the group catches them first. Resolve with an
escape, a firefight, a betrayal at the ship's hatch, or a tense stand-down,
depending on how play went.

**What success looks like.** Best case: all four of the pickup are out and
aboard. Middle case: some are out; the others are in the Mass. Floor: the
**fobs and the codeword** get at least two PCs off Jorjor. The Mass keeps
the rest (Appendix H, §H.9).

## 3.4 — PACING NOTES

- Don't let any single room's thread eat more than ~20 minutes uninterrupted —
  cut between rooms (in Act I) and between the cab and the trailer (in Act II)
  to keep all six players engaged.
- If the table is moving faster than expected, the Graveyard complications
  (§3.2) can be introduced early. If slower, cut them, or reveal them as a
  false alarm to buy time. In Act III, skip the Maintenance Yard haggling and
  go to the drill.
- Every action in the Reserve raises the **Alert track** (Chapter 8, §8.1).
  Use it to keep pressure on without a hard clock.
- For a clock-time reference across all three acts — when the complications
  land, when the dust storm advances, and when each room's scheduled phone
  check-in call comes through — see Appendix E, §E.4.
