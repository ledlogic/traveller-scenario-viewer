## 8.0: WHAT THIS CHAPTER DOES

Chapter 7 says how the Gut came to be. This chapter says what it is like to walk into it tonight: the fourteen numbered areas of the Halvorsen Basin Biological Reserve, the alert track that reacts to what the players do, the creatures and plants that live there, the staff who are covering it up, and the three ways in. Act II and Act III of the scenario (Chapter 3) are played here.

**There is no version of tonight where the table skips the park.** The pickup's ship sits just outside it, and the pickup is inside it, trapped, with a rescue service that has been told to stand down and paperwork that disagrees with itself (§8.8 and §8.9). Going in is the scenario.

**All area descriptions are original to this document set.** They are not taken from any published adventure, supplement, or map.

**Scale.** The Reserve is a bowl roughly 14 km across. Distances in the Areas table are from the Gate (Area 1), by service track.

## 8.1: THE ALERT TRACK

The Reserve tracks one number: **Alert Points (AP)**, 0–12. Tonight it **starts at 6 (Red)**.

| AP | State | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Green / Amber** | Tours run. Areas 12–14 closed to the public (Amber). Nobody searches vehicles. |
| 4–7 | **Red** *(tonight's start)* | **Areas 12–14 closed, camping reservations frozen.** Gate checks manifests. Rangers patrol in pairs. Photography banned. |
| 8–12 | **Black** | Reserve sealed. Rangers armed and ordered to "contain." Feed Terminal stops pumping. Quill stops pretending. |

**Raising AP.** The referee adds points when the table does any of these in or near the Reserve:

| Action | AP |
|---|---|
| Gunfire (any) | +1 |
| Explosives (any) | +2 |
| TL10+ emission detected (comms, scanner, drive) | +1 |
| Psionics used near the basin (Telepathy, any talent) | +1 |
| Killing a ranger or staff member | +2 |
| Mole drilling through the Mass, per 10 minutes | +1 |
| Cutting open an Egg-bloom, each | +1 |
| Being caught lying to a stamped form | +1 |

**Lowering AP.** −1 for 30 minutes of true quiet; −1 for a successful Form 14-R (§8.4); −1 when Quill or Pruitt is persuaded or bribed (once each). **AP cannot fall below 4 while the dust storm is above Stage 1** (§8.7).

The AP is the table's *clock*. Reaching Black does not end the scenario; it makes every scene harder and removes the quiet routes.

### Areas 12, 13, 14: the closed zone

**Area 12: The Terrace.** A broken shelf, three to ten metres above the Sink. The ground rings hollow underfoot. Dozens of **sinkholes** (Athletics (dexterity) or Recon check to walk safely; failure drops you 1D metres into a void with a warm, damp floor). Two surviving **tunnels** from the Y1113 collapse run toward the Sink; the best natural place to find *trapped* pickup members, alive but unable to climb out. Fluid seeps through the walls.

**Area 13: The Orchard.** The Egg-bloom grove: 30-odd plants 6–10 metres tall, spaced a few metres apart, closed like enormous eggs at midday and slowly opening at dusk. This is where the pickup pitched camp, and where the Mass took them. Captured crew members hang **inside open or half-open Egg-blooms** in a sedated, upright sleep. Their tents are still staked out, neatly, a few metres from the nearest bloom.

**Area 14: The Sink.** A breathing mat of tissue about a kilometre wide. Heat shimmer, the smell of a butcher's yard in summer, and a soft rhythmic *pulse* every few seconds that the body feels through the boots. A person can *lose* a pickup member here (wandering, hallucinating from the spores) but cannot easily *find* one. The mole's best approach is from below.

## 8.3: WHAT LIVES HERE, DRIFTERS, WADERS, EGG-BLOOMS

Everything alive on the surface is, one way or another, **a part of the Mass**. Three kinds matter at the table, and each has a size and a job. (Minis: use the sizes below. Drifters on flight stems or clear rods; Waders on tall legs; Egg-blooms as a large egg-shaped terrain piece with opening "petal" sections if you have them.)

### Where they are and what they're doing

| Area | Drifters (floating blobs) | Waders (long-legged blobs) | Egg-blooms (6–10 m egg plants) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | A few overhead at dusk. Tourists photograph them. | None | None |
| 3–4 | Hover over the steam, riding the thermals | **Feeding**: wading the slurry, picking at floating scraps | None |
| 5–6 | Rare | Rare (one or two at the lagoon edge) | None |
| 7 | **Watching**: a ring of Drifters circles the tower at dusk (rangers read them as an alarm) | None | None |
| 8 | A few; attracted by feed smell | **Waiting**: stand motionless at the docks as feed trucks unload | None |
| 9 | None | None | None |
| 10 | Some; follow the seep | Some; travel in pairs | None |
| 11 | **Grazing**: dense herds, hundreds, drifting with the wind | Scattered | A few young (3–5 m) |
| 12 | **Gathered**: low and tight above the sinkholes | **Patrolling** the rim: they have been *called in* | None |
| 13 | **Pollinating**: moving bloom to bloom | **Carrying**: ferrying wrapped bundles toward the blooms | **The Orchard**: 30+ at full size |
| 14 | Clustered thick; "sleeping" low on the mat | Few; stand in the mat like reeds | A handful at the edge |

**Drifters (floating blobs).** Translucent, gas-filled bags 1–3 metres across, with trailing stinging filaments. They float on the basin's warm thermals and the evening wind and drift upwind in the day to seed spores. They are the Mass's **eyes**: a cluster of Drifters converging on a spot is the Reserve's alarm. They descend and cling to Waders and Egg-blooms in strong wind.

- **Hits 12 · Speed 3 m (drifts with the wind) · Armour 0**
- **Sting (filaments):** 1D damage; END 8+ or lose 1D DEX for 1D minutes (paralytic). Weapons that burst the bag release a **spore cloud** (1D rounds of reduced visibility; breathing without a filter means END check or hallucinations).
- **Behaviour:** ignore anyone coated in feed slurry (§8.4). Converge on gunfire, explosives, TL10+ emissions, or psionics.

**Waders (long-legged blobs).** Stilt-legged blobs 3–5 metres tall, with a soft, bag-like body slung between four long legs and a cluster of grasping tendrils below. They graze slurry at the lagoon and **carry** things, wrapped bundles, including live people, to the Orchard. The Mass's hands.

- **Hits 40 · Speed 6 m · Armour 2**
- **Tendrils (grapple):** Melee 2, STR 12. A grappled victim is wrapped (STR check to break) and carried 6 m a round.
- **Stab (leg):** 3D damage, only against anything that has hurt them.
- **Behaviour:** slow and clumsy on boardwalk and uneven ground, **fast and sure across mud**. Do **not** attack anything smelling of feed slurry; they treat it as "part of the Mass." They are blind in a dust storm and hunt by smell and ground vibration (mole drilling attracts them).

**Egg-blooms (egg-shaped flower plants, 6–10 m).** Tall, egg-shaped buds of overlapping petals on a thick, wet stalk. At midday they stay **closed**; at dusk the petals unfurl slowly like a flower opening in reverse, revealing a **cradle**; a deep, warm, nectar-filled pit lined with soft filaments. They are the Mass's **stomach and nursery**: they hold captured creatures in a sedated, upright sleep and sample them for days. Prey is not dissolved quickly; it is *kept*.

- **Hits 60 (6 m) to 120 (10 m) · Speed 0 · Armour 3 (petals), 0 (calyx at base)**
- **Embrace:** a petal sweep grapples anyone within 2 m (STR check to break).
- **Cradle:** anyone placed in or entering the cradle: END 8+ each round or fall into a sedated sleep (**helpless**, but alive and unhurt).
- **Pollen burst:** when struck hard, releases a cloud (as Drifter spore cloud) and a **shriek** audible for a kilometre (+1 AP).
- **Behaviour:** open to **warmth and light** at dusk, close fast in wind, dust, cold, or loud noise. In the dust storm (§8.7) every bloom **seals tight**, which makes cutting a victim out harder (Armour 5) but also makes the Orchard the quietest place in the Reserve.
- **Getting someone out:** open it with a bright, warm lamp (Routine check, 1D minutes, silent), or cut the calyx (Hits 25, **no AP**, but the bloom empties). Vibro-blades cut easily; slugs are slow.

### Psionics

A character using **Telepathy** near the basin (even Life Detection) finds the Mass **looking back**: the whole basin registers as one vast, slow mind. The Mass notices telepaths: +1 AP, and Drifters turn toward them. If the pickup's aggravation was psionic (Appendix G, §G.6), Dr. Hale did exactly this at 16:20 today.

### Neural.AI image prompts (creatures)

- **Drifter:** A translucent, gas-filled bag-like creature two metres across floating above steaming mud at dusk, long trailing stinging filaments, soft internal glow. Photorealistic, science fiction nature photography, no text, no logos.

## 8.4: THREE WAYS IN

The Reserve is 11 km from the motel by road, on a spur most maps leave off. At Red, the Gate (Area 1) checks everything. Three routes in: plan for different Acts, and expect the table to invent a fourth.

**Route A: The Big Rig.** Del Ashworth's rig, *the Gravy Train* (Appendix B, §B.5; Appendix C, §C.2), is a **contract Feed Line hauler** carrying **28 tonnes of slaughterhouse offal** on a **stamped manifest (Consignment 9-4471)**. Even at Red, a stamped manifest gets a feed truck through the Gate to Area 8: "you can't question a properly stamped form." Once at the Terminal, the rig can follow the service track to Area 9. **Cost:** Del needs the rig fixed (Act II), and he needs to be persuaded, or hired, bribed, or forced. He will not abandon his truck. A character who rides in the cab or sleeper is clean; one on the catwalk or tank hatch is **coated in feed**, which Drifters and Waders ignore (§8.3).

**Route B: The Form.** **Form 14-R (Request to Retrieve Property from a Closed Area)** is the legitimate way to ask for a lost bag. A PC with **Admin or Bureaucracy** can file one at the Gate: Average (8+) check, DM +2 if they have the pickup's permit number (Area 13 walk-in #0614) or a Jorjor Civil Service seal. Success: a ranger escort to Area 7, with a reduction of AP by 1. Failure: +1 AP and a suspicious Compliance Officer Pruitt. Pruitt can be an ally (§8.5).

**Route C: On Foot.** Over the fence-line, across the dry wash, and into the closed Areas from the north (the same line the pickup's ship sits behind). Eleven kilometres with the dust front arriving. Effects: no Gate-check, but Drifters and Waders, rangers on patrol, and the weather. The ship lies along this route (Appendix G, §G.2).

## 8.7: THE DUST STORM CLOCK

The storm established in Appendix B is a **haboob**, a dust front rolling in from the south-west. It reaches the Reserve in stages.

| Stage | When | At the motel | At the Reserve |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1: Distant wall** | Act I (21:00–22:15) | Orange horizon, static on the radio | Tours cancelled. Drifters low. |
| **2: First gusts** | Act II (≈22:15–00:15) | Wind, grit, lights flicker | Visibility 50 m. Egg-blooms close. Gate shut except for manifests. |
| **3: Full storm** | Act III (≈00:15–05:00) | Highway closed. Visibility near zero. | Visibility 5 m. Waders blind. Egg-blooms sealed. **Surface patrols withdrawn.** Aircraft grounded. |
| **4: Passing** | ≈05:00–05:45 | Clearing sky | Dawn. Everything is visible again. |

**The storm helps and hurts.** It hides the players from rangers and hides the rig's headlights; it also grinds filters, blinds sensors, chokes engines, and is a hard cap on how long anyone can be outside.

**Environmental hazards (Stage 3):**
- Atmosphere 4 (thin, tainted) *plus dust*: a filter is mandatory, and wears out in 4 hours (**Survival** to stretch it).
- Wind: Athletics (strength) to cross open ground carrying a load.
- Vehicles: Drive check each 30 minutes or stall.

## 8.8: THE RESERVE RESCUE SERVICE ("THE ORANGE VESTS")

Like any big water park, the Reserve keeps trained rescuers on call, because visitors get into trouble every day. The **Reserve Rescue Service (RRS)** has eight staff and a handful of volunteers in orange vests, a Rescue Station at Area 5, a rescue sled, a first-aid clinic annex at the Gate, and a whistle for every ranger. They respond to a few hundred calls a year, from twisted ankles to the 10% (§7.6) who simply never come back.

**Why they have not rescued the pickup.** At 16:34 the RRS dispatcher logged a call from the Terrace (shots, a bang, a collapse, four visitors). The response team was rolling when Quill raised the alert to Red at 17:10. **Policy RS-9** says that when the Reserve is at Red, *no recovery in a closed area proceeds without the Supervisor's written authorisation.* Quill has not signed one. The team stood down at 17:12 and has been sitting on its hands ever since.

### How the table works it out (the Trace)

Treat the investigation as a scene with **three kinds of check**. Each success narrows the answer; none is automatic.

| Check | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Admin (records) or Bureaucracy,** matching names across offices | Finds the entries above. Each success gives one record. |
| **Bureaucracy,** knowing which paper wins | In the Bureau's eyes the Incident file outranks the Gate ledger, which outranks the Rescue log. The true story is mostly in the *lowest-ranked* paper. A success tells the table to trust Pell's whiteboard over Quill's file. |
| **Persuade or Streetwise** with Pell, Teller or the clinic volunteer | Gets the human memory behind the paper: the shouting, the scream, the mole slip. |

| Successes | What the table learns |
|---|---|
| 0 | They are somewhere in the Reserve. The Gate says they left, which is a lie. |
| 1 | The **kind** of trouble: held by staff, trapped, lost, or taken. |
| 2 | **Which Area** each is probably in (7, 12, 11 or 14, 13). |
| 3 | Area plus **how long they have** (G.7, "Condition by state") and who else is looking. |

**Contradictions are the clue.** The Gate says four left; the Property room still holds four packs; the clinic has a "Hall"; Pell heard shouting at 17:40. Four who "left" do not leave their packs, and do not shout from a tunnel.

**Paper also gets people out.** A **Form 31-T (Transfer of Custody)** moves a person from the Incident Room to a named carer. A **Form 14-R** (§8.4) fetches property. A **Policy RS-9 waiver** signed by Quill releases Pell's team. All three are weapons in a bureaucracy that does not question a stamped form.

## 8.10: GETTING OFF PLANET

The *Soft Landing* (Appendix G) sits in a dry wash **11 km north of Area 13**, under sensor-shadow. Reaching it, unlocking it, and lifting off is the final scene.

- **To unlock the ship:** any **two** of the four **key fobs** (Appendix G, §G.3). If the crew are lost, the ship's cache can be opened with the captain's **spoken codeword**, found on a rescued crew member or in her tent.
- **To lift:** the ship can fly itself with a PC at the controls. A pilot is better (Pilot (spacecraft)); an autopilot hop out of the storm is possible.
- **The Imperium.** If a **Version: Imperium** PC is aboard, the mole's endgame is to signal an Imperial cutter (Chapter 2). An ISS vessel arrives at **dawn (≈05:45)**. The ship must be airborne before then.
