# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Appendix D — The Restaurant (Semi-Closed)

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## D.0 — TONE REFERENCE

This space is built to evoke the atmosphere of *Dying Room Only* (1973) — a
Richard Matheson TV movie in which a couple stops at a remote desert diner, the
husband vanishes from an enclosed space (the men's room) without a trace, and the
diner's two staff/regulars respond to the wife's search with cold, evasive
non-help that reads as either hostility or complicity, never clearly which. No
special effects, no monster — just an insular, unhelpful small space that makes
outsiders feel unwelcome and increasingly unsafe. This appendix borrows that
register, not the plot; nothing here should resolve as supernatural.

---

## D.1 — THE SPACE

Attached to the motel but not the same building as the lobby (Chapter 4, §4.1) —
a separate, low structure a short walk across the gravel lot, connected by a
covered walkway. **Officially semi-closed:** posted hours say lunch and early
dinner only, but the lights are sometimes on late, and whether it's "open" on any
given night depends entirely on whether the staff feel like it.

Worn vinyl booths, a long counter, a pass-through kitchen window, a jukebox older
and less functional than the one in the lobby (Appendix C, §C.1), and — the one
detail worth making concrete for the table — a **single, small restroom hallway**
at the back with one entrance and no other way out, just large enough for one
person to be out of sight of the dining room for a stretch of time without it
being unusual.

---

## D.2 — STAFF

Three fixtures, always present when the place is "open," never seen arriving or
leaving. **No waitress and no cashier** — there's no one taking orders at the
table or working a register; the cook handles the counter and the till himself,
and the arrangement (order at the counter, pay at the counter, seat yourself) is
part of what makes the place feel understaffed and slightly off.

- **The cook** — also the manager, for whatever that's worth in a place this
  small; runs the counter, the kitchen, and the till himself. Age 57. UPP:
  786655 *(STR 7 (0), DEX 8 (0), END 6 (0), INT 6 (0), EDU 5 (-1), SOC 5
  (-1))*. No career terms of note — worked kitchens and short-order lines
  since he was young, on Jorjor and off it, before landing here longer than
  he probably meant to stay. Quiet, economical with words, answers questions
  with fewer words than the question contained. Never rude exactly, never
  actually helpful either. Skills: Steward 1, Mechanic 0, Mechanic
  (repair) 1, Streetwise 0.
- **The regular** — Age 49. UPP: 998443 *(STR 9 (+1), DEX 9 (+1), END 8 (0), INT 4 (-1), EDU 4 (-1), SOC 3 (-1))*. No career terms — background labor skills only, the kind a big,
  quiet local picks up doing whatever work is around. A heavyset, permanently-
  seated customer at the counter who never appears to order, eat, or pay, and
  who seems to know exactly when to insert himself into a conversation to make
  it less productive. Skills: Melee 0, Melee (unarmed) 1, Athletics 0,
  Athletics (strength) 1.
- **The washboy** — a teenage boy, kept mostly out of sight in the back
  washing dishes, barely spoken to and barely speaking. Age 15. UPP: 655544
  *(STR 6 (0), DEX 5 (-1), END 5 (-1), INT 5 (-1), EDU 4 (-1), SOC 4 (-1))*.
  No career terms — local kid, first job. No notable skills beyond
  Mechanic 0 *(dishwashing/kitchen upkeep, nothing above 0)*. Present but
  essentially voiceless at the table — a pair of eyes in the back room more
  than a character, useful if a referee wants a witness who saw something
  but can barely be gotten to say what.

None of the three needs more than this to run at the table — their entire
function is atmospheric friction, and the stats above exist for the rare
moment a check is actually called for, not to seed a backstory. **Referee
note:** resist the temptation to make them secretly SolSec, Imperial, or
anything else tied to the main plot.
Their unsettling quality works specifically *because* it never resolves into
something explicable — same as the source material's own refusal to give its
diner staff supernatural or even fully coherent motives.

## D.3 — OPTIONAL USE: THE VANISHING BEAT

If a referee wants a hard, memorable Graveyard-shift complication (Chapter 3,
§3.2) beyond what's already in Appendix B, this space supports one directly:
**someone goes to the restroom and doesn't come back.**

- Best used on a low-stakes NPC rather than a PC — **Del or Priya Ashworth**
  (Appendix C, §C.2) is the natural choice, since their disappearance
  genuinely worries the table without derailing anyone's own character arc.
- Play the staff exactly as unhelpful as the tone reference: not obstructive
  enough to look like a smoking gun, just unhelpful enough that a search feels
  harder than it should. "Ain't seen anyone come through here tonight" delivered
  flatly, even if it isn't quite true.
- **Resolve this mundanely.** The point isn't a hidden trapdoor or a kidnapping
  ring — it's the *feeling* of one for as long as the search takes. Good mundane
  resolutions: the missing person stepped out the back to get sick from the
  heat and lost track of time; they fell asleep in a booth in a back room the
  staff didn't think to mention; they walked back to Room 4 without saying
  anything, assuming everyone would notice. Let the dread do the work; don't
  cash it in for anything stranger than an ordinary, slightly embarrassing
  explanation.
- If a table wants higher stakes than that, the ceiling should still stay
  mundane and human — a genuine but ordinary crime of opportunity (theft,
  a scuffle) rather than anything connected to SolSec, Wei, or Osei. Keeping
  this space's mystery separate from the main plot's is what makes it work as
  pure atmosphere rather than a second, competing mystery.

## D.4 — NEURAL.AI IMAGE PROMPTS

**Exterior/interior establishing shot:** A run-down 1970s roadside diner attached
to a motel by a covered walkway, worn neon "OPEN" sign with several letters dark,
seen at dusk across a gravel lot. Warm interior light spilling from dust-streaked
windows, otherwise the desert around it fading into darkness. Photorealistic,
science fiction/Americana crossover scene, no text, no logos.

**The cook:** A lean, weathered man in a stained apron standing at a pass-through
kitchen window, spatula in hand, regarding the viewer with a flat, unreadable
expression — not hostile, not friendly, just unreadable. Dim fluorescent kitchen
light behind him. Photorealistic, science fiction character portrait, no text, no
logos.

**The regular:** A heavyset man in worn overalls seated at a diner counter,
posture too still and too permanent, an untouched cup of coffee in front of him,
watching the entrance without quite looking at it. Warm, grimy diner lighting.
Photorealistic, science fiction character portrait, no text, no logos.

**The washboy:** A thin teenage boy in a stained apron, sleeves rolled up,
elbow-deep in a sink of dishwater in a cramped back kitchen, glancing toward
the dining room through a half-open doorway rather than looking at the viewer
directly. Dim, greasy fluorescent light. Photorealistic, science fiction
character portrait, no text, no logos.
