# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Chapter 5 — The Setting

*Referee Reference Document*

## 5.0 — REAL-WORLD BASIS

The motel and its surrounding region are built on the footprint of **Amboy,
California** — a near-ghost town on the historic alignment of Route 66 through the
Mojave Desert, roughly halfway between Barstow and Needles. It's a strong real-world
match for Jorjor's tone: isolated, sun-blasted, once busier than it is now, kept alive
almost entirely by one roadside business.

Key real details worth building the fake region around:

- **One business carries the whole town.** Amboy's real anchor is Roy's Motel and
  Café — gas station, motel, and cafe under one 24-hour operation, with a
  50-foot neon sign visible for miles across flat desert. At its peak in the
  postwar years it employed a meaningful fraction of the town's own population.
  Jorjor's motel should carry the same weight: it isn't *a* business in town, it's
  functionally the reason the settlement exists at all.
- **A bypass gutted it.** Amboy thrived on through-traffic until Interstate 40
  opened in 1972 on a route that didn't connect to Amboy's stretch of road at
  all — business reportedly went to zero overnight. This maps directly onto
  Jorjor's "hellworld, never became the industrial power it hoped to be"
  characterization (Chapter 1, §1.1): pick or seed a regional bypass — a newer
  highway alignment, a rerouted rail spur, a shifted starport shuttle corridor —
  that quietly stranded this stretch of road years ago, explaining why it's this
  quiet and this easy to disappear into.
- **Population that shrank to almost nothing.** Amboy's population ran from
  roughly 700 at its peak down to single digits, and by some recent counts, zero
  — privately owned, kept alive as much by preservation effort as by real
  commerce. Jorjor's motel town should read the same way: a real place with real
  history, not a purpose-built stage set, currently sustained by just enough
  passing traffic and stubbornness to stay open.
- **Distances to real services.** Amboy sits roughly 97km from Twentynine Palms
  and comparable distances from Barstow and Needles in the other directions —
  the actual real-world distances behind the "60km town garage, 90km industrial
  site" figures already used in Appendix B, §B.5's vehicle-repair table. That
  table's distance and risk framing was built to match this kind of real desert
  isolation, not invented arbitrarily.
- **A striking natural landmark just outside town.** Amboy sits beside Amboy
  Crater, a volcanic cinder cone with black basalt lava flows, visible from the
  highway. This is the real-world seed for the **Fence-Line Hum** (Appendix B,
  §B.3) — reskin the crater as the fenced "geological survey" site if a referee
  wants a concrete visual anchor for that rumor rather than leaving it purely
  verbal.

## 5.1 — BUILDING THE FAKE REGION

For a referee building out a full fictional region around this footprint (custom
place names, a hex map, surrounding settlements), the following structure keeps
continuity with everything already established in Chapters 1–4 and Appendix B:

| Real-world element | Jorjor equivalent | Already established where |
|---|---|---|
| Route 66 (National Trails Highway) | The two-lane highway outside the motel | Chapter 4, §4.1; Appendix B, §B.0 |
| Amboy itself | The motel's home settlement — Motel Yermo is named (Chapter 1, §1.0); the surrounding town/settlement itself remains open to naming | Chapter 1, §1.0–1.1 |
| Roy's Motel and Café's 24-hour, gas+food+lodging combo | Motel Yermo, its office, and lobby (vending, jukebox, sometimes food) | Chapter 4, §4.1; Appendix C |
| I-40 bypass (1972) | A regional highway/rail/shuttle realignment that stranded this stretch | New — referee's call on specifics |
| Barstow / Needles / Twentynine Palms | The 15km filling station, 60km town garage, 90km industrial site | Appendix B, §B.5 |
| Amboy Crater | The Fence-Line Hum site | Appendix B, §B.3 |
| Amboy's Googie/mid-century "retro-future" architecture | The motel's own dated signage and design — a visual choice for Neural.AI prompts and any custom art | Chapter 6; Appendices A, D |

**Naming:** none of the fake region's place names need to echo the real ones —
the goal is matching *geography and isolation logic*, not a reskin with different
labels. A referee free-building this out should feel free to invent local names
entirely; the table above exists to keep the underlying distances and
relationships consistent with what's already written elsewhere in this document
set.

## 5.2 — WHY THIS MATTERS AT THE TABLE

Route 66 ghost towns carry a specific, real emotional register — genuine Americana,
faded rather than sinister, populated by people who stayed out of stubbornness or
had nowhere better to go — that suits this scenario's tone better than a generic
"spooky desert motel" would. Leaning on Amboy's real texture (one business holding a
whole town together, a highway that used to matter more than it does now, distances
that make "just drive to the next town" a real ordeal rather than an inconvenience)
gives every other chapter's atmospheric and logistical details a grounded reason to
be true, rather than existing only because the plot needs them to.

## 5.3 — THE STRANGE PARK (OPTIONAL, BACKGROUND ONLY)

Roughly an hour's drive from the motel, off a spur road most maps don't bother
labeling, sits a fenced, half-abandoned tourist site the locals just call
**the Gut** — officially the **Halvorsen Basin Biological Reserve**, discovered
by a rancher-turned-prospector decades before tonight and briefly a real
regional attraction before a disaster in **Y1113** quietly ended public
access. What's actually down there — a colossal, still-living subsurface
organism of uncertain extent, first mapped in fragments and never fully
understood — is entirely original to this document set, not drawn from any
existing published setting, and exists purely as background texture for a
referee's wider Jorjor campaign. **It has no mechanical connection to No
Contact's SolSec/Imperium plot** unless a referee deliberately builds one.

### Why the biowaste trucks

The Reserve's continued (unpublicized) survival depends on a standing
contract, administered — inevitably — through Jorjor's Civil Service
Bureaucracy (Chapter 1, §1.2): regional slaughterhouses and agricultural
operations pay a modest fee to have their organic waste hauled to the edge of
the flats and dumped, under a permit whose paperwork describes it as routine
"organic waste processing." In practice, it's feed — a decades-old, half-
forgotten arrangement that keeps the organism sedated and quiet rather than
hungry and active. Almost nobody administering the contract today knows *why*
it exists; it survives the same way Osei's compromised clearances do (Chapter
1, §1.2) — because a bureaucracy this committed to procedure treats a
correctly-filed permit as self-justifying, and questioning a decades-old
arrangement is itself the irregular act.

**Referee use:** the truck route to the flats is a good source of incidental
color for the highway traffic table (Appendix B, §B.2) — a heavy, slow-moving
truck at an odd hour, smelling faintly wrong, that nobody local thinks twice
about. Played straight, it's just one more piece of the region's mundane,
slightly-off texture; a referee running a longer Jorjor campaign can dig much
further into it whenever they're ready.

### The ancient satellite and the shutter

High overhead, in a stable orbit nobody local claims to have put it there, sits
an **Ancients-era orbital platform** — precursor technology, vastly older than
current Imperial or Solomani spaceflight, generally assumed derelict. Local
legend (mostly dismissed as superstition, the same register as the Fence-Line
Hum, Appendix B, §B.3) holds that the platform contains a massive optical
system — effectively a solar concentrator — behind a **controllable aperture,
"the shutter."** In its current, presumably-jammed-open-or-closed state it does
nothing. But if someone understood how to command it, opening the shutter at
the right orbital alignment could focus enough sunlight onto a target region to
produce a genuine, sustained heatwave — not a laser, not a single blast, but
enough concentrated solar energy over a wide area to scorch, dry out, or
ignite whatever's underneath.

**This is optional deep lore, not a plot device already wired into No
Contact.** It's included here as a seed for a referee who wants a bigger,
slower-burning campaign thread across multiple sessions: Who built it? Does
anyone currently living know how to control it? Could Osei's bureaucracy, the
Ancients themselves, SolSec, or the Imperium already be quietly aware of it?
None of those questions need answers for tonight's motel scenario to work —
they're here so the referee has somewhere to go next.

## 5.4 — WHERE THE MONEY GOES: THE BODY BANK

Same optional-background status as §5.3 — none of this is wired into
tonight's motel scenario, but it answers the natural follow-up question of how
a fenced, half-forgotten reserve and a derelict Ancients platform both stay
funded on a poor, unremarkable hellworld nobody's watching closely.

**Imperium-wide UBI, bureaucratic style:** somewhere in the Third Imperium's
history, a baseline guaranteed-income program was established — administered,
Jorjor being Jorjor, exactly the way a Civil Service Bureaucracy would run it:
not as a single cash deposit, but as several **locked-purpose accounts** per
citizen — Food, Housing, Transit, and a handful of others — each replenished
on its own schedule, non-transferable between categories. Less a modern
universal basic income than a permanent, comprehensive descendant of a
wartime ration-and-stamp system: everyone gets exactly enough for exactly
what the account is named for, administered by exactly the kind of
procedure-worshipping apparatus Jorjor's government already runs on (Chapter
1, §1.2).

**The body bank:** the dead bound for the Reserve (§5.3) as biomass pass
through the town's own processing office on their way there. Properly, a
death registration should trigger account closure the same day. In practice,
closure requires a *separate* form, filed by a *different* department than
the one that handles death registration — a classic silo failure, and one
that happens with quiet, grinding regularity given how routine this specific
transfer has become to the clerks who process it. An account nobody closes
keeps receiving its scheduled Imperium deposits indefinitely.

The local financial institution holding these accounts — call it something
like the **Jorjor Provincial Trust** — quietly uses the accumulated float
for ordinary banking purposes: loans, investments, covering shortfalls
elsewhere. Nothing about this trips a normal audit, because each individual
account still nominally balances; the fraud isn't in any one account, it's in
the aggregate nobody's ever had reason to add up. It's a genuinely hidden
channel of Imperium money, flowing into this specific town and quietly
repurposed there, invisible by design rather than by conspiracy — nobody
planned this, the bureaucracy just never built a mechanism that could notice
it.

## 5.5 — WHAT THE MONEY FUNDS: THE SHUTTER PROJECT

**Yes — this is exactly where a meaningful share of that float goes.** A
small, informal, decades-old effort — locals who know about it at all just
call it **the Shutter Project** — quietly funded through the body-bank float,
attempting to study, reverse-engineer, or establish some working control over
the ancient orbital platform's shutter mechanism (§5.3). No official budget
line exists for it and never will; a project like this would never survive
contact with a real Imperium funding review, which is exactly why it has to
live inside a hidden channel instead of an approved one.

Consistent with Jorjor's TL7 baseline (Chapter 1, §1.3–1.4) even for its most
ambitious secret project, picture the aesthetic as genuinely **analog,
vacuum-tube-era radio and optical instrumentation** — home-built antenna
arrays, hand-plotted orbital tracking, racks of dials rather than displays —
scrappy 1950s-style electromagnetics research rather than anything that would
read as anomalously advanced if stumbled upon. The people running it aren't a
unified conspiracy with one motive: some genuinely believe they're protecting
the world by quietly monitoring and probing it themselves rather than
reporting it and risking Imperium seizure or exploitation; some see it as a
path to real local power the Civil Service Bureaucracy would otherwise never
grant them; a few are just true believers, drawn in by half-understood
Ancients reverence more than by any practical plan.

## 5.6 — THE PIT'S SHADOW: A MALIGNANT PSIONIC CORRUPTION

**Yes, as an optional lore layer a referee can choose to run with or ignore
entirely.** Something in the Reserve's depths radiates a faint, chronic
psionic corruption — not overt control, nothing anyone could point to or
prove, just a slow, ambient dampening effect across the whole town: pervasive
low-grade suspicion of outsiders, resistance to change or self-betterment, a
kind of learned communal fatalism that outsiders read as small-town
stubbornness and locals themselves would never think to name.

This fits Traveller's own setting texture well: psionics are officially
suppressed and viewed with deep suspicion across most of the Imperium, so
nobody in town would ever reach for that word even if they somehow understood
what they were describing. They just call the population "tired," "set in
their ways," or "not much for outsiders" — and the bureaucracy, true to form,
has no interest whatsoever in investigating something that would only
generate more paperwork and unwelcome Imperium scrutiny if it were ever
written down.

## 5.7 — DAILY LIFE & INSTITUTIONS

Texture for how all of the above actually feels to live inside, day to day:

- **School** — reading only; no testing. Literacy is maintained as a bare
  procedural formality. Testing would generate records, records would
  generate audits, and audits are the one thing this town's whole quiet
  arrangement can't survive.
- **Medicine** — weak salves, creams, and home therapy. No real clinic or
  pharmacy investment; anything serious means a long trip elsewhere, which
  circles back to Appendix B, §B.5's vehicle-repair problem being a genuine
  ordeal, not an inconvenience.
- **Law** — frontier justice by bureaucratic whim: standard practice for a
  dispute is to detain *both* parties until the relevant paperwork is fully
  settled, which — given the Big Queue below — can take weeks. Nobody wants
  the law involved for anything short of true necessity, which is exactly
  the outcome the arrangement quietly depends on.
- **Entertainment** — radio dramas, drawn out even further than old
  real-world serials like *Dark Shadows*; the pacing itself feels like
  another symptom of everything else here running at half-speed.
- **The Big Queue ("the BQ")** — the single ordering system for essentially
  every civil action: vehicle repairs, road and bridge fixes, permit
  approvals, anything requiring the bureaucracy to actually do something. An
  infamous, sprawling, strictly first-come queue that never seems to move —
  a running local joke and a running local despair in equal measure, and a
  reliable, low-cost detail to drop into any NPC's small talk (the motel
  owner grumbling about a repair still stuck in the BQ, a restaurant regular
  complaining about a permit filed months ago) without it needing to connect
  to anything else in this document set.
