# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Appendix C — The Lobby & Civilian Guests (Room 4)

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## C.0 — WHY THIS MATTERS

Room 4 and the lobby exist to make the motel feel like a real, occupied place rather
than a stage set built only for the plot — and to give the referee somewhere to put
low-stakes texture, red herrings, and cross-room mingling without every scene being
about SolSec tradecraft.

---

## C.1 — THE LOBBY

Small, worn, and functional: a few vinyl-cushioned chairs, a coin-op **vending
machine** (soda, cigarettes, stale snacks — TL7, coin-operated, no smart tech to
malfunction dramatically), a **jukebox or radio** playing low static-tinged music
most of the night, and — inconsistently, depending on the owner's mood — a pot of
coffee or a plate of something at the front desk.

It is the **only route** from any guest room to the parking lot and highway (Chapter
4, §4.1), which makes it the natural place for any two guests, PC or civilian, to
cross paths without it being suspicious. Use it as a pressure valve: a scene that's
gotten too intense in one room can spill into the lobby and suddenly involve someone
who has no idea what's actually going on.

**Random lobby event table** — roll or pick 1D6 whenever a PC passes through, or once
per shift as scene-setting:

| 1D6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | The jukebox skips on the same few bars of a song for an uncomfortably long stretch before someone fixes it. |
| 2 | The vending machine eats someone's coins. A small, human, deflating moment of frustration — good icebreaker material between strangers. |
| 3 | The motel owner (Appendix A) is at the desk, in a talkative mood, with an opinion about the weather, the storm, or "the usual crowd" this stretch of highway gets. |
| 4 | One of the Room 4 civilian guests (§C.2) is already here, doing something mundane — reading a paper, nursing a coffee, watching the door. |
| 5 | The lobby is briefly, completely empty — an unsettling contrast if the last few scenes have been tense elsewhere. |
| 6 | Someone new comes through the front door — not a guest checking in, just passing through to ask directions or use the payphone (which, per Appendix B §B.0, is dead due to the storm). |

## C.2 — ROOM 4: CIVILIAN GUESTS

Two ordinary travelers, entirely unconnected to SolSec, Imperial Intelligence, or
Baldwin Wei's network. They exist to be real — a grounding presence the PCs can
misread, investigate, or simply overhear, and a reminder that not everyone at the
motel tonight is a spy.

**Suggested pair:** a long-haul freight driver and a spouse or road-partner riding
along, stranded by the same storm blocking regional traffic (Appendix B, §B.0) as
everyone else.

- **Del Ashworth** — Age 54. UPP: 897765 *(STR 8 (0), DEX 9 (+1), END 7 (0), INT 7 (0), EDU 6 (0), SOC 5 (-1))*. Career: two terms **Merchant** (tramp-freighter crew) in his 20s
  before he aged out of ship-work and bought his own rig, then twenty-plus
  years running independent regional freight ever since — the same route,
  more or less, for most of that time. Irritable about the delay, mostly wants
  to sleep and get back on the road. Skills: Drive (wheeled) 1, Mechanic 0,
  Mechanic (repair) 1, Electronics 0, Electronics (comms) 1, Streetwise 0.
  No gear of consequence beyond CB radio chatter about the storm and highway
  conditions — a legitimate, mundane source of the traffic information
  referenced in Appendix B, §B.2.
- **Priya Ashworth** — Age 47. UPP: 665787 *(STR 6 (0), DEX 6 (0), END 5 (-1), INT 7 (0), EDU 8 (0), SOC 7 (0))*. No formal career terms — homeworld background skills only, from
  years of small-town admin and community work before she started riding
  along with Del rather than staying home alone. Restless and a little bored,
  the more likely of the two to strike up conversation in the lobby or notice
  something odd next door. Skills: Persuade 1, Streetwise 1, Admin 0,
  Admin (records) 1.

**Referee use:** The Ashworths should never turn out to be secretly anything. Their
value is entirely in being ordinary — a red herring PCs can investigate and rule out,
a source of harmless CB-radio traffic gossip, and a pair of genuine bystanders whose
safety quietly raises the stakes if the night turns violent. If a PC does something
overtly strange near them (uses visibly anomalous tech, raises their voice about
extraction), the Ashworths are a good, low-cost way to make a tech-discipline failure
(Appendix B, §B.1) feel immediately consequential without escalating to Imperial
Security Service involvement.

**Neural.AI image prompt (Del):** A heavyset, weathered man in his 50s wearing a
faded trucker cap and a flannel shirt, sitting on the edge of a motel bed working
loose the knot in his boot laces, a CB radio handset resting nearby. Tired,
irritable-but-harmless expression. Cheap motel room, warm lamp light. Photorealistic,
science fiction character portrait, no text, no logos.

**Neural.AI image prompt (Priya):** A woman in her 40s in a patterned housecoat,
sitting cross-legged on a motel bed flipping through a well-worn paperback, restless
energy in her posture as if she'd rather be anywhere else. Cheap motel room, warm
lamp light. Photorealistic, science fiction character portrait, no text, no logos.
