# TRAVELLER: NO CONTACT
## Appendix F — Skill Spotlight & Combat Hooks

*Referee Reference Document*

---

## F.0 — WHAT THIS COVERS

A cross-reference guide, not new mechanics: for each of the six PCs, at least
three of their listed skills (Chapter 6) tied to a concrete beat already
present elsewhere in this document set, so no player is sitting on an unused
skill all night. Also covers where to lean for a table with a
combat/military-minded player, since this scenario runs investigation-forward
by design.

---

## F.1 — HONEST FRAMING FOR A COMBAT-LEANING PLAYER

*No Contact* is built as a social-deduction/investigation scenario first —
that's a real tradeoff worth naming at the table, not hiding. That said, there
are genuine levers:

- **Big Tomas Reyes** (Chapter 6, §6.6) is the built-in action valve in both
  versions — Melee/Gun Combat focus, a literal kill-order as his Confederation
  secret objective, "positioned to physically neutralize the cell" as
  Imperium. He should feel useful the moment a scene turns physical.
- **Deputy Marsh** (Appendix E, §E.1) and the **ISS Watcher** version of the
  idling stranger (Appendix E, §E.2) both carry real weapons and real stakes
  once talking stops working.
- Chapter 3, §3.3 explicitly allows a **firefight** as one of three valid
  endings, alongside escape and stand-down.
- **Wei's black-op threshold** (Chapter 2, §2.4) is the strongest lever: a
  player who pushes the table toward aggression or carelessness is the one
  most likely to trigger it, which hands a combat-hungry player real
  narrative agency over whether the night turns violent — without the
  referee needing to force a fight that doesn't fit the rest of the table.

## F.2 — SKILL SPOTLIGHT BY CHARACTER

### Corvin Danh (Chapter 6, §6.1) — Persuade, Deception, Streetwise, Religion, Melee

- **Religion** — the cult-front cover is a legitimate lens for interpreting
  the Fence-Line Hum rumor (Appendix B, §B.3) or explaining late-night
  "ritual" activity if anyone notices Corvin awake and moving at odd hours.
- **Persuade/Deception** — talking down Deputy Marsh (Appendix E, §E.1)
  without resorting to Tomas's fists, or holding up under Yeva's
  cold-reading if she debriefs Corvin without knowing who he is (Chapter 6,
  §6.3).
- **Melee (unarmed)** — a real option if the restaurant's vanishing beat
  (Appendix D, §D.3) turns into a physical confrontation with staff.

### Dabney Kroll, "The Bulb Man" (Chapter 6, §6.2) — Streetwise, Recon, Stealth, Electronics

- **Recon** — the built-in payoff skill for the highway traffic table
  (Appendix B, §B.2); he's the PC who should roll here most often.
- **Electronics (comms)** — the natural skill to notice the ISS Watcher's
  TL12 scanner suite (Appendix E, §E.2) is off, or to investigate why a room
  check-in call (Appendix E, §E.4) sounded strange.
- **Stealth** — moving to check Room 4 or the restaurant without drawing
  attention, especially useful during the free-form Graveyard shift.

### Yeva Kessler (Chapter 6, §6.3) — Persuade, Deception, Investigate, Insight

- **Insight** — reading the lobby's random events (Appendix C, §C.1) for who's
  genuinely rattled versus performing calm — a strong fit for her established
  "debriefs people without them realizing" secret objective.
- **Investigate** — piecing together what Corrin's data chip or Corvin's
  rendezvous protocol might mean from overheard fragments, without directly
  asking.
- **Persuade/Deception** — extracting real information from Deputy Marsh or
  the motel owner (Appendix A) under a "reading" pretext.

### Dex (Chapter 6, §6.4) — Streetwise, Stealth, Athletics, Deception

- **Stealth** — the PC best positioned to move between rooms and the lobby
  unnoticed, given the "least suspicious" framing already built into both
  versions.
- **Athletics** — a real, earned use if the idling stranger (Appendix E,
  §E.2) or the vanishing-beat search (Appendix D, §D.3) turns into a chase
  or requires covering ground fast.
- **Deception** — holding the runaway cover story under direct questioning
  from Marsh or a suspicious fellow guest.

### Corrin Ashe (Chapter 6, §6.5) — Streetwise, Investigate, Persuade, Deception, Carouse

- **Investigate** — the PC mechanically built to connect her own data chip to
  Osei's compromised position (Chapter 1, §1.2) if she starts asking the
  right questions about why the handoff never happened.
- **Carouse** — a legitimate way to get the Ashworths or the motel owner
  talking in the lobby (Appendix C) without it reading as an interrogation.
- **Persuade/Deception** — reporter's-instinct needling that can pull real
  information out of Marsh or a fellow guest under a "just curious" cover.

### Big Tomas Reyes (Chapter 6, §6.6) — Melee, Gun Combat, Persuade, Streetwise

- **Gun Combat/Melee** — the scenario's primary combat-capable skill set;
  see §F.1 above for where this can actually come into play.
- **Persuade** — his "intersection of big money and low taste" cover
  supports an intimidation-flavored Persuade check that doesn't require
  drawing a weapon, useful for de-escalating without losing his edge.
- **Streetwise** — reading Marsh, the idling stranger, or the restaurant's
  regular (Appendix D, §D.2) through a criminal-world lens the other five
  PCs don't share.

## F.3 — REFEREE USE

None of the above needs to be scripted or forced — it's a checklist for
noticing, mid-session, when a PC hasn't had a moment to use their sheet, and a
quick answer for "what could I actually do here" if a player asks. If a table
leans hard into one thread (say, everyone chasing the mole-hunt) and a PC like
Tomas or Dabney is going unused, these are the fastest bridges back into the
scene for them specifically.
