## 4.1: THE MOTEL OWNER

![Earlene Vance, motel owner](img/motel-owner.webp)

A civilian anchor NPC; a non-player exposition and stakes source who is utterly unaware any of tonight's events are happening. Useful as:

- A "why should we care if this goes wrong" civilian who could get hurt.
- A red herring PCs suspect first, since they can investigate the owner without blowing their own cover.
- A source of mundane setting texture, front-desk gossip, who's stayed here before, what the highway traffic normally looks like.

**Name:** Earlene Vance

**Age:** 61

**UPP:** 578986 *(STR 5 (-1), DEX 7 (0), END 8 (0), INT 9 (+1), EDU 8 (0), SOC 6 (0))*

**Career history:** Two terms **Drifter** (Scavenger) out of her teens and twenties, chasing salvage and odd jobs across a couple of subsectors before the money ran out and the road did too. Bought into the motel with a partner twenty-nine years ago; been running it alone since the partner passed. Never left Jorjor again.

**Skills:** Streetwise 1, Persuade 1, Mechanic 0, Mechanic (repair) 1, Recon 1, Gambler 1, Carouse 1 *(the drifter years and three decades of running a roadside business, respectively; no specialization above 0 outside what's listed)*

**Gear:** None of consequence, cash box, a shotgun kept under the front desk (legal, unremarkable, TL7), a rotary phone.

**Role at the table:** Cash-only, asks-no-questions. Genuinely doesn't know what any of tonight's guests are. Should never be given secret SolSec/Imperial information by the referee; the whole point is that they're the one truly innocent presence in the building.

**Neural.AI image prompt:** A weathered, heavyset woman in her early 60s wearing a stained cardigan, standing behind a cluttered front desk lit by a flickering neon "VACANCY" sign visible through the window behind them. ## 4.2: ROOM 4, CIVILIAN GUESTS

![Del Ashworth](img/del-ashworth.webp)

![Priya Ashworth](img/priya-ashworth.webp)

Two ordinary travelers, entirely unconnected to SolSec, Imperial Intelligence, or Baldwin Wei's network. They exist to be real; a grounding presence the PCs can misread, investigate, or simply overhear, and a reminder that not everyone at the motel tonight is a spy.

**Suggested pair:** a long-haul freight driver and a spouse or road-partner riding along, stranded by the same storm blocking regional traffic (Chapter 2, §2.5) as everyone else.

- **Del Ashworth**: Age 54. UPP: 897765 *(STR 8 (0), DEX 9 (+1), END 7 (0), INT 7 (0), EDU 6 (0), SOC 5 (-1))*. Career: two terms **Merchant** (tramp-freighter crew) in his 20s before he aged out of ship-work and bought his own rig, then twenty-plus years running independent regional freight ever since; the same route, more or less, for most of that time. Irritable about the delay, mostly wants to sleep and get back on the road. Skills: Drive (wheeled) 1, Mechanic 0, Mechanic (repair) 1, Electronics 0, Electronics (comms) 1, Streetwise 0. No gear of consequence beyond CB radio chatter about the storm and highway conditions; a legitimate, mundane source of the traffic information referenced in Chapter 2, §2.7.
- **Priya Ashworth**: Age 47. UPP: 665787 *(STR 6 (0), DEX 6 (0), END 5 (-1), INT 7 (0), EDU 8 (0), SOC 7 (0))*. No formal career terms, homeworld background skills only, from years of small-town admin and community work before she started riding along with Del rather than staying home alone. Restless and a little bored, the more likely of the two to strike up conversation in the lobby or notice something odd next door. Skills: Persuade 1, Streetwise 1, Admin 0, Admin (records) 1.

**Del's load (Chapter 7):** Del is a **contract Feed Line hauler**. His tanker holds **28 tonnes of slaughterhouse offal** on a **stamped manifest (Consignment 9-4471)** bound for the Reserve's Feed Line Terminal (Chapter 2, §2.30, Area 8), stranded by the dust storm and a fouled fuel line (Chapter 5, §5.3). That is why his rig is the one vehicle that can pass a Red-alert gate. He knows the Reserve's drivers and hears its news on the CB: this afternoon it "closed up tight at five"; there were **shots, then a bang** around 4:20; and **areas 12 through 14 are shut** to camping. He mentions it unprompted if asked about the Reserve, and as idle gossip otherwise. He knows nothing about the pickup.

**Referee use:** The Ashworths should never turn out to be secretly anything. Their value is entirely in being ordinary; a red herring PCs can investigate and rule out, a source of harmless CB-radio traffic gossip, and a pair of genuine bystanders whose safety quietly raises the stakes if the night turns violent. If a PC does something overtly strange near them (uses visibly anomalous tech, raises their voice about extraction), the Ashworths are a good, low-cost way to make a tech-discipline failure (Chapter 2, §2.6) feel immediately consequential without escalating to Imperial Security Service involvement.

## 4.6: THE IDLING STRANGER

A car parked across the highway or at the lot's edge, engine running, headlights off. Deliberately built as **two interchangeable versions**, pick one before the session, or decide live based on how the table's doing, the same way the mole mechanic works (Chapter 1, §1.5).

### Version 1: Mundane

Nothing to do with tonight's plot. A genuinely lost or exhausted traveler who pulled over to nap, check a map, or wait out the worst of the storm before continuing, exactly the kind of ordinary, deflating explanation Chapter 2, §2.7 encourages for traffic events in general.

**Age:** 44

**UPP:** 655766 *(STR 6 (0), DEX 5 (-1), END 5 (-1), INT 7 (0), EDU 6 (0), SOC 6 (0))*

**Career history:** No terms of particular note; a traveling sales rep or similar, homeworld background skills only.

**Skills:** Persuade 1, Streetwise 0, Drive (wheeled) 1

**Gear:** Nothing of consequence; a TL7 sedan, a road map, a thermos.

**Referee use:** should resolve fast and mundanely if investigated; a tired, slightly embarrassed stranger who didn't realize how suspicious idling outside a motel at 3am looks. Good for deflating tension right when a table most expects it to escalate.

### Version 2: Shutter Project Watcher

Not Imperial at all; genuine surveillance, but local, and tied to the Strange Park's hidden funding scheme rather than the SolSec/Imperium plot (Chapter 2, §2.25–2.27). This person is quietly attached to the Shutter Project, watching the motel because unusual activity nearby (stranded storm traffic, an unfamiliar police presence, six new guests in one night) is exactly the kind of attention that could get someone asking questions about the body-bank accounts or the Project itself. Their concern is entirely selfish and local: protecting a decades-old secret, not stopping an extraction. Use this version when a referee wants dread and outside attention to feel real without pulling Imperial Intelligence into a scenario that's meant to stay self-contained. Now that the Reserve is the reason the extraction never came (Chapters 7–8), the Watcher is also a small, early warning of the Reserve's own attention: he can mention "the closed areas" if cornered.

**Age:** 41

**UPP:** 776655 *(STR 7 (0), DEX 7 (0), END 6 (0), INT 6 (0), EDU 5 (-1), SOC 5 (-1))*

**Career history:** No formal career terms; a lifelong Jorjor local, recruited into the Shutter Project's watch rotation the way most of its people are: knew someone, needed the quiet extra income the body-bank float can discreetly provide, and never asked too many questions about where the money actually came from.

**Skills:** Recon 1, Stealth 1, Electronics 0, Electronics (comms) 1, Streetwise 1, Persuade 0

**Gear:** A genuinely homemade TL7 directional radio receiver, wired together from surplus and salvaged parts; the same scrappy, vacuum-tube-era electromagnetics the whole Shutter Project runs on (Chapter 2, §2.26), not anything that would read as anomalous if searched. No weapon beyond whatever's locally unremarkable to carry (Chapter 2, §2.1's High Law firearms allowance).

**Referee use:** should never approach or make contact voluntarily; the whole point is passive observation from a safe distance, same caution as any nervous amateur trying not to be noticed. If confronted directly, the watcher drives off rather than escalates, and under real pressure would rather admit to small-time local snooping (fear of a jealous spouse, petty surveillance for the town's rumor mill) than reveal anything about the Project; a plausible, mundane lie that happens to be protecting something much stranger underneath. This version deliberately has **no TL gap** to notice, unlike the Version B mole's gear (Chapter 5, §5.1), there's nothing here to mechanically detect, which keeps it a genuine, separate thread rather than a second copy of the same tell.

**Neural.AI image prompt:** A weathered, unremarkable local in a worn jacket, sitting low in the driver's seat of a plain sedan parked at the edge of a dark desert highway, engine running, headlights off, a homemade radio receiver with exposed wiring resting on the passenger seat. Nervous, furtive posture rather than professional stillness. Night desert backdrop. Photorealistic, science fiction scene, no text, no logos.

## 4.7: DEPUTY COMPTROLLER YANNICK OSEI (THE PRIZE)

![Deputy Comptroller Yannick Osei](img/yannick-osei.webp)

The answer isn't a thing. It's a stamp.

Jorjor's government is a **Civil Service Bureaucracy**, and per the pattern this government type follows, its administration runs on exactly the kind of fastidious, jealously-guarded procedural minutiae that makes a "Jobsworth's World": permits and restrictions abound, demarcation lines between departments are exactly drawn, and inspections and manifests are treated as sacred process rather than practical security.

**That rigidity is the vulnerability.** SolSec doesn't want anything buried, mined, or built on Jorjor. It wants **quiet, repeatable control of one procedural chokepoint**: the office that stamps off-world cargo and personnel manifests as inspected and cleared.

### The compromised official

**Deputy Comptroller Yannick Osei**, Off-World Manifest & Clearance Office, Jorjor Civil Service Bureaucracy; a career bureaucrat, twenty-two years in the department, utterly unremarkable on paper.

**Age:** 55

**UPP:** 546598 *(STR 5 (-1), DEX 4 (-1), END 6 (0), INT 5 (-1), EDU 9 (+1), SOC 8 (0))*

**Career history:** Four terms **Bureaucrat**, entirely within Jorjor's Civil Service Bureaucracy, climbing by seniority and procedural correctness rather than any particular ability, exactly the promotion path the government type rewards. Twenty-two years in the Off-World Manifest & Clearance Office specifically.

**Skills:** Admin 2, Persuade 1, Streetwise 0, Electronics 0 *(genuinely minimal, see below)*, Gambler 1 *(the debt that got him turned in the first place)*

SolSec identified Osei four years ago through a pattern any patient handler would recognize: chronic gambling debt, quietly spiraling, invisible to his superiors because his departmental paperwork was flawless. A soft approach (help with the debt, no strings, "a friend") became leverage inside eighteen months. Osei now has the authority to issue **administrative clearance stamps** that route a shipment or a travel manifest past physical inspection entirely; a routine, boring, correctly filed exception that a bureaucracy this proud of its own procedure never double-checks, because *questioning a properly stamped form* is itself procedurally irregular.

**What this actually buys SolSec:** not a single prize, but a standing **smuggling corridor**; the ability to move personnel, documents, and material through Jorjor's Class B starport undetected, repeatedly, for as long as Osei's position and cover hold. That's why this motel, in this jurisdiction, on this world: it sits inside Osei's practical reach, close enough for an asset in trouble to be plausibly cleared through his office on short notice.

### Inept with technology, holds the keys anyway

Osei's **Electronics skill sits at 0**, not a gap in the writeup, a real and important fact about him. He does not understand, in any functional sense, the systems his office nominally controls. He can operate the terminal that issues a clearance stamp because the interface is built to require nothing more than that, and because he's done the same handful of keystrokes for over two decades. Ask him to troubleshoot it, explain how it verifies anything, or notice when something about it is behaving oddly, and he's lost.

**How he passes Imperial competency reviews anyway:** Jorjor's civil service requires periodic technical-proficiency testing for anyone holding a clearance-issuing post. Osei passes these using a **mind-enhancing pharmaceutical** (referee's call on a period-appropriate name and TL, something TL8-9, short-acting, legitimately prescribed for exam performance rather than exotic or illegal) that sharpens recall and test-taking focus for a few hours at a time without granting any lasting understanding. He walks out of every review having technically passed, and forgets most of what the test covered within a day. Nobody above him has ever had reason to look closer, because the test result is the only thing the bureaucracy's own procedure actually checks, "questioning a properly stamped form is itself procedurally irregular" applies just as well to questioning a properly passed exam.

**Referee use:** if a PC ever needs Osei to *do* something technical under pressure (investigate an anomaly, verify a system remotely, adapt on the fly) he cannot, full stop, regardless of how much he's threatened or bribed. This is a hard limit, not a skill check to beat. It's also a quiet source of dramatic irony: SolSec's whole corridor runs through a man who doesn't understand the system he's exploiting any better than the bureaucracy that employs him does.

### The quota

Osei's other survival mechanism is entirely mundane: he **processes a steady quota of ordinary, legitimate manifest clearances** every cycle, enough real, boring, correctly-handled cases that his output looks like bare-minimum competent diligence to anyone doing a surface review. The SolSec-compromised exceptions are a small, carefully limited fraction hidden inside a much larger body of genuine work. He treats the quota itself as his real job security, more than the bribe money or the blackmail leverage, "keep the numbers normal" is his own survival instinct as much as SolSec's operational requirement.

**Referee use:** this is the mechanism behind the "internal audit" explanation below for why this ties to no extraction. An audit wouldn't find Osei's compromised clearances by volume, his numbers look ordinary, it would only find them by pattern, which is exactly the kind of narrow, specific anomaly a jealous rival department (per Jorjor's administrative culture) might stumble onto while looking for something else entirely.

### He asks questions sometimes

Osei is not a pushover or a fool, and playing him as one undersells the character. Every so often (unpredictably, not on any schedule a PC could game) genuine scrutiny surfaces: he'll ask a real, specific question about a manifest, push back on a request, or notice a detail that doesn't fit. This isn't competence breaking through the fog; it's exactly the fastidious, procedure-worshipping instinct the whole bureaucracy runs on, firing on him the same as it would on anyone else in the department, entirely independent of whether he understands the technology behind what he's looking at.

**Referee use:** a good beat if Osei ever appears directly (he doesn't need to, see below) is to let him ask one sharp, correct, procedurally-minded question that has nothing to do with technology and everything to do with paperwork, and let it briefly, genuinely rattle whichever PC is dealing with him, before he moves on and signs off anyway.

### Neural.AI image prompt

A soft, unremarkable man in his mid-50s in a rumpled civil-service uniform, seated at a cluttered desk stacked with paper forms rather than any visible technology, wire-rimmed glasses pushed up on his forehead, an absent, slightly-too-focused expression as if concentrating hard on something simple. Fluorescent government-office lighting, beige walls, a small pill bottle half-hidden under a stack of forms. Photorealistic, science fiction character portrait, no text, no logos.

### Why this ties to "no extraction"

Bureaucracies built on Jorjor's exacting, procedure-worshipping model are also *compulsive record-keepers*, precisely the trait that made Osei useful is the same trait that could expose him. Pick one (or seed both as red herrings):

- **An internal audit.** Some other department, jealously competing for budget and relevance per the world's own administrative culture, ran a routine cross-check and flagged an anomalous pattern of clearances bearing Osei's stamp. If this is the cause, Osei may currently be in an "interview" with Internal Compliance, not arrested, not yet, just delayed, and unreachable.
- **Osei got cold feet.** Four years is a long time to live compromised. He may have simply stopped answering, uncertain whether the safer move is to keep serving SolSec or to confess and throw himself on the bureaucracy's notoriously procedural mercy (which, per the government's own texture, tends to go easy on anyone willing to correctly fill out the right form of contrition).
- **Imperial counter-intelligence found him first**: independently of, or in coordination with, the Version: Imperium mole seeded among tonight's guests (Chapter 1, §1.5). If so, Osei may already be turned, and the "clearance" the PCs are waiting on could arrive from the wrong direction entirely.

### Referee use

Osei never needs to appear in person for this to work; his absence, like Wei's, is load-bearing. If a PC (in either version) tries to independently reach the manifest office tonight, that's a legitimate way to surface Graveyard-shift information (Chapter 7, §7.1): a busy signal, a night-shift clerk who's never heard of any "administrative exception," or, worse, someone on the other end who asks a few too many careful questions before hanging up.

## 4.8: THE PICKUP: OVERVIEW

The extraction the six guests are waiting for is real. A four-person SolSec team came for them. They landed **a day early**, in a dry wash just outside the Reserve fence, because their controller (Baldwin Wei) told them only to "check that nothing has gone bad," and being good at their jobs they went to look. They used the spare day to walk into the park on a day tour, as any tourist would. They were intrigued by the one thing within an hour's drive of the motel that appears on no chart of normal worlds: the Halvorsen Basin Biological Reserve (Chapter 2, §2.17; Chapter 7). They took a tour. There was an accident.

This section gives you the team, their ship, the key fobs that unlock it, what they did that provoked the Mass, and what happened to them.

## 4.12: RESERVE RESCUE SERVICE AND CAPTAIN PELL

Like any big water park, the Reserve keeps trained rescuers on call, because visitors get into trouble every day. The **Reserve Rescue Service (RRS)** has eight staff and a handful of volunteers in orange vests, a Rescue Station at Area 5, a rescue sled, a first-aid clinic annex at the Gate, and a whistle for every ranger. They respond to a few hundred calls a year, from twisted ankles to the 10% (§2.24) who simply never come back.

**Why they have not rescued the pickup.** At 16:34 the RRS dispatcher logged a call from the Terrace (shots, a bang, a collapse, four visitors).
